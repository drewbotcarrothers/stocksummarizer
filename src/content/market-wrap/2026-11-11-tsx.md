## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 11

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **36,411.72**, up **241.27 points (+0.67%)** on Remembrance Day. That won back most of Tuesday's 275-point drop. Canadian stock markets stay open on November 11.

The index opened near 36,337, dipped to a low around 36,239, and touched about 36,495 at its best. Volume was light, at about 205 million shares. Breadth was positive: advancers beat decliners on the Toronto exchange by 557 to 405, with 70 unchanged.

The Canadian dollar slipped to about **70.2 US cents**, down roughly a third of a cent from Tuesday.

Wall Street had a stronger day. The Dow rose about 255 points to roughly 52,486, the S&P 500 gained 98 points (+1.3%) to 7,619.38, and the Nasdaq jumped 400 points (+1.6%) to 25,466.19.

## What drove the TSX on November 11

**The AI trade came back.** US cloud company CoreWeave rallied about 20% after its third-quarter revenue beat expectations and it raised its 2026 guidance. Nvidia also rebounded after back-to-back losing sessions. That lifted chip and AI-linked names on both sides of the border, and Canadian tech was the best group on the TSX.

**Oil held firm, and energy stocks ran.** US crude edged up about 0.4% to roughly US$86.37 a barrel, while Brent dipped slightly to about US$92.55. Canadian producers still rallied, and energy was the second-best group.

**Gold kept cooling.** Gold fell about US$38, or 0.9%, to roughly US$3,992 an ounce, a second straight day of losses after Monday's big rally. That kept gold miners and the wider materials group in the red.

**Bank of Canada minutes.** Notes from the Bank of Canada's last meeting, where it raised rates for the first time in more than three years, showed policymakers see a lot of uncertainty about higher energy prices and the US trade war. They also agreed that uncertainty would not stop them from acting again if needed. Baystreet said markets were pricing about a 48% chance of another hike.

## Sectors on the move

Eight of the 12 main subgroups finished higher, according to Baystreet.

- **Information technology: about +2.8%**, the session leader, led by Celestica, Kinaxis and BlackBerry.
- **Energy: about +2.1%**, with Cenovus, Suncor and Canadian Natural higher.
- **Financials: about +1%**, as all of the big five banks rose between 0.7% and 1.1%.
- **Industrials** were also among the leaders, helped by Bird Construction.
- **Gold: about −1.5%**, the weakest group.
- **Materials: about −1.1%**, and **telecom: about −0.4%**.

- **Celestica (CLS): about +8.2% to +8.4%**, to roughly $345, the top Composite gainer, as the AI hardware trade rebounded.
- **Kinaxis (KXS): about +6.4%**, to $229.37, a record high, adding to Tuesday's gain.
- **Bird Construction (BDT): about +5.6%**, to $76.89.
- **BlackBerry (BB): about +5.1%**, to $13.62.
- **Freehold Royalties (FRU): about +4.6%**, to $17.20, and **Spartan Delta (SDE): about +4.5%**, to $11.69, leading energy.
- **Cenovus (CVE): about +3%**, to $40.51, and **Shopify (SHOP): about +2.1%**, to $212.57.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Risk appetite improved. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX, a gauge of expected volatility, fell about 6% to 15.52. Small caps lagged, though: the TSX Venture Exchange slipped about 5 points to 817.02.

The US bond market was closed for Veterans Day, so there was no fresh US Treasury signal. The loonie eased against the US dollar, and the Bank of Canada minutes kept the door open to more hikes. The next rate decision is **December 9**.

A rebound in the AI trade and firm oil lifted Canadian tech, energy and banks, while softer gold and a few sharp earnings misses kept the gain in check.
