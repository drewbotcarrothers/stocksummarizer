## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 6

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,789.09**, up **183.82 points (+0.52%)**. That clawed back part of Thursday's 736-point slide and left the index up about 169 points, or 0.48%, for the week.

The index opened near 35,501, dipped to about 35,397 early, then climbed to a high near 35,946 before settling. Volume was roughly 315 million shares, lighter than Thursday's heavy tape. Breadth improved: advancers beat decliners on the Toronto exchange by about 583 to 345.

The Canadian dollar weakened to about **70.80 US cents**, from roughly 71.48 on Thursday.

Wall Street was mixed but calmer than Thursday. The Nasdaq rose about 264 points (+1.1%) to 24,579.43 as chip names bounced. The S&P 500 edged up about 23 points to 7,376.92, while the Dow slipped about 237 points to 51,286.21. All three major US averages were still on track for weekly losses after Thursday's rout.

## What drove the TSX on November 6

**A US tech bounce spilled north.** After Thursday's AI and chip selloff, dip buyers returned. Nvidia, Micron and other US tech names recovered, and Canada's technology group followed. Celestica and Shopify both rose after sharp losses the day before.

**Canada's jobs report was soft, but the tape still finished higher.** Statistics Canada said employment was little changed in October (−4,700; 0.0%) and the unemployment rate rose 0.2 percentage points to **6.7%**, above the 6.5% rate many economists expected. Private-sector jobs fell by 53,000 while public-sector employment rose by 50,000. Alberta lost 24,000 jobs; British Columbia gained 12,000. The index opened lower on the print, then reversed as US tech and resource names improved.

**US payrolls were much stronger.** Nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October, well above forecasts near 60,000, and the US unemployment rate edged down to 4.1%. That supported risk appetite even as US Treasury yields ticked higher.

**Budget 2026 got its first trading day.** Ottawa tabled the budget after Thursday's close. Early market coverage noted trimmed deficit forecasts through 2030/31 and slightly softer 2027 growth assumptions. Analysts also flagged defence, space, northern infrastructure and mineral-exploration tax-credit extensions as possible supports for select names. The broader index move looked more like a tech and materials rebound than a pure budget trade.

**Gold stocks rose even as the metal slipped.** December gold fell about US$39–US$41 to roughly US$4,073–US$4,074 an ounce, yet the gold subgroup still led. Earnings updates and the budget's exploration incentives helped the group. Oil was little changed near US$90.67–US$90.78 a barrel.

## Sectors on the move

- **Gold: about +3%**, among the strongest groups, led by Iamgold and Allied Gold.
- **Health care: about +3%**, with Bausch Health and Chartwell among the better-known gainers.
- **Materials: about +2.5%**, helped by Lundin Mining and Perpetua Resources.
- **Information technology:** among the session leaders after Thursday's about −3.5% to −3.8% drop, with Shopify and Celestica recovering.
- **Consumer discretionary: about −0.8%**, the weakest named group, with Aritzia and Linamar lower.
- **Telecom and industrials** were among the softer areas, with Telus, Rogers, NFI Group and WSP Global down.

Seven of the 12 main subgroups finished higher. (Sector figures differ slightly by source.)

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Perpetua Resources (PPTA): about +17.5% to +17.7%**, to roughly $27.62–$27.67, the session's top Composite gainer.
- **Iamgold (IMG): about +8.1%**, to $26.44, after reporting third-quarter EPS of $0.42 versus $0.30 a year earlier.
- **5N Plus (VNP): about +8.3%**, to $33.19, and **Denison Mines (DML): about +7.8%**, to $3.13.
- **Allied Gold (AAUC): about +6.8%**, to $32.54, extending a strong week after an earnings outlook update.
- **Lundin Mining (LUN): about +5.5%**, to $29.59.
- **Shopify (SHOP): about +4.6%**, to $210.47, and **Celestica (CLS): about +1.3%**, to $292.16, recovering part of Thursday's tech washout.
- **Trisura Group (TSU): about −29.3%**, to $29.83, after third-quarter results showed pressure from unfavourable casualty claims in US programs even as operating earnings held up.
- **Parex Resources (PXT): about −17.4%**, to $19.61, after third-quarter EPS missed estimates.
- **NFI Group (NFI): about −6.9% to −8.5%**, and **WSP Global (WSP): about −4.2%**, to $181.45.
- **Telus (T): about −2.9%**, to $12.81, and **Rogers (RCI.B): about −1.3%**, to $47.12.

Smaller names also swung hard on earnings. Ensign Energy Services jumped about 7.5% after swinging to a third-quarter profit. Interfor fell about 12% after reporting a net loss.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The Canadian dollar was the soft spot. It slipped to about 70.80 US cents as the softer domestic jobs print weighed on rate-cut expectations relative to a hotter US payrolls number. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.86% from about 4.84%.

Fear eased a little. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX slipped about 0.6% to 17.31, still elevated after Thursday's jump. Small caps did better than the big board: the TSX Venture Exchange rose about 15.71 points, or 2%, to 822.03.

The Bank of Canada's next rate decision remains **December 9**.

## What to watch next

- **Monday–Tuesday open:** Whether the tech rebound holds after a week that included a midterm-driven AI scare and a Friday bounce.
- **Oil and the Strait of Hormuz:** Crude is still near US$90 after a violent two-day swing earlier in the week, so energy remains a large swing factor for the index.
- **Budget 2026 follow-through:** Defence, space, northern infrastructure and mineral-exploration measures may keep showing up in single-name moves even if they do not drive the whole tape.
- **Friday, December 4:** The next Labour Force Survey.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision.

## Bottom line

A US tech bounce, stronger US jobs data, and leadership from gold and materials helped the TSX reclaim about a quarter of Thursday's drop, even as Canada's unemployment rate rose to 6.7% and the loonie slipped.
