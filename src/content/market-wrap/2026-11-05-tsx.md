## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 5

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,605.27**, down **736.13 points (-2.03%)**. That was its biggest one-day drop, in both points and percent, since June 5. It gave back most of Wednesday's 809-point jump.

The index opened near 36,510, touched a high of about 36,549 early, then slid all day to a low of about 35,514. Volume was roughly 391 million shares, close to Wednesday's heavy level. Breadth was poor: decliners beat advancers on the Toronto exchange by about 732 to 227.

The index is now about 3.7% below its record close of 36,957.63 from August 25, but still up about 12.3% for the year.

The Canadian dollar slipped to **71.48 US cents**, from 71.63 on Wednesday.

Wall Street had a much worse day. The Dow fell 1,833.06 points (about -3.4%) to 51,523.06, the S&P 500 dropped 334.89 points (about -4.4%) to 7,353.73, and the Nasdaq sank 1,421.52 points (about -5.5%) to 24,315.00.

## What drove the TSX on November 5

**A US tech selloff spilled into Canada.** Chip and data-centre stocks in New York fell for a second day. Investors worried that Democrats' sweep of Congress and key state races could bring tougher rules for the AI buildout. Nvidia and Broadcom each fell about 9%, and Qualcomm dropped about 10% on a weak outlook even after beating estimates. Canada's tech group followed, led lower by Celestica, which makes hardware for data centres.

**Not a full retreat from stocks, one manager said.** BMO Global Asset Management's chief investment officer, Sadiq Adatia, told Reuters there was "a bit of nervousness on the technology side," but called the selloff a sector rotation rather than a broader exit from equities. The day's winners, telecoms and gold, fit that read: money moved toward defensive and hard-asset groups.

**Earnings hit a few big names hard.** Gildan Activewear missed third-quarter estimates and cut its full-year outlook. Sun Life beat on underlying earnings but drew two analyst downgrades over US fund outflows and disability claims. Both are covered below.

**Oil bounced back.** December crude rose US$2.40, or about 2.7%, to US$90.52 a barrel, after new attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz renewed supply worries. That only partly made up Wednesday's 8% drop, and energy stocks still fell.

**Ottawa's budget landed after the bell.** Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne tabled Budget 2026 after the close, so it did not affect Thursday's trading. Early commentary said the government kept to its fiscal anchors: the deficit is projected at about 1.8% of GDP this fiscal year and next, with federal debt near 40% of GDP. Friday's trading will be the first market reaction.

## Sectors on the move

- **Information technology: about -3.5% to -3.8%**, the worst group. Celestica and Shopify led the drop.
- **Financials: about -2.9% to -3.2%**, a big drag because it is the index's heaviest group. Sun Life and IGM Financial were the weakest names.
- **Consumer discretionary: about -2.7% to -2.9%**, pulled down by Gildan and Linamar.
- **Energy: about -2.5%**, even with oil higher.
- **Industrials: about -1.9%**, with Bombardier down sharply.
- **Telecom: about +2.4%**, one of only two groups to rise. BCE and Quebecor led.
- **Gold: about +2%**, as December gold rose US$40.50 to US$4,113.50 an ounce.

All but two of the 12 main subgroups ended lower. (Sector figures differ slightly by source.)

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Gildan Activewear (GIL): about -18.9%**, to roughly $49. Third-quarter sales and adjusted earnings missed estimates, and the apparel maker lowered its full-year guidance, citing weaker demand.
- **Russel Metals (RUS): about -13%**, to roughly $77, after reporting third-quarter results.
- **Celestica (CLS): about -12.7%**, to $288.43, as the AI hardware trade sold off.
- **Sun Life Financial (SLF): -10.2%**, to $102.83. Underlying EPS rose 10% to $2.05 and the dividend went up 4%, but reported profit fell 29%, US disability claims came in worse than expected, and its US fund manager MFS had heavy outflows. TD Cowen and National Bank both downgraded the stock.
- **Bombardier (BBD.B): about -8.4%**, even after raising its 2026 free cash flow forecast on strong private jet demand.
- **IGM Financial (IGM): -6.2%**, to $79.51, and **Shopify (SHOP): -5.4%**, to $201.23.
- **Labrador Iron Ore Royalty (LIF): about +8.5%**, to about $22, one of the session's top gainers.
- **OceanaGold (OGC): about +6.5%**, **Allied Gold (AAUC): +6.4%**, and **Torex Gold (TXR): about +6.6%**, with gold.
- **BCE (BCE): +4.6%**, to $30.83, and **Quebecor (QBR.B): +4.4%**, to $74.76.

Two other big reporters moved less. Canadian Natural Resources beat third-quarter profit estimates on higher crude prices and output but slipped about 1% to $68.48. Nutrien missed estimates on higher sulfur costs and lower nitrogen volumes, yet rose 74 cents to $101.10.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Bonds rallied as stocks fell. Canada's 10-year yield eased to about 3.67%–3.70%, its lowest in more than two months, from about 3.70%–3.74% on Wednesday. The US 10-year Treasury yield slipped to about 4.84% from 4.88%. The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate last month, and its next decision is December 9.

Fear rose. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX jumped about 19% to 17.65, a six-month high. Small caps fell too: the TSX Venture Exchange lost about 1% to 796.97.

## What to watch next

- **Friday, November 6:** Canada's October Labour Force Survey and the US October jobs report, both key reads for the Bank of Canada and the Fed. Friday is also the market's first chance to react to Budget 2026.
- **AI and chip stocks:** Whether the US tech selloff keeps going. Celestica and Shopify are Canada's most direct exposure.
- **Oil and the Strait of Hormuz:** Crude swung 8% down then about 3% up in two days, so energy remains a big swing factor for the index.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision.

## Bottom line

A second day of US tech selling, plus earnings shocks at Gildan and Sun Life, gave the TSX its worst day since June. Telecoms and gold were the only groups that held up.
