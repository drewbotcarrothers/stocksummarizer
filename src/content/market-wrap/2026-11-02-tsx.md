## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 2

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,801.09**, up **181.06 points (+0.51%)**. That won back a little under a third of Friday's 629-point drop. The index opened at 35,723.41 and was up more than 300 points by mid-morning, reaching a high of 36,042.35. It then gave back about 241 points into the close. The low of the day, 35,682.49, stayed above Friday's close of 35,620.03. Volume was about 287 million shares, lighter than Friday's heavy selloff but still busy.

The Canadian dollar eased to **71.54 US cents**, from 71.63 on Friday.

Wall Street was mixed. The Dow rose 213.67 points to 51,983.98, but the S&P 500 slipped 38.67 points to 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq fell 272.68 points (about 1.1%) to 25,164.32. Investors kept moving out of tech and into "old economy" stocks.

## What drove the TSX on November 2

**Oil fell hard on hopes for Middle East peace talks.** Tehran said talks with the US to end the eight-month war were ongoing after Qatari-mediated discussions produced revised proposals, though no breakthrough. December crude settled down US$7.66 (about 7.4%) at US$96.46 a barrel. That's a big one-day move, and it hit Canadian energy producers. It also helped everything else, because lower oil eases inflation worries and takes some pressure off central banks.

**Gold bounced from a one-year low.** December gold rose US$70.70 to US$3,969.60 an ounce, about 1.8%, a day after its lowest close in roughly a year. Lower oil cooled fears that the Federal Reserve would have to keep raising rates, and gold tends to do better when expected rates fall. Gold miners bounced harder than the metal, the same leverage that hurt them on Friday.

**New-month repositioning.** After a rough October for materials, investors put money back to work. One Raymond James portfolio manager called it "re-positioning for the new month," helped by oil and Middle East news.

**Trade headlines were mildly positive.** US President Donald Trump said on Sunday his administration has a framework for a new trade deal with Canada, and Prime Minister Mark Carney called the talks constructive. On the other side, S&P Global's Canada manufacturing PMI fell to 49.6 in October from 51.5, the first contraction this year. Readings below 50 mean activity is shrinking. Tariffs and geopolitical uncertainty were cited.

## Sectors on the move

- **Industrials: about +2.5%**, one of the best groups, led by engineering and construction names.
- **Real estate: about +2.6%**. Rate-sensitive stocks gained as bond yields fell.
- **Materials: about +2.3%**, after falling 13.1% in October. The gold subgroup gained about 2.9%.
- **Financials: about +1%**. Because banks are such a large part of the index, this did a lot of the lifting. Royal Bank rose about 0.8% and Bank of Montreal about 1.1%.
- **Energy: about -4.8%**, by far the worst group, as crude slid. Canadian Natural Resources fell about 4.2%.
- **Information technology: about -1%**, still under pressure from the US tech selloff.

Only two major sectors finished lower, energy and tech. Gainers beat decliners on the Toronto exchange by about 526 to 436 in late trade.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Colliers International (CIGI): +8.0%**, to $146.13, and **FirstService (FSV): +5.0%**, to $212.31, leading real estate services.
- **Aecon Group (ARE): +7.7%**, to $53.01, and **WSP Global (WSP): +5.4%**, to $196.53, as industrials added to gains. Air Canada and Boyd Group were each up close to 6% in the morning.
- **Eldorado Gold (ELD): +5.4%**, to $45.01, and **B2Gold (BTO): +4.2%**, to $6.68, as gold rebounded. B2Gold won back about half of Friday's drop.
- **Baytex Energy (BTE): about -11%**, to $6.69, among the index's worst performers. **Strathcona Resources (SCR)** fell about 10% and **Vermilion Energy (VET)** about 8.5%. Oil-weighted producers took the brunt of the crude slide.
- **Celestica (CLS): -5.7%**, to $348.45, extending Friday's tech losses.

The pattern was the reverse of Friday. When oil drops this much, energy falls but most other groups gain, because fuel costs and inflation worries ease. The TSX carries a heavy energy weight, which is why the index ended only modestly higher even with broad gains elsewhere.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Bond yields fell. Canada's 10-year yield eased 4.9 basis points to 3.750%, after touching a two-month low of 3.737% during the day. The Bank of Canada has worried that high energy costs could spread into broader inflation, so a sharp drop in oil makes more hikes look less likely. The US 10-year Treasury yield edged down to about 4.91%.

The loonie slipped as oil, one of Canada's main exports, fell. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX, a measure of expected volatility, fell to about 15.7. Risk appetite didn't reach small resource stocks: the TSX Venture Exchange slipped 3.47 points to 800.31.

## What to watch next

- **Monday evening, November 2:** Apple reports after the US close, the next test for tech sentiment after a rough week for AI-related stocks.
- **Tuesday, November 3:** US midterm elections. Traders have cited them as a reason for caution, along with their impact on fiscal policy.
- **Thursday, November 5:** The federal budget, with attention on infrastructure spending and how much fiscal room remains.
- **Friday, November 6:** Canada's October Labour Force Survey and the US monthly jobs report, key reads for the Bank of Canada and the Fed.

## Bottom line

A 7% drop in oil sank Canadian energy stocks but lifted almost everything else, and the TSX recovered part of Friday's loss to start November.
