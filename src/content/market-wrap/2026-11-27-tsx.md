## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 27

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **34,839.87**, up **412.99 points (+1.20%)**. Reuters said it was the index's highest close since November 17. It was the TSX's third straight gain.

The bigger story was the week. The Composite rose **4.97%** from last Friday's close, its biggest weekly gain since October 2023, Reuters said. That came right after an **8.1%** drop the week before. Even so, the index is still about **2.2%** below its October 30 close of 35,620.03, with one trading day left in November.

The index opened higher and stayed there. Delayed Yahoo Finance data showed it opened at 34,734.66 and traded between **34,648.90 and 34,877.81**, never falling below Thursday's close.

Breadth was strong. Advancers beat decliners **718 to 235**, with 71 unchanged, Investing.com said. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX, a gauge of expected volatility, fell 2.32% to 18.11. The small-cap TSX Venture Exchange rose **16.58 points** to **737.25**, Stockhouse reported.

Wall Street reopened after Thanksgiving for a shortened session that ended at 1 p.m. ET. The Dow rose 591.99 points to 50,774.94, the S&P 500 added 114.71 points to 7,297.49 and the Nasdaq gained 543.70 points to 24,129.50, The Canadian Press said.

## What drove the TSX on November 27

**Gold and copper bounced hard.** Reuters said the price of gold rose 2.4%, extending its recovery from a 14-month low earlier in the week, while copper added 3.4%. The Canadian Press had the February gold contract up **US$103.90 at US$3,668.50** an ounce. Metal miners are a big part of the TSX, so the rebound did much of the lifting.

**AI stocks recovered.** Investors went back into the AI-linked shares that were hit hard earlier this month, on both sides of the border. Big Tech led the gains in New York, The Canadian Press said, and Canadian tech followed. "It's because we're at the point in the cycle where AI profitability is sharply in scope," David Wong of CIBC Global Asset Management told Reuters, describing a market with a "staccato pace."

**Oil eased but held its ground for the week.** US crude futures settled 0.7% lower at **US$83.08 a barrel**, Reuters said, but still broke a run of weekly losses. Attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy infrastructure kept Middle East supply worries alive.

**A softer current account.** Statistics Canada said Canada's current account surplus narrowed to **$4.5 billion** in the third quarter from $6.1 billion in the second. It was still the first time since 2022 that Canada posted two surpluses in a row. The goods surplus held steady at $10.7 billion. The report did little to move the market.

## Sectors on the move

- **Materials: +4.6%**, the clear leader, as gold and copper rallied (Reuters).
- **Information technology: +2.5%**, as AI-linked names bounced back.
- **Industrials: +0.7%** and **energy: +0.4%**, with energy holding up despite softer oil.
- Only two of the major sectors fell, Reuters said. **Consumer staples** led the decline at **−1%**.
- **Banks lagged** the broader market, FX.co said, as worries grew that energy-driven inflation could keep rates higher. **Royal Bank (RY)** slipped 0.3% to $267.95, while **TD (TD)** rose 0.4% to $166.23.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Seabridge Gold (SEA): +10.29%** to $30.54, the top gainer on the Composite, Investing.com said.
- **Americas Gold and Silver (USA): +9.79%** to $3.14, adding to Thursday's leading gain.
- **Skeena Resources (SKE): +9.51%** to $41.58.
- Large gold producers rallied: **Kinross Gold (K)** rose about 5.6% to $28.39, **Agnico Eagle (AEM)** gained about 5.2% to $245.85 and **Barrick (ABX)** added about 4.3% to $51.98, based on Yahoo Finance closing data.
- In copper, **First Quantum (FM)** rose about 4.8% to $27.21 and **Teck (TECK.B)** gained about 3.7% to $76.92.
- In tech, **Shopify (SHOP)** gained about 4.9% to $202.90 and **Celestica (CLS)** added about 3.6% to $290.26.
- **Allied Properties REIT (AP.UN): −3.55%** to $4.08, the biggest decliner on the Composite. **Ero Copper (ERO)** fell 2.96% to $31.16, missing out on the copper rally.
- **Alimentation Couche-Tard (ATD): −1.68%** to $85.48, giving back Thursday's gain. Grocers also slipped, with **Loblaw (L)** down about 1.2% and **Metro (MRU)** down about 1.0%.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The loonie was little changed, at **70.19 US cents** compared with 70.21 on Thursday, The Canadian Press said. It was a quiet end to a week that began with the US dollar at a 19-month high against the Canadian dollar on Monday, FXStreet noted.

Bond yields moved higher as investors bought stocks again. Reuters said Canada's 10-year yield rose about 3 basis points to around **3.67%**, while the US 10-year yield traded near 4.9%. Swap markets are pricing roughly an **80%** chance that the Bank of Canada raises its key rate again in December, Investing.com said, citing Reuters. The central bank raised its rate by a quarter point to 2.5% in October.

The risk tone improved a lot over the week. But the moves have been large in both directions, so a single strong Friday does not settle where the market goes next.

## What to watch next

- **Monday, November 30:** Statistics Canada releases third-quarter GDP. RBC economists expect growth to slow to a 1.5% annualized pace, after 3.3% in the second quarter.
- **Friday, December 4:** Canada's November jobs report. RBC expects the unemployment rate to fall to 6.5%. US jobs data are also due next week.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The Bank of Canada's last rate decision of 2026.

## Bottom line

Rebounding gold and copper, plus a recovery in AI-linked tech, gave the TSX its best week since October 2023. Consumer staples and banks lagged, and December rate-hike bets held.
