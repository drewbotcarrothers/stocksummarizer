## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 23

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **34,081.44**, up **891.59 points (+2.69%)**. That ended a seven-session losing streak and won back about 30% of last week's 2,916-point drop. Investing.com said in the afternoon that the index was on track for its best day in more than three years.

The index opened at **33,953.39**, which was also the session low, and reached **34,268.75** at its high. The S&P/TSX 60 rose **2.57%** to **2,035.15**, and the TSX Venture Composite gained **3.72%** to **712.63**. Advancers beat decliners **756 to 236** on the Toronto Stock Exchange, and the S&P/TSX 60 VIX volatility gauge fell 8.96% to **20.22**, Investing.com reported.

The Canadian dollar kept sliding. The Canadian Press had it at **69.59 US cents**, down from 69.81 on Friday.

Wall Street had a bigger day. The Dow rose 1,447.98 points (+3.01%) to 49,595.31, the S&P 500 gained 326.78 points (+4.73%) to 7,237.17 and the Nasdaq jumped 1,471.83 points (+6.51%) to 24,089.56. All three had closed Friday in correction territory.

## What drove the TSX on November 23

**Nvidia brought back its CEO.** Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, effective immediately, after he resigned earlier this month. Reuters said the board had gained confidence that he does not pose a national security risk. Nvidia shares soared 31%, Baystreet.ca reported, and other AI names followed: Micron gained 19%, CoreWeave 22% and AMD almost 7%. "That's really triggering a broad recovery in stocks related to AI and you're seeing that positive sentiment spill into Canadian markets," Anish Chopra of Portfolio Management Corp. told The Canadian Press.

**Gold bounced off a one-year low.** The December gold contract rose **US$136.00 to US$3,651.40 an ounce**, The Canadian Press said, and Investcom put silver up about 4.5% late in the day. Last week's slide in precious metals had hit Canadian miners hard, so they led the rebound. Chopra called stronger gold and renewed tech enthusiasm "a double boost" for the TSX.

**Oil fell.** The January crude contract dropped **US$3.24 to US$80.17 a barrel**. Reuters tied the drop to rising Middle East supply and the restart of a Libyan pipeline. That kept energy stocks out of the rally.

**Canadian data was firmer.** Statistics Canada's early estimate shows factory sales rose **2.3%** in October, led by petroleum and coal products and primary metals.

Even so, Chopra said the bounce does not settle bigger worries about AI valuations, spending and profits, or about higher interest rates. "I don't think today takes away from that," he said.

## Sectors on the move

Sector figures are from Baystreet.ca. Ten of 12 subgroups rose.

- **Gold: +7.3%**, the best group, as bullion recovered.
- **Materials: +6.7%**, with base-metal and silver miners joining the gold rally.
- **Information technology: +4.9%**, led by Shopify and Celestica.
- **Financials**, the heaviest group in the index, were up about 2.5% in morning trading, Reuters said.
- **Energy: −1.5%** and **telecom: −1.4%** were the only two decliners, on weaker oil and a drop in Telus.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Montage Gold (MAU): +12.5%** to $13.81, the top gainer on the Composite, and **NovaGold Resources (NG): +12%** to $7.46, as gold rebounded.
- **G Mining Ventures (GMIN): +10.9%** to $41.50. **Barrick Mining (ABX)** rose 7.8% and **Agnico Eagle (AEM)** 6.1%, Baystreet said.
- **Hammond Power Solutions (HPS.A): +11.8%** to $191.10. Coverage did not cite company-specific news.
- **Shopify (SHOP): +9%** to $192.96, **Celestica (CLS): +6.4%** to $306.54 after Friday's 16% drop, and **BlackBerry (BB): +8.4%** to $12.03.
- **Sitka Gold (SIG): +23.5%** after AngloGold Ashanti agreed to buy a 15% stake at a 40% premium, a commitment of at least $72.8 million, Investcom reported.
- **Strathcona Resources (SCR): −5.5%** to $35.99, the biggest decliner, along with **Parex Resources (PXT): −3.9%** to $17.99 and **Vermilion Energy (VET): −3.3%** to $14.25 as oil fell.
- **Telus (T): −4.9%** to $15.09 on the day Telus and Quebecor executives were due to meet about Quebecor's takeover proposal. No outcome from the meeting had been reported by the close.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The loonie fell for a 10th straight session and touched a 19-month low against the US dollar, Reuters said. That is its longest daily losing streak since January 2016. "Between the widening (interest) rate gap, softer oil prices and ongoing trade uncertainty, there is very little in the near term to provide meaningful support for the loonie," said Tony Valente of AscendantFX.

Bonds barely moved. Reuters had Canada's 10-year yield down **2.2 basis points at 3.687%** in the afternoon, about 125 basis points below the US 10-year. Investing.com said the two-year yield rose about 1 basis point. Markets expect a Federal Reserve hike in December, which kept the US dollar strong.

## What to watch next

- **Tuesday, November 24:** Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem speaks on the Canadian economic outlook.
- **This week:** The US October PCE price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, for clues on a December hike.
- **Thursday, November 26, and Friday, November 27:** US markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early Friday, which usually means thinner trading in Toronto.
- **Ongoing:** Any news from the Telus–Quebecor talks, and whether the bounce in gold holds.

## Bottom line

Nvidia's CEO reversal and a rebound in gold gave the TSX a big up day that ended its seven-day losing streak. Oil producers and Telus fell, and the loonie kept weakening.
