## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 12

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **36,137.19**, down **274.53 points (−0.75%)**. That erased Wednesday's 241-point gain and kept the choppy pattern of recent sessions going. On Monday the index had posted its highest close in two months.

The index opened near 36,200, below Wednesday's close, and never got back into positive territory. Its best level was about 36,367 and its low was about 36,081. Volume picked up to about 230 million shares.

The Canadian dollar was little changed at about **70.15 US cents**.

Wall Street also slipped. The Dow fell about 189 points to 52,297.09, the S&P 500 lost about 27 points (−0.4%) to 7,592.40, and the Nasdaq dropped 100 points (−0.4%) to 25,366.09.

## What drove the TSX on November 12

**Gold fell hard.** The December gold contract dropped about US$115, or close to 3%, to roughly US$3,885 an ounce. Reuters said gold touched its lowest level since October 2025. That was the third straight daily loss, and it hit Canadian miners hard. Materials was by far the worst group on the index.

**Middle East hopes faded, and oil jumped.** US President Donald Trump posted that the US was no longer interested in a deal with Iran, which cooled hopes that the Strait of Hormuz would reopen soon. US crude settled about 2.6% higher at US$88.74 a barrel. Higher oil fed worries about stickier inflation and higher interest rates. That is part of why gold, which pays no interest, lost its shine.

**Nvidia's CEO stepped down.** Jensen Huang resigned after reports about his personal conduct, and Nvidia named finance chief Colette Kress interim CEO. Nvidia shares fell, but money moved into rival chipmakers such as AMD and Broadcom. In Toronto, that rotation helped Celestica, which works with some of those chip companies, so Canadian tech actually rose.

**Light Canadian data.** Statistics Canada said building permits fell 4.1% to $12.9 billion in September. It was not a market mover.

## Sectors on the move

Only four of the 11 main sectors rose, according to RTTNews.

- **Information technology: about +0.8%**, the top group, led by Celestica and Constellation Software.
- **Energy: about +0.8%**, helped by higher crude, with Vermilion, CES Energy Solutions and Kelt Exploration among the gainers.
- **Consumer staples: about +0.4%**, and **consumer discretionary** was roughly flat (+0.06%), with Canadian Tire a standout.
- **Financials: about −0.5%**, a heavy weight on the index given its size.
- **Real estate: about −0.9%** and **communication services: about −0.9%**.
- **Health care: about −1.9%**.
- **Materials: about −3.2%**, the weakest group. Baystreet put the gold subgroup down about 3.5%.

(Sector figures can differ slightly by source.)

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Cargojet (CJT): about +10.3%**, to $108.77, after the air cargo company's third-quarter revenue and adjusted profit beat analyst estimates.
- **Canadian Tire (CTC.A): about +7.2%**, after third-quarter earnings of $4.27 a share topped estimates on higher gas sales, and it raised its annual dividend to $7.30 a share, its 17th straight yearly increase.
- **Celestica (CLS): about +4.8%**, up $16.49 to roughly $362, adding to Wednesday's 8% jump as investors bet rival chipmakers could gain ground on Nvidia.
- **Vermilion Energy (VET): about +2.2%** and **CES Energy Solutions (CEU): about +2.2%**, riding higher oil.
- **Constellation Software (CSU): about +1.2%**.
- **Aya Gold & Silver (AYA): about −6.9%**, **Capstone Copper (CS): about −6.5%** and **G Mining Ventures (GMIN): about −6.5%**, among the worst-hit miners as metals fell.
- **Curaleaf (CURA): about −6.6%**, among the biggest decliners.
- **AtkinsRéalis (ATRL): down 67 cents to $89.95**, even after it signed a deal with Turkey's state-owned nuclear company to study a possible expansion of Turkey's nuclear power program.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Bond yields climbed. Canada's 10-year yield rose 7.7 basis points to about **3.79%**, following US Treasuries after the US bond market reopened from Veterans Day. Baystreet said the US 10-year yield rose to about 5.12% from 5.01%. Rising yields and a sharp fall in gold point to markets worrying more about inflation and rates staying higher for longer.

Small caps had a rough day. The TSX Venture Exchange fell about 14 points, or 1.7%, to 802.78, weighed down by its heavy mix of junior miners. The loonie held near 70 US cents.

## What to watch next

- **Monday, November 16:** Canada's consumer price index for October, the next big read on inflation before the Bank of Canada meets.
- **Friday, December 4:** The next Labour Force Survey.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision. The Bank raised rates at its last meeting, and its minutes kept the door open to more.
- **Ongoing:** Nvidia's leadership change and any new US–Iran headlines. Both moved the tape today through AI stocks, oil and gold.

## Bottom line

A slide in gold to a one-year low sank Canadian miners and outweighed gains in tech, energy and a few strong earnings reports, leaving the TSX back below 36,200.
