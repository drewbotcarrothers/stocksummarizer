## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 20

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **33,189.85**, down **838.88 points (−2.47%)**. It was the seventh straight losing session and a new six-month low. The Canadian Press said the index is now down 8.8% since November 11 and more than 10% below the record it set in late August.

The index opened lower at **33,814.80**, reached **34,067.54** early, then sold off to a low of **33,170.90**. The S&P/TSX 60 fell **2.32%** to **1,984.16**, and the TSX Venture Composite dropped **4.05%** to **687.07**. Decliners beat advancers **830 to 185** on the Toronto Stock Exchange, Investing.com reported, and the S&P/TSX 60 VIX volatility gauge jumped 13.8% to **22.21**, another six-month high.

For the week, the TSX lost **2,916.05 points, or 8.1%**, from its November 13 close of 36,105.90. Baystreet.ca and RTTNews had flagged it during the day as the index's steepest weekly decline in more than four years.

The Canadian dollar was little changed. The Canadian Press had it at **69.81 US cents**, against 69.85 on Thursday.

Wall Street had an even worse day. The Dow fell 949.73 points (−1.93%) to 48,147.33, the S&P 500 lost 265.44 points (−3.70%) to 6,910.39 and the Nasdaq sank 1,261.47 points (−5.28%) to 22,617.73. Baystreet said the Nasdaq turned negative for the year and the S&P 500 joined the Dow in correction territory.

## What drove the TSX on November 20

**The AI trade kept unwinding.** Anthropic, which jumped about 17% in its Nasdaq debut on Thursday, fell about 10% on its first full day, Baystreet reported. Chipmakers fell harder: Nvidia dropped more than 11%, AMD 12% and Micron more than 13%. Selling in Nvidia picked up after Reuters reported that the US Securities and Exchange Commission is looking into possible accounting issues at the company. In Toronto, AI hardware supplier Celestica took the worst of it.

**Gold and silver kept falling.** The December gold contract dropped **US$170.10 to US$3,515.40 an ounce**, The Canadian Press said, and Investcom put silver down about 8% late in the day. Investcom said gold hit a 14-month low and was headed for its worst week in eight months as traders bet on a Federal Reserve rate hike in December, which keeps the US dollar firm. Copper slid to a seven-month low, on track for its biggest weekly drop in 19 months, as speculators gave up on AI-driven demand bets.

**Investors moved to safety.** Money went into defensive stocks and government bonds, so yields fell sharply (more below).

**Canadian data was soft.** Statistics Canada said retail sales rose **0.8% to $75.3 billion** in September, led by gas stations and fuel vendors. Reuters noted most of that gain came from higher prices, and the agency's early estimate points to a drop in October. "The run of weak Canadian data continues," BMO's Benjamin Reitzes wrote.

**Oil did not move much.** Crude stayed near US$83–84 a barrel. Reports disagreed on the direction, depending on the contract and the time of the quote: The Canadian Press had January crude down 97 cents at US$83.41.

## Sectors on the move

Sector figures are from Baystreet.ca.

- **Information technology: −6%**, the worst group by a wide margin, led down by Celestica.
- **Gold: −5%** and **materials: −4.8%**, as precious metals and copper slid together.
- **Telecom: +3.2%**, the best group for a second day, with Quebecor and Rogers higher.
- **Consumer staples: +1.3%** and **utilities: +0.4%**, as investors looked for steadier earnings.
- Seven of 12 subgroups fell. RTTNews said the big banks were down roughly 1.7% to 3% at midday.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Celestica (CLS): −16.4%** to $288.00, the session's biggest decliner and a 52-week low. There was no company news; the stock fell with AI hardware names worldwide.
- **AbraSilver (ABRA): −11.5%** to $8.19, and **Vizsla Silver (VZLA): −10.6%** to $3.63, as silver tumbled.
- **Montage Gold (MAU): −10%** to $12.28, and **Eldorado Gold (ELD): −7.9%** to $35.56, on the gold slide.
- **Firan Technology Group (FTG): −8%** to $28.15, part of the tech selloff.
- **Bausch + Lomb (BLCO): −6.9%** to $27.67 after its experimental eye drop failed to significantly reduce pain in the first 24 hours after laser eye surgery in a mid-stage study.
- **Quebecor (QBR.B): +5.7%** to $61.26, the top gainer, building on Thursday's rebound as the takeover contest for Telus continues.
- **Brookfield Infrastructure Partners (BIP.UN): +4.6%** to $61.66, an all-time high, and **Boyd Group Services (BYD): +4.4%** to $105.81.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Bonds rallied as stocks fell. Reuters said Canada's 10-year yield was down **10.6 basis points at 3.703%** around midday, its lowest since November 12, and Baystreet had the US 10-year at **4.92%**, down from 5.05%. TD Economics said Canadian two- and 10-year yields both fell about 12 basis points over the week.

The loonie held near 70 US cents but was set for a third straight weekly loss, after touching its weakest level since April 2025 on Thursday, Reuters said. Markets put roughly even odds on another Bank of Canada hike in December, after the bank raised rates in October for the first time in three years.

## What to watch next

- **Monday, November 23:** Whether the TSX can end its seven-day losing streak, and Quebecor's scheduled meeting with Telus management.
- **Ongoing:** Nvidia and the AI chip group after the SEC report, plus whether gold finds a floor near US$3,500.
- **Ongoing:** Fed commentary on a possible December hike, which has been driving the US dollar, gold and bond yields.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision, with markets split on a hike.

## Bottom line

The TSX fell for a seventh straight day and finished its worst week in more than four years, down 8.1%, as the AI selloff hit tech and falling metals hit miners. Telecoms, staples and bonds were where investors hid.
