## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 16

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,722.13**, down **383.77 points (−1.06%)**. It was the third straight decline, the lowest close since November 5, and what Reuters called an 11-day low.

The S&P/TSX 60 fell **0.93%** to **2,132.10**. The Venture Composite dropped **2.7%** to **776.91**.

The Canadian dollar slipped to about **69.94 US cents**, compared with 70.14 US cents on Friday.

Wall Street fell more sharply as AI-linked stocks sold off ahead of Nvidia's earnings and a planned public offering from Anthropic later in the week. The Dow dropped 739.38 points to 51,608.17, the S&P 500 lost 145.08 points (−1.9%) to 7,416.59, and the Nasdaq fell 626.51 points (−2.5%) to 24,509.76.

## What drove the TSX on November 16

**Oil and gold did most of the damage.** US crude futures settled **3.2% lower at US$85.72 a barrel**, down US$2.84, on signs of recovering oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Gold slid to a more than one-year low, with the December contract down US$33.30 at **US$3,853.00 an ounce**, as a firm US dollar and expectations of another Federal Reserve hike in December weighed on bullion. Materials, including the gold miners, were the weakest group on the index.

**Canada's inflation report was a little softer than feared.** Statistics Canada said the consumer price index rose **3.1%** from a year earlier in October, matching September and coming in below the 3.2% rate economists had forecast. Gasoline remained the main upward pressure, up 30.9% from a year earlier, while cheaper cellphone plans and slower rent growth helped offset firmer groceries and clothing. "This at least means that there's no additional pressure on the Bank of Canada to increase interest rates," Colin Cieszynski, chief market strategist at SIA Wealth Management, told Reuters. Investors now see a roughly **40%** chance of a December hike, down from 54% before the data.

**Takeovers cut both ways.** Canadian Natural Resources said it will buy Advantage Energy in a deal valued at **C$2.53 billion**, including debt. Advantage jumped; Canadian Natural declined with the rest of the energy group. In telecom, Telus confirmed an unsolicited, non-binding proposal from Quebecor and said its board is reviewing that approach and other strategic alternatives.

**The AI hangover stayed a US story.** Nvidia's earnings and questions about the company's leadership kept pressure on US tech. In Toronto, resources led the tape rather than a repeat of Friday's Celestica-led slide.

## Sectors on the move

Figures below are from Reuters, Baystreet.ca and the official TSX daily report and can differ slightly by source.

- **Materials: about −3.6% to −3.7%**, the weakest group, as gold and base-metal miners sold off.
- **Energy: about −2.1% to −2.4%**, tracking the drop in crude.
- **Gold stocks: about −2.8%**, according to Baystreet's subgroup reading.
- **Financials** were a heavy drag on the index, with Royal Bank, TD, Bank of Montreal and Scotiabank all among the largest point detractors.
- **Consumer staples: about +1.4%**, the top gainer among the groups Baystreet listed.
- **Health care: about +1.3%**, and **real estate: about +1%**.
- **Communication services** were lifted by Telus, plus gains in BCE and Rogers, even as Quebecor tumbled.
- **Information technology** was not the main weight on the TSX. Shopify was one of the index's top point contributors, a contrast with the selloff in US AI names.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Advantage Energy (AAV): +23.74%** to $13.45, after Canadian Natural agreed to buy the company.
- **Telus (T): +12.85%** to $13.70. Quebecor's proposal would pay $6.35 in cash and 0.17 Quebecor Class B shares for each Telus share, a package it valued at about $19.45 — a roughly 60% premium to Friday's $12.14 close. Telus still finished well below that headline value and flagged significant regulatory and execution risks.
- **Rogers (RCI.B): +4.55%**, and **BCE (BCE): +3.37%**, as investors reassessed the telecom group.
- **Kelt Exploration (KEL): +10.70%**, among the index's top advancers even as crude fell.
- **Quebecor (QBR.B): −15.33%** to $65.25, down $11.81, as the market weighed the cost of a cash-and-stock bid for a much larger rival.
- **Canadian Natural Resources (CNQ): about −2.7%** to $68.96.
- **Hudbay Minerals (HBM): −8.27%**, **First Quantum (FM): −7.57%**, and **Capstone Copper (CS): −7.33%**, as base metals and miners sold off.
- **Agnico Eagle (AEM)** was the index's largest point detractor, with Wheaton Precious Metals and Barrick also among the heaviest weights.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The inflation report took some heat out of Canadian rate-hike bets, and the US 10-year yield eased to about **5.12%**, from about 5.15% on Friday, as stocks fell. That was not enough to support gold, which is still dealing with a strong dollar and a market that has fully priced a December Federal Reserve hike. The softer loonie tracked cheaper oil and a risk-off tone in equities.

## What to watch next

- **Tuesday, November 17:** Nvidia reports results after the US close, its first earnings since the chief executive change. US October retail sales are also due.
- **Wednesday–Thursday, November 18–19:** Anthropic's IPO is expected to price on Wednesday and begin trading on Thursday, another test for the AI trade.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision. Monday's inflation print lowered the odds of a hike, but they have not gone to zero.
- **Ongoing:** Crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz, and whether gold miners can stabilize after bullion's drop to a more than one-year low.

## Bottom line

A drop in oil and a fresh one-year low in gold pulled Canadian resource stocks lower, and the TSX logged its third straight decline. A steady inflation report eased some Bank of Canada hike fears, and deal news created sharp single-stock moves, but that was not enough to offset the slide in miners, energy and the banks.
