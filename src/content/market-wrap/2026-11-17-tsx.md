## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 17

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,315.30**, down **406.83 points (−1.14%)**. It was the index's fourth straight decline and, according to Reuters, its lowest close since October 8, a nearly six-week low.

The index traded between **35,198.61** and **35,705.91**, so it never got back to Monday's close of 35,722.13. The TSX Venture Composite fell **1.7%** to **764.03**.

The Canadian dollar was roughly unchanged at about **69.97 US cents**, compared with 69.94 US cents on Monday.

Wall Street fell again ahead of Nvidia's results. The Dow dropped 848.75 points (−1.6%) to 50,759.42, the S&P 500 lost 76.06 points (−1.0%) to 7,340.53, and the Nasdaq fell 181.37 points (−0.7%) to 24,328.38.

## What drove the TSX on November 17

**The AI jitters spread to Canadian software.** Investors sold technology stocks before Nvidia's first earnings report since its former chief executive resigned last week. In Toronto, that showed up most in software names such as BlackBerry, Constellation Software and Descartes, plus Shopify. "Geopolitical uncertainty, inflationary uncertainty, and now the AI trade. That combination is why we're seeing weak sentiment in the markets," Michael Dehal, a senior portfolio manager at Raymond James, told Reuters. Late in the US session, a report of an outage on a classified US National Security Agency network added to the nerves, though CNN's sources said there was no evidence of a cyberattack.

**The selling was broad, not just tech.** Industrials and the heavily weighted banks both fell, and Reuters said materials was the only major sector to finish higher. With tech, industrials and financials all lower together, the index had little to lean on.

**The Quebecor–Telus bid kept hitting telecom.** Quebecor extended Monday's slide after its unsolicited proposal for Telus. Ottawa has said the Competition Bureau and the CRTC will review the roughly $31-billion proposal, and analysts told The Canadian Press the path ahead is "challenging." Telus gave back part of Monday's jump.

**Oil and gold steadied.** December crude edged up 29 cents to **US$86.01 a barrel**, and December gold rose US$11.20 to **US$3,864.20 an ounce**, steadying near Monday's more than one-year low. That was enough to lift the gold and copper miners against the tide.

**Trade and housing news was mostly background.** Prime Minister Mark Carney and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed in Shenzhen to extend and expand a trade arrangement. China will cut tariffs on about **$400 million** of Canadian exports, including frozen pork and some seafood, from January 1, 2027. In return, Canada will extend and expand tariff relief on some Chinese steel and aluminum. At home, October housing starts fell to **244,600** units, below forecasts, and CREA said home sales rose **0.3%** from September.

## Sectors on the move

Figures below are from Reuters and Baystreet.ca and can differ slightly by source.

- **Telecom: about −3.9%**, the weakest subgroup on Baystreet's list, led by Quebecor and Telus.
- **Information technology: about −2.8% to −2.9%**, on software losses and a slide in Shopify.
- **Industrials: about −2.6% to −2.8%**, with Waste Connections, Aecon and the railways lower.
- **Financials: about −1.4%.** All of the Big Five banks fell between about 1.6% and 2.1%, and Brookfield Corp. lost about 2.1%.
- **Energy** slipped less than the market. Canadian Natural fell about 1%, Enbridge eased 0.4%, and Suncor and Imperial Oil edged higher.
- **Gold: about +0.8%**, and **materials: about +0.6%**, the only gainers on the board.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Quebecor (QBR.B): −12.64%** to $57.00, down $8.25, a second straight steep drop as the market weighed the cost and regulatory risk of its Telus proposal.
- **Waste Connections (WCN): about −8.1%** to $259.09. It fell after a methane gas eruption at its Chiquita Canyon Landfill in Los Angeles County hospitalized six people and prompted a county order to offer residents relocation, Seeking Alpha and local reports said.
- **BlackBerry (BB): −7.3%** to $11.63, the hardest-hit software name.
- **Aecon Group (ARE): −5.8%**, among the weakest industrials.
- **Shopify (SHOP): −4.07%** to $202.83, and **Constellation Software (CSU): −3.5%** to $3,316.93, along with **Descartes Systems (DSG): −3.6%** to $131.07.
- **Telus (T): −3.28%** to $13.25, giving back part of Monday's 12.85% gain.
- **First Quantum Minerals (FM): +3.7%** to $30.90, the top gainer on Baystreet's list, after Panama said it will open talks on a new agreement for the shuttered Cobre Panama copper mine.
- **Franco-Nevada (FNV): +2.8%** to $353.40, and **Agnico Eagle (AEM): +1.3%** to $247.49, as gold firmed.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Bonds firmed modestly. Canada's 10-year yield held around **3.82%–3.84%**, below last week's two-week high, after Monday's softer inflation report. The US 10-year yield eased to about **5.10%** from 5.13%. US October retail sales rose 0.2%, in line with expectations, so the data did little to change Federal Reserve bets. The loonie barely moved. Overall it was a risk-off day for equities rather than a rates shock.

## What to watch next

- **After Tuesday's close:** Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of **US$115.8 billion**, up 103% from a year earlier, and guided to about US$130 billion. Its shares rose about 2% in after-hours trading. Wednesday's open will show whether that relief reaches Canadian tech names such as Shopify, Celestica and the software group.
- **Wednesday–Thursday, November 18–19:** Anthropic's IPO is expected to price Wednesday and begin trading Thursday, another test for the AI trade.
- **Ongoing:** Regulatory reaction to the Quebecor–Telus proposal, and any update from Waste Connections on Chiquita Canyon.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision. Markets cut the odds of a hike after Monday's inflation print.

## Bottom line

The TSX logged a fourth straight loss and a nearly six-week low as AI jitters hit software, and industrials and banks fell with it. Steadier gold and a Cobre Panama headline lifted the miners, but that was not enough to offset broad selling across the heavier sectors.
