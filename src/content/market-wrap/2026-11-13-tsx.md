## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 13

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **36,105.90**, down **31.29 points (−0.09%)**. It was the second straight down day after Thursday's 274-point drop, but a much quieter one.

Zoom out and the week still looked fine. The index gained about **317 points, or 0.9%**, over the five sessions. Reuters noted it was the TSX's second straight weekly gain.

The index spent most of the day close to flat. By late morning The Canadian Press had it up less than a point, and the selling in tech never turned into a broad retreat.

The Canadian dollar was essentially unchanged at about **70.14 US cents**, compared with 70.15 US cents on Thursday.

Wall Street was mixed. The Dow rose about 50 points to 52,347.55, the S&P 500 fell about 31 points (−0.4%) to 7,561.67, and the Nasdaq dropped about 230 points (−0.9%) to 25,136.27.

## What drove the TSX on November 13

**The Nvidia hangover hit tech again.** Nvidia shares fell for a second day after the exit of chief executive Jensen Huang, and HSBC downgraded the stock to hold until the company names a permanent successor. Investors are asking whether the turmoil could slow spending on AI and data centres. In Toronto, that pulled down Celestica, the biggest AI hardware name on the index, and the tech group with it.

**Earnings kept the floor in place.** "We have corporate profits which continue to exceed expectations," Edward Jones strategist Angelo Kourkafas told Reuters, calling it an offset to worries about AI, technology and inflation. Stantec was the clearest example on the day.

**Inflation and rates stayed in the background.** US producer prices rose 0.3% in October, a bit softer than the 0.4% economists expected, but they were up 6.5% from a year earlier. Reuters said the data did not change expectations for more Federal Reserve rate hikes. Here at home, investors see about a 64% chance the Bank of Canada raises rates in December, and Monday's inflation report is the next big test.

**Light Canadian data.** Statistics Canada said wholesale trade rose 0.7% to $92.5 billion in September, while manufacturing sales were essentially unchanged.

## Sectors on the move

Defensive groups did most of the lifting while tech and materials lagged. Figures below are from Reuters and Baystreet.ca and can differ slightly by source.

- **Real estate: about +1.1%**, the top group, led by beaten-up office and diversified REITs.
- **Energy: about +0.9%**, even though crude ended a touch lower.
- **Consumer staples: about +0.8%**, with Metro and George Weston higher.
- **Utilities: about +0.7%**, helped by Northland Power and Superior Plus.
- **Industrials: about +0.2%**, led by Stantec.
- **Financials: about −0.2% to −0.3%**, a small but heavy drag given their weight in the index.
- **Materials: about −0.4% to −0.6%**, as copper fell about 2%.
- **Information technology: about −1.3%**, the weakest group.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Stantec (STN): about +13.8%**, up $14.81 to $122.20, after the engineering firm's third-quarter revenue and adjusted profit beat analyst estimates.
- **Allied Properties REIT (AP.UN): about +4.9%** to $4.06, and **H&R REIT (HR.UN): about +4.2%** to $9.75.
- **Superior Plus (SPB): about +4.6%** to $5.67.
- **Celestica (CLS): about −5.3%**, down $19.34 to $342.58, giving back part of its strong run earlier in the week as AI chip worries returned.
- **Firan Technology (FTG): about −7%** to $31.82.
- **Lithium Americas (LAC): about −7.8%** to $2.12, and **Capstone Copper (CS): about −4.8%** to $10.23 as base metals slipped.
- **Brookfield (BN): about −1.5%** to $58.42.
- **Royal Bank (RY): down $1.02 to $288.37**, on a day when reports said Canada's largest bank had cut several senior climate and ESG roles in a reorganization.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Yields edged higher again. Baystreet said the US 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 5.16%. Rising yields and a market still pricing in central bank hikes help explain why investors leaned toward steady payers like utilities, staples and REITs while trimming richly valued tech.

Commodities were quiet. The December crude contract slipped 18 cents to US$88.56 a barrel, and December gold was little changed at about US$3,886 an ounce after Thursday's slide.

Small caps stayed soft. The TSX Venture Exchange fell 4.32 points to 798.46 and lost about 2.9% on the week.

## What to watch next

- **Monday, November 16:** Canada's consumer price index for October. Economists expect annual inflation to rise to 3.2%, per Reuters.
- **Friday, December 4:** The next Labour Force Survey.
- **Wednesday, December 9:** The next Bank of Canada rate decision, with markets leaning toward another hike.
- **Ongoing:** Nvidia's search for a permanent CEO and what it means for AI spending, which has been moving Celestica and Canadian tech all week.

## Bottom line

Tech's Nvidia hangover nudged the TSX lower on Friday, but strong earnings and gains in defensive sectors kept the dip small and left the index with its second weekly gain in a row.
