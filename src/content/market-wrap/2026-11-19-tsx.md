## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 19

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **34,028.73**, down **549.32 points (−1.59%)**. That was the sixth straight losing session and the lowest close since May 19, a six-month low, according to IUX24's closing report. Together with Wednesday's 737-point drop, the index has shed about 1,287 points, or roughly 3.6%, in two days.

The index opened a little higher at **34,633.72** and touched **34,666.02** early, then faded all day to a low of **33,927.25**. The S&P/TSX 60 fell **1.54%** to **2,031.34**, and the TSX Venture Composite dropped **3.7%** to **716.09**. Breadth was lopsided: decliners beat advancers **852 to 144** on the Toronto Stock Exchange, Investing.com reported, and the S&P/TSX 60 VIX volatility gauge rose to **19.51**, a six-month high.

The Canadian dollar was roughly flat near 70 US cents. The Canadian Press had it at **69.85 US cents**, up from 69.80 on Wednesday, while Baystreet.ca put it at 69.70 US cents. Readings differ by timing.

Wall Street had a rougher day than Toronto. The Dow fell 1,101.11 points (−2.19%) to 49,097.06, the S&P 500 lost 147.80 points (−2.02%) to 7,175.83 and the Nasdaq dropped 550.68 points (−2.25%) to 23,879.20.

## What drove the TSX on November 19

**Gold kept sliding.** Spot gold fell about **2.5%** to roughly **US$3,628 an ounce**, a 14-month low, Reuters reported. Silver lost more than 4%. The same forces as Wednesday were at work: a stronger US dollar and rising bets that the Federal Reserve could raise rates again. Investing.com said markets were pricing in at least a quarter-point Fed hike in December.

**The AI trade cracked, even on a record IPO day.** Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq after pricing its offering at US$83 a share. It opened at US$111.50 and closed at **US$97.17**, up about 17% from the IPO price, The Canadian Press said. But US stocks hit their session lows right as trading began, Baystreet noted, and chipmakers such as Nvidia and Micron gave back earlier gains. Reuters said the debut "failed to allay investor concerns about AI returns." Canadian software names followed the Nasdaq lower.

**The US dollar stayed strong.** Reuters said the dollar index rose for an eighth straight session, sitting near 19-month highs. A firmer greenback makes dollar-priced metals more expensive for other buyers, which adds pressure on gold miners, Canada's biggest commodity group after energy.

**Oil was a small offset.** US crude was up modestly late in the day, near US$83.81 a barrel per Reuters, after Iranian state media reported that two oil tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters could not verify the report. RTTNews said worries about possible US strikes on Iran revived oil-linked inflation fears, which feed straight back into rate-hike bets.

**Producer prices stayed hot.** Statistics Canada said its raw materials price index fell 1.0% in October from September but was up 23.5% from a year earlier. The industrial product price index rose 0.3% on the month and 12.6% on the year.

## Sectors on the move

Sector figures are from Baystreet.ca.

- **Information technology: −3.2%**, the weakest group, as AI worries spread from Wall Street.
- **Gold: −3.1%** and **materials: −2.6%**, as bullion fell to a 14-month low.
- **Telecom: +4.5%**, the only one of 12 subgroups to rise, lifted by Telus.
- Everything else was lower. With 11 of 12 groups down and decliners outnumbering gainers by about six to one, this was a broad selloff, not just a commodity story like Wednesday.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Telus (T): +13.7%** to $15.50, the top gainer. The Globe and Mail reported that BCE has held talks with Telus as it weighs a response to Quebecor's takeover proposal. Quebecor then issued a statement arguing a BCE–Telus deal would hurt consumers and said it will meet Telus management on November 23.
- **Quebecor (QBR.B): +7.5%** to $58.40, rebounding after sharp losses earlier in the week.
- **Seabridge Gold (SEA): −29.3%** to $25.92, its lowest level since November 2025, after the B.C. Supreme Court reaffirmed that its June ruling set aside the "substantially started" designation for the KSM project.
- **NovaGold (NG): −9.2%** to $6.90, and **Allied Gold (AAUC): −5.7%** to $26.70, on the gold slide.
- **Centerra Gold (CG): −4.2%** to $25.75, also tracking bullion lower.
- **Constellation Software (CSU): −4.3%**, down $143.15 to $3,180, and **Coveo Solutions (CVO): −7.8%** to $4.47, as tech sold off.
- **Headwater Exploration (HWX): +3.8%** to $11.78, one of the few gainers, helped by firmer oil.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Risk appetite was weak. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased slightly to **5.05%** from 5.06%, Baystreet said, but the dollar kept climbing on Fed hike expectations. Reuters quoted Corpay's Karl Schamotta describing "a scramble for US dollar liquidity" as global financial conditions tighten. In Canada, the policy rate is 2.5%. Bank of Canada Deputy Governor Toni Gravelle's speech on Wednesday focused on how the bank uses scenarios to communicate. It gave no signal on the December decision.

## Bottom line

The TSX fell for a sixth straight day to a six-month low as gold miners and tech stocks sold off on a strong US dollar and fading AI enthusiasm. Telecom was the lone bright spot as the Telus takeover contest heated up.
