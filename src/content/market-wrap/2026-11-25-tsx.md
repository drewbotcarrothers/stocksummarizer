## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 25

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **34,179.99**, up **217.69 points (+0.64%)**. Reuters said it was the index's highest close since last Wednesday.

The finish hid a rough morning. The index fell to **33,252.52** early in the session, more than 700 points below Tuesday's close, before climbing back, Canada News Media reported from TMX figures. That put the close about 927 points above the day's low.

Breadth was positive. Advancers beat decliners **557 to 446**, with 57 unchanged, and the S&P/TSX 60 VIX volatility gauge fell 3.97% to **19.84**, Investing.com said. The small-cap TSX Venture Composite rose **17.52 points (+2.54%)** to **708.11**, recovering most of Tuesday's drop.

The Canadian dollar rose to **70.33 US cents**, up from 69.95 on Tuesday, The Canadian Press said.

Wall Street also came well off its lows. The Dow rose 164.25 points to 50,182.95 after falling more than 1,100 points at one point, while the S&P 500 slipped 18.31 points to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq lost 147.73 points to 23,585.80.

## What drove the TSX on November 25

**The AI selloff hit the open, then faded.** Nvidia fell about 4% and other AI-linked names such as Micron and Oracle also dropped, Baystreet.ca said. Reuters said new US tariff proposals on a group of trading partners added to the gloomy mood. But the selling lost steam as the day went on. "A lot of the sellers cleared out and you've got bargain hunters stepping in, trying to push the market back up before the holiday," Colin Cieszynski of SIA Wealth Management told Reuters.

**Miners turned the day around.** Gold touched its lowest level in more than a year earlier in the session, Reuters said, as US inflation data backed bets on a Federal Reserve hike next month. It then recovered to close up about 0.4%, and Canadian gold and copper miners swung from big losses to big gains.

**Oil rose on a refinery attack.** The January crude contract gained **US$1.63 to US$83.68 a barrel**, The Canadian Press said. Reuters said Yemen's Houthis claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery.

**US data stayed hot.** The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the PCE price index, rose to **3.8%** a year earlier in October, and core PCE was 3.3%, Baystreet said. Investing.com said a second estimate showed the US economy grew at a **4.3%** annualized pace in the third quarter, above forecasts.

## Sectors on the move

- **Materials: +3%**, the best group, Reuters said. TMX data cited by Canada News Media had the materials index up 2.99% and the global gold index up 2.73%.
- **Energy: +1%** and **industrials: +0.9%**, according to Reuters.
- **Real estate: +1%**, per Baystreet, led by Altus Group.
- **Information technology: −0.6%**, Reuters said, as Celestica fell again.
- **Financials: −0.32%**, according to TMX data, with most big banks lower.
- **Utilities (−0.6%)** and **health care (−0.4%)** also slipped, Baystreet said. Consumer staples and communication services edged lower too.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **5N Plus (VNP): +7.54%** to $31.93, the top gainer on the Composite, Investing.com said. Coverage did not cite company-specific news.
- **First Quantum Minerals (FM): +6.51%** to $26.01, bouncing back after Tuesday's 10% drop.
- **Avino Silver & Gold (ASM): +6.05%** to $5.26 and **Snowline Gold: +5.1%** to $11.50 as precious metals recovered.
- **Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM): +4.6%** to $174.13. **Agnico Eagle (AEM)** rose 2.7% to $229.30 after being down 4.5% shortly before 10 a.m. ET. **Kinross (K)** gained 2.9% and **Barrick (ABX)** 2.3%.
- **Altus Group (AIF): +4.9%** to $52.61, Baystreet said, without a stated catalyst.
- **Canadian Natural Resources (CNQ): +1.4%** to $64.74, the most heavily traded stock on the exchange at about 14.7 million shares.
- **Alimentation Couche-Tard (ATD): +0.9%** to $85.30, a day after reporting quarterly results and raising its dividend. The CEO told analysts the company is cutting back on promotions in Canada while it reviews pricing, The Canadian Press reported.
- **Celestica (CLS): −2.81%** to $272.92, the biggest decliner and a new 52-week low, Investing.com said. It was down nearly 11% in the first half hour. **Shopify (SHOP)** erased an early drop of more than 4% to close up 0.4% at $189.67.
- **Extendicare (EXE): −2.22%** to $29.04 and **Strathcona Resources (SCR): −2.03%** to $34.80 were also among the biggest decliners.
- Among the banks, **CIBC (CM)** fell 1.6%, **Scotiabank (BNS)** 1.3% and **BMO** 0.8%, while **National Bank (NA)** rose 0.6%.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The loonie hit a **two-week high**, Reuters said, trading at 1.42 per US dollar, or **70.42 US cents**, late in the day. It touched 1.4194 earlier, its strongest level since November 11. The US dollar fell broadly as the AI selloff deepened, and investors still expect the Bank of Canada to hike in December after Tiff Macklem's speech on Tuesday. "All central banks are trying to restore credibility on inflation," Adam Button of investingLive told Reuters. Baystreet said traders saw about a 74% chance of a quarter-point hike in December.

Bond yields fell with US Treasuries. Reuters had the Canadian 10-year yield down **4.2 basis points at 3.651%**, near the bottom of its recent range. The US 10-year yield eased to **4.87%** from 4.92%, Baystreet said.

## Bottom line

A brutal open driven by the AI selloff turned into a one-week high for the TSX as gold and copper miners rallied and oil rose. Tech and banks still lagged, and the loonie climbed on rate-hike bets.
