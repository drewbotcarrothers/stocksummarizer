## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 10

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **36,170.45**, down **275.81 points (−0.76%)**. That gave back part of Monday's 657-point jump, which had left the index at its highest close since September 4.

The index opened near 36,212, touched about 36,354 early, and spent most of the day below that, with a low near 36,080. Volume was about 274 million shares, lighter than Monday. Breadth tilted negative: decliners beat advancers on the Toronto exchange by about 572 to 406, with 69 unchanged.

The Canadian dollar slipped to about **70.5 US cents**, down from about 70.8 cents on Monday.

Wall Street also fell, but more gently. The Dow lost about 157 points to roughly 52,232, the S&P 500 fell about 29 points to 7,520.93, and the Nasdaq dropped about 187 points to 25,065.88.

## What drove the TSX on November 10

**US inflation came in hot.** The US consumer price index rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% economists expected. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast. That pushed up bets that the US Federal Reserve will raise rates next month.

**A Fed governor raised the stakes.** Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in a speech that he thinks a December rate hike is likely, and he left the door open to a larger half-point move. The US 10-year Treasury yield jumped to about 4.99% from 4.87% on Monday.

**Gold gave back gains, and miners followed.** Higher expected rates make non-yielding gold less attractive. December gold futures fell about US$78, or 1.9%, to roughly US$4,036 an ounce, and copper also traded lower. That hit the materials group, which was the biggest drag on the index.

**Why it matters for Canada.** As one wealth manager quoted by Reuters put it, faster Fed tightening could slow the US economy and spill over into Canada, which sends about 70% of its exports south.

**Oil was a little softer, but energy stocks held up.** December crude settled about 0.9% lower at roughly US$86.04 a barrel after US officials played down the need for more military action against Iran. Energy shares still finished higher.

## Sectors on the move

- **Gold: about −4.4%**, the weakest group, after Monday's big rally.
- **Materials: about −3.7% to −3.9%**, depending on the source, as gold and copper fell.
- **Consumer discretionary: about −0.8% to −1%**, with Aritzia and Gildan lower.
- **Real estate: about −0.7%**, as higher yields weighed on rate-sensitive names.
- **Energy: about +1%**, the session leader, helped by Paramount Resources and IPCO.
- **Utilities: about +0.4%**, with AltaGas and Canadian Utilities higher.
- **Telecom: about +0.3%**.

Only a handful of the main groups finished in the green. (Sector figures differ slightly by source.)

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Trisura Group (TSU): about +4.8%**, to $30.88, the top Composite gainer.
- **Paramount Resources (POU): about +3.9%**, to $32.06, and **IPCO: about +3%**, to $37.05.
- **Kinaxis (KXS): about +3.2%**, to $215.63, a three-year high.
- **AltaGas (ALA): about +2.7%**, to $54.72.
- **GFL Environmental (GFL): about −7.2%**, according to Reuters, after the waste company said it had rejected buyout proposals and will stay public. Baystreet had it near $59.25.
- **Novagold (NG): about −9%**, to $8.84, the worst Composite performer, giving back Monday's gain.
- **Perpetua Resources (PPTA): about −8%**, to $27.45, and **Americas Gold and Silver (USA): about −8.6%**, to $4.26.
- **Equinox Gold (EQX)** fell to $15.97 after third-quarter adjusted earnings missed estimates, and **Endeavour Silver (EDR)** dipped about 2.8% to $11.47 after narrowing its gold production guidance.
- **Aritzia (ATZ): about −3.1%**, to $157.02.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Risk appetite cooled but didn't crack. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX rose only about 0.6% to 16.52. Small caps had a rougher day, with the TSX Venture Exchange down about 14 points, or 1.7%, to 822.09.

The loonie lost about a third of a cent as the US dollar firmed; the US Dollar Index futures rose about 0.6%. The jump in US yields was the main rate story of the day.

The Bank of Canada's next rate decision remains **December 9**.

## Bottom line

A hotter-than-expected US inflation report revived Fed hike fears, knocking gold and the miners lower, while energy, utilities and telecom offered a little shelter.
