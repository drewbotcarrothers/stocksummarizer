## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on October 30

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,620.03**, down **629.00 points (-1.74%)**. That was a sharp turn from Thursday's flat finish. The index opened near 36,050, which turned out to be the high of the day. It slid to an intraday low near 35,310 before recovering a little into the close. Volume was heavy at roughly 322 million shares, well above the 240 million-plus of the previous few sessions.

The drop left the TSX down about 168 points (-0.47%) for the week, its second weekly loss in a row. The Canadian dollar firmed to **71.63 US cents**, up from 71.51 on Thursday.

Wall Street had a rougher day. The S&P 500 fell 140.32 points to 7,580.07, the Nasdaq lost 747.73 points (about 2.9%) to 25,437.00, and the Dow dropped 572.25 points to 51,770.31.

## What drove the TSX on October 30

**AI spending worries hit tech on both sides of the border.** Amazon reported weaker third-quarter results than expected and a large negative free cash flow while it kept spending heavily on AI. Intel fell close to 30% in New York after a weak outlook. That came a day after Alphabet had its worst day on record. Investors are asking whether the hundreds of billions going into AI will pay off, and Friday's answer was to sell first. As one Canadian economist put it, the TSX was "borrowing the move from the U.S. stock market."

**Gold fell hard, and the miners fell harder.** December gold dropped US$132.50 to US$3,898.90 an ounce, its lowest level since November 2025. Higher oil prices and sticky US inflation have kept a December Federal Reserve rate hike on the table. Futures pricing earlier in the day put the odds at roughly 72%. Gold doesn't pay interest, so higher expected rates make it less attractive. Because mining costs don't fall as quickly as the metal price, gold stocks usually move more than gold itself.

**Oil went the other way.** A tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz renewed worries about Middle East supply, and December crude rose US$2.12 to US$104.12 a barrel. That lifted Canadian energy producers, one of the few groups that finished higher.

**Canada's economy stalled again.** Statistics Canada said real GDP was essentially unchanged in August, the second flat month in a row. Economists had expected 0.2% growth. The agency's early estimate points to a 0.1% decline in September. The weak reading has economists questioning how soon the Bank of Canada will hike again after Wednesday's increase to 2.5%.

## Sectors on the move

- **Gold: about -6.4%**, the weakest subgroup.
- **Materials: about -5.7%**, the biggest drag on the index. Base metals fell along with gold.
- **Information technology: about -3.4%**, tracking the US tech selloff.
- **Financials** were lower too. The big banks slipped, with Royal Bank down about 1.5% and TD down about 2.3%. Because banks are such a large part of the index, even modest drops added to the losses.
- **Telecom: about +1.9%**, the best group. Investors moved into steadier, dividend-paying names.
- **Energy: about +1.3%** on higher crude.
- **Consumer staples: about +0.9%**. Only three of 12 subgroups finished higher.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Cameco (CCO): -7.7%**, to $107.60. It was one of the index's biggest losers after third-quarter revenue missed estimates. At one point in the morning it was down about 9.5%.
- **B2Gold (BTO): -7.8%**, to $6.41, and **IAMGOLD (IMG): -7.9%**, to $21.53, as the gold price slumped.
- **Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM): -6.0%**, to about $230.79. It added to Thursday's 4.1% drop.
- **BlackBerry (BB): -8.6%**, to about $11.75, and **Celestica (CLS): -6.8%**, to about $369.32, as tech sold off. **Shopify (SHOP)** fell about 5.7% to roughly $220.37.
- **Imperial Oil (IMO): +5.4%**, to $193.48, leading the energy gainers. **Suncor (SU)** rose about 2.3%.
- **Rogers Communications (RCI.B): +3.4%**, to about $45.48, and **Telus (T): +2.4%**, to $12.37, as telecom led the defensive groups.

Days like this show how much the TSX depends on commodities. A drop in one metal can wipe out gains in energy, telecom and consumer staples combined.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Money moved into safer assets. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.92% from 4.96%, and US consumer staples and health care stocks gained while chipmakers sold off. The loonie edged higher even though Canadian GDP missed, helped by higher oil prices. Risk appetite was weakest among smaller resource companies. The TSX Venture Exchange fell about 2.8% to 803.78.

For the Bank of Canada, Reuters cited LSEG data showing traders widely expect a hold at the December meeting. That's a cooler outlook than the one markets had right after Wednesday's hike.

## What to watch next

- **Tuesday, November 3:** The US midterm elections. Traders cited them as a reason to take money off the table into month-end.
- **Wednesday, November 4:** Canada's September merchandise trade figures, which matter for energy and resource exporters.
- **Thursday, November 5:** The federal budget, with attention on infrastructure spending and how much fiscal room remains.
- **Friday, November 6:** The October Labour Force Survey, a key read on whether the Bank of Canada needs to hike again.

## Bottom line

A US-led selloff in AI stocks and a slump in gold sent the TSX down 1.7% to end October. Energy and telecom offered only partial cover.
