## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 24

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **33,962.25**, down **119.87 points (−0.35%)**. It gave back a small slice of Monday's 891-point jump, which Reuters said was the index's biggest gain since April 2025.

The dip was narrow. Eight of the 12 TSX subgroups rose, Baystreet.ca said, but losses in heavyweight miners and tech outweighed them. Decliners beat advancers **536 to 440**, with 68 unchanged, and the S&P/TSX 60 VIX volatility gauge rose 2.18% to **20.66**, Investing.com reported. The small-cap TSX Venture Composite had a much worse day, falling **22.04 points (−3.09%)** to **690.59**.

The Canadian dollar bounced. The Canadian Press had it at **69.95 US cents**, up from 69.59 on Monday.

Wall Street was mixed. The Dow rose 423.39 points (+0.85%) to 50,018.70, back above 50,000, while the S&P 500 fell 36.08 points (−0.50%) to 7,201.09 and the Nasdaq dropped 356.02 points (−1.48%) to 23,733.54.

## What drove the TSX on November 24

**AI stocks cooled after Monday's surge.** Nvidia fell 5.9%, the Associated Press said, after soaring on Monday when Jensen Huang returned as CEO. Baystreet said Micron dropped more than 10% and AMD 9%. A Wall Street Journal report that the US is preparing to raise tariffs on dozens of countries, including China, added to the caution, according to Baystreet and Reuters.

**Copper miners sold off.** First Quantum Minerals said a contract worker died after an accident at its Kansanshi operation in Zambia and that work in that area was temporarily suspended. Its shares fell to their lowest level since September 2025, Reuters said, and other copper names fell with it.

**Gold slipped again.** The December gold contract fell **US$89.50 to US$3,561.90 an ounce**, The Canadian Press said. Reuters tied the drop to hawkish signals from Federal Reserve officials.

**Macklem kept a December hike in play.** In a speech to the Economic Club of Canada in Toronto, Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said "this second-round effect of the oil shock will add to inflation persistence," CBC News reported. FXStreet quoted him as saying "if inflation is going to be too high for too long, we would need to hike rates." Investors now see about an **80% chance** of a hike on December 9, up from 65% before the speech, Reuters said. The bank raised its policy rate to 2.50% last month.

**Oil rose.** The January crude contract gained **US$1.88 to US$82.05 a barrel**. Reuters said new attacks were reported on ships in and around the Strait of Hormuz.

"The market is going to be volatile going into the end of the year," Michael Constantino of Webull Securities Canada told Reuters, pointing to US trade tariffs and the chance of another Bank of Canada hike.

## Sectors on the move

Sector figures are from Baystreet.ca unless noted.

- **Real estate: +1.8%**, the best group, led by apartment landlord CAPREIT.
- **Consumer staples: +1.4%**, helped by George Weston's earnings and gains at Empire.
- **Health care: +1.2%**, with Bausch Health and Chartwell Retirement Residences higher.
- **Energy: +0.7%** on stronger oil, Reuters said.
- **Materials: −2.4%** and **gold: −2.1%**, the main drags, as copper and gold miners fell.
- **Information technology: −2.1%**, led lower by Celestica.
- **Financials** slipped 0.27%, according to TMX Money data cited by Canada News Media.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **First Quantum Minerals (FM): −10.35%** to $24.42, the biggest decliner on the Composite, after the fatal accident at Kansanshi.
- **Capstone Copper (CS): −7.95%** to $7.99, **Ivanhoe Mines (IVN): −6.9%** to $6.07 and **Teck Resources (TECK.B): −4.12%** to $72.01 as copper names fell.
- **Celestica (CLS): −8.4%** to $280.80, more than erasing Monday's 6.4% gain as AI hardware stocks slid. **Shopify (SHOP)** fell 2.13% to $188.85.
- **Agnico Eagle (AEM): −2.88%** to $223.35 as gold fell.
- **West Fraser Timber (WFG): +6.83%** to $98.18, the top gainer on the Composite. Coverage did not cite company-specific news.
- **CAPREIT (CAR.UN): +3.89%** to $30.98 and **Thomson Reuters (TRI): +3.75%** to $152.91, also without a news catalyst in the coverage.
- **George Weston (WN): +2.3%** to $116.53 after reporting third-quarter adjusted earnings of **$1.44 a share, up 8.3%**. Its grocery arm **Loblaw (L)** rose 1.03% to $72.65, a new 52-week high, MarketWatch said.
- **Canadian Natural Resources (CNQ): +1.41%** to $63.82 as oil rose.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The loonie hit an **11-day high**, trading at 1.42715 per US dollar, or **70.07 US cents**, late in the day, Reuters said. That snapped a 10-session losing streak that had taken it to a 19-month low on Monday. Karl Schamotta of Corpay said Bank of Canada officials "see the balance of risks favoring pre-emptive action," which should help narrow the rate gap with the US.

Canadian bond yields were mixed on a flatter curve. Reuters had the two-year yield up **2 basis points at 3.235%**, while Investing.com had the 10-year down 0.9 basis points at **3.678%** in mid-afternoon. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.92% from 4.93%, Baystreet said.

## Bottom line

The TSX gave back a little of Monday's big rebound as copper miners, gold and AI hardware stocks fell. Most sectors still rose, and a hawkish Macklem pushed up rate-hike bets and the loonie.
