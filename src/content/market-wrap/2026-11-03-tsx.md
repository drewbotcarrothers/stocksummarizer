## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 3

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **35,532.28**, down **268.81 points (-0.75%)**. That erased Monday's 181-point gain and left the index at its lowest close since October 8. The index opened lower at 35,518.78, climbed to a high of 35,707.20, and dipped to a low of 35,437.80, but never got back to Monday's close. Volume was about 256 million shares, a little lighter than Monday.

The Canadian dollar slipped to **71.46 US cents**, from 71.54 on Monday.

Wall Street went the other way on tech. The S&P 500 rose 27.52 points to 7,568.92 and the Nasdaq gained 207.65 points (about 0.8%) to 25,372.01 as chipmakers bounced. The Dow fell 197.26 points to 51,786.72, weighed down by Apple, which dropped about 1.9% after warning that rising memory-chip costs would pressure its holiday-quarter margin.

## What drove the TSX on November 3

**Two big tech names fell hard after earnings.** Shopify dropped about 8% after its third-quarter revenue slightly missed estimates, with growth in gross merchandise volume and subscription revenue slower than expected. One Raymond James portfolio manager noted it was the first time in about four years that Shopify reported revenue below what the Street expected. Thomson Reuters fell 9% even though its revenue rose. Some investors worry that heavier spending on artificial intelligence will eat into profit. Because both companies are among the largest on the index, their drops did a lot of the damage.

**Miners gave back Monday's bounce.** Gold, silver and copper all fell. December gold settled down US$29.20 at US$3,940.40 an ounce, with a firmer US dollar and higher US Treasury yields cited as headwinds. Gold miners had jumped more than the metal on Monday, and they fell more than the metal on Tuesday.

**Caution ahead of the US midterms.** Americans voted on Tuesday, with polls favouring Democrats to win the House and the Senate seen as too close to call. One US money manager called the vote a long-anticipated volatility event that markets would be glad to put behind them. US President Donald Trump also said a trade deal with Canada was close, including fertilizer moving into the US without tariffs. One Toronto money manager said investors want concrete details first, which could take weeks or months.

**Oil was quiet after Monday's plunge.** December crude settled down 37 cents at US$96.09 a barrel, its lowest close since October 14. Traders weighed peace talks against fresh supply worries, and a senior Iranian official said Iran and the US were still far from a deal.

## Sectors on the move

- **Information technology: about -1.7%**, the biggest drag, led by Shopify and Thomson Reuters.
- **Materials: about -1.5%**, with the gold subgroup down about 1.7%. Agnico Eagle fell about 1.8% and Barrick about 2.2%.
- **Health care: about +4.3%**, the best group, helped by Curaleaf.
- **Industrials: about +0.8%**, with Toromont the standout.
- **Utilities: about +0.4%**, as investors leaned toward defensive stocks.
- **Financials:** most of the big banks barely moved.

Seven of the 12 main subgroups ended lower. Decliners outnumbered advancers on the Toronto exchange by about 494 to 442 in late trade.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **5N Plus (VNP): about +25%**, the top gainer. The specialty semiconductor and materials maker reported third-quarter revenue up 30% to US$136.4 million, well ahead of estimates, and said it expects to reach the high end of its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance.
- **Curaleaf (CURA): about +10%**, after reporting record third-quarter revenue.
- **Toromont Industries (TIH): +8.5%**, to $209.40, after its third-quarter results beat estimates.
- **Thomson Reuters (TRI): -9.0%**, to $155.07, on worries that AI spending will weigh on profit.
- **Shopify (SHOP): about -8%**, to $200.19, after the slight revenue miss.
- **Allied Properties REIT (AP.UN): about -8%**, extending its slide after its recent distribution cut.
- **Centerra Gold (CG): -5.3%**, to $27.26, with the gold group.
- **Fairfax Financial (FFH): down $1.75**, to $2,488.86. Its proposed $5.5-billion takeover of India's IDBI Bank faces opposition from employees and political parties.

Earnings set the tone. Beats were rewarded with big gains, and even small misses from heavyweights were punished hard. That mix, plus the drop in miners, is why the index fell more than its breadth alone would suggest.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem told the House of Commons finance committee that the government's current fiscal policy is not making it harder to fight inflation. He repeated that more rate hikes might be needed. Markets are pricing in close to one more quarter-point hike before year-end.

Canada's 10-year yield rose 1.7 basis points to 3.770%, following US Treasury yields higher. The 2-year yield slipped 0.4 basis points to 3.268%, so expectations for the next few BoC moves barely changed.

The S&P/TSX 60 VIX, a measure of expected volatility, rose to about 16.5, a three-month high. Small resource stocks stayed weak: the TSX Venture Exchange fell 6.14 points to 794.69.

## What to watch next

- **Tuesday evening, November 3, into Wednesday:** US midterm election results. A clear outcome either way could move tech and AI-related stocks. AMD also reports after the US close.
- **Thursday, November 5:** The federal budget. The deficit is expected at about 2% of GDP, and markets will watch spending plans after Macklem's comments.
- **Friday, November 6:** Canada's October Labour Force Survey and the US monthly jobs report, key reads for the Bank of Canada and the Fed.

## Bottom line

Earnings misses at Shopify and Thomson Reuters, plus weaker metals, pushed the TSX to its lowest close in almost four weeks while investors waited on the US vote.
