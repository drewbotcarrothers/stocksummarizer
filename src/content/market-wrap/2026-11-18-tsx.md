## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 18

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **34,578.05**, down **737.25 points (−2.09%)**. Reuters said it was the index's steepest one-day drop since June 5 and its lowest close since June 10, a five-month low. It was also the fifth straight losing session.

The index opened near its high of **35,060.21** and slid most of the day to a low of **34,554.46**, finishing close to the bottom of the range. The TSX Venture Composite fell **2.8%** to **743.66**.

The Canadian dollar slipped to about **69.80 US cents**, from 69.97 US cents on Tuesday, as the US dollar strengthened.

Wall Street was mixed and much calmer. The Dow fell 561.25 points (−1.1%) to 50,198.17 and the S&P 500 lost 16.90 points (−0.2%) to 7,323.63, while the Nasdaq rose 101.50 points (+0.4%) to 24,429.89. The gap between a 2.1% drop in Toronto and a 0.2% dip in the S&P 500 says this was a Canadian commodity story, not a global stock selloff.

## What drove the TSX on November 18

**Metals fell hard.** Gold dropped about **3.4%** to a 14-month low, and copper fell more than **5%**, according to Reuters. December gold futures settled down US$122.30 at **US$3,741.90 an ounce**, The Canadian Press reported. Silver fell nearly 5%. London copper touched its weakest level since April, extending a five-session slide, Proactive reported, as computer-driven selling kicked in and investors questioned AI-related demand for data centres and power grids.

**A hawkish Fed lifted the US dollar.** Reuters tied the gold drop to hawkish comments from Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller, which pushed the US dollar higher and raised expectations that the Fed could hike rates again. A stronger greenback makes dollar-priced commodities more expensive for other buyers and tends to weigh on the loonie, too.

**Oil slid on supply worries.** US crude futures settled about **3.2%** lower at **US$83.26 a barrel**, Reuters said, after US crude and fuel inventory data added to expectations of a supply surplus. The Canadian Press quoted the January contract down US$2.34 at US$82.31.

**The commodity cushion is wearing thin.** "The TSX has held up relatively better than other markets for the last few years on the strength of commodities," Greg Taylor, chief investment officer at PenderFund Capital Management, told Reuters. "That's coming back in the last month or so and it's definitely leaving a mark on a lot of portfolios." Even after this week, Reuters noted the index is still up about 9% in 2026 after a 28% gain in 2025.

**Trade headlines stayed in the background.** Prime Minister Mark Carney and US President Donald Trump discussed efforts to reach a trade deal on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific summit in China, Baystreet reported. At home, Statistics Canada said its new housing price index fell **0.2%** in October, after a similar drop in September.

## Sectors on the move

Figures below are from Reuters and Baystreet.ca and can differ slightly by source.

- **Gold: about −5.4%**, and **materials: about −5.3% to −5.4%**, the biggest drags. Agnico Eagle fell about 6.3%.
- **Energy: about −3.7% to −3.8%**, tracking the drop in crude.
- **Financials: about −2.2%.** Because the banks and insurers are the index's largest weight, this added a lot of points to the decline.
- **Consumer discretionary: about +0.9%**, the best group, with **real estate** and **health care** each up about 0.8%.
- Baystreet said seven of its 12 subgroups actually finished higher. The problem was that the five losers, led by resources and financials, carry far more weight in the index.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Ero Copper (ERO): −11.5%** to $34.19, one of the hardest-hit copper names as the metal slid.
- **Lundin Mining (LUN): about −10%** to $24.64, also on the copper selloff.
- **Iamgold (IMG): −7.1%** to $22.68, and **Equinox Gold (EQX): −6.7%** to $13.14, as gold hit a 14-month low.
- **Agnico Eagle (AEM): about −6.3%**, the heavyweight gold miner, per Reuters.
- **Baytex Energy (BTE): −6.2%** to $5.65, and **CES Energy Solutions (CEU): −4.5%** to $19.85, on weaker oil.
- **Fairfax Financial (FFH): about −1.2%** after selling nearly all of its stake in India's IIFL Finance in a block deal worth about US$370 million to US$375 million.
- **Canadian Tire (CTC.A): +2.4%** to $222.59, and **Linamar (LNR): +1.5%** to $85.84, leading the consumer group.
- **Bausch Health (BHC): +3.5%** to $12.52, and **Colliers International (CIGI): +1.9%** to $132.38, among the gainers outside resources. Loblaw also touched a new 52-week high of $71.13 after announcing that its product catalogue is now available through Muse, Meta's AI-powered personal agent.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The main macro signal was a firmer US dollar on hawkish Fed talk, which Proactive said kept the greenback near a 19-month high. That pressured commodities and nudged the loonie lower by about 0.17 US cents. Outside resources, the tone was not panicky: seven of 12 TSX subgroups rose and the Nasdaq finished higher. This was a sharp sector rotation out of commodities rather than a broad flight from risk.

## Bottom line

The TSX logged its worst day since June as gold, copper and oil all slid on a stronger US dollar and supply worries. Most smaller sectors rose, but the heavyweight miners, energy producers and financials pulled the index down to its lowest close in five months.
