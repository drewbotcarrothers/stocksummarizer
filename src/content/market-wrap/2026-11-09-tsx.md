## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 9

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **36,446.26**, up **657.17 points (+1.84%)**. That was its best level in about a month and more than erased last Thursday's 736-point AI-driven slide.

The index opened at its low of the day near 36,038 and never looked back, reaching about 36,576 before settling a little below the high. Volume was roughly 306 million shares. Breadth was strong: advancers beat decliners on the Toronto exchange by about 687 to 285, with 64 unchanged.

The Canadian dollar was flat at about **70.82 US cents**.

Wall Street had an even bigger day. The Dow jumped about 1,104 points (+2.2%) to 52,389.52, the S&P 500 rose about 173 points (+2.3%) to 7,549.81, and the Nasdaq climbed about 674 points (+2.7%) to 25,253.14.

## What drove the TSX on November 9

**Oil fell hard, and that eased inflation worries.** December crude dropped about US$3.9, or roughly 4.3%, to about US$86.9 a barrel, and January Brent fell nearly 6%. Coverage tied the drop to hopes for US–Iran diplomacy. Cheaper oil cooled inflation fears and nudged Canadian yields a little lower, which helped rate-sensitive groups.

**Gold and base-metal miners led.** December gold rose about US$76–78 to roughly US$4,151–US$4,153 an ounce. Barrick reported higher third-quarter profit as stronger gold prices outweighed lower production, and the gold subgroup gained about 4.7%. Copper names joined in, pushing the broader materials group up about 4.6%.

**Banks got some relief.** With yields easing, the big lenders rallied. Royal Bank rose about 1.5%, and TD, BMO, Scotiabank and CIBC each gained close to 2%, according to Trading Economics figures cited by Canada News Media.

**A US tech rebound continued, with a twist.** Chip stocks such as AMD and Broadcom surged, extending Friday's bounce from last week's selloff. Nvidia was the exception, falling about 4–5% after its board said it would investigate CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a businesswoman whom US intelligence agencies describe as a Chinese spy. In Toronto, the tech group still lagged because of Constellation Software.

**Oil-patch deal news.** Strathcona Resources said Sunday it would buy Greenfire Resources in a deal valued at roughly $2.7 billion to $2.9 billion, including debt, expanding its Alberta oil sands footprint.

## Sectors on the move

- **Gold: about +4.7%**, the session leader, with Iamgold, Novagold and Barrick among the gainers.
- **Materials: about +4.6%**, helped by Ero Copper and Hudbay Minerals.
- **Industrials: about +2.6%**, led by Hammond Power Solutions and Air Canada.
- **Financials:** the big banks rose roughly 1.5% to 2% as yields eased.
- **Energy: about −0.9%**, one of only two lower groups, as crude fell; Vermilion and Advantage Energy slipped.
- **Telecom: about −0.5%**, with Telus and Cogeco Communications lower.

Ten of the 12 main subgroups finished higher. (Sector figures differ slightly by source.)

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Greenfire Resources (GFR): about +38%**, to $10.94, after agreeing to be acquired by Strathcona. **Strathcona (SCR)** slipped 34 cents to $36.00.
- **Hammond Power Solutions (HPS.A): about +10.6%**, to roughly $207, the top Composite gainer.
- **Energy Fuels (EFR): about +9.8%**, to $10.32, and **Silvercorp Metals (SVM): about +9.1%**, to $13.73.
- **Ero Copper (ERO): about +8.4%**, to $44.15, and **Hudbay Minerals (HBM): about +7.1%**, to $32.71.
- **Iamgold (IMG): about +5.7%**, to $27.95, and **Novagold (NG): about +7.4%**, to $9.71.
- **Air Canada (AC): about +6.3%**, to $23.30, as lower oil eased jet-fuel worries.
- **Barrick Mining (ABX): about +4.5%**, to $59.76, after third-quarter results.
- **Constellation Software (CSU): about −3.8%**, to $3,392.29, after reporting a 16% year-over-year drop in third-quarter free cash flow.
- **Thomson Reuters (TRI): about −3.6%**, to $153.18, and **Curaleaf (CURA): about −4.2%**, to $12.68.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

Fear faded. The S&P/TSX 60 VIX fell about 6.5% to 16.42. Small caps joined the rally, with the TSX Venture Exchange up about 14 points, or 1.7%, to 836.07.

The loonie held steady near 70.8 US cents even as the US Dollar Index slipped about 0.6%. The US 10-year Treasury yield was little changed at about 4.87%.

The Bank of Canada's next rate decision remains **December 9**.

## What to watch next

- **This week:** US AI-linked earnings from CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials, which will test whether last week's tech selloff is over.
- **Oil and US–Iran headlines:** Crude has swung several dollars in a single day more than once lately, so energy remains a big swing factor for the index.

## Bottom line

Lower oil, stronger gold and a broad US rebound pushed the TSX to a one-month high, with miners, industrials and banks doing the heavy lifting while energy, telecom and Constellation Software lagged.
