## Tape snapshot: S&P/TSX on November 26

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at **34,426.88**, up **246.89 points (+0.72%)**. Reuters said it was the index's highest close since November 18, an eight-day high. Investing.com noted it was the first back-to-back daily gain since November 9.

Toronto had the stage to itself. US stock markets were closed for Thanksgiving, and Investing.com described the session as holiday-thinned trade. Unlike Wednesday's wild swing, this was a calm, steady climb. Delayed TMX quote data showed the index opened at 34,304.77 and traded between **34,286.59 and 34,465.51**, so it never dipped below Wednesday's close.

Breadth was strong. Advancers beat decliners **681 to 230**, with 69 unchanged, Investing.com said. The small-cap TSX Venture Exchange rose **12.56 points (+1.8%)** to **720.67**, according to Baystreet.ca.

The Canadian dollar eased to **70.21 US cents**, down from 70.33 on Wednesday, The Canadian Press said.

## What drove the TSX on November 26

**Oil rose on Middle East supply risks.** Yemen's Houthis said they hit Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery, and Iran said mines struck ships in the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported. Brent futures rose 1.5% to about **US$96 a barrel** and US crude futures added 1.2% to **US$84.72**. The Canadian Press noted there was no settlement price for crude or gold contracts because of the US holiday, so treat those as indicative quotes.

**Tariff fears faded.** Wednesday's selloff followed a US proposal for new tariffs on 14 trading partners. Canada was not on the list. On Thursday, analysts told Reuters the plan held no nasty surprises: the new rates would not stack on top of July's tariffs and likely would not start until February or March. Stocks in Europe and Asia rallied, and US stock futures rose about 0.7% even with Wall Street shut.

**A break from AI jitters.** With US markets closed, the volatile AI trade took a pause. "We do think that a lot of the excesses in the markets have been washed out and we could be set up for a year-end rally," Michael Dehal of Raymond James told Reuters.

**Canadian jobs data held steady.** Statistics Canada said payroll employment rose by **9,700 (+0.1%)** in September, after falling a combined 31,400 in July and August. Average weekly earnings were up 3.3% from a year earlier, at C$1,357.92, Investing.com said.

## Sectors on the move

- **Energy: +1.9%**, the co-leader, as oil climbed (Reuters and Baystreet).
- **Materials: +1.7% to +1.9%**, depending on the source, as copper rebounded on bargain hunting. Baystreet had the gold subgroup up 1.5%.
- **Information technology: +0.9%** and **industrials: +0.6%**, Reuters said. Investing.com had the S&P/TSX Battery Metals index up 3.8%.
- Baystreet said nine of the 12 subgroups gained ground. The three laggards were rate-sensitive groups: **telecom (−0.4%)**, **real estate (−0.1%)** and **utilities (−0.01%)**.

## Big movers on the TSX

- **Americas Gold and Silver (USA): +6.72%** to $2.86, the top gainer on the Composite, Investing.com said.
- **Aya Gold & Silver (AYA): +4.40%** to $29.44, extending Wednesday's gain of more than 5%.
- **Bombardier (BBD.B): +4.20%** to $303.93, a standout outside the resource groups.
- **Avino Silver & Gold (ASM): +4.8%** to $5.51, **Discovery Silver (DSV): +4.1%** to $9.22 and **K92 Mining (KNT): +3.4%** to $23.30, per Baystreet.
- **Alimentation Couche-Tard (ATD): +1.9%** to $86.94. CEO Alex Miller said the company does not plan to raise its offer for Polish convenience store operator Żabka or extend the acceptance period, Reuters and Baystreet reported.
- **Enbridge (ENB)** slipped 9 cents to $66.59. The Globe and Mail reported Wednesday that the company is in talks to sell a 6% stake in its Line 3 crude pipeline to a group of Indigenous communities for about $1.5 billion.
- **Brookfield Infrastructure Partners (BIP.UN): −1.98%**, the biggest decliner on the Composite, Investing.com said. **Curaleaf (CURA)** fell 1.72% and **Linamar (LNR)** fell 1.33% to $89.62, giving back part of Wednesday's 5.5% jump.
- In telecom, **Rogers (RCI.B)** fell 36 cents to $50.33 and **Quebecor (QBR.B)** lost 45 cents to $60.33.

## Rates, CAD & risk tone

The loonie slipped about 0.3% to around 70.2 US cents in thin holiday trading, Investing.com said. Rate views did not change much. Markets still expect the Bank of Canada to hike in December after Governor Tiff Macklem warned earlier this week that higher energy prices could keep feeding into inflation. In the US, futures imply a **98%** chance of a quarter-point Fed hike in December, Reuters said, while talk of a half-point move has faded.

The softer rate-sensitive groups (telecom, real estate and utilities) fit that backdrop. Higher-rate bets tend to weigh on dividend-heavy sectors while commodity producers lead.

## Bottom line

With Wall Street closed, energy and mining stocks pushed the TSX to an eight-day high in a calm, broad rally. Telecom, real estate and utilities lagged as December rate-hike bets held.
