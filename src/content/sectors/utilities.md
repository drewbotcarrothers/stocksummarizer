Eight liquid Canadian names — Fortis, Hydro One, Emera, Canadian Utilities, Brookfield Renewable, Capital Power, ATCO and Algonquin — add up to about **CA$144 billion** of market value on the October 25, 2026 screener. The sum double-counts Canadian Utilities, because ATCO still controls it. Over the past month the peer median is up about **3.1%**, ahead of the S&P/TSX Composite’s approximate **+1.4%**. Over one year the median is only about **+1.5%**, far behind the Composite’s approximate **+18%**. Setup for this living note: a yield sector that did not get the bank or pipeline re-rating, with a late-2027 merger sitting on top of three of the rows.

## What is driving Canadian utility stocks: the Emera deal, November plans and the Bank of Canada

1. **October 6, 2026 — Emera, ATCO and Canadian Utilities sign a definitive agreement.** Emera is buying Canadian Utilities in an all-share deal valued at about **C$14.3 billion**. Class A holders other than ATCO get **0.755** of an Emera share; Class B holders get **0.819**. ATCO holders get **0.865** of an Emera share plus a share of “New ATCO,” the spin-off for housing, defence, ports and retail energy. After closing, existing Emera holders own about **60%** and former Canadian Utilities and ATCO holders about **40%**. Closing is targeted for the **third or fourth quarter of 2027**, subject to the usual regulators. Canadian Utilities CEO Bob Myles said on the call that this is not a synergy deal. The combined plan is about **C$32 billion** of capital spending through 2030, **7% to 8%** rate-base growth off a base near **C$45 billion**, with about **95%** of earnings regulated and about **80%** from Florida and Alberta. Emera kept a **1% to 2%** dividend-growth target.

2. **November 3, 2026 — Fortis third quarter and a new five-year plan.** Fortis said on October 5 that it will release third-quarter results and the **2027–2031** capital outlook before the open on November 3, with a call at **8:30 a.m. ET**. David Hutchens is CEO and Jocelyn Perry is CFO. For the largest name in this table, that plan is the catalyst, not the Emera merger.

3. **November 6, 2026 — Emera’s quarter and its own 2027–2031 plan.** Emera said on October 9 that it will release third-quarter results and the capital and funding plan before the open on November 6, with a call at **9:30 a.m. Atlantic / 8:30 a.m. ET**. That is the first full look at how Emera funds the deal and the rate base at the same time. Second-quarter adjusted EPS was **C$0.69**, down from **C$0.79** a year earlier (August 7).

4. **November 10, 2026 — Hydro One.** Hydro One will release third-quarter results before the open on November 10, with a call at **8:00 a.m. ET** (company release, October 9). Hydro One is the Ontario wires pure play and the name in this set with the lowest yield.

5. **October 28, 2026 — Bank of Canada decision.** The overnight target was still **2.25%** after the September 2 hold. For utilities the channel is allowed returns, discount rates and the cost of funding those capital plans, not bank net-interest margins. A firmer rate path raises the hurdle on a sector that already lagged the TSX over one year.

6. **Algonquin is the recovery row, not a second Fortis.** Algonquin’s five-year price return is about **−60%** and the ten-year is about **−36%**. It is liquid (the high-volume common) and it is in this note because investors still trade it as a utility turnaround. It is not the same business as Hydro One’s rate base.

## Which Canadian utility stocks are leading and lagging

**One month:** **ATCO (+10.8%)** leads, then **Fortis (+7.9%)** and **Hydro One (+6.5%)**. Softest: **Brookfield Renewable (−2.8%)**, **Capital Power (−2.6%)** and **Canadian Utilities (−2.3%)**. The month belongs to the deal parent and the two large regulated holds, not to the power producers.

**One year:** **ATCO (+50.3%)** is in a different zip code, then **Canadian Utilities (+22.8%)** and **Fortis (+12.3%)**. **Capital Power (−14.1%)** is the laggard, then **Algonquin (−6.9%)**. Emera is about flat (**−0.6%**). Relative to the TSX’s approximate **+18%** one-year move, only ATCO and Canadian Utilities clearly won. The merger premium is already in ATCO. It is not in the sector median.

## How regulated utilities differ from power producers and from the merger

**Fortis** is the diversified regulated hold: Canada, the United States and the Caribbean, and the name whose November 3 plan sets the tone for the group. **Hydro One** is Ontario transmission and distribution, lowest yield, highest price-to-book in the regulated cluster (**2.51x**). **Emera** is Florida-heavy and the company issuing the shares. **Canadian Utilities** is the Alberta gas and electric rate base being acquired. **ATCO** is the parent, and after the spin-off it is also the housing, defence, ports and retail-energy stub.

**Brookfield Renewable** and **Capital Power** are the power rows. Brookfield Renewable is a global yieldco partnership with a trailing loss and the highest yield in the set. Capital Power is the Alberta-centred contracted generator whose trailing and forward multiples say earnings are still thin relative to the price. **Algonquin** is the post-reset utility, cheap on book, still negative over five and ten years.

The merger is not a fourth business model. It is a clock. Nothing in the **C$32 billion** plan shows up as combined earnings until late 2027. Until then, Emera, Canadian Utilities and ATCO can move on deal headlines while Fortis and Hydro One move on their own capital plans.

## Bull case for Canadian utility stocks

1. **The coupon is still near 4% while the one-year tape lagged the TSX.** A median indicated yield of **4.00%** against a median one-year price return of about **+1.5%** is the set-up income investors wanted from banks and did not get. Fortis at **3.16%** and Emera at **4.31%** are the core versions.

2. **Rate-base growth has dates on it.** Fortis (November 3), Emera (November 6) and Hydro One (November 10) all put new numbers in front of the market within two weeks. The Emera deal’s **7% to 8%** rate-base path is a published target, not a slogan, even if it is a 2027 closing story.

3. **Capital Power and Emera are not priced like Fortis.** Capital Power’s **CA$79.80** target and Emera’s **CA$76.25** target are the two CAD gaps above 10%, with 10 and 9 analysts. That is a different bet from owning Hydro One at **23x** forward earnings.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian utility stocks

1. **The sector already lost a year to the index.** Median **+1.5%** versus about **+18%** for the TSX is the cost of a bond-proxy tape. If the Bank of Canada’s October 28 decision firms the rate path, the discount rate on those rate bases goes up before the November plans can answer.

2. **The merger is dilution now and growth later.** Emera is issuing shares for a deal management says is not about synergies, with closing in late 2027. ATCO is already on its **CA$81.86** target. There is not much left in the parent if the spin-off is the piece investors already paid for in October.

3. **Thin trailing earnings and stretched payouts.** Canadian Utilities at **292.9x**, Capital Power at **114.8x** trailing and **42.8x** forward, ATCO and Algonquin with payouts above **100%**, and Brookfield Renewable’s loss all say “yield” and “earnings coverage” are not the same sentence. A soft regulatory decision hits the dividend debate faster here than it does at Hydro One.

## What to watch next for Canadian utilities: three capital plans, the Bank of Canada and the deal

1. **October 28, 2026 — Bank of Canada overnight-rate decision.** The funding-cost backdrop for every capital plan in this note.

2. **November 3 — Fortis third quarter and the 2027–2031 outlook, 8:30 a.m. ET.** The largest regulated hold, and the cleanest read on rate-base growth without a merger overlay.

3. **November 6 — Emera third quarter and the 2027–2031 capital and funding plan, 8:30 a.m. ET.** How many shares, how much debt, and whether the **1% to 2%** dividend-growth target survives the first full update after the deal.

4. **November 10 — Hydro One, 8:00 a.m. ET.** Ontario rate base, with no Emera share issuance in the story.

5. **Deal mechanics into 2027.** AUC, FERC and the other approvals the October briefs already flagged, plus whatever “New ATCO” is actually worth when the stub starts trading. Canadian Utilities’ depressed trailing earnings need a bridge to the **19.2x** forward multiple before that close.
