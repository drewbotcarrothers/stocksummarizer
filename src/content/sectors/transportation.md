Nine liquid names — CPKC, CN Rail, Waste Connections, GFL Environmental, TFI International, Exchange Income, Air Canada, Mullen Group and Cargojet — are about **CA$336 billion** of market value on the October 23, 2026 close. The two railways are about **CA$215 billion** of that. Over the past month the peer median is about **+0.8%**, close to an S&P/TSX Composite that was about **+0.1%** on the same exchange-close window. Over one year the median is about **+18%**, in line with a Composite near **+18.6%**. The median hides the tails. Mullen is up about **100%** over the year. Air Canada is down about **24%** over the month. Waste Connections is up about **19%** over the month and only about **6%** over the year. Setup for this living note: a sector that matched the index in the middle, and three tapes that did not.

## What is driving Canadian transportation stocks: a rail downgrade, a waste beat and a buyback price

1. **October 19, 2026 — Raymond James downgrades CN.** Steve Hansen cut CN to Market Perform from Outperform and trimmed the target to **C$195 from C$200**. The October 20 brief is the write-up. In the first two weeks of the fourth quarter, CN’s revenue ton miles were down **4.7%** and CPKC’s were up **1.2%**. Third-quarter traffic was **+3.8%** at CN and **+6.1%** at CPKC. CN moved a record **7.94 million tonnes** of western grain in the third quarter. The second-quarter print, on July 24, was revenue of **C$4.75 billion**, up **11%**, diluted EPS of **C$2.06** and adjusted EPS of **C$2.08**. The outlook was low-single-digit growth in revenue ton miles and mid-to-high-single-digit growth in adjusted EPS, with capital spending of about **C$2.8 billion**. The operating ratio worsened **80 basis points** to **62.5%**, and fuel expense was **C$659 million**. CN reports third-quarter results on **October 30, 2026**, before the open, with a call at **8:30 a.m. ET**. The October 23 close is **$172.50**, above the October 19 close of **$169.63** in that brief. The one-year return is **+28.5%**. The CAD target on this page is **CA$183.11** from **20** analysts, **+6.2%**. Hansen’s **C$195** is one broker above that average.

2. **October 21, 2026 — Waste Connections beats, raises, and hikes the dividend 10%.** The October 22 brief is the write-up. Third-quarter revenue was **US$2.638 billion**, up **7.3%**. Adjusted EPS was **US$1.58**. Adjusted EBITDA was **US$895.8 million**, a **34.0%** margin. The 2026 outlook moved to revenue of about **US$10.09 billion**, net income of about **US$1.18 billion**, adjusted EBITDA of about **US$3.345 billion**, and adjusted free cash flow of **US$1.40–1.45 billion**. The quarterly dividend went to **US$0.385** from **US$0.35**, or **US$1.54** a year, payable November 19 to holders of record November 5. Buybacks passed **US$1 billion**. Those figures are US dollars. The October 21 TSX close, before the release, was **C$236.45** (NYSE **US$168.28**). The October 23 TSX close is **$261.10**, and the NYSE close that day is **US$186.60**. The one-month return in this table is **+19.3%**, the best in the set. The one-year return is only **+5.5%**.

3. **October 22, 2026 — Mullen’s record quarter, and a spike that did not hold the next day.** Revenue was **C$653.2 million**, up **16.3%**. Adjusted EPS was **C$0.52**. Net income was **C$51.5 million**, up **55%**. Adjusted OIBDA was **C$118.6 million**, margin **18.2%**. The October 23 brief is the write-up. The shares closed October 22 at **C$32.28**. They closed October 23 at **$28.76**. The one-year return is **+100.4%**. The six-month return is **+47.9%** and the year-to-date return is **+81.0%**, so the rally was not one session. On October 19 Mullen agreed to buy Willson International, expected to add about **C$80 million** of annual revenue, with a closing targeted around **November 2**. The CAD target on this page is **CA$30.22** from **8** analysts, **+5.1%** versus the October 23 close and below the October 22 close. The brief’s pre-print S&P average was **C$29.45** from 11. The stock spent one day through the targets and gave part of it back.

4. **September 28, 2026 — Air Canada’s C$800 million issuer bid, and a stock now under that price.** The company bought **27,586,206** shares at **C$29.00**, about **9.8%** of the shares, in a Dutch auction that cleared at the bottom of a **C$29–C$33** range. The September 29 brief is the write-up. The stock was about **C$26.15** after the preliminary results. The October 23 close is **$21.11**. That is about **27%** below the price the company paid. The one-month return is **−23.6%**, the worst in the set. The one-year return is still **+15.1%**. There is no dividend. Trailing P/E is **14.8x** and forward P/E is **29.0x**, so the next-year earnings estimate is lower than the trailing run-rate. This note does not have a separate October release that explains the last leg from the mid-20s to **$21.11**. The price is the fact. The October 29 calendar date is the next place to look.

5. **The rest of the calendar.** TradingView’s earnings calendar, checked October 27, shows October 28 for CPKC and GFL, October 29 for TFI and Air Canada, November 11 for Cargojet and November 12 for Exchange Income. CN’s October 30 date and 8:30 a.m. call are from the October 20 brief, not from that calendar. Waste Connections’ next date on the calendar is February 17, 2027, and Mullen’s is February 10, 2027. The waste and trucking prints that matter for this table have already happened, except TFI and GFL.

6. **What this note leaves out on purpose.** Westshore Terminals (about **CA$2.7 billion**) is a coal terminal. The October 20 CN brief left it out, and it stays out. Chorus (about **CA$0.7 billion**) is below the scale of this table. CDRs of Union Pacific, Uber and UPS are not Canadian operators. Engineering firms in the October 22 Waste Connections brief (WSP, AtkinsRéalis, Stantec, Badger, Bird, Aecon) are a different industry. GFL is in, even though that brief could not see it in the October 8 export. It is about **CA$28 billion** and liquid. Cargojet is about **CA$1.2 billion**, with a 10-day average volume near **43,000** shares, the thinnest line here. It stays because both the CN brief and the Mullen brief used it as the air-cargo peer.

## Which Canadian transportation stocks are leading and lagging

**One month:** **Waste Connections (+19.3%)** leads, then **Mullen (+8.2%)** and **GFL (+7.9%)**. **CN (+2.3%)** and **CPKC (+0.8%)** are the flat middle, which is why the median is about **+1%**. **Air Canada (−23.6%)** is the laggard, then **TFI (−10.8%)** and **Exchange Income (−7.1%)**. The waste names and Mullen had the news. The airline and the large trucker did not participate.

**One year:** **Mullen (+100.4%)** leads by a wide margin, then **Exchange Income (+45.6%)**, **CN (+28.5%)** and **TFI (+26.3%)**. **GFL (−1.0%)** is the only name under zero. **Cargojet (+1.8%)** and **Waste Connections (+5.5%)** are the other laggards on this window. The one-month leader is not the one-year leader. CPKC at **+18.1%** is the median stock, and it matched the TSX. CN beat both its railway peer and the index over the year, which is the backdrop for a downgrade that is about the next quarter’s traffic, not about a broken one-year chart.

## How the rails differ from waste, trucking and the airline

**CN and CPKC** are the franchise. CN is cheaper on earnings and more expensive on book. The downgrade is about traffic growth handing the lead to CPKC, not about a broken balance sheet. Both CAD targets sit within **8%** of the price.

**Waste Connections** is a US-dollar compounder with a Canadian listing, a **42x** trailing multiple, and a Toronto target page that should not be averaged with CN’s. **GFL** is the other Canadian-listed waste company, with a trailing loss and a US target that is the one with a range. They do not belong in one multiple.

**TFI and Mullen** are the truckers. TFI is the scale, down **27%** over three months, with the liquid target on the NYSE. Mullen is the one-year chart, a record quarter, and a price that spent October 22 above the CAD target and October 23 just under it. **Exchange Income** is aviation plus manufacturing, acquired and held, with a **2.56%** yield and a **30%** CAD gap. **Cargojet** is the air-cargo line the briefs already use. **Air Canada** is the passenger airline that bought stock at **C$29** and now trades at **$21.11**.

## Bull case for Canadian transportation stocks

1. **CN is still the cheaper railway, and the one-year chart agrees.** **22x** trailing against CPKC’s **29x**, a one-year return of **+28.5%** against **+18.1%**, and a 20-analyst CAD target that is still above the price. The downgrade cut the enthusiasm. It did not put the target under the stock.

2. **Waste Connections just printed the quarter the multiple is paying for.** Revenue, adjusted EPS, the free-cash-flow range and a sixteenth consecutive double-digit dividend increase are in the October 21 release, in US dollars. The NYSE target is still above the NYSE price. The one-year return of only **+5.5%** means the stock did a lot of nothing before this month’s **+19%**.

3. **Air Canada’s CAD gap is eleven analysts, and the company was a buyer at C$29.** **CA$32.73** is **+55%** above **$21.11**. The trailing sales multiple is **0.25x**. That is the statistical case. It sits next to a forward P/E of **29x**, which is the reason it may be cheap for a reason.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian transportation stocks

1. **The rails are priced for the index they already matched.** CPKC’s one-year return is the sector median and the TSX. CN’s CAD upside is **6%**. A target that close to the price needs the October 30 quarter to cooperate. The downgrade’s evidence is already a weaker fourth-quarter start and a grain comparison that gets harder.

2. **The Toronto target on Waste Connections is a trap, and GFL does not have the earnings.** C$200 with no range, against a US$202 target that means something different, will produce a fake “overvalued by 23%” headline. GFL’s **78.7x** forward multiple and trailing loss are the other waste stock. Averaging the two is how a sector multiple becomes nonsense. The forward average in the table includes that **78.7x** on purpose, so it is visible.

3. **Mullen’s chart and Air Canada’s buyback price can both be warnings.** A stock up **100%** in a year that spent one day above every fresh target can mean the quarter was good and the entry was late. A company that paid **C$29** for stock now at **$21** can mean the tender was the top of the range for a reason. TFI’s **−27%** three-month move is the cyclical version of the same doubt. Cargojet’s five-year **−58%** is the long version.
