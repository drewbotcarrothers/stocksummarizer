The Big Six — Royal Bank, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC and National Bank — plus challenger EQB make up about **CA$1.24 trillion** of liquid market value on the October 25, 2026 screener. Over the past year the peer median is up about **37%**, roughly double the S&P/TSX Composite’s approximate **+18%** one-year price return. The last month cooled: the peer median slipped about **1.4%** while the Composite was roughly flat to slightly up. Setup for this living note: strong trailing tape, a beat-heavy Q3, and a Street that no longer offers much “easy” upside to average targets.

## What is driving Canadian bank stocks: Q3 earnings, buybacks and the Bank of Canada

**November 9, 2026 update.** On **October 28, 2026** the Bank of Canada raised the overnight target by 25 basis points to **2.50%**, its first hike since July 2023 and the end of seven straight holds at 2.25%. It said further increases may be needed but it would proceed carefully; the next decision is **December 9**. Banks can earn more on loans when rates rise, but Statistics Canada’s October labour survey showed unemployment up 0.2 points to **6.7%**, which keeps credit costs in view. On the screener the peer median price moved **−0.6%** between the November 3 and November 9 files (EQB +2.4%, National Bank +0.2%; Scotiabank −1.6%, TD −1.2%). The S&P/TSX Composite closed at 35,789.09 on November 6, up 0.48% on the week. Calendar items below dated November 3–8 have since reported; this update summarizes only the results named here.

1. **August 25–28, 2026 — Q3 earnings sweep.** The big banks reported fiscal Q3 (quarter ended July 31). Headlines from the group roundup: RBC profit about **CA$6.0B** (EPS CA$4.23; adjusted EPS about CA$4.28, above Street); TD reported about **CA$4.6B** / adjusted **CA$4.7B** with adjusted EPS **CA$2.77** (+26% YoY); Scotiabank called a **record** quarter with adjusted ROE about **14.2%** and CET1 **13.1%**; CIBC earned about **CA$2.41B** (adjusted EPS about CA$2.73 vs ~CA$2.50 expected); BMO’s reported profit fell on sale-related items but **adjusted EPS about CA$3.96** beat ~CA$3.75; National Bank earned about **CA$1.31B** with adjusted EPS about **CA$3.39** vs ~CA$3.21. The common thread: beats, capital return, and credit that was manageable in the quarter.

2. **August 27, 2026 — TD’s capital-return illustration.** On the Q3 call TD sketched a path to return **over CA$13B** in fiscal 2027 (mostly buybacks, on top of the dividend) if organic capital and RWA growth stay on track — an illustration, not a hard commitment. That narrative, plus a plan for about **100 U.S. branches by 2028** (subject to approvals), is why TD still leads the one-year tape in this set even after a big run.

3. **Late August 2026 — buybacks and dividends held or edged.** BMO announced a plan to buy back **25 million** common shares and held its quarterly dividend at **CA$1.71**. National Bank declared a **CA$1.32** quarterly common dividend (payable November 1, 2026). Across the group, payout ratios on the screener still sit mostly in the **40–58%** range — covered, not stretched like a pure income trust.

4. **National Bank × Canadian Western Bank.** NA’s Q3 commentary still carries acquisition and integration noise from the **Canadian Western Bank** deal (and Laurentian-related items). That is why CWB no longer sits as a separate liquid peer here — the western challenger story is folding into National Bank’s scale story.

5. **October 28, 2026 — Bank of Canada hike.** On **October 28, 2026** the Bank of Canada raised the overnight target by 25 basis points to **2.50%**, its first hike since July 2023 and the end of seven straight holds at 2.25%. It said further increases may be needed but it would proceed carefully; the next decision is **December 9**. For banks, higher rates can support net interest margins but also test borrowers and loan growth; October unemployment rose to 6.7%.

6. **U.S. regulatory and franchise risk (ongoing).** TD’s AML remediation remains priority one for management; BMO and TD both carry meaningful U.S. footprints where returns still trail the Canadian books. That overhang is quieter than it was a year ago, but it has not disappeared from the risk list.

## Which Canadian banks are leading and lagging

**One month (screener):** only **TD (+1.3%)** finished green. Softest: **EQB (−6.2%)**, then **National Bank (−4.0%)** and **BMO (−3.0%)**. The Big Six median was roughly flat-to-down — a cooldown after the summer rerating, not a sector break.

**One year:** **TD (+54.2%)** still leads by a wide margin, then **Scotiabank (+44.9%)** and **CIBC (+40.5%)**. The pack from RBC through EQB sits in a tight **+34% to +37%** band. Relative to the TSX’s approximate **+18%** one-year move, every name in this peer set won — the question is how much of that win is already in the price.

## How the Big Six differ from challenger bank EQB

The **Big Six** share OSFI capital rules, national deposit franchises, and wealth/capital-markets engines that smooth the cycle. Differences still matter: RBC is the quality/scale benchmark; TD is the U.S. + capital-return story; BMO is the other large U.S. book, still working on returns; Scotiabank is the international pivot with the highest yield in this set; CIBC is the Canada-centred operator that screens cheapest on trailing earnings; National Bank is the smaller compounder absorbing CWB.

**EQB** (Equitable Bank’s parent) is the liquid challenger in this note: smaller, more mortgage- and alternative-banking tilted, cheaper on forward earnings and book, and more volatile on the one- and three-month tape. It is here as a second lens on Canadian banking — not as a seventh Big Six name. Laurentian remains too small for this table; CWB is no longer a standalone peer.

## Bull case for Canadian bank stocks

1. **Earnings and capital return still compound.** Q3 showed beats across the group, CET1 ratios that support buybacks, and payout ratios that leave room. If loan losses stay contained, buybacks (TD’s FY27 illustration, BMO’s 25M authorization, NA’s program) can keep EPS growing even if revenue growth cools.

2. **Rate path may help NIMs again.** After the October 28 hike to **2.50%**, a firmer Bank of Canada path into late 2026 would not automatically hurt banks — higher policy rates can widen lending spreads, especially for deposit-rich franchises, as long as credit costs do not spike.

3. **Discount names still exist inside the pack.** CIBC on trailing P/E, Scotiabank on yield, EQB on forward P/E and P/B, and BMO/NA on upside-to-target are different ways the sector is not a single crowded trade. A DIY investor can still choose exposure rather than “own the index of banks blindly.”

## Bear case and risks for Canadian bank stocks

1. **The re-rating is largely done.** Median +37% over one year vs ~+18% for the TSX, with several names at or above consensus targets, leaves less cushion if Q4 credit or capital-markets fees disappoint.

2. **Credit and household leverage.** Canadian mortgages, HELOCs and commercial books are still the swing factor. A harder landing, sticky unemployment, or a sharper rate shock would show up in provisions faster than in dividends.

3. **U.S. and regulatory overhang.** TD’s remediation and U.S. returns, BMO’s U.S. profitability rebuild, and ongoing OSFI/U.S. supervisory attention are slower-burn risks that can cap multiples even when Canadian quarters look fine.

## What to watch next for Canadian banks: rates, Q4 earnings and Street targets

1. **December 9, 2026 — next Bank of Canada decision.** After the October 28 hike to 2.50%, whether a second hike follows is the near-term macro pivot for NIMs and the mortgage pipeline.

2. **Fiscal Q4 / year-end prints (December 2026 window).** Confirm whether Q3’s beat streak and PCL guidance still hold into year-end; watch CET1 and buyback pace.

3. **TD U.S. remediation milestones and branch-plan approvals.** Progress (or setbacks) will keep moving the leader of the one-year tape.

4. **National Bank / CWB integration updates.** Cost saves, credit migration and capital trajectory will decide whether NA’s discount to the larger banks narrows.

5. **Street target refreshes after Q4.** If average targets stop chasing the tape, relative value inside the sector gets clearer; if they keep lagging price, the “priced in” warning gets louder.
