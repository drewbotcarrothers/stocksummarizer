Seven liquid Canadian midstream names — Enbridge, TC Energy, Pembina, AltaGas, Keyera, South Bow and Gibson Energy — add up to about **CA$334.3 billion** of market value on the October 25, 2026 screener. Over the past year the peer median is up about **28%**, ahead of the S&P/TSX Composite’s approximate **+18%**. The last month stayed positive: the peer median is up about **3.2%**, against a Composite that was roughly up about **1%**. Setup for this living note: a yield sector that has worked, a giant (Enbridge) that has not kept up over one year, and a west-coast oil pipeline that is now a federal project of national interest without being a company cash-flow line yet.

These are not producer multiples. The median trailing P/E is about **26x** and the median forward P/E is still about **23x**. Investors pay that because the cash distribution is the product: the median indicated yield is about **4.2%**, and several trailing payout ratios sit above **100%** of accounting earnings. Enbridge and South Bow yield about **5.8%**. AltaGas, the utility-and-midstream hybrid, yields about **2.4%** and is the income laggard in the set.

## What is driving Canadian pipeline stocks: Pacific Link, Enbridge’s equity and the gas buildout

1. **October 1, 2026 — Pacific Link is listed as a project of national interest.** Ottawa listed the west-coast oil pipeline, now called Pacific Link, under the Building Canada Act. Pembina’s October 1 release says its economic interest through construction is **10%**, with a chance to buy up to another **10%** after commercial operation, full discretion on any final investment decision, and no at-risk development capital before that decision. The project is described as about **one million barrels a day** from Alberta to the west coast. The Prime Minister’s office said Canada and Alberta will share equal ownership, Indigenous communities will be offered at least **10%**, and the Major Projects Office is working toward final conditions by **September 1, 2027**. For Pembina’s stock, this is an option on a future pipe, not a barrel in this year’s EBITDA.

2. **August–September 2026 — Enbridge funded crude deals with equity.** Company releases on the Enbridge news index: an August 26 agreement to acquire Salt Creek Midstream’s crude gathering business; an August 27 joint venture with KKR, described as a **C$2.7 billion** transaction, to fund the Aspen Point and Sunrise expansion programs on the Westcoast gas system in British Columbia; a September 8 succession (Greg Ebel retiring December 31, 2026, Michele Harradence becoming CEO on January 1, 2027); a September 9 agreement to acquire Tallgrass’s crude transportation business and a **C$2.6 billion** bought deal; and a September 14 close of that offering at **C$3.0 billion** including the over-allotment. Enbridge is the one-year laggard in this set (**+0.6%**) and the only name down over six months (**−6.4%**). The equity cheque is a large part of why.

3. **September 21, 2026 — TC Energy’s Mexico sale.** TC Energy agreed to sell the Guadalajara–Manzanillo gas pipeline for about **C$560 million**, a small recycling deal relative to a **CA$91 billion** company. The October 1 StockSummarizer brief covers the terms. On this screener TC Energy is up about **20%** over one year and about **3.2%** over one month, with an indicated yield of about **4.0%**, the low end of the pure-pipe group.

4. **October 5, 2026 — Enbridge’s third-quarter webcast.** Enbridge will host its third-quarter call and webcast on **November 6, 2026, at 9 a.m. ET**. That is the next place to hear how management talks about the equity issued in September, the crude acquisitions, the Westcoast expansion funding, and the dividend.

5. **South Bow is the liquids stub.** South Bow is the liquids pipeline company spun out of TC Energy. It is the cheapest trailing earnings multiple in the set (**17.2x**) and yields about **5.75%**, in line with Enbridge. The screener’s five-year and ten-year returns are the same **+77%**, which is what a short trading history looks like. Read those cells as “since listing,” not as a decade of compounding.

## Which Canadian pipeline stocks are leading and lagging

**One month (screener):** **Pembina (+9.2%)** leads, then **Gibson (+8.2%)** and **AltaGas (+5.3%)**. TC Energy is the median at **+3.2%**. **Enbridge (−0.2%)** is the only red print. Pembina’s month includes the October 1 Pacific Link listing.

**One year:** **Gibson (+39.0%)** leads, then **South Bow (+33.5%)**, **Pembina (+30.4%)**, **AltaGas (+28.3%)** and **Keyera (+26.2%)**. TC Energy is **+20.3%**. **Enbridge (+0.6%)** is the laggard by a wide margin, and it is also the worst three-month and six-month name (**−15.3%** and **−6.4%**). The sector’s one-year win versus the TSX was earned by the names smaller than Enbridge.

## How crude pipes, gas pipes and processors differ

**Enbridge** is the diversified system: liquids (including the Mainline), gas transmission, gas utilities and a smaller power book. The September equity and the crude acquisitions are why it screens as the yield name that did not re-rate. **South Bow** is the pure liquids spin-out. **Pembina** is Western Canadian pipelines plus processing, and it is the listed company with a defined, optional stake in Pacific Link.

**TC Energy** is the gas transmission and power company after that spin, including the Mexico pipes it is still pruning. The Michigan marker in the photo above is one segment of a system that also crosses into Ontario. **Keyera** and **Gibson** are the processing, liquids and terminal businesses. They are smaller, they led the one-year tape, and Gibson in particular is already at the average target. **AltaGas** mixes regulated gas distribution with midstream export and processing. Its lower yield and its utility multiple are why it sits in this note as a hybrid, not as a second Enbridge.

## Bull case for Canadian pipeline stocks

1. **The coupon is still high versus the TSX, and two giants are below the Street.** A median indicated yield near **4.2%**, with Enbridge and South Bow near **5.8%**, is the income case. Enbridge’s **CA$77.63** target and TC Energy’s **CA$96.29** target are still above the October 25 prices.

2. **Volume and sanctioned gas projects have a home.** The Westcoast expansions Enbridge and KKR are funding, and TC Energy’s contracted gas network, are the growth that does not need a new oil-price spike. Pacific Link, if it is built, is extra takeaway for the producers in the oil-and-gas note — and a fee opportunity for Pembina only after a final investment decision.

3. **The smaller names have already shown the sector can re-rate.** Gibson, South Bow, Pembina, AltaGas and Keyera are all up more than **26%** over one year. That is evidence the market will pay up for midstream cash flow when the equity story is clean.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian pipeline stocks

1. **Enbridge’s catch-up is an equity story as much as an earnings story.** The company issued **C$3.0 billion** of stock in September. A target **15%** above the screener price can sit there for a long time if cash flow per share has to digest the new shares. The **62.5x** price to free cash flow is the screener’s version of that warning.

2. **Pacific Link is not a 2026 cash flow.** Pembina has said it has no at-risk development capital before a final investment decision, and the federal timetable points at conditions by September 2027. A headline can move the stock, as October did. It does not move the dividend.

3. **Accounting payouts over 100% leave less room for a mistake.** Enbridge, Keyera and Gibson all distribute more than trailing earnings on this merge. If a project slips or a regulatory decision trims tolls, the yield the sector is priced for is the first thing that gets questioned.

## What to watch next for Canadian pipelines: Enbridge’s quarter, Pacific Link and targets

1. **November 6, 2026 — Enbridge third-quarter webcast, 9 a.m. ET.** Equity deployed, dividend coverage, and how management frames the Tallgrass, Salt Creek and Westcoast projects.

2. **Pacific Link process after the October 1 listing.** Route, ownership and Pembina’s final-investment-decision language. The federal conditions date in the government release is September 1, 2027.

3. **TC Energy’s Mexico closing timetable.** The Guadalajara–Manzanillo sale is small. The question is whether more mature-asset sales follow, and at what multiple.

4. **Keyera’s target range.** A **CA$56 to CA$112** span around a **CA$73** average is too wide to treat **+33%** upside as a consensus. The next note that moves the average will matter more than the average itself.

5. **Whether Gibson and Pembina stay ahead of their targets.** Both led the recent tape and both now sit at or through the CAD consensus. Further gains need cash flow, not another look at the same target.
