## What is driving Canadian gold stocks: the October pullback, Barrick’s IPO and a results pile-up

1. **October 16 to October 23, 2026 — gold gives back about US$170 an ounce.** COMEX gold futures settled near **US$4,181** on October 16 and near **US$4,008** on October 23. The October 19 Barrick brief had spot gold near **US$4,190** on October 16, which matches that futures print. CIBC’s gold deck, cut the same week, is **US$4,500** for the fourth quarter of 2026, **US$5,200** for 2027 and **US$5,500** for 2028. The October 23 settlement is under that fourth-quarter figure. The whole peer set is red over the month. Eldorado is the extreme, at **−26.7%**.

2. **October 15, 2026 — CIBC downgrades Barrick.** Anita Soni cut Barrick to Neutral from Outperformer and lowered the New York target to **US$49 from US$57**. The October 19 brief is the write-up. Her case was price, not the assets: Barrick had already outperformed, and cash from the planned North American IPO is still months away. The October 23 TSX close is **$53.36**, under the October 16 close of **$57.74** in that brief. The one-year return is still **+19.4%**.

3. **The IPO is still a year-end item, and the second quarter is still the last full print.** Barrick’s August 10, 2026 release: gold production **796,000 ounces**, revenue **US$5.3 billion**, EPS **US$0.73**, adjusted EPS **US$0.82**, operating cash flow **US$1.70 billion**, attributable free cash flow **US$141 million**. The company returned about **US$1.5 billion**, mostly **US$1.2 billion** of buybacks, and ended with net cash of about **US$1.25 billion**. Capital-spending guidance was **US$3.8–4.2 billion**. The Newmont settlement put **US$1.95 billion** in, folded Fourmile into Nevada Gold Mines (a complex of nearly **100 million ounces**), and cleared the path for the listing. Mark Hill is to lead the new company. Management has said the listing is targeted for year-end and could slip into January. Third-quarter results are **November 9, 2026**. All-in sustaining costs in that second quarter were about **US$1,866** an ounce, up **11%**. Those operating figures are US dollars. The table below is Canadian dollars.

4. **October 8, 2026 — Lundin Gold’s record quarter, and a pop that did not hold.** Fruta del Norte produced **153,736 ounces**, about **26%** above the year-earlier quarter, with year-to-date output of **392,472 ounces** against guidance of **475,000 to 525,000**. The October 12 brief is the write-up. The shares were about **C$95.82** on October 9. The October 23 close is **$84.52**, under the October 8 screener price of **$85.01** in that brief. Sales had been lagging production, with management expecting the catch-up in the fourth quarter. The one-month return in this table, **−7.8%**, is the least-bad month in the set. The one-year return is **−9.0%**, the worst.

5. **October 28, then the first week of November.** TradingView’s earnings calendar, checked October 27, 2026, shows October 28 for Agnico Eagle, Kinross, Alamos and Eldorado, November 4 for Pan American and Lundin Gold, November 5 for Wheaton, and November 11 for Franco-Nevada. Barrick’s November 9 date is the company’s, from the October 19 brief. Those TradingView dates are a calendar, not a confirmation of the call time. Four producers on one day is the nearest fact.

6. **What this note leaves out on purpose.** Equinox (about **CA$16.5 billion**), Endeavour Mining (about **CA$17.4 billion**, London-headquartered), IAMGOLD (about **CA$13.0 billion**), B2Gold (about **CA$8.8 billion**), Osisko Royalties (about **CA$8.6 billion**) and Triple Flag (about **CA$8.3 billion**) are liquid and not in the table. The eight names in the brief’s starting set stay, plus Lundin Gold, because that brief already exists and the line is liquid. Coeur Mining is a US issuer. Centerra is filed under precious metals (about **CA$5.5 billion**) and is left off both this page and the base-metals page, so the copper and zinc names are not mixed in here. Cameco is uranium and has its own brief. Copper-heavy producers are on the base-metals page.

## Which Canadian gold stocks are leading and lagging

**One month:** nobody is green. **Lundin Gold (−7.8%)** fell the least, then **Barrick (−10.8%)** and **Agnico Eagle (−11.7%)**. **Eldorado (−26.7%)** is the laggard, then **Kinross (−19.6%)** and **Wheaton (−17.5%)**. The streamers did not hide from the gold pullback. Wheaton’s one-month drop is worse than Barrick’s.

**One year:** **Wheaton (+23.2%)** leads, then **Eldorado (+20.7%)**, **Franco-Nevada (+19.8%)** and **Barrick (+19.4%)**. **Lundin Gold (−9.0%)**, **Kinross (−6.4%)** and **Alamos (−5.8%)** are the three names under zero. Agnico, the largest company in the set, is only **+5.8%** over the year after a **+19.6%** three-month move. The sector story is a high gold price that the median stock did not turn into index-beating one-year returns, and an October that took a piece of it back.

## How the miners differ from the streamers and the single-asset name

**Agnico** is the scale and the multiple: the biggest market cap, a forward P/E that matches the trailing P/E, and a one-year return that did not beat the TSX. **Barrick** is the turnaround that still screens cheap, with the IPO and a US$141 million free-cash-flow quarter sitting next to **CA$9.0 billion** of trailing profit in the CAD field. **Kinross** is the cheaper senior. **Alamos** and **Eldorado** are the mid-tier producers. Eldorado’s month is the drawdown. Alamos’s free-cash-flow multiple is the cash question.

**Wheaton** and **Franco-Nevada** do not run the mines. They buy metal at contracted prices, which is why the market pays **27–30x** earnings for a few billion of revenue. Their one-year returns led or matched Barrick. Their one-month returns did not.

**Pan American** is the silver line in a gold note, **12.2x** earnings, with a Canadian target page too thin to average. **Lundin Gold** is one mine. The production record is real. The October 9 price is not the October 23 price. Book value at **26.49x** says the equity account is not the way to value Fruta del Norte.

## Bull case for Canadian gold and precious-metals stocks

1. **The miners are not priced like the streamers.** Kinross at **9.0x** forward and Barrick at **10.8x** forward sit next to Wheaton at **26.6x** and Franco-Nevada at **28.6x**. A buyer who wants gold exposure without a royalty multiple has a number.

2. **The CAD gaps at Kinross, Eldorado and Barrick are five-analyst pages or better.** **+55%**, **+37%** and **+24%** are MarketBeat TSE consensuses pulled October 27, not a US target relabelled. Agnico’s **+20%** is the same kind of page.

3. **Barrick’s operating story is already in a company release.** Net cash, the Newmont settlement, and a North American complex of nearly 100 million ounces are August and October facts. The IPO has not happened. The November 9 quarter is the next test of whether production stays inside guidance.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian gold and precious-metals stocks

1. **The one-month tape is the gold price, and it is down.** Nine of nine names are red. The median is about **−14%** against a TSX that was flat on this window. COMEX gold near **US$4,008** is under CIBC’s **US$4,500** fourth-quarter figure and under the October 16 print. A further leg down in the metal hits every row, including the streamers.

2. **The one-year median lost to the TSX.** About **+11%** against about **+19%** for the Composite. Wheaton and Barrick beat the index. The middle of the set did not. A high gold price is not the same sentence as “the stocks beat the market.”

3. **US targets and thin CAD pages will mislead anyone who averages them.** Alamos, Pan American and Lundin Gold do not have a five-analyst Canadian price target. Barrick’s NYSE average is **US$52.59**, and CIBC’s **US$49** is one cut inside it. Agnico’s US$224 and C$293 are different panels. Lundin Gold’s two-analyst **CA$108** is not the 14-analyst figure in the October 12 brief. The PEG column, mostly under **0.20**, is a gold-price growth rate, not a quality score. Lundin Gold’s five- and ten-year returns dominate those averages.
