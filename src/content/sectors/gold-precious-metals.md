## What is driving Canadian gold stocks: the October pullback, Barrick’s IPO and a results pile-up

**November 23, 2026 update.** Gold kept falling. The December contract settled at US$3,853.00 an ounce on November 16, a more than one-year low, steadied at US$3,864.20 on November 17, then dropped US$122.30 to US$3,741.90 on November 18 after hawkish comments from Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller lifted the US dollar. Spot gold fell to about US$3,628 on November 19, and the December contract lost another US$170.10 to US$3,515.40 on November 20, a 14-month low and, per Investcom, its worst week in eight months. Agnico Eagle was the index’s largest point detractor on November 16 and fell about 6.3% on November 18. The gold subgroup lost about 5.4% on November 18, 3.1% on November 19 and 5% on November 20, when Eldorado fell 7.9% to C$35.56. Between the November 14 and November 23 screener files the peer median price moved **−15.7%**, with every name lower (Eldorado −21.3%, Pan American −18.2%; Franco-Nevada −9.1% and Lundin Gold −9.8% fell least). On the November 23 MarketBeat re-pull Franco-Nevada’s CAD consensus rose to C$375.50 from C$358.78, while Kinross (C$48.17) and Eldorado (C$58.75) edged lower.

**November 16, 2026 update.** Gold reversed. December futures rose to roughly US$4,151 an ounce on November 9, when Barrick reported higher third-quarter profit as stronger prices outweighed lower production, then fell for three straight sessions: about US$4,036 on November 10 after a hot US October CPI lifted Fed-hike bets, about US$3,992 on November 11 and about US$3,885 on November 12, which Reuters described as the lowest level since October 2025. The contract was little changed near US$3,886 on November 13. On November 11 Barrick filed for a New York initial public offering of its North American business, and Eldorado fell about 5.2% that day. Between the November 9 and November 14 screener files the peer median price moved **−6.2%**, with every name lower (Eldorado −10.3%, Alamos −9.2%, Pan American −8.0%; Franco-Nevada −2.3% and Wheaton −2.5% held up best). Canada’s October consumer price index is due on November 16 (economists expected annual inflation of 3.2%, per Reuters), the next Labour Force Survey is December 4, and the next Bank of Canada decision is December 9.

**November 9, 2026 update.** Gold traded near US$4,060 an ounce on November 6 against about US$3,875 a week earlier (TD Securities; the 52-week high in that table is US$5,417). Between the November 3 and November 9 screener files the peer median price moved **+12.2%**, led by Alamos Gold (+15.8%) and Lundin Gold (+15.6%). Wheaton reported record third-quarter revenue of US$772 million and net earnings of US$410 million and kept 2026 production guidance. Lundin Gold reported record production, revenue of US$633 million and adjusted earnings of US$297 million. Pan American reported net earnings of US$357 million but cut 2026 gold guidance and raised its gold AISC guidance. Barrick’s quarter, dated November 9 in an earlier item below, is not summarized here. Calendar items below dated November 3–8 have since reported; this update summarizes only the results named here.

## How the miners differ from the streamers and the single-asset name

**Agnico** is the scale and the multiple: the biggest market cap, a forward P/E that matches the trailing P/E, and a one-year return that did not beat the TSX. **Barrick** is the turnaround that still screens cheap, with the IPO and a US$141 million free-cash-flow quarter sitting next to **CA$9.0 billion** of trailing profit in the CAD field. **Kinross** is the cheaper senior. **Alamos** and **Eldorado** are the mid-tier producers. Eldorado’s month is the drawdown. Alamos’s free-cash-flow multiple is the cash question.

**Wheaton** and **Franco-Nevada** do not run the mines. They buy metal at contracted prices, which is why the market pays **27–30x** earnings for a few billion of revenue. Their one-year returns led or matched Barrick. Their one-month returns did not.

**Pan American** is the silver line in a gold note, **12.2x** earnings, with a Canadian target page too thin to average. **Lundin Gold** is one mine. The production record is real. The October 9 price is not the October 23 price. Book value at **26.49x** says the equity account is not the way to value Fruta del Norte.
