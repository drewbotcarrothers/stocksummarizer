Ten liquid Canadian producers — Canadian Natural, Suncor, Imperial Oil, Cenovus, Tourmaline, Whitecap, Strathcona, Tamarack Valley, Athabasca Oil and Peyto — add up to about **CA$542.1 billion** of market value on the October 25, 2026 screener. Over the past year the peer median is up about **77%**, far ahead of the S&P/TSX Composite’s approximate **+18%**. The last month kept going: the peer median is up about **10.9%**, against a Composite that was roughly up about **1%**. Setup for this living note: a commodity tape that has already been paid for in the large oil-sands stocks, a gas producer that did not get the same ride, and reporting dates that start at the end of this week.

Forward earnings are the cleaner multiple. The median forward P/E is about **11.4x**, and the average of the ten forward multiples is **11.4x** as well. Trailing earnings are not: Tamarack Valley’s **248x** on **CA$24.5 million** of trailing profit pulls the average trailing P/E to **44.6x**. Tourmaline, the large gas producer, is the other stretched trailing figure (**69.4x**) with a much more ordinary forward multiple (**12.8x**). The sector is cheap on next year’s earnings only if those earnings, which embed a strong oil tape, arrive.

## What is driving Canadian oil and gas stocks: the oil price, heavy differentials and Q3 dates

1. **October 23, 2026 weekly close — oil is still high, and the Canadian heavy discount is wide.** Oil Sands Magazine’s weekly table shows **WTI at US$103.27** a barrel, **Western Canadian Select at US$74.77** (a **US$28.50** discount to WTI) and **synthetic crude at US$108.97**. That is the split that matters inside this peer set. Integrated producers with refineries (Suncor, Imperial, Cenovus) and light-oil names do not wear the same WCS discount as a heavy or bitumen barrel. The October 18 Canadian Natural brief recorded Brent touching **US$110** on October 16. The October 23 weekly close says the tape stayed elevated into the screener date.

2. **The large oil stocks are through the average target.** On MarketBeat’s TSE pages pulled October 27, Canadian Natural’s average target is **CA$70** against a **CA$77.12** screener price, Suncor’s is **CA$103.06** against **CA$111.18**, and Imperial’s is **CA$152.83** against **CA$188.72**. Imperial’s screener rating is **Sell**. MarketBeat’s own label on the same pull is **Reduce** (4 sells and 6 holds, no buys, 10 analysts). The year’s rally is in the price.

3. **Reporting dates that are already on company calendars.** Imperial’s October 20 advisory sets the third-quarter release and the call for **October 30, 2026** (call at 9 a.m. MT). Suncor’s October 20 advisory sets results for **November 3** and the analyst webcast for **November 4** at 8:30 a.m. Alberta time. The October 18 Canadian Natural brief flags a **November 5** third-quarter report. Enbridge’s pipes are a separate note; the producer question this week is cash flow, buybacks and how management talks about the WCS discount.

4. **November 15, 2026 — the Pathways date in the Cenovus brief.** The October 4 Cenovus brief, drawing on the July 2 memorandum with Ottawa and Alberta, puts binding definitive agreements for the Pathways carbon-capture plan on a **November 15, 2026** target. The partners named there are Canadian Natural, Suncor, Cenovus, Imperial and ConocoPhillips Canada. It is still pre-final investment decision. It is the policy date on the same calendar as the quarters.

5. **Gas did not get the oil year.** Tourmaline, the largest gas producer in the set, is up about **7%** over one year. Peyto is up about **26%**. Every oil-weighted name in the table is ahead of both. Tourmaline’s trailing payout of about **206%** and its **124.5x** price to free cash flow are what depressed gas earnings look like on a screener, next to a forward P/E of **12.8x**.

## Which Canadian oil and gas stocks are leading and lagging

**One month (screener):** every name is green. **Athabasca (+17.0%)** leads, then **Suncor (+16.8%)**, **Tamarack (+13.8%)** and **Canadian Natural (+13.3%)**. The softest oil print is **Cenovus (+3.1%)**. Imperial is **+7.2%**. The month extended a rally that was already long.

**One year:** **Tamarack (+141%)** leads, then **Suncor (+99%)**, **Whitecap (+96%)**, **Cenovus (+92%)**, **Canadian Natural (+78%)** and **Athabasca (+76%)**. Imperial (**+51%**) beat the TSX and lagged the other large oil names. **Peyto (+26%)** and **Strathcona (+25%)** are the middle of the laggards. **Tourmaline (+7.3%)** is the one-year laggard, and its year-to-date gain is about **8%** against Suncor’s **83%** and Cenovus’s **98%**.

The five-year average of **+278%** is not a typical stock. Athabasca’s five-year print is **+970%**. The median five-year return in this set is about **+206%**.

## How integrated producers differ from oil sands, light oil and gas

**Suncor, Imperial and Cenovus** refine as well as produce. Refining is why their revenue is large relative to Canadian Natural and why a wide WCS discount is partly an internal transfer rather than only a lost sale. Imperial is the Exxon-controlled name and the one the Street has marked down hardest. Cenovus is the cheap forward multiple. Suncor is the one-year leader of the three.

**Canadian Natural** is the upstream bellwether: the most profit in the set, a **3.24%** yield, and a price about **9%** above the average target. **Athabasca** and **Strathcona** are the smaller oil-sands and heavy producers. Athabasca pays no dividend on this merge and has the wild long-run chart. Strathcona is the one large-ish name whose five-year print is only **+4%**.

**Whitecap and Tamarack** are the light-oil and condensate growers. Both had huge one-year runs. Tamarack’s trailing earnings do not support the price; the forward multiple of **14.4x** is the number that does. **Tourmaline and Peyto** are the gas pair. Tourmaline lagged because gas lagged. Peyto’s trailing earnings held up better, which is why it is the cheap earnings multiple and the high yield at the same time.

## Bull case for Canadian oil and gas stocks

1. **Forward multiples are still ordinary if the oil price holds.** A median forward P/E near **11x**, with Cenovus at **7.7x** and Suncor at **9.0x**, is not a bubble multiple. It is a multiple on earnings that already assume a strong commodity. Oil Sands Magazine’s October 23 weekly close still had WTI at **US$103.27**.

2. **Gas is the part of the group that has not re-rated.** Tourmaline’s one-year gain is about **7%**, against a sector median near **77%**. Peyto yields about **5.6%** at about **11x** trailing earnings. If gas pricing catches up, those two are the stocks that did not already receive the oil year.

3. **Shareholder returns are in the model at the majors.** Payout ratios at Canadian Natural, Suncor, Imperial and Cenovus sit between about **23% and 43%** of trailing earnings, which leaves room for the buybacks those companies have been running alongside the dividend. The October company briefs on Canadian Natural and Suncor are the longer versions of that capital-return story.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian oil and gas stocks

1. **The Street is below the tape on the stocks that dominate the TSX.** Canadian Natural, Suncor and Imperial are **7% to 19%** above their average targets. The sector’s average upside is negative. A flat oil price from here does not have to fall for those stocks to disappoint anyone who is buying the one-year chart.

2. **The heavy discount is still US$28.50.** WCS at **US$74.77** against WTI at **US$103.27** is a wide gap. Bitumen and heavy barrels wear it. Pipes and egress sit in the midstream note, including the Pacific Link process, and they do not close that gap this quarter.

3. **Trailing earnings at the smaller names are not the bull case.** Tamarack at **248x** trailing earnings and Tourmaline at **69x**, with payout ratios above **200%**, will look expensive the moment the forward estimates come down. Athabasca’s five-year chart (**+970%**) is a recovery from a low base, not a base rate.

## What to watch next for Canadian oil and gas: quarters, Pathways and the WCS discount

1. **October 30, 2026 — Imperial’s third-quarter release and call (9 a.m. MT).** The first large print, and the stock with the widest gap below the Street.

2. **November 3–5, 2026 — Suncor, then Canadian Natural.** Suncor’s results are set for November 3 and the webcast for November 4. The October 18 Canadian Natural brief places that company’s quarter on November 5. Watch funds flow, buybacks and any comment on the heavy differential.

3. **November 15, 2026 — Pathways agreements, if the July 2 timetable holds.** The date is the one in the October 4 Cenovus brief. Binding agreements would be a policy event for every oil-sands name in this table. A slip would be one too.

4. **The weekly WCS discount versus WTI.** Oil Sands Magazine’s October 23 spread was **US$28.50**. A move in that spread changes realized prices for the upstream names faster than a revision to a 2027 target.

5. **Whether any average target is raised through the current price.** Canadian Natural’s high target is **CA$86**, above the **CA$77** screener price, while the average is **CA$70**. One or two raises do not move the average through the market. A cluster of them would.
