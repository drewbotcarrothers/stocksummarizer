Twelve liquid names — Choice Properties, Chartwell, RioCan, Granite, First Capital, CAPREIT, SmartCentres, CT REIT, Dream Industrial, Boardwalk, Crombie and H&R — are about **CA$60 billion** of market value on the October 23, 2026 close. Over the past month the peer median is about **+2.6%**, ahead of an S&P/TSX Composite that was about **+0.1%** on the same exchange-close window. Over one year the median is about **+7.9%**, against a Composite near **+18.6%**. First Capital is the one-year leader, up about **24%**, with a trailing profit larger than its revenue. CAPREIT is the laggard, down about **17%**. Setup for this living note: a group that lagged the index, a rate decision on October 28, and an accounting multiple that is not the distribution.

Under IFRS, Canadian REIT profit includes unrealized fair-value changes on investment property. A trailing profit larger than revenue, or a loss next to a distribution that is still being paid, is that accounting. The forward column and the yield are the usable comparisons. This note does not have a funds-from-operations field, and it does not invent one.

## What is driving Canadian REIT stocks: a rate decision, an occupancy update and a November stack

1. **October 28, 2026 — the Bank of Canada, still at 2.25% until it says otherwise.** On September 2 the Bank held the overnight target at **2.25%**, with the Bank Rate at 2.5% and the deposit rate at 2.20%. The next announcement, with a Monetary Policy Report, is October 28. It has not happened. A Reuters poll taken October 21–23 found **19 of 34** economists expecting a quarter-point hike to **2.50%**. The C.D. Howe Institute’s Monetary Policy Council, voting October 22, had **six of seven** members calling for a hold at 2.25%. REIT prices are a long-duration claim on rent minus interest. The decision is the backdrop for this table. It is not a result yet.

2. **October 5, 2026 — Boardwalk’s occupancy pickup, and a Grande Prairie sale without a price in this note.** Preliminary total-portfolio occupancy was **97.0%** at the start of September and **97.1%** at the start of October, against **96.4%** at the start of June. Same-property occupancy was **97.2%** in both September and October. Sam Kolias said the trust had completed the sale of the Northern Lights portfolio in Grande Prairie in September and was redeploying proceeds into the normal-course issuer bid. This note does not have a sale price. Third-quarter results are after the close on **November 3**, with a call on **November 4 at 1:00 p.m. ET**. The October 23 close is **$62.35**. The one-year return is **−1.5%**. The CAD target is **CA$78.53** from **9** analysts, **+26%**.

3. **October 15, 2026 — the monthly distributions, which are the run-rate, not a new policy.** RioCan declared **9.65 cents** for October, payable November 6 to holders of record October 30. That is **$1.158** annualized, against the annual DPS field of about **$1.15** in the table and an indicated yield of **5.40%** on **$21.44**. CAPREIT declared **$0.12917**, or **$1.55** annualized, payable November 16 to holders of record October 30. The table’s DPS is **$1.55** and the yield is **4.92%** on **$31.53**. Choice declared **$0.065**, or **$0.78** annualized, payable November 16 to the same record date. The table’s DPS is about **$0.77** and the yield is **5.21%** on **$14.89**. These are the October cheques. They are not earnings.

4. **The portfolios those cheques sit on, as of June 30, 2026.** RioCan’s release describes **164** properties and about **31 million** square feet at RioCan’s interest, necessity-based retail. CAPREIT’s release describes about **45,400** residential suites and townhomes, excluding about 100 suites held for sale, with a fair value of about **$14.4 billion**. Choice’s release calls itself Canada’s largest REIT. George Weston’s annual information form for the year ended December 31, 2025 says Weston held about **61.7%** of Choice at that date, and about **52.6%** of Loblaw. Loblaw’s management proxy circular still had Weston at about **52.6%** as of March 16, 2026. Choice’s market cap in this table is the market’s value of the REIT. It is not additive with Weston’s market cap on the [retail page](/sectors/retail-consumer/).

5. **The November stack that is actually scheduled.** RioCan releases third-quarter results after the close on **November 2**, with a call on **November 3 at 10:00 a.m. ET**. Boardwalk is after the close on **November 3**. Granite expects to release after the close on **November 4**, with Kevan Gorrie chairing the call on **November 5 at 11:00 a.m. ET**. CAPREIT’s next distribution pays on November 16. This note did not find a CAPREIT third-quarter date in the company sources opened for it, and it does not use a Choice date that was not on a company release opened here.

6. **What this note leaves out on purpose.** **SMU.UN** is not a current listing. Summit Industrial was taken private on February 17, 2023, at **C$23.50** a unit, in a **C$5.9 billion** deal with debt. Dream Industrial owns **10%** of that joint venture with GIC. **IIP.UN** was privatized on July 9, 2026, at **$13.55** a unit, enterprise value about **$4 billion**. **MI.UN** was taken private on August 7, 2026, at **$18** a unit, enterprise value about **$2.3 billion**. **Allied (AP.UN)** is still listed, at about **CA$0.8 billion**, down about **68%** over the year and about **26%** over the month. Share volume is high because the unit is about **$5.61**. It is the office line, and it is below the scale of this table. **Primaris (PMZ.UN)** is about **CA$2.9 billion**, liquid, and up about **49%** over the year — the strongest one-year tape in the screen, and not in the table, so the twelfth seat stays with H&R, the diversified name. **Killam (KMP.UN)** is about **CA$2.1 billion**, the smaller apartment REIT beside CAPREIT and Boardwalk, with trailing earnings of about **$8 million** and a trailing P/E near **570x**. Same accounting warning as Chartwell, at a smaller cap.

## Which Canadian REIT stocks are leading and lagging

**One month:** **CT REIT (+4.3%)** leads, then **RioCan (+4.0%)**, **Chartwell (+3.9%)** and **H&R (+3.8%)**. The median is about **+2.6%** because the bottom half is quiet: **CAPREIT (0.0%)** and **Choice (−0.2%)** are the laggards, and Dream Industrial, Boardwalk and SmartCentres are between **+1.6%** and **+1.7%**. Nothing in the set had a one-month move like the waste or apparel names on the other sector pages. The month was a drift higher than the TSX, not a re-rating.

**One year:** **First Capital (+24.5%)** leads, then **RioCan (+16.8%)**, **CT REIT (+11.0%)**, **Crombie (+10.3%)** and **Granite (+10.1%)**. **CAPREIT (−17.2%)** is the laggard, then **H&R (−12.5%)** and **Boardwalk (−1.5%)**. The two apartment names and the diversified name are the ones under zero. The retail REITs, other than Choice at **+3.4%**, did the compounding the median did not. Primaris, left out above, would have been the leader of a wider screen. It is not the leader of this table.

## How retail REITs differ from apartments, industrial and retirement

**Choice, RioCan, First Capital, SmartCentres, CT REIT and Crombie** are the retail sleeve. Choice is the Weston group’s REIT, at a forward multiple of **39x** and a trailing loss. RioCan is the necessity retail portfolio with a **5.40%** yield and a November 2 quarter. First Capital is the one-year chart and the accounting P/E. SmartCentres is the **6.81%** yield with a three-analyst target. CT REIT is the low earnings multiple with almost no CAD room. Crombie is the smaller grocery-anchored trust with a target page that is a single number.

**CAPREIT and Boardwalk** are the purpose-built rental names. CAPREIT is the national book that lost money on the trailing line, lagged the year, and has a negative next-year EPS estimate. Boardwalk is the Alberta-weighted book that just printed a higher occupancy and still yields **2.77%**. **Granite and Dream Industrial** are the warehouses. Granite is the cleaner multiple. Dream Industrial is the ten-year compound that has been flat for five. **Chartwell** is retirement residences, the second-largest cap in the table, and the multiple that breaks the average. **H&R** is the diversified trust, down over one year and over ten, at **0.62x** book, yielding **6.12%** against a large trailing loss.

## Bull case for Canadian REIT stocks

1. **The income is in the price, and the month beat the TSX.** A median yield near **5.3%** against a one-month median of about **+2.6%**, with the Composite at about **+0.1%**, is a group that paid and drifted up. RioCan’s **9.65-cent** cheque and CAPREIT’s **$1.55** annualized rate are declared, not hoped for.

2. **CT REIT, RioCan and Granite have earnings multiples a buyer can read.** **10x to 14x** trailing where the earnings exist, forward multiples in the same range for RioCan and Granite, and CT REIT at **8.4x** free cash flow. That is a different purchase from Chartwell’s **797x** or First Capital’s **4.8x**.

3. **Boardwalk’s CAD gap is nine analysts, and the occupancy moved first.** **CA$78.53** is **+26%** above **$62.35**. Total-portfolio occupancy at the start of October was **97.1%**, up from **96.4%** at the start of June. The quarter on November 3 is the test. The target is not a two-analyst print.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian REIT stocks

1. **The sector lagged a TSX that was up about 19%, and the rate decision is unmade.** A median one-year return of about **+8%** is a real gap. If October 28 is a hike, the duration works the other way. If it is a hold, the Reuters poll’s hike camp is the positioning that has to unwind. This note does not know which.

2. **The average P/E in the valuation table is not a REIT multiple.** **129x** trailing because of Chartwell, **33x** forward because of Chartwell’s **141x**. First Capital’s **4.8x** is the mirror image, a profit larger than revenue. Averaging those with CT REIT’s **10x** is how a sector multiple becomes nonsense. The medians in the snapshot are the figures that compare. Five names have no trailing P/E because accounting earnings are a loss, and several of those still yield **5%**.

3. **The wide CAD gaps are the thin pages or the broken earnings.** CAPREIT’s **+41%** is three analysts and a negative forward EPS. Crombie’s **C$18** has no range. Choice’s **+13%** is four analysts on an **CA$11 billion** REIT. H&R’s **6%** yield sits on a **CA$571 million** trailing loss and a ten-year decline. A distribution that the payout column cannot cover is a distribution that can be reset.
