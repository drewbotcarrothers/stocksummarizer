Twelve liquid names — Loblaw, Couche-Tard, Dollarama, Restaurant Brands, George Weston, Metro, Aritzia, Empire, Canadian Tire, the North West Company, Pet Valu and Canada Goose — are about **CA$363 billion** of market value on the October 23, 2026 close. That sum double-counts Loblaw inside George Weston. Over the past month the peer median is about **+7.5%**, against an S&P/TSX Composite that was about **+0.1%** on the same exchange-close window. Over one year the median is about **+15.9%**, close to a Composite near **+18.6%**. The median hides the tails. Aritzia is up about **64%** over the year, and the fiscal 2032 plan was published this morning, after this table’s close. Pet Valu is down about **44%**. Canada Goose is down about **40%**. Setup for this living note: a middle that almost matched the index, a growth plan that is not in the October 23 price, and a target column that has to stay in Canadian dollars.

## What is driving Canadian retail stocks: a five-year plan, a discount beat and a grocer’s strike

**November 9, 2026 update.** Canada Goose reported second-quarter fiscal 2027 on November 5: revenue up 5.9% to 274.0 million, comparable DTC sales down 7.5%, and fiscal 2027 guidance reaffirmed at low-single-digit revenue growth with an 11–12% adjusted EBIT margin. Between the November 3 and November 9 screener files the peer median price moved **+1.5%** (Canada Goose +7.4%, North West +3.3%; Empire −1.4%, George Weston −0.4%). Unemployment rose 0.2 points to 6.7% in October, and the Bank of Canada hiked to 2.50% on October 28. Calendar items below dated November 3–8 have since reported; this update summarizes only the results named here.

1. **October 27, 2026 — Aritzia’s fiscal 2032 plan, before the open, and not in this table.** The company set a fiscal 2032 net-revenue target of **$9.5 billion to $10.5 billion**, a **15% to 17%** compound annual growth rate off the fiscal 2027 outlook, adjusted EBITDA of **21%** of net revenue, and EPS growth faster than revenue. North America digital revenue is targeted at about **$4.1 billion to $4.5 billion**. The plan assumes **220** boutiques by fiscal 2032, **12 to 14** new US boutiques a year, about **$2 billion** of net capital spending over five years, and about **$2 billion** of buybacks, half of free cash flow. Amounts in that release are Canadian dollars unless marked otherwise. The webcast was scheduled for **10:15 a.m. ET**. The **$155.15** in the table is the October 23 close. The one-year return is already **+63.6%**. The one-month return is **+30.0%**, which includes the October 9 jump the October 10 brief wrote up, from the October 8 close of **$123.28** to **$146.91**.

2. **October 8, 2026 — the quarter that jump was about.** Second-quarter fiscal 2027 net revenue was **$1.17 billion**, up **44.1%**. Comparable sales were up **34.5%**. US revenue was **$779.4 million**, up **60.3%**, **66.6%** of the total. Adjusted EPS was **$1.31**. Reported net income of **$201.7 million** included about **$97 million** of US tariff refunds; the October 10 brief is the write-up. The full-year outlook stayed at **$4.78 billion to $4.88 billion** of revenue in the investor-day release’s footnotes, growth of about **29% to 32%**, and that outlook excludes further tariff refunds. Trailing net income on this page is about **$592 million**, against **$457 million** in the October 8 screener the brief used. Trailing P/E here is **31.3x** on a higher price. The multiple did not expand one-for-one with the stock because the quarter is in the trailing earnings.

3. **September 16, 2026 — Dollarama raises the Canadian guide.** Second-quarter fiscal 2027 sales were **$2,026.6 million**, up **17.6%**. Canadian comparable store sales were up **5.4%**. Diluted EPS was **$1.29**, against **$1.16**. Canada had **1,734** stores at August 2, up from **1,665** a year earlier. The Canadian comparable-sales guide for fiscal 2027 moved to **4.0% to 4.5%** from **3.0% to 4.0%**, and net new Canadian stores to **65 to 75** from **60 to 70**. Other Canadian ranges were unchanged. Australia is still expected to post a net loss for the year. The board declared a quarterly dividend of **$0.1200**, payable November 6 to holders of record October 9. That is **$0.48** annualized. The table’s DPS field is about **$0.42**, and the indicated yield on **$207.45** is **0.22%**. The October 23 close is **16%** above the start of the one-month window. The CAD target is **CA$216.06** from **12** analysts, **+4.2%**. The one-year return is only **+11.7%**. The multiple did the work before this year.

4. **August 12, 2026 — Metro’s Laval quarter, still the one-year gap inside grocery.** Third-quarter sales were **$6,970.4 million**, up **1.4%**. Food same-store sales were **−1.5%**. Pharmacy same-store sales were **+4.8%**. Net earnings were **$211.3 million**, down **34.6%**. The company estimated the Laval produce-distribution labour conflict cost **$66 million** after tax, **$0.32** a share, and it did not adjust earnings for that. The one-year return in this table is **+3.7%**. Loblaw, the other large grocer, is **+18.5%**. Empire is **+5.8%**. Metro is the grocer that did not compound. Loblaw said on October 22 that it will report third-quarter results on **November 19** at about **6:30 a.m. ET**, with a call at **10:00 a.m.** The second-quarter headline, from the July 30 call, was revenue growth of **4.1%** and adjusted diluted EPS growth of **11.9%**. The quarterly dividend declared for payment on October 1 was **$0.155183**. Four times that is about **$0.62**. The table’s annual DPS field is about **$0.55**, and the indicated yield is **0.86%** on **$68.67**. Loblaw’s four-for-one split was effective August 18, 2025. The ten-year return below is split-adjusted.

5. **October 9, 2026 — Couche-Tard’s Irving agreement, with Żabka still the larger bet.** The October 15 brief is the write-up. The Irving deal is the Québec and Ontario retail sites, fuel supply and cardlocks, with no price disclosed in that brief. The Żabka tender, launched July 31, was the larger number in the brief, about **PLN 32.6 billion**. Couche-Tard reports in US dollars. The table is the CAD field. The October 8 screener price in the brief was **$77.11**. The October 23 close is **$83.33**. Trailing P/E is **17.4x**, still the low teens-to-high-teens multiple against Dollarama’s **42x**. The CAD target is **CA$99.13** from **12** analysts, **+19%**. There is no separate USD target page in this pull. MarketBeat’s NYSE address for ATD returns the same Canadian-dollar consensus.

## Which Canadian retail stocks are leading and lagging

**One month:** **Aritzia (+30.0%)** leads, then **Dollarama (+16.4%)** and **Pet Valu (+12.0%)**. **Loblaw (+10.9%)** is the grocer that moved. **Restaurant Brands (+4.4%)** is the laggard on this window, then **Weston (+5.3%)** and **Couche-Tard (+6.2%)**. The median is about **+7.5%** because almost the whole set was up. The month was a retail tape, not a single-stock tape, with Aritzia on top of it.

**One year:** **Aritzia (+63.6%)** leads by a wide margin, then **North West (+24.4%)**, **Canadian Tire (+22.1%)** and **Weston (+20.2%)**. **Loblaw (+18.5%)** matched the TSX. **Pet Valu (−43.9%)** is the laggard, then **Canada Goose (−39.9%)**. **Metro (+3.7%)** and **Empire (+5.8%)** are the grocery laggards against Loblaw. **Dollarama (+11.7%)** is a modest one-year return on a **42x** multiple. The one-month leader and the one-year leader are the same stock. That was not true in transportation. The two apparel-and-specialty names at the bottom of the one-year list are also the two smallest market caps in the table.

## Bull case for Canadian retail stocks

1. **The one-month tape beat the TSX, and the one-year median almost matched it.** A median month of about **+7.5%** against a Composite at about **+0.1%**, and a one-year median of about **+16%** against about **+19%**, is a sector that participated. It is not the transportation median that matched exactly, and it is not a sector that was left behind.

2. **Couche-Tard’s CAD gap is twelve analysts, at a multiple the dollar store does not have.** **CA$99.13** is **+19%** above **$83.33**. Trailing P/E is **17.4x** against Dollarama’s **41.6x**. The Irving agreement is the small deal. The brief’s Żabka tender is the one that can change the earnings. The target is a Canadian-dollar page with a range, not a single-number print.

3. **Aritzia’s seventeen-analyst target was still above the October 23 price, and the plan is a separate document.** **CA$177.53** is **+14%** above **$155.15**. The fiscal 2032 revenue range is **$9.5 billion to $10.5 billion**. The table does not include this morning’s reaction. The bull case is that the multiple of about **30x** forward is the price of a plan the company just put a number on. The bear case is the next paragraph.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian retail stocks

1. **Aritzia’s chart has already done a 64% year, and the October 23 price is before anyone trades the new plan.** A stock up **30%** in a month can mean the quarter was good and the entry is late. The PEG of **0.30** uses trailing growth that includes the tariff refund. The investor-day targets are forward-looking. They are not trailing earnings.

2. **The Toronto target on Restaurant Brands is a trap, and Canada Goose’s CAD page is two analysts.** C$84 with no range, against a US$83.52 target that means **+11%** on the NYSE and **−20%** if you use it as Canadian dollars, will produce a fake “overvalued by a fifth” headline. Canada Goose’s **+46%** USD gap and **+11%** CAD gap are not the same opinion. Averaging either TSE print into Loblaw’s target is how a sector upside becomes nonsense.

3. **Dollarama is priced for the guide it just raised, and two specialty names are down for a reason until the quarter says otherwise.** A **4%** gap to a twelve-analyst target, at **40x** forward earnings, needs the **4.0% to 4.5%** Canadian comparable-sales range to hold. Pet Valu’s **−44%** year and Canada Goose’s **−40%** year are not a screen glitch. Empire’s **92%** payout is the depressed trailing profit, not a generous policy. Metro’s food same-store sales were negative in the quarter that is still the latest one on the record.
