Eight liquid Canadian insurance names — Manulife, Great-West Lifeco, Sun Life, Power Corporation, Fairfax, Intact, iA Financial and Definity — add up to about **CA$431.5 billion** of listed market value on the October 25, 2026 screener. Over the past year the peer median is up about **29%**, ahead of the S&P/TSX Composite’s approximate **+18%** one-year price return. The last month was quieter: the peer median slipped about **0.4%** while the Composite was roughly up. Setup for this living note: a strong year for the lifecos, a soft year for the big P&C names, and a November reporting window that starts with a catastrophe estimate already on the table.

Power Corp’s market value sits on top of its stake in Great-West, so that CA$431.5 billion figure double-counts the overlap. It is the sum of these listed stocks, not a sum of separate insurance franchises.

## What is driving Canadian insurance stocks: catastrophe losses, Boots and November earnings

**November 9, 2026 update.** Sun Life reported after the close on November 4: underlying EPS of C$2.05 (+10%) and a 4% dividend increase to C$1.00, but US$81 million of U.S. experience losses on long-term disability and US$26.9 billion of MFS net outflows. Shares fell about 10% on November 5 and National Bank and TD Cowen downgraded to Sector Perform and Hold. Fairfax reported net earnings of US$929.6 million (down 19% on investment losses) with strong insurance and reinsurance operating income, and its shares jumped nearly 10%. Between the November 3 and November 9 screener files the peer median price moved **−1.9%**: Fairfax +7.3% and Intact +4.3%; Sun Life −10.4% and Power Corp −4.5%. On **October 28, 2026** the Bank of Canada raised the overnight target by 25 basis points to **2.50%**, its first hike since July 2023 and the end of seven straight holds at 2.25%. It said further increases may be needed but it would proceed carefully; the next decision is **December 9**. Manulife and Great-West also reported on November 4 and are not summarized here. Calendar items below dated November 3–8 have since reported; this update summarizes only the results named here.

1. **October 6, 2026 — Intact’s catastrophe estimate.** Intact said third-quarter catastrophe losses were about **CA$660 million** pre-tax and net of reinsurance, or about **CA$2.76** per diluted share after tax. Personal property was about **CA$390 million** of that, commercial lines about **CA$173 million**, and Canada about **CA$615 million**, with smaller amounts in the UK & Ireland and the US. Management tied the quarter to storms, flooding, wind and wildfires in British Columbia, and said catastrophe losses over the past 12 months were about **CA$1.29 billion**, modestly above annual guidance of **CA$1.20 billion**. Results themselves come after the close on **November 3**, with the call on **November 4**.

2. **October 7, 2026 — Fairfax and Wittington agree to buy Boots.** Fairfax agreed to partner with Wittington Investments, the Weston family’s holding company, to acquire The Boots Group. Fairfax’s equity commitment is up to about **US$2.3 billion** for **50%** of Boots’ equity, with Wittington taking operational control and closing expected in the **first quarter of 2027**. On this screener Fairfax is the one-month leader (**+9.5%**) and still the cheapest name on earnings and book. The October 8 StockSummarizer brief walked through the cheque; the tape since then is the new information.

3. **October 21–22, 2026 — lifeco reporting dates.** Great-West, Manulife and Sun Life each set third-quarter releases for after the close on **November 4, 2026**, with calls on **November 5** (Great-West at 9:30 a.m. ET, Manulife at 8:00 a.m. ET, Sun Life at 11:00 a.m. ET). Sun Life’s advisory still cites assets under management of **$1.70 trillion** as of June 30, 2026. None of those quarters are out as of this note.

4. **October 28, 2026 — Bank of Canada hike.** On **October 28, 2026** the Bank of Canada raised the overnight target by 25 basis points to **2.50%**, its first hike since July 2023 and the end of seven straight holds at 2.25%. It said further increases may be needed but it would proceed carefully; the next decision is **December 9**. Insurer book values, discount rates and the yield on new investments all feel that print; the channel is the investment portfolio and the liability discount, not the loan book.

5. **Life versus P&C is already in the one-year tape.** Great-West (**+52.7%**), Power Corp (**+48.3%**) and Manulife (**+38.7%**) did the heavy lifting. Intact is about flat over one year (**+0.7%**) and Definity is up about **9%**. A beat-or-miss November will land on two different starting points: lifecos that have already re-rated, and P&C names that have not.

## Which Canadian insurers are leading and lagging

**One month (screener):** **Fairfax (+9.5%)** leads by a wide margin, then **Intact (+4.1%)**. Definity (**+0.7%**) and Manulife (**+0.2%**) are roughly flat. The lifeco complex is the soft patch: **Great-West (−2.8%)**, **iA (−2.5%)**, **Sun Life (−2.0%)** and **Power Corp (−1.1%)**.

**One year:** the order flips. **Great-West (+52.7%)** leads, then **Power Corp (+48.3%)** and **Manulife (+38.7%)**. iA (**+30.2%**) and Sun Life (**+28.7%**) still beat the TSX’s approximate **+18%**. **Definity (+9.3%)**, **Fairfax (+6.4%)** and **Intact (+0.7%)** are the laggards. Five-year returns still belong to Fairfax (**+387%**). The recent lag there is a pause after a long run, visible again in a year-to-date decline of about **5.7%**.

## How life insurers differ from P&C insurers and from Power Corp

**Manulife, Sun Life, Great-West and iA** are the operating life and health franchises: Canadian and international insurance plus asset management. Their one-year returns cluster from about **+29% to +53%**, and their forward P/E ratios sit between about **13x and 15x**. Differences still matter. Manulife is the largest and the highest yield in that quartet. Great-West led the year and trades at the richest price to book. Sun Life’s yield is the highest in the whole peer set. iA is the smaller domestic compounder, with the strongest 10-year price return in the table (**+314%**).

**Intact and Definity** are property and casualty. Intact is the global P&C platform; Definity is the smaller Canadian one. Both lagged the lifecos over one year, both are down over three months, and both still show double-digit upside to the average target. Intact’s October 6 catastrophe estimate is the number that has to clear in the November 3 print.

**Fairfax** is an insurance holding company with a large investment portfolio, which is why it screens cheap on earnings and book and why a non-insurance cheque (Boots) moves the debate. **Power Corp** is the other holding company: it controls Great-West and also owns asset management. It is in this table because it is how many Canadian investors own that complex. It is also the stock trading above the average target after a **+48%** year.

## Bull case for Canadian insurance stocks

1. **The lifeco re-rating has an earnings base under it.** Forward multiples in the low-to-mid teens, payout ratios mostly in the **30–62%** range, and a year of outperformance versus the TSX leave the group looking like a completed re-rating only if November disappoints. If core earnings hold, the multiples are ordinary.

2. **P&C still has a gap to the Street.** Intact and Definity are the two names with double-digit upside to the October 27 consensus. A catastrophe quarter that matches the October 6 estimate, without a worse full-year revision, is the kind of print that lets a laggard catch up.

3. **Fairfax is still the valuation outlier.** At **8.7x** trailing earnings and **1.33x** book, with a **CA$2,666** average target against a **CA$2,468** screener price, the holding-company discount is still on the screen after the Boots announcement.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian insurance stocks

1. **The leaders are close to the targets.** Great-West, Manulife, Sun Life and iA have mid-single-digit implied upside or less. Power Corp is already about **6%** above its average target. A soft quarter or a higher discount rate does not have a large cushion in those prices.

2. **Catastrophe season can still get worse.** Intact has put **CA$660 million** on the third quarter and **CA$1.29 billion** on the trailing 12 months, above **CA$1.20 billion** of guidance. Definity does not have an equivalent pre-announcement in this note. A second revision, or a pricing response that takes longer than the market hopes, is the P&C risk.

3. **Holding-company cheques cut both ways.** Fairfax’s Boots commitment is up to about **US$2.3 billion** of equity, with closing expected in early 2027. If investors decide that capital belongs in buybacks, the cheap multiple can stay cheap. Power Corp’s premium to its own target is the other version of the same argument: the wrapper can overshoot the operating company.

## What to watch next for Canadian insurers: catastrophe results, lifeco earnings and targets

1. **December 9, 2026 — next Bank of Canada decision.** After the hike to 2.50%, the level of rates still feeds insurer investment yields and liability discounting.

2. **November 3–4, 2026 — Intact results and call.** The test is whether the **CA$660 million** catastrophe estimate holds and what management says about pricing after it.

3. **November 4–5, 2026 — Manulife, Great-West and Sun Life.** Three lifeco quarters in two days. Watch core earnings against multiples that already assume a decent year, and any comment on buybacks and capital.

4. **Fairfax book value and the Boots timetable.** The stock’s one-month lead needs a book-value print and a regulatory path toward the first-quarter 2027 close.

5. **Street target refreshes after the November prints.** Intact and Definity are the names where the average target is still far from the tape. If those targets come down with the catastrophe quarter, the “laggard upside” disappears.
