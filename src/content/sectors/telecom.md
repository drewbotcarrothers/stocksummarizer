Five liquid Canadian names — BCE, Rogers, TELUS, Quebecor and Cogeco Communications — are about **CA$88 billion** of market value on the October 25, 2026 screener. Over the past month the peer median is about **−3.4%**, against an S&P/TSX Composite that was roughly **+1.4%**. Over one year the median is about **−16%**, against a Composite near **+18%**. One name did the opposite: Quebecor is up about **48%** over the year. Setup for this living note: a cheap-looking forward multiple, a yield that only means something after the dividend cuts, and a November earnings pile-up.

## What is driving Canadian telecom stocks: resets, MLSE, spectrum and a November cluster

1. **July 31, 2026 — TELUS cuts the dividend.** Under CEO Victor Dodig, TELUS reset the quarterly dividend to **C$0.1875**, or **C$0.75** a year, from about **C$1.67**. The annual DPS field on the screener still shows about **C$1.65**. That trailing figure is stale. The indicated yield at the October 23 close is **6.08%** (**C$0.75 ÷ $12.34**), not the teens. The July 31 guide that still frames the repair: 2026 service revenue flat to down 2%, adjusted EBITDA down 2% to 4%, capital spending around **C$2.6 billion**, free cash flow near **C$1.8 billion**, and a leverage path toward **3.0x by 2028**. A November capital-returns update was already flagged on that quarter. TELUS had not posted a confirmed third-quarter date in the sources checked for this note. MarketBeat’s calendar estimates **November 6, 2026**, and labels that date as an estimate.

2. **BCE’s dividend is C$1.75, not the screener’s C$2.31.** The September 18 brief used the reset annualized rate and showed trailing EPS inflated by one-time items, which made a trailing P/E near 4x meaningless. The same pattern is in this file: trailing P/E **4.3x** on about **C$6.4 billion** of trailing earnings. Use the forward multiple (**11.1x**) and the **C$1.75** dividend. BCE said on October 14, 2026 that it will report third-quarter results on **November 5, 2026**, with a call at **10:00 a.m. ET** (Mirko Bibic and Curtis Millen). The Saskatchewan AI Fabric memorandum from September 14 is still the growth story next to that dividend: a path toward 1.2 GW, with the cash question unchanged — capital spending versus free cash flow.

3. **October 1, 2026 — Rogers closes the rest of MLSE.** Rogers completed the purchase of Kilmer’s 25% of Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment for **C$4.35 billion**, after the earlier Bell stake. The October 3 brief is the write-up. Rogers then reported third-quarter 2026 results before the open on **October 23**, the morning of the close in this table, with a call at 8:00 a.m. ET. This note does not restate that quarter. The price is the same day’s close. Use the **RCI.B** line. RCI.A volume is a rounding error next to it.

4. **October 9, 2026 — the spectrum selloff.** SpaceX spectrum headlines hit the group, Cogeco included. The October 11 Cogeco brief is the tape. Cogeco reports fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 results after the close on **October 28**, with a call on **October 29**. The U.S. Breezeline write-down, about **C$1.7 billion**, is why trailing earnings are a loss and why the payout cell below is blank. The dividend that matters is the current **C$0.987** quarterly rate, about **C$3.95** a year, not the trailing **C$3.69** in the annual DPS field.

5. **November 5, 2026 — Quebecor, then BCE.** Quebecor said on September 30 that Pierre Karl Péladeau and CFO Hugues Simard will hold the third-quarter call on Thursday, November 5, at **9:00 a.m. ET**. The replay runs on quebecor.com until February 3, 2027. BCE’s call is an hour later. Quebecor is the one-year leader in this set (**+48.3%**). The November print is where that re-rating either gets a number or meets a target that is only **7%** above the October 23 price.

6. **October 28, 2026 — Bank of Canada, as a funding-cost backdrop.** The overnight target was still **2.25%** after the September 2 hold. Telecom is not a margin story the way the banks are. It is a debt-cost and competitive story: BCE’s net debt was about **C$41 billion** in the September brief, and TELUS is explicitly on a deleveraging path.

## Which Canadian telecom stocks are leading and lagging

**One month:** only **Quebecor (+2.8%)** and **TELUS (+2.7%)** are green. **BCE (−6.2%)** is the softest, then **Cogeco (−3.7%)** and **Rogers (−3.4%)**. The median is about **−3.4%**. Nothing in the last month looks like a sector rebound.

**One year:** **Quebecor (+48.3%)** is the entire leadership story. Every other name is down: **TELUS (−41.6%)**, **Cogeco (−19.6%)**, **Rogers (−16.0%)**, **BCE (−14.3%)**. Against the TSX’s approximate **+18%**, four of the five lost, and TELUS lost by a wide margin. That is the dividend-cut tape, not a mystery.

## How the national carriers differ from Quebecor and Cogeco

**BCE** is the national incumbent with the reset dividend and the AI Fabric spending plan. The trailing P/E is a trap. The forward multiple and the free-cash-flow guide are the file. **Rogers** is the wireless scale name, now the full owner of MLSE, with the highest payout in the set and a forward multiple under **10x**. **TELUS** is the deepest drawdown (**−41.6%** over one year, **−56%** over five) and the explicit deleveraging story. The **C$0.75** dividend is the new contract with income holders.

**Quebecor** (Videotron and Freedom) is the fourth national player and the only compounder in the table. It trades at a premium book and a low yield because the market already paid for the growth. **Cogeco** is regional cable in Canada and a shrinking U.S. Breezeline unit. The screener does not even file it under communications. It is here for the **7.31%** indicated yield and the **6.1x** forward earnings, with a trailing loss that keeps the payout blank.

## Bull case for Canadian telecom stocks

1. **Four of the five trade far under the CAD target.** BCE **+29.7%**, Rogers **+29.6%**, TELUS **+32.1%** and Cogeco **+31.7%** are not one-analyst gaps. Each line has at least eight analysts. Forward earnings at Rogers and Cogeco are under **10x**.

2. **The dividends have already been reset.** TELUS at **C$0.75** and BCE at **C$1.75** are the rates in the company guides, not the stale screener DPS. A buyer who uses the **6%** yield is at least using the right coupon. Cogeco’s **C$3.95** is the current quarterly rate, and the October brief’s cash test was that the dividend used under half of free cash flow.

3. **Quebecor shows the sector can re-rate.** **+48%** over one year and **+107%** over five years is the existence proof. It does not make BCE a Quebecor. It does say a Canadian telecom equity is not condemned to the five-year drawdown if the operating story changes.

## Bear case and risks for Canadian telecom stocks

1. **The one-year tape already voted.** Median **−16%** against a TSX near **+18%**, with TELUS **−42%**, is a lot of bad news that a target can sit above for a long time. TELUS’s rating is still Neutral.

2. **Cash, not the trailing P/E, pays the dividend.** BCE’s **4.3x** and **26%** payout are flattered by one-time earnings. The September brief put the **C$1.75** dividend at roughly three-quarters of the free-cash-flow guide, with an AI build still in front of it. Rogers’ payout is already above **100%** of trailing earnings. Cogeco’s earnings are a loss.

3. **Competition and spectrum are not finished.** Quebecor’s gain is someone else’s lost pricing. The October 9 spectrum move showed how fast a headline still hits the whole group, including a cable name the screener files elsewhere.

## What to watch next for Canadian telecom: Cogeco’s year-end, then November 5

1. **October 28–29, 2026 — Cogeco fourth-quarter fiscal 2026.** After the close on the 28th, call on the 29th. U.S. cash flow and whether the **C$3.95** dividend rate still has a growth sentence attached.

2. **October 28 — Bank of Canada decision.** A debt-cost check for BCE and TELUS, not a margin check.

3. **November 5 — Quebecor at 9:00 a.m. ET, BCE at 10:00 a.m. ET.** The leader’s quarter and the reset incumbent’s quarter on the same morning. Watch whether Quebecor’s **CA$70** target still looks close, and whether BCE’s free cash flow still covers **C$1.75**.

4. **TELUS’s date, whenever the company confirms it.** Do not trade a MarketBeat estimate as a company release. When the date is real, the questions are the **C$1.8 billion** free-cash-flow guide and the path to **3.0x** leverage, not the stale 13% yield.

5. **Rogers after MLSE.** The October 23 quarter is in the price. The next proof is whether the sports asset and the wireless book show up as cash, given a **120%** trailing payout.
