REIT investors compare P/FFO all the time, and the comparison is useful when it is done within one property type, on one FFO definition and one unit count. Done carelessly, it ranks REITs by leverage, capex intensity or property type rather than by price.

This guide builds a fair P/FFO peer table for TSX-listed REITs and adds the columns that explain why multiples differ. The general peer method is in [how to use peer tables without cherry-picking](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/); the P/FFO worksheet is in [how to calculate price/FFO in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-price-ffo-in-cad-from-filings/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Compare REITs of the **same property type**, each on **REALPAC FFO**, **TTM or forward for all**, **per diluted unit including exchangeable LP units**, priced on the **same date** in the **same currency**. Rank by P/FFO against the **peer median**, then add **FFO per unit growth**, **AFFO conversion** (and P/AFFO), **debt to gross book value** and **P/NAV**. A REIT that is cheap on P/FFO but also has the slowest growth, the weakest conversion and the most debt is probably fairly priced.

## Alignment rules

| Rule | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Same property type (or similar mix) | Industrial, multi-family, retail, office and seniors housing have different growth and capex |
| REALPAC FFO for everyone | "Adjusted" or "normalized" FFO differs by REIT |
| Same period: TTM for all, or forward for all | Mixing flatters growing REITs |
| Diluted units incl. Class B LP units | Otherwise FFO per unit is overstated for REITs with LP units |
| Same price date and currency | Some TSX REITs report or trade in US dollars |
| Canadian (REALPAC) and US (Nareit) peers kept apart or flagged | Definitions differ |

A rough cross-check combines price and growth: **AFFO yield plus FFO per unit growth**. It is not an expected return (AFFO is not all paid out, and growth may not persist), but it puts cheap-and-slow and expensive-and-fast REITs on a closer footing.

| REIT | AFFO yield (1 ÷ P/AFFO) | + FFO/unit growth | Rough total |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Your REIT | 6.1% | 2.5% | 8.6% |
| Peer A | 5.5% | 3.0% | 8.5% |
| Peer B | 6.4% | 1.0% | 7.4% |
| Peer C | 5.0% | 4.5% | 9.5% |
| Peer D | 6.3% | 1.5% | 7.8% |

On this view the "cheapest" REIT by P/FFO (Peer B) ranks last, and the most expensive (Peer C) ranks first. Growth assumptions drive that result, so test them; [when PEG misleads](/investing/valuation/when-peg-misleads-tsx/) covers the risk of trusting growth rates.

## Typical differences by property type

Multiples differ across property types for durable reasons, which is why they should not share one peer table.

| Property type | What tends to push P/FFO up | What tends to push it down |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Industrial | Rent growth on lease renewals, low capex | New supply, tenant concentration |
| Multi-family | Steady demand, rent growth | Rent regulation, capex on older buildings |
| Grocery-anchored and necessity retail | Stable tenants | Limited rent growth, redevelopment capex |
| Enclosed malls and other retail | Redevelopment upside | Tenant failures, high leasing costs |
| Office | Long leases | Vacancy, high tenant improvement and leasing costs |
| Seniors housing | Demographic demand | Operating risk, labour costs, occupancy swings |

## Canada-first issues

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| Class B LP units at many Canadian REITs | Include them in diluted units if FFO adds back their distributions |
| REITs with US properties reporting in US dollars | Convert FFO or price so both are in one currency |
| Diversified REITs | Compare on a property-mix basis, or with diversified peers only |
| US-listed REIT peers | Nareit FFO under US GAAP; flag or keep separate |
| Units in a TFSA, RRSP or taxable account | Tax character of distributions differs from dividends; see [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) |
