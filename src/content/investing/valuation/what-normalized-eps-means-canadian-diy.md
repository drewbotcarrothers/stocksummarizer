A P/E ratio is only as good as the "E". For a steady business, last year's earnings per share are a fair guide to a typical year. For a steelmaker, a sawmill operator, a fertilizer producer or any company that just booked a big write-down, last year's EPS can be wildly unrepresentative, and the P/E built on it points the wrong way. **Normalized EPS** is the attempt to estimate what the company earns in an ordinary year.

This page is the method hub for normalization on StockSummarizer. The multiples that use normalized EPS, such as [P/E](/investing/valuation/what-pe-means-canadian-diy/), [forward P/E](/investing/valuation/what-forward-pe-means-canadian-diy/) and [earnings yield](/investing/valuation/what-earnings-yield-means-canadian-diy/), have their own pages; this one explains the methods behind the denominator. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

**Normalized EPS is an estimate of earnings per share in a typical year: one-off items removed, the business cycle smoothed out, a sustainable tax rate applied and today's diluted share count used.** There is no single formula. The five common methods are (1) **remove one-offs** after tax, (2) **average EPS over a full cycle**, (3) **mid-cycle margin × revenue at current capacity**, (4) **normalize the tax rate** and (5) **use the current diluted share count**. Use at least two and see whether they agree. Normalized EPS is your estimate, not a reported figure, and it is different from the "adjusted EPS" companies publish.

Cyclical stocks often look cheapest on trailing P/E at the top of the cycle, when earnings are high, and most expensive at the bottom. Normalizing is the main defence against buying peaks and selling troughs. | Method | How it works | Best for | Main weakness |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Remove one-offs | Add back impairments, restructuring, gains or losses on asset sales, after tax | Any company with unusual items | "One-time" items that recur |
| 2. Mid-cycle average | Average EPS over a full cycle, often 7 to 10 years | Commodity and industrial cyclicals | Business may have changed since the early years |
| 3. Margin × revenue | Average operating margin over the cycle × revenue at today's capacity, less normal interest and tax | Cyclicals that have grown or shrunk | Needs a defensible mid-cycle margin |
| 4. Tax rate | Replace an unusual effective rate with the statutory or sustainable rate | Years with tax recoveries, loss use or one-time charges | Loss carryforwards can keep cash taxes low for years |
| 5. Share count | Divide by today's diluted shares, not past counts | Companies that issued or bought back stock | Easy to forget when averaging old EPS |

The idea is old. Benjamin Graham and David Dodd suggested averaging earnings over several years in *Security Analysis* (1934), and Robert Shiller's cyclically adjusted P/E (CAPE) applies a ten-year average of inflation-adjusted earnings to the whole market.

## Normalized EPS vs adjusted EPS

| | Company "adjusted EPS" | Your normalized EPS |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Who sets the rules | Management, disclosed under NI 52-112 | You, the same way for every company |
| Typical adjustments | One-offs, often amortization of acquired intangibles and sometimes share-based pay | One-offs, plus cycle, tax and share count |
| Smooths the cycle? | No | Yes, for cyclicals |
| Risk | Adjustments that only ever go one way | Your own assumptions |

Start from the company's reconciliation, then decide which adjustments you accept. [How to read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/) and [how to spot one-time items in Canadian statements](/investing/analyze/spot-one-time-items-canadian-statements/) cover that first step.

## Which TSX companies need it most

| Type | Why normalize | Main method |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Steel, aluminum, forest products, chemicals | Prices and margins swing with the cycle | Mid-cycle average or margin × revenue |
| Energy producers and miners | Earnings track commodity prices | Margin × revenue at a mid-cycle price deck |
| Industrials and autos | Volumes follow the economy | Mid-cycle average |
| Banks and insurers | Credit losses and catastrophes cluster | Through-cycle ROE or loss ratios |
| Companies with a big write-down or gain | One year distorted | One-off removal |
| Utilities, telecoms, staples | Earnings already steady | Usually only one-off removal |
