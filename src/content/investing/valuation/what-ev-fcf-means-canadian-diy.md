**EV/FCF** (enterprise value to free cash flow) is the **unlevered** cousin of [P/FCF](/investing/valuation/what-p-fcf-means-canadian-diy/). P/FCF compares the market cap with the cash left for common shareholders after lenders are paid. EV/FCF compares the value of the whole business, debt included, with the cash it generates **before** anyone is paid interest. That makes it the cash-flow multiple to use when peers carry very different amounts of debt.

This post is educational; all figures are hypothetical. The foundations are in [what is free cash flow?](/investing/glossary/what-is-free-cash-flow/) and [how to analyze free cash flow for stock decisions](/investing/analyze/free-cash-flow-analysis/); this post does not repeat them.

## Direct answer

EV/FCF = **enterprise value ÷ unlevered free cash flow**. Unlevered FCF (sometimes called free cash flow to the firm) is operating cash flow **before interest**, after taxes as if the company had no debt, minus capital spending. It belongs to every capital provider, so EV is the matching numerator. Use it instead of P/FCF when comparing companies with different leverage: a levered company always looks cheaper on P/FCF than it really is. Its inverse, **unlevered FCF ÷ EV**, is an FCF yield on the whole business.

## Same business, different debt

| Hypothetical, C$ millions | Company A (no debt) | Company B (levered) |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Market cap | 4,000 | 2,000 |
| Net debt | 0 | 2,000 |
| **Enterprise value** | **4,000** | **4,000** |
| Unlevered FCF | 250 | 250 |
| Interest paid | 0 | 120 |
| Tax saved by deducting interest (illustrative 26.5%) | 0 | 32 |
| FCF to common (unlevered − interest + tax saved) | 250 | 162 |
| P/FCF | 16.0x | 12.3x |
| **EV/FCF** | **16.0x** | **16.0x** |

B's lower P/FCF is not a bargain; it is the price of B's leverage. Shareholders of B get a smaller slice of the same cash flow and take more risk to get it. EV/FCF strips that out so you can compare the businesses first and the capital structures second.

## From P/FCF's numerator and denominator to EV/FCF's

The [P/FCF worksheet](/investing/valuation/calculate-p-fcf-in-cad-from-filings/) builds FCF **to common**: after all capex, lease payments, interest, minority distributions and preferred dividends. To go unlevered, add back whatever you included as a claim in EV.

| Claim | If it is in EV | If it is not in EV |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Debt | Add back after-tax interest | (EV always includes debt) |
| IFRS 16 lease liabilities | Do not deduct lease payments | Deduct lease payments (pre-IFRS 16 style) |
| Preferred shares | Do not deduct preferred dividends | Deduct them |
| Non-controlling interests | Do not deduct NCI distributions | Deduct them |
| Cash | Exclude interest earned on it | Keep interest income |

Interest classification under IAS 7 varies between companies (operating or financing), so the add-back depends on where each company puts it. The step-by-step version is in [how to calculate EV/FCF in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-fcf-in-cad-from-filings/).

## When EV/FCF is useful, and when it is not

| Situation | EV/FCF useful? | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Peers with very different debt levels | Yes | Neutralizes leverage |
| Stable, cash-generative businesses (rails, waste, telecom, some industrials) | Yes | FCF is meaningful and repeatable |
| Building a simple DCF | Yes | Unlevered FCF is the standard DCF input; see [a simple DCF without fake precision](/investing/valuation/simple-dcf-without-fake-precision/) |
| Heavy build phases (pipelines, utilities, miners developing projects) | Low | Growth capex can push FCF negative for years |
| Banks and insurers | No | Debt and deposits are their raw material; use [P/B and ROE](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/) |
| Early-stage companies with negative FCF | No | Use [EV/Sales](/investing/valuation/what-ev-sales-means-canadian-diy/) |

## How EV/FCF relates to the other multiples

| Multiple | Numerator | Denominator | Leverage-neutral? |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| P/FCF | Market cap | FCF to common | No |
| EV/FCF | Enterprise value | Unlevered FCF | Yes |
| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise value | EBITDA (before capex and tax) | Yes, but ignores capex and tax |
| EV/Sales | Enterprise value | Revenue | Yes, but ignores margins |

EV/FCF is the strictest of the EV multiples: it charges the business for capital spending and taxes, which [EV/EBITDA](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-for-canadian-diy-investors/) does not. In capital-heavy Canadian sectors that difference is often the whole story.

## Canada-first notes

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| IFRS 16 lease liabilities | Pick one basis (leases in EV, or lease payments deducted) and use it for every peer |
| US GAAP peers | Operating-lease payments are already inside US operating cash flow; adjust Canadian peers to match |
| Resource issuers with tax pools | Low cash taxes flatter FCF until the pools run out; see [when EV/FCF misleads](/investing/valuation/when-ev-fcf-misleads-tsx/) |
| USD reporters | FCF at the average rate, EV items at the closing rate |
