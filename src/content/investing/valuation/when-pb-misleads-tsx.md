A stock trading below book value sounds like buying a dollar for less than a dollar. Sometimes it is. Often, the book value is not a dollar. This post covers the ways **P/B misleads for non-bank TSX issuers**: insurers, industrials, resource companies and holding companies. The bank-specific issues (credit cycles, CET1, ROE through the cycle) are covered in [valuation for banks: P/B, ROE, and why P/E alone fails](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/).

It is educational; examples are hypothetical and not about any specific company.

## Direct answer

P/B misleads when **book value does not match the earning power or realizable value** of the assets. Book is often too low for companies with old, depreciated assets, valuable land or intangible assets kept off the balance sheet, and for companies that have bought back a lot of stock. It is often too high when it holds **goodwill** from expensive acquisitions, assets that should be **impaired**, or resource assets nearing the end of their life. And a low P/B often just reflects a **low ROE**: the market expects the assets to earn less than shareholders require. Always check P/B against ROE and the quality of what is in book.

## The P/B traps outside banks

| Trap | What happens | Where it shows up on the TSX | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Low P/B, low ROE | Market expects assets to earn below cost of equity | Mature industrials, some forest products | Compare with ROE; fair P/B ≈ ROE ÷ cost of equity |
| Goodwill-heavy book | Book includes premiums paid for acquisitions | Serial acquirers, roll-ups | Look at goodwill as a share of equity; see tangible book |
| Pending impairment | Assets carried above what they can earn (IAS 36) | Resource issuers after a commodity drop | Read impairment notes and sensitivity disclosures |
| Historical cost understates assets | Land, rail lines, pipelines carried at old cost | Railways, real-estate-rich operators | Use replacement cost or NAV as a cross-check |
| Buybacks shrink equity | Repurchases above book cut equity; P/B rises | Mature cash generators | Use P/E or FCF yield instead |
| Negative or tiny equity | P/B becomes huge or meaningless | Heavily levered or buyback-heavy issuers | Mark P/B as n/m |
| Insurer accounting | Under IFRS 17, unearned profit (CSM) is a liability | Life insurers | Look at book plus after-tax CSM, labelled |
| Holding company structure | Book may carry holdings at cost or equity method | Holding companies | Compare price with a sum-of-the-parts NAV |
| FX translation | AOCI swings move book for foreign operations | Multinationals reporting in CAD | Note the AOCI change; it is not operating profit |
| Cyclical book | Book swells near peaks from retained profits, then falls with write-downs | Steel, chemicals, energy services | Use mid-cycle ROE |

## Quick tests before trusting a low P/B

| Test | Pass if |
|:--|:--|
| ROE | ROE over a full cycle is near or above a reasonable cost of equity |
| Goodwill | Goodwill and intangibles are a modest share of book |
| Impairment risk | Recoverable amount comfortably exceeds carrying value in the notes |
| Asset age | You know whether assets are near-new or fully depreciated |
| Buybacks | Equity has not been shrunk by large repurchases |
| Cash generation | FCF supports the idea that the assets earn their keep |
| Catalyst | Something could close the gap: better returns, asset sales, buybacks below book |

For impairment wording, see [how to read IFRS notes that trip up US-trained readers](/investing/analyze/read-ifrs-notes-us-trained-readers/); for asset age and replacement needs, see [how to read capex plans in an MD&A](/investing/analyze/read-capex-plans-mdna/).
