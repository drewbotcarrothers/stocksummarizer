Comparing P/NAV across miners is one of the most common shortcuts in Canadian resource investing, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Each company's NAV is an estimate, and the ratio means something in a peer table only when every NAV rests on the **same price deck and the same discount rate**. Even then, a lower P/NAV is often explained by shorter mine life, higher costs, riskier jurisdictions or more debt rather than by a mispricing.

This guide builds a fair P/NAV peer table for TSX producers and shows how to explain the gaps. The general peer method (choosing true peers, using the median) is in [how to use peer tables without cherry-picking](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/); the NAV build is in [how to calculate price/NAV in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-price-nav-in-cad-from-filings/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Put every peer on **one deck, one discount rate, one currency and one date**, using diluted shares. Compare only companies at the **same stage** (producers with producers, developers with developers, royalty companies with royalty companies). Rank by P/NAV and compare with the **peer median**, then add columns that explain the gap: **reserve life**, **AISC relative to the group**, **jurisdiction**, **share of NAV in the top asset**, **leverage** and **price to cash flow**. A discount that those columns explain is not a bargain; a discount they do not explain is worth researching.

## Alignment rules

| Rule | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| One commodity price deck for all | A higher deck inflates NAV and lowers P/NAV mechanically |
| One discount rate (state real or nominal) | A 2-point change in rate can move NAV more than a year of production |
| Same treatment of resources outside mine plans | One analyst's "exploration upside" is another's zero |
| All claims deducted the same way | Closure, hedges, streams and G&A included for everyone |
| NAV and price in CAD on the same date | Most producers report in US dollars |
| Diluted shares, after funded financing | Developers otherwise look too cheap |

## Do not mix stages

| Group | Why it trades where it does | Compare with |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Senior and mid-tier producers | Cash flow today, multiple mines | Other producers |
| Single-asset producers | Concentration and operating risk | Other single-asset producers, with a discount for concentration |
| Developers | Financing, permitting and construction risk ahead | Other developers at a similar stage |
| Royalty and streaming companies | No operating or capex costs, diversified | Other royalty companies; usually above producer multiples |
| Explorers | No reserves; NAV is speculative | Usually better valued on in-situ resources than P/NAV |

A useful habit: write down which column explains each peer's position. If you cannot name one, check your NAV inputs before concluding the stock is mispriced.

## Canada-first issues

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| Most TSX miners report in US dollars | Convert NAV and compare with the TSX price in CAD on the same date |
| Dual-listed peers (TSX and NYSE) | Use one listing and one currency for the whole table; see [researching dual-listed Canadian stocks](/investing/analyze/research-dual-listed-canadian-stocks/) |
| Canadian jurisdictions vs abroad | Jurisdiction risk changes the discount rate investors apply; consider a higher rate for riskier countries rather than mixing |
| Royalty companies on the TSX | Keep them in their own peer group |
| Oil and gas producers | Use NI 51-101 reserve values at one price forecast; compare with producers of similar reserve life and product mix |

## Cross-check with cash flow

P/NAV tells you about the long life of the assets; price to cash flow tells you about the next year or two. A producer cheap on both is more interesting than one cheap on NAV but expensive on cash flow, which often means the NAV depends on distant ounces or a high deck. [P/FCF vs peers](/investing/valuation/p-fcf-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/) covers the cash-flow side.
