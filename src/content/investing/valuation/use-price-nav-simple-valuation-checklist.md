A P/NAV ratio becomes useful when it turns into a decision: what price range would make sense for this miner, REIT or holding company, on assumptions you wrote down in advance? This one-page checklist does that in five steps. It uses the NAV worksheet from [how to calculate price/NAV in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-price-nav-in-cad-from-filings/), the traps in [when price/NAV misleads](/investing/valuation/when-price-nav-misleads-tsx/), and the peer method in [price/NAV vs peers](/investing/valuation/price-nav-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/).

It is a framework for organizing your own research, not a buy or sell signal. All figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

(1) **Gate:** use P/NAV only when the value sits in discrete assets (mines, reserves, properties, stakes). (2) Build **your own NAV** on one stated deck and rate. (3) Choose a **P/NAV band** for the company's stage from history and peers on the same assumptions. (4) Multiply NAV per share by the band in **bear, base and bull** cases to get a price range. (5) **Cross-check** with price to cash flow, leverage and a catalyst. Write it all on one page before looking at the share price.

## The checklist

| Step | Question | Pass if | Notes |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Gate | Is most of the value in discrete, separately valued assets? | Yes: producer, developer, royalty company, REIT, holdco | Operating companies: use earnings or cash flow multiples instead |
| 2a. NAV inputs | Are the deck, discount rate (or cap rate) and date written down? | Yes | One deck for every scenario's base |
| 2b. Claims | Debt, closure, hedges, streams, leases and G&A deducted? | All checked | See the claims table in the worksheet post |
| 2c. Shares | Diluted, and after any financing still needed? | Yes | Critical for developers |
| 3. Band | Is the band based on the company's stage, history and peers? | Yes, and written before checking the price | Not "1.0x because NAV is fair value" |
| 4. Scenarios | Bear, base and bull NAV per share? | Three NAVs from three decks | The deck is the biggest swing factor |
| 5a. Cash flow | Is price to cash flow reasonable vs peers? | Not far above the peer median | Avoid NAVs that rely only on distant ounces |
| 5b. Balance sheet | Can the company survive the bear deck? | Net debt manageable at the bear deck | Covenants in [debt notes](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) |
| 5c. Catalyst | What would close the gap to NAV? | A specific event | Production ramp, de-risking, buybacks, asset sale |

## Choosing a band by stage

Bands differ by stage because risk and optionality differ. The ranges below are **illustrative placeholders** to show the structure; build your own from the company's history and a same-assumption peer table.

| Stage | Illustrative band | Why higher or lower |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Royalty and streaming | Above 1.0x | No operating costs, diversified, optionality on partners' exploration |
| Senior or mid-tier producer, long life | Around 0.8x–1.1x | Proven operations, diversified |
| Single-asset or short-life producer | Around 0.6x–0.9x | Concentration and replacement risk |
| Developer (permitted, financing unclear) | Around 0.3x–0.6x | Financing, construction and dilution ahead |
| Holding company | Discount to sum of parts | Holdco costs, complexity, private-stake uncertainty |
| REIT | Around NAV, depending on cap-rate view | See the cap-rate check in [valuation for REITs](/investing/valuation/valuing-reits-nav-ffo-affo/) |

## Worked example: a mid-tier producer (hypothetical)

From the worksheet post: base-deck NAV is C$12.50 per diluted share. The company has a 12-year reserve life and two Canadian mines, so the checklist uses an illustrative band of 0.70x–1.00x, with 0.85x as the base.

| Scenario | Price deck | NAV per share | P/NAV used | Price per share |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | Low | $9.00 | 0.70x | $6.30 |
| Base | Base | $12.50 | 0.85x | $10.63 |
| Bull | High | $16.00 | 1.00x | $16.00 |

The width of that range is the honest result. If the share price is near the base value, P/NAV alone does not make a case either way; the decision then depends on the cross-checks and on which deck you believe.

## Cross-checks

| Check | Hypothetical result | Read |
|:--|:--|:--|
| P/CF vs peer median | 6.0x vs 6.0x | In line |
| Net debt ÷ EBITDA at the bear deck | 1.6x | Manageable |
| Share of gross asset value in the top mine | About 60% | Moderate concentration |
| Share from risked projects and exploration | About 12% | Most value is in producing mines |
| Catalyst | Expansion at Mine B due in 18 months | Specific, dated, checkable |
