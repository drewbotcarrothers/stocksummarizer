Price-to-sales looks like the safest multiple because revenue is hard to fake and rarely negative. That is exactly why it misleads: a low P/S feels like a bargain even when the business keeps almost none of its revenue, carries heavy debt, or bought its sales.

This post lists the traps. For the concept and the **P/S = P/E × net margin** link, see [what P/S means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/what-ps-means-canadian-diy/); for the worksheet (TTM revenue, gross vs net revenue, average-rate FX), see [how to calculate P/S in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-ps-in-cad-from-filings/). It is educational; all numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

P/S misleads when the revenue in the denominator does not turn into profit or cash at a rate similar to the companies you compare it with. The main traps on the TSX are **thin or falling margins**, **pass-through revenue** that moves with commodity prices or fees, **balance-sheet debt** that market cap ignores, **acquired revenue**, **cyclical peak sales**, and **revenue-basis changes** (principal vs agent). Before calling a stock cheap on P/S, check gross profit, net margin, net debt and organic growth.

## The trap table

| Trap | What happens to P/S | Typical TSX setting | Quick check |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Thin structural margins | Very low P/S that is fully deserved | Grocers, distributors, refiners, contractors | Net margin and P/E |
| Falling margins | P/S stays flat while profit shrinks | Retail or industrials facing cost pressure | Gross margin trend, last 8 quarters |
| Commodity pass-through | Revenue jumps with prices, P/S drops | Fuel distributors, metals traders, energy marketers | Gross profit per unit, not revenue |
| Debt ignored | Levered company looks as cheap as a debt-free one | Telecom, utilities, roll-ups | Net debt ÷ market cap; EV/Sales |
| Acquired revenue | Sales grow faster than value | Serial acquirers | Organic growth; see [organic vs acquisition growth](/investing/analyze/compare-organic-vs-acquisition-growth/) |
| Cyclical peak | Record revenue flatters P/S | Industrials, building products, energy services | Mid-cycle revenue; backlog trend |
| Revenue-basis change | Switch from gross to net (or reverse) moves P/S overnight | Marketplaces, agencies, travel, logistics | IFRS 15 revenue note |
| Currency translation | USD revenue rises in CAD terms with no volume change | Canadian multinationals | Constant-currency growth |
| Unprofitable growth | High sales growth with negative unit economics | Early-stage tech | Contribution margin; cash burn |

## Trap 1: pass-through revenue makes P/S fall for no reason

When a company passes commodity costs on to customers, revenue swings with the commodity price while the dollars it keeps barely move. P/S then "improves" exactly when nothing about the business has changed.

The fix is to compare on **gross profit** (price ÷ gross profit, sometimes called P/GP) or on EBITDA when revenue includes large pass-throughs. Commodity-exposed issuers also disclose price sensitivities; see [how to read commodity price sensitivity for resource issuers](/investing/analyze/read-commodity-price-sensitivity-resource-issuers/).

## Trap 2: debt hides behind market cap

P/S uses equity value only. Two companies with the same revenue and the same P/S can have very different total claims on that revenue.

| Hypothetical | Company A | Company B |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Revenue | C$1,000M | C$1,000M |
| Market cap | C$1,500M | C$1,500M |
| P/S | 1.5x | 1.5x |
| Net debt | C$0 | C$1,200M |
| Enterprise value | C$1,500M | C$2,700M |
| EV/Sales | 1.5x | 2.7x |

Company B is far more expensive per dollar of sales once lenders are counted. That is why levered businesses are compared on EV/Sales; the debt-and-cash bridge is explained in [what EV/Sales means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/what-ev-sales-means-canadian-diy/).

## Trap 3: margins decide everything

A low P/S only means "cheap" if margins are similar to the peers you compare with, or likely to improve. Use the identity from the concept post: if a retailer earns a 2% net margin, a P/S of 0.4x is already a 20x P/E. Falling gross margins are the early warning; see [how to analyze gross margin trends](/investing/analyze/analyze-gross-margin-trends/) and [operating leverage](/investing/analyze/analyze-operating-leverage/).

## Trap 4: revenue that is not comparable

| Situation | Effect | What to read |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Principal vs agent judgment (IFRS 15) | Gross reporters show much larger revenue than net reporters | Revenue recognition policy note |
| Contract changes or reclassifications | Prior periods restated, TTM mixes bases | "Change in presentation" note |
| Bill-and-hold or long contracts | Timing of revenue shifts between quarters | Contract assets and liabilities |
| Discontinued operations | Sold segments still in older periods | Continuing operations line |
| Currency | USD sales translated at a weaker loonie look like growth | MD&A constant-currency commentary |

If you suspect lower-quality revenue, work through [how to analyze revenue quality for Canadian issuers](/investing/analyze/analyze-revenue-quality-canadian-issuers/) and [how to identify recurring vs cyclical revenue](/investing/analyze/identify-recurring-vs-cyclical-revenue/).

## A 60-second P/S sanity check

| Question | If "no" |
|:--|:--|
| Are net margins similar to the peers? | Compare P/E or EV/EBITDA instead |
| Is gross profit growing roughly with revenue? | Suspect pass-through or price inflation |
| Is net debt small relative to market cap? | Use EV/Sales |
| Is most growth organic? | Strip out acquisitions |
| Is revenue near mid-cycle, not a peak? | Use average revenue over a cycle |
| Is the revenue basis unchanged over the period? | Rebuild TTM on one basis |
