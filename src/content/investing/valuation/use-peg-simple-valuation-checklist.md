This is the one-page version. It assumes you can already [calculate PEG in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-peg-in-cad-from-filings/) and know [when PEG misleads](/investing/valuation/when-peg-misleads-tsx/). The P/E steps are in [how to use P/E in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/) and [the forward P/E checklist](/investing/valuation/use-forward-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/); this one adds an eligibility gate, a growth-quality score and a way to turn PEG into a price range. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use PEG only for **profitable companies with steady, mostly organic growth**. Score the quality of the growth, run **bear, base and bull** growth cases, and turn each into a price using a **hurdle PEG you choose in advance**. If the current price only works in the bull case, the stock is pricing in a lot. Always finish with a cross-check against ROE, debt and free cash flow.

## The PEG checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Is the company profitable with positive growth? | Positive EPS; growth above about 3% |
| 2 | Cyclicality | Are earnings near a cyclical peak or trough? | No; or use mid-cycle EPS |
| 3 | P/E | Built on a clear basis (trailing or forward, IFRS or adjusted)? | Basis written down |
| 4 | Growth source | Where does the growth rate come from? | One source, 3–5 year window, dated |
| 5 | Growth quality | Is the growth organic, recurring and funded? | Score of 6 or more out of 10 (below) |
| 6 | Scenarios | What is PEG at bear, base and bull growth? | Base case PEG at or below your hurdle |
| 7 | Price range | What prices match your hurdle in each case? | Current price within or below the base range |
| 8 | Dividends | Is it a high payer? | If yes, PEGY tells a similar story |
| 9 | Cross-check | Do ROE, debt and FCF support the view? | No contradictions |

## Growth quality score (simple)

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Organic share of growth | Under 50% | 50–80% | Over 80% |
| Revenue type | Mostly cyclical | Mixed | Mostly recurring |
| Funding | Debt or heavy dilution | Some debt | Internal cash flow |
| Estimate coverage | 0–2 analysts or wide range | 3–5 analysts | 6+ analysts, tight range |
| History | Missed guidance often | Mixed | Usually met or beat guidance |

A score of 8–10 means the growth input is fairly reliable; 6–7 is usable with caution; below 6, rely on other tools. For help on the inputs, see [how to identify recurring vs cyclical revenue](/investing/analyze/identify-recurring-vs-cyclical-revenue/) and [how to evaluate management guidance language](/investing/analyze/evaluate-management-guidance-language/).

## Turning PEG into a price range (hypothetical)

Rearranging PEG gives a price: **price = hurdle PEG × growth rate × EPS**. Suppose you decide in advance that you will pay up to a PEG of 1.5 for this kind of business, and TTM EPS is $2.40.

| Scenario | Growth | Price at PEG 1.5 | Calculation |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|
| Bear | 6% | $21.60 | 1.5 × 6 × 2.40 |
| Base | 10% | $36.00 | 1.5 × 10 × 2.40 |
| Bull | 14% | $50.40 | 1.5 × 14 × 2.40 |

If the stock trades at $48.00 (a 20x P/E), it is close to the bull-case price, so the market is pricing in growth near 14%. That is not automatically wrong, but you should be able to explain why the bull case is likely. The hurdle PEG is a personal assumption, not a market standard.
