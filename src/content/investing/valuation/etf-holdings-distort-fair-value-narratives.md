When a stock rallies, a story usually follows: the market has "recognised the value". Sometimes that is true. Sometimes the buying came from index funds and ETFs that had to own the stock because of an index change, a sector rebalance or a wave of inflows into a theme, and the narrative was written afterwards. For Canadian investors, where a few index and sector ETFs hold meaningful slices of many TSX companies, it helps to separate flow-driven price moves from changes in business value.

This post explains how ETF and index ownership can distort apparent fair value narratives and how to check for it. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Passive funds buy and sell based on **index rules and fund flows**, not on valuation. An index addition, a weight change or heavy inflows into a sector ETF can push a price up (or down on outflows) with no change in earnings or cash flow. Before accepting "the market sees value", check: **did the index weight change, how much of the float do index funds and ETFs hold, and did estimates or fundamentals actually move?** If price moved and fundamentals didn't, the higher multiple is a flow effect until proven otherwise.

| Event | Why passive funds trade | What it does not tell you |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Addition to the S&P/TSX Composite or S&P/TSX 60 | Index trackers must buy the new weight by the effective date | Whether the business got better |
| Quarterly index rebalance | Float, share count or sector weight changes | Anything about value |
| Theme or sector ETF inflows (gold, uranium, AI, banks) | New money is spread across holdings by weight | Which holdings are cheap |
| Covered call or dividend ETF demand | Funds buy high-yield names that fit their rules | Whether the dividend is safe |
| ETF outflows or index deletion | Forced selling | That the company got worse |

Index methodology for Canadian benchmarks is published by S&P Dow Jones Indices; see [what the S&P/TSX Composite measures](/investing/indexes-etfs/what-sp-tsx-composite-measures/).

A company that sits in several popular ETFs (a broad index, a sector fund and a dividend fund, say) can have a large share of its free float held by funds that will all buy or sell together. That concentration matters most for mid-caps, where daily trading is thinner; see [Canadian mid-cap valuation quirks vs mega-cap liquidity premiums](/investing/valuation/canadian-mid-cap-valuation-liquidity-premiums/).

## Checks before you believe the narrative

| Check | Where to look | What a flow-driven move looks like |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Index changes | Index provider announcements, company news releases | Rally into an effective date, then fades |
| ETF holdings | ETF fact sheets and holdings files; see [how to read an ETF factsheet](/investing/indexes-etfs/how-to-read-etf-factsheet/) | Stock is a top-10 holding of a fund with big recent inflows |
| Peer moves | Compare with peers not in the same ETFs | Only the ETF members moved |
| Estimates | Consensus EPS or cash flow revisions, company guidance | Price up, estimates flat |
| Multiple vs history | Current multiple vs the stock's own range | Multiple expands without a reason in the filings; see [multiple expansion hopes vs earnings growth](/investing/valuation/multiple-expansion-hopes-vs-earnings-growth/) |

## A worked example (hypothetical)

| | Before index add | After index add |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Share price | $30.00 | $35.40 |
| Next-year EPS estimate | $2.00 | $2.00 |
| Forward P/E | 15.0x | 17.7x |
| Peer median forward P/E | 15.5x | 15.5x |

The business is unchanged. The 18% price gain is all multiple. If the narrative now says the stock "deserves a premium", ask what changed in the business to justify it. If you already owned it, the extra multiple may be a gift; if you are buying, you are paying for flows. Compare with [forward P/E vs peers](/investing/valuation/forward-pe-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/).

## When passive ownership does matter for value

- **Liquidity and cost of capital.** Index inclusion can widen the shareholder base and narrow spreads, which may justify a modestly higher multiple over time.
- **Governance.** Large passive holders vote, and their voting policies can shape board decisions.
- **Reflexivity in sector funds.** Miners or energy names that rise on ETF flows can issue shares at better prices, which does change per-share value; see [dilution per-share math](/investing/valuation/dilution-per-share-valuation-math/).

## Common mistakes

- Reading a rally as proof of a thesis without checking index or ETF events.
- Assuming a stock is cheap because it fell during ETF outflows, without checking whether fundamentals also slipped.
- Comparing multiples with peers that sit outside the same funds and calling the gap "mispricing".
