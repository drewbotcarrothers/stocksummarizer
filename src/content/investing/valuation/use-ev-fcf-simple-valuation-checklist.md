This is the one-page EV/FCF checklist. It assumes you can [calculate EV/FCF in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-fcf-in-cad-from-filings/), know [when EV/FCF misleads](/investing/valuation/when-ev-fcf-misleads-tsx/) and have a [peer comparison](/investing/valuation/ev-fcf-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). The structure mirrors [how to use P/FCF in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-p-fcf-simple-valuation-checklist/), where fair P/FCF = (1 + g) ÷ (r − g) with r the cost of equity. The unlevered version changes two things: the discount rate is the **weighted average cost of capital (WACC)**, because the cash flow belongs to lenders and shareholders together, and the result is a fair **enterprise** value that has to be walked back to a price per share. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use EV/FCF for steady, cash-generative businesses where leverage differs across peers. Normalize unlevered FCF, set a **fair multiple** from (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g) and check it against the peer median, multiply to get a **fair EV**, then subtract net debt, leases, preferreds, NCI and debt-like items (and add non-core investments) to reach **equity value per share**. Compare the range with today's price. The multiple is a one-line DCF, so treat the inputs with the same humility.

## The EV/FCF checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Positive, fairly stable unlevered FCF; not a bank, insurer or REIT? | Yes |
| 2 | Basis | One lease basis; interest, tax shield and investment income handled? | Built per the calculation post |
| 3 | Normalize | Three-year average or mid-cycle; pro forma for deals? | Not a peak or an acquisition-gap year |
| 4 | Tax | Cash tax rate near statutory, or normalized? | Yes, or both versions shown |
| 5 | Capex | Capex ÷ depreciation sensible for the business? | Not starving the assets |
| 6 | Fair multiple | (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g), bear / base / bull | Within the peer range, or explained |
| 7 | Claims | Net debt, leases, prefs, NCI, pension, decommissioning | All listed with dates |
| 8 | Per share | Fair EV − claims + investments ÷ diluted shares | Calculated |
| 9 | Cross-check | EV/EBITDA and P/FCF tell the same story? | No contradictions |

## Setting the fair multiple

| Scenario | WACC | Long-run growth (g) | Fair EV/FCF = (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 8.0% | 1.5% | 15.6x |
| Base | 7.5% | 2.0% | 18.5x |
| Bull | 7.0% | 2.5% | 22.8x |

All inputs are hypothetical. Small changes matter: in the base case, moving WACC from 7.5% to 7.0% lifts the multiple from 18.5x to 20.4x. Growth should be a long-run rate the business can sustain, not next year's guidance. If the result sits far outside the peer range, revisit the inputs before trusting it. The same logic, with more stages, is in [how to build a simple DCF without fake precision](/investing/valuation/simple-dcf-without-fake-precision/).

## From fair EV to price per share (hypothetical)

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| Unlevered FCF depends on a low cash tax rate that is ending | Normalized FCF is lower than it looks |
| Capex well below depreciation for several years | FCF is borrowed from the future |
| Debt maturities exceed a few years of FCF | Refinancing risk falls on equity; see [debt notes and covenants](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) |
| Large, growing pension or decommissioning obligations | Claims may grow faster than the business |
| Fair multiple only works with WACC − g below about 4 points | The answer is mostly assumption |
