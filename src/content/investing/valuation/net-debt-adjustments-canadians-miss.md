Net debt looks like the simplest number in a valuation: total debt minus cash. In practice, many Canadian companies carry obligations that behave like debt but sit elsewhere on the balance sheet or in the notes: lease liabilities, preferred shares, pension deficits, asset retirement obligations, convertible debentures, earn-outs. Leave them out and enterprise value is understated, leverage looks lower than it is, and the equity value per share you back into from a DCF or EV/EBITDA valuation is too high.

This post lists the adjustments most often missed on TSX companies and shows their combined effect with hypothetical numbers. The bridge itself, and when to use EV vs equity value, are covered in [enterprise value vs equity value: when each matters](/investing/valuation/enterprise-value-vs-equity-value-when-each-matters/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Start with **borrowings minus cash**, then add the **debt-like claims** that fit your EBITDA definition and the company's situation: **lease liabilities** (if EBITDA is after IFRS 16), **preferred shares and hybrid notes**, **after-tax pension and OPEB deficits**, **after-tax asset retirement (decommissioning) obligations**, **convertible debentures** (if likely to stay debt), **deferred acquisition payments and earn-outs**, and remove **cash that is not freely available** (trapped, restricted, or needed for operations). Apply the same rules to every peer, and say which items you included.

Every dollar of debt-like claim you miss is a dollar of value you hand to the common shareholder that is not theirs. The more leveraged the company, the bigger the error as a share of equity value.

## The adjustments, one by one

| Adjustment | Include when | Watch out for |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Lease liabilities | Your EBITDA excludes lease costs (IFRS 16 basis) | Mixing with US GAAP peers whose operating leases still run through EBITDA; see [IFRS quirks](/investing/valuation/adjust-multiples-ifrs-quirks/) |
| Preferred shares | Almost always at face or market value | Rate-reset prefs are common on the TSX; see [preferred shares and hybrid capital](/investing/analyze/analyze-preferred-shares-hybrid-capital/) |
| Hybrid and subordinated notes | Treat as debt for equity valuation, even if rating agencies give partial equity credit | Double counting if interest is already deducted in your cash flow |
| Pension and OPEB deficits | Defined benefit plans in deficit, after tax | Surpluses may not be fully accessible; see [pension and long-term liabilities](/investing/analyze/evaluate-pension-long-term-liabilities/) |
| Asset retirement obligations | Energy, mining, utilities, telecom towers | Discount rate assumptions in the provisions note |
| Convertible debentures | As debt if out of the money; as shares if deep in the money | Not both; see [dilution per-share math](/investing/valuation/dilution-per-share-valuation-math/) |
| Earn-outs and deferred consideration | Serial acquirers | Contingent amounts measured at fair value can change |
| Trapped or restricted cash | Cash held abroad with repatriation limits, or pledged | Also keep some operating cash; not all cash is "excess" |
| Customer deposits, factoring | Retail, construction, receivables sold with recourse | Off-balance-sheet arrangements; see [off-balance-sheet commitments](/investing/analyze/analyze-off-balance-sheet-commitments/) |
| Non-controlling interests | When EBITDA includes 100% of non-wholly owned subsidiaries | Market value can differ a lot from book value |

## Consistency rules

| Rule | Example |
|:--|:--|
| Match EBITDA and leases | EBITDA after IFRS 16 → leases in net debt; EBITDAaL (after lease costs) → leases out |
| Do not double count | If pension service cost is in EBITDA, include only the deficit, not future contributions too |
| Tax-effect where appropriate | Pension deficits and AROs are usually tax-deductible when paid |
| Same rules for every peer | Otherwise the "cheap" company may just be the one with the most items missed |
| Covenant definitions differ | Lender leverage tests often exclude leases or prefs; read [debt notes and covenants](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) |

## A quick checklist

1. Read the debt, leases, provisions, employee benefits and business combinations notes.
2. List every claim ahead of common shareholders.
3. Decide include or exclude for each, consistent with your EBITDA or cash flow measure.
4. Recompute leverage (net debt/EBITDA) and interest coverage on the adjusted number; see [interest coverage for levered TSX companies](/investing/analyze/analyze-interest-coverage-levered-tsx/).
5. Recompute equity value per diluted share.
