Valuation questions for DIY Canadians usually collapse into two families: **relative** (how does this price compare to peers or history?) and **intrinsic** (what cash flows might justify a price range?). You need both languages — and the humility to keep ranges wide.

Educational only — not advice or price targets.

## Direct answer

Use relative valuation to stay honest versus peers and history; use intrinsic thinking to test whether the story’s cash flows could *ever* support the price. Cross-check them. If they violently disagree, your assumptions — or the market’s — need work.

## Relative valuation (multiples)

Examples: P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF, P/B, sector-native metrics (P/AFFO, NAV discounts). Strengths: fast, peer-aware, hard to entirely fake if peers are chosen well. Weaknesses: peers can be collectively wrong; multiples embed growth and risk assumptions silently.

**DIY checklist:** same business model, similar accounting, clear cycle stage, and a written reason for each peer’s inclusion or exclusion.

## Intrinsic valuation (cash-flow reasoning)

Examples: simple DCF, dividend discount sketches, owner-earnings capitalisation. Strengths: forces explicit growth, margin, and reinvestment assumptions. Weaknesses: spreadsheet precision theatre; tiny terminal-growth changes swing values wildly.

**DIY rule:** prefer a **one-page scenario range** (bear / base / bull) over a three-decimal “fair value.”

## A practical split for TSX work

| Situation | Lean relative | Lean intrinsic |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Banks / insurers with stable peers | P/B, ROE context, P/E | Only as a sanity check |
| Mature dividend payers | Yield + coverage vs peers | Simple dividend/owner-earnings sketch |
| Cyclical producers | Mid-cycle multiple vs history | Scenario on commodity + costs |
| Unique growth story | Few true peers | Driver-based scenarios (still wide ranges) |

## Triangulation habit

1. Relative screen → shortlist.
2. Filings → business quality.
3. Intrinsic sketch → “could cash flows support this?”
4. Peer check again → “am I paying a premium for nothing?”
