P/AFFO is the multiple REIT investors lean on when they want to know what they pay for cash-like earnings. On its own it still says nothing about whether the AFFO is honest, whether the distribution is covered or what a fair multiple is. A checklist answers those questions in order, before you look at the quote.

This page pulls together the rebuild in [how to calculate price/AFFO in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-price-affo-in-cad-from-filings/), the traps in [when price/AFFO misleads](/investing/valuation/when-price-affo-misleads-tsx/) and the peer method in [price/AFFO vs peers](/investing/valuation/price-affo-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). It continues the hypothetical REIT from the worksheet: AFFO of $0.91 per unit on a three-year actual capex basis, a $0.84 distribution and a $15.00 unit price. It is a framework for your own research, not a recommendation.

## Direct answer

(1) **Gate:** use P/AFFO for REITs and other real-estate owners where AFFO or ACFO can be built. (2) **One capex basis:** AFFO on a three-year average of actual sustaining capex and leasing costs. (3) **Payout gate:** AFFO payout on that basis below about 100%, and the cash payout after the DRIP understood. (4) **Balance sheet:** leverage and maturities acceptable, with AFFO adjusted for refinancing. (5) **Fair band:** from the REIT's own history, same-type peers and a fair AFFO yield of roughly **r − g**. (6) **Price range:** forward AFFO per unit × the band in bear, base and bull cases, then cross-check with P/FFO and P/NAV.

## The checklist

| Step | Question | Pass if |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Gate | REIT or real-estate owner with AFFO or ACFO you can build? | Yes |
| 2a. Capex basis | AFFO rebuilt on 3-yr actual sustaining capex and leasing costs? | Done |
| 2b. Units | Diluted, including Class B LP units? | Yes |
| 3a. AFFO payout | Distribution ÷ AFFO (your basis) | Below about 100%, with room in the bear case |
| 3b. Cash payout | Cash distributions after DRIP ÷ AFFO | Understood; DRIP dilution noted |
| 4a. Leverage | Debt ÷ gross book value in line with same-type peers? | Yes, or explained |
| 4b. Maturities | Debt maturing within two years repriced in forward AFFO? | Done |
| 4c. Lease expiries | Leasing costs lifted if a large share of GLA rolls soon? | Done |
| 5. Fair band | Set from history, peers and r − g, written before checking the price | Yes |
| 6. Range | Bear, base and bull forward AFFO × band | Done |
| 7. Cross-check | P/FFO and P/NAV consistent with the range? | No contradiction, or explained |

## The payout gate (hypothetical)

| Measure | Value | Read |
|:--|--:|:--|
| AFFO per unit, REIT's own reserve | $0.97 | Headline |
| AFFO per unit, 3-yr actual capex basis | $0.91 | Use this |
| Distribution per unit | $0.84 | |
| AFFO payout, REIT's basis | 87% | Looks comfortable |
| **AFFO payout, your basis** | **92%** | Covered, with little room |
| AFFO payout, bear-case forward AFFO ($0.86) | 98% | At the edge |
| DRIP participation | 10% | Cash payout lower, but units issued every month |

A REIT that fails this gate can still be worth owning, but the distribution is then part of the risk rather than part of the return. The dividend-side view is in [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/).

## Setting the fair band

Use three inputs and look for overlap.

| Input | Hypothetical result |
|:--|:--|
| The REIT's own P/AFFO on your basis over a full rate cycle | 15x to 19x |
| Same-type peer median on one capex basis | 18.0x |
| Fair AFFO yield = r − g (below) | 15.4x to 18.2x |
| **Band used** | **15x (bear) to 18x (bull), 16.5x base** |

**Fair AFFO yield.** If AFFO per unit grows at a steady rate g and you require a return r, a rough fair price is next year's AFFO ÷ (r − g). Equivalently, the fair AFFO yield on forward AFFO is about r − g.

| Inputs | Fair AFFO yield (r − g) | Fair P/AFFO (forward) |
|:--|--:|--:|
| r = 8%, g = 2.5% | 5.5% | 18.2x |
| r = 8.5%, g = 2.5% | 6.0% | 16.7x |
| r = 9%, g = 2.5% | 6.5% | 15.4x |
| r = 8%, g = 1.5% | 6.5% | 15.4x |

The formula assumes AFFO is paid out or reinvested at about r, and it is very sensitive to r − g: one percentage point moves the answer by about three turns. Treat it as a cross-check, not the answer. The logic is the same dividend discount model used in [how to build a simple DCF without fake precision](/investing/valuation/simple-dcf-without-fake-precision/).

## Cross-checks

| Check | Hypothetical result | Read |
|:--|:--|:--|
| P/FFO at the base price | 12.4x on $1.24 forward FFO | Inside the 11x to 14x band from the [P/FFO checklist](/investing/valuation/use-price-ffo-simple-valuation-checklist/) |
| P/NAV at the base price | About 0.95x | No large discount or premium |
| Implied cap rate | Close to recent transaction cap rates the REIT cites | Consistent |
| ACFO per unit | Within a few cents of AFFO on your basis | Capex basis believable |
| Bear-case leverage | Debt ÷ GBV under 50% | Manageable |

If the cross-checks disagree (a fair P/AFFO but a large NAV premium, or ACFO well below AFFO), return to the capex and payout gates before trusting the range. [Valuation for REITs](/investing/valuation/valuing-reits-nav-ffo-affo/) shows how to work out the implied cap rate.
