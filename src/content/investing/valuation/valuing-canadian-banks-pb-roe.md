Banks are the biggest weight in the S&P/TSX Composite and in many Canadian DIY portfolios — and the sector where generic valuation habits go most wrong. EV/EBITDA doesn't apply, free cash flow isn't meaningful, and P/E can look cheapest exactly when risk is highest. The workhorse instead is **price-to-book (P/B) read through return on equity (ROE)**.

Educational only — not financial advice. Examples are **hypothetical**; check current figures in each bank's latest filings.

## Direct answer

A bank's balance sheet *is* its business, so value it against **book value** (equity) and judge whether it earns enough on that equity. **Justified P/B ≈ (ROE − g) ÷ (r − g)**, where r is your required return and g long-run growth. A bank earning an ROE above its cost of equity deserves to trade above book; one earning below deserves a discount. Use P/E as a cross-check — but normalize provisions for credit losses, because earnings swing with the credit cycle.

## Why the usual tools fail for banks

| Tool | Why it breaks for banks |
|:--|:--|
| EV/EBITDA | Debt (deposits, borrowings) is raw material, not financing — "enterprise value" has no clean meaning |
| Free cash flow / P/FCF | Lending growth flows through operating cash; FCF swings wildly and says little |
| P/E alone | Provisions for credit losses (PCLs) are low at the top of the cycle, flattering EPS right before they rise |
| Dividend yield alone | High yield can reflect a market expecting earnings pressure |

Background: [what EV/EBITDA means](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-for-canadian-diy-investors/) and [what P/E means](/investing/valuation/what-pe-means-canadian-diy/).

## The core link: ROE drives P/B

**P/B = share price ÷ book value per share.** Many analysts use **tangible** book value (excluding goodwill and intangibles from acquisitions) to compare banks with different M&A histories.

**Justified P/B = (ROE − g) ÷ (r − g)**

**How to use it:** take a bank's current P/B, then ask what *sustainable* ROE it implies. If a bank trades at 1.6× book with a 10% cost of equity, the market is assuming it can sustain roughly a 14% ROE. Is that believable through a credit cycle?

## Why P/E alone fails: a hypothetical credit cycle

| | Good year | Credit downturn | Recovery |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Pre-provision profit ($M) | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,050 |
| Provisions for credit losses ($M) | −100 | −400 | −150 |
| Pre-tax income ($M) | 900 | 600 | 900 |
| If share price unchanged: P/E (indexed) | 10.0× | **15.0×** | 10.0× |

*Hypothetical. In the good year, P/E looks cheap because provisions are low. A better habit: look at **pre-provision, pre-tax profit** and a through-cycle PCL ratio to normalize earnings.*

## The bank metrics DIY investors should track

| Metric | What it tells you | Where to find it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| ROE / return on tangible common equity | Profitability on shareholder capital | Quarterly report to shareholders |
| CET1 ratio | Loss-absorbing capital vs risk-weighted assets; OSFI sets minimums plus buffers | Capital section of MD&A / supplementary pack |
| PCL ratio (bps of loans) | Credit-loss pace vs history | Credit risk section |
| Impaired loans | Problem loans building up | Credit risk section |
| Net interest margin (NIM) | Lending spread | Segment results |
| Efficiency ratio | Expenses ÷ revenue (lower = better) | Highlights |
| Book value / tangible book per share | The "B" in P/B | Highlights |
| Dividend payout ratio | Room to keep raising | Highlights |

Canadian banks publish a supplementary financial information package each quarter — that's where the detail lives. Their fiscal year ends October 31. Reading order: [quarterly earnings](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-quarterly-earnings-tsx/) → [earnings calls](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-earnings-call-transcript/), reading the Big Six together.

## A bank valuation workflow

1. Gather P/B (and tangible P/B), ROE, CET1, PCL ratio and P/E for the peer group — start with the [banks deep dive](/sectors/banks/).
2. Plot P/B against ROE: banks above the line are priced for more profitability than peers; below, for less.
3. Explain outliers with capital strength, credit quality, geographic mix (e.g. US exposure), and growth.
4. Normalize P/E with a through-cycle PCL assumption.
5. Write the bull / bear / watch in your [stock brief](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/) — the [TD stock summary](/summaries/2026-09-23-td-toronto-dominion/) is an example to critique.

## Insurers and other financials

Life insurers (see [insurers](/sectors/insurers/)) are also valued mainly on P/B, ROE and core EPS, with LICAT capital ratios in place of CET1. Diversified financials and asset managers often suit P/E or fee-based metrics better.
