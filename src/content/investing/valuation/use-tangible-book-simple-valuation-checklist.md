For a bank or other lender, tangible book value is the closest thing to a balance-sheet anchor, and the return it earns is the closest thing to a growth engine. A checklist puts the two together: build TBV consistently, test whether it can be trusted, set a fair P/TBV from a through-cycle return, and turn it into a price range before you look at the quote.

This page pulls together the worksheet in [how to calculate tangible book in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-tangible-book-in-cad-from-filings/), the traps in [when tangible book misleads](/investing/valuation/when-tangible-book-misleads-tsx/) and the peer method in [tangible book vs peers](/investing/valuation/tangible-book-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). It continues the hypothetical lender from the worksheet: TBV of $15.50 per share and a $27.00 share price. It is a framework for your own research, not a recommendation.

## Direct answer

(1) **Gate:** use P/TBV for banks, lenders and insurers, and only as a cross-check elsewhere. (2) **Your TBV:** one set of deductions, period-end shares, CAD. (3) **Quality gates:** capital ratio comfortably above requirements, loan loss allowance in line with peers, unrealized losses understood. (4) **Through-cycle ROTCE**, not last year's. (5) **Fair P/TBV ≈ (ROTCE − g) ÷ (r − g)**, checked against history and peers. (6) **Price range:** forward TBV per share × fair P/TBV in bear, base and bull cases. Then cross-check with P/E and the dividend.

## The checklist

| Step | Question | Pass if |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Gate | Is the company a bank, lender or insurer whose value sits in its balance sheet? | Yes (otherwise use P/TBV only as a cross-check) |
| 2a. TBV | Goodwill, intangibles (incl. software), preferred shares, LRCNs and NCI removed? | Yes, consistently with peers |
| 2b. Shares and currency | Period-end shares, TBV in CAD? | Yes |
| 3a. Capital | CET1 (or LICAT, MCT) comfortably above the regulatory requirement and the company's target? | Yes |
| 3b. Reserves | Allowance for credit losses ÷ loans in line with peers for a similar book? | Yes, or TBV adjusted |
| 3c. AOCI | Unrealized securities losses inside and outside equity understood? | Yes |
| 4. Return | ROTCE averaged over a full credit cycle (five or more years) | Written down |
| 5. Fair band | (ROTCE − g) ÷ (r − g), plus history and peer median | Written before checking the price |
| 6. Range | Bear, base and bull forward TBV × P/TBV | Done |
| 7. Cross-check | P/E, P/B, dividend payout and the market-implied ROTCE | No contradiction, or explained |

## Setting the fair P/TBV

**Justified P/TBV ≈ (ROTCE − g) ÷ (r − g).** A lender that earns exactly its required return is worth about 1.0x tangible book; each point of ROTCE above r adds value. Growth g should be consistent with ROTCE: sustainable growth ≈ ROTCE × the share of earnings retained after dividends and buybacks.

| Input (hypothetical) | Value | Note |
|:--|--:|:--|
| Latest ROTCE | 18.2% | From the worksheet; a good year |
| Through-cycle ROTCE (5-yr average incl. a credit downturn) | 15% | Use this |
| Required return, r | 10% | Your own choice |
| Long-run growth, g | 4% | 15% ROTCE × about 27% retained after dividends and buybacks |
| Justified P/TBV | **1.83x** | (15 − 4) ÷ (10 − 4) |

| Scenario | Through-cycle ROTCE | Justified P/TBV |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Bear (credit losses run higher, margins compress) | 12% | 1.33x |
| Base | 15% | 1.83x |
| Bull (better mix, lower credit costs) | 17% | 2.17x |

Sense-check the band against the lender's own P/TBV range over a full cycle and the peer median from the peer table. If all three disagree, the ROTCE assumption is usually the problem.

Forward TBV per share grows at about g: $15.50 × 1.04 = **$16.12**.

**Reverse check.** At $27.00 the shares trade at 1.67x forward TBV. Solving the same formula for ROTCE gives 1.67 × 6% + 4% ≈ **14%**: the market is pricing in a through-cycle return a point below your base case. That is the question to research, not the price.

**Sensitivity to r.** At an 11% required return, the base-case multiple falls to (15 − 4) ÷ (11 − 4) ≈ 1.57x and the base price to about $25.33. Small changes in r move the answer a lot; that is why the reverse check is often more useful.

## Cross-checks

| Check | Hypothetical result | Read |
|:--|:--|:--|
| P/E at the base price | About 12.5x on $2.37 of earnings (15% × average TBV) | Within a typical range for a lender; compare with [P/E vs peers](/investing/valuation/pe-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/) |
| P/B at the base price | About 1.26x | Consistent with goodwill at about a third of common equity |
| Dividend payout | Covered in the bear case? | Check with the [dividend yield checklist](/investing/valuation/use-dividend-yield-simple-valuation-checklist/) |
| Capital after a bear year | CET1 still above target? | If not, expect slower buybacks or dividend growth |

## Insurer variant

For life insurers, read TBV with the after-tax contractual service margin (CSM) under IFRS 17, use LICAT instead of CET1 and core ROE as the company defines it under NI 52-112. For P&C insurers, use MCT and an ROE averaged over several catastrophe years. The [insurers vs banks](/investing/analyze/analyze-insurers-vs-banks/) post covers the differences.

## Record it

Keep the page with your notes and update it each quarter when the company reports TBV, capital and credit costs. The [bull, bear, watch decision card](/investing/frameworks/bull-bear-watch-decision-card/) is a compact format.
