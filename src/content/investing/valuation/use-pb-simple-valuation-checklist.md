This is the one-page P/B checklist for **non-bank asset-heavy companies**: insurers, industrials, materials and holding companies. Banks have their own workflow in [valuation for banks: P/B, ROE, and why P/E alone fails](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/). It assumes you can [calculate P/B in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-pb-in-cad-from-filings/) and know [when P/B misleads](/investing/valuation/when-pb-misleads-tsx/). The general format mirrors [how to use P/E in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/). It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use P/B where the balance sheet holds the real earning assets. Clean the book value, score its **quality**, then set a fair range using **ROE scenarios**: with no growth, fair P/B ≈ ROE ÷ cost of equity. Multiply by book value per share to get a price range and compare it with today's price. If the stock only looks cheap because ROE is low, the low P/B is probably deserved.

## The P/B checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Does book value reflect the assets that earn the profit? | Asset-heavy, insurer or holding company |
| 2 | Equity basis | Common equity only? | NCI, preferreds and hybrids removed |
| 3 | Goodwill | How much of book is goodwill and intangibles? | Under about a third, or tangible book also checked |
| 4 | Asset values | Old cost, fair value, or recently impaired? | You know which, and the direction of error |
| 5 | ROE history | What is the 5-year or through-cycle ROE? | Written down, not just the latest year |
| 6 | Cost of equity | What return do you require? | Your own assumption, recorded |
| 7 | Scenarios | Fair P/B at bear, base and bull ROE? | Calculated |
| 8 | Price range | Fair P/B × BVPS vs current price | Current price at or below the base value |
| 9 | Cross-check | Do P/E, FCF yield and dividend cover agree? | No contradictions |

## Book quality score (simple)

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Goodwill and intangibles share of book | Over 50% | 20–50% | Under 20% |
| Impairment history (5 years) | Large write-downs | Small ones | None |
| Asset age vs useful life | Near end of life, big capex due | Mid-life | Recently renewed |
| Buybacks above book | Large, shrinking equity | Some | None or below book |
| Accounting comparability | Unusual policies | Some differences | Standard for the peer group |

8–10: book value is a solid anchor. 6–7: usable, with care. Below 6: lean on P/E, FCF or NAV instead. For asset age, see [how to read capex plans in an MD&A](/investing/analyze/read-capex-plans-mdna/).

## Turning ROE scenarios into a price range (hypothetical)

Book value per share is $15.00 and you require a 9% return on equity.

| Scenario | ROE | Fair P/B (ROE ÷ 9%) | Fair price (× $15.00) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 6% | 0.67x | $10.00 |
| Base | 10% | 1.11x | $16.67 |
| Bull | 13% | 1.44x | $21.67 |

If the stock is at $18.00 (1.20x book), it sits between the base and bull cases: the market expects ROE a little above 10%. Adding 2% growth gives (ROE − g) ÷ (cost of equity − g): 0.57x, 1.14x and 1.57x. Growth only adds value when ROE beats the cost of equity. The cost of equity is your assumption, not a published rate.
