This is the one-page P/FCF checklist. It assumes you can [calculate P/FCF in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-p-fcf-in-cad-from-filings/), know [when P/FCF misleads](/investing/valuation/when-p-fcf-misleads-tsx/) and have a [peer comparison](/investing/valuation/p-fcf-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). Whether the cash flow itself is durable is covered by the [FCF quick checklist for stock decisions](/investing/analyze/fcf-stock-decisions-quick-checklist/); this page uses that answer to set a price range. The format mirrors [how to use P/E in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/). It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use P/FCF where free cash flow is positive, reasonably stable and measured after all capex, leases, interest and minority claims. Normalize it, score its quality, then set a fair multiple from **your required return (r)** and a **long-run growth rate (g)**: fair P/FCF ≈ (1 + g) ÷ (r − g). Multiply by normalized FCF per share to get a range, and compare it with today's price. Keep g modest; most of the answer comes from it.

## The P/FCF checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Is FCF positive in most of the last 5 years? | 4 of 5 years, not a bank or insurer |
| 2 | Definition | After capex, lease principal, interest, NCI and preferreds? | Rebuilt per the calculation post |
| 3 | Normalize | 3-year average or mid-cycle used? | Within about 25% of the latest TTM, or the gap explained |
| 4 | Quality | FCF quality score (below)? | 6 or more |
| 5 | Required return | What annual return do you require? | Your own assumption, recorded |
| 6 | Growth | Long-run FCF per share growth? | Below your required return and near nominal GDP or less |
| 7 | Fair multiple | (1 + g) ÷ (r − g) at bear, base and bull g | Calculated |
| 8 | Price range | Fair P/FCF × normalized FCF per share | Current price at or below base value |
| 9 | Cross-check | Do P/E, EV/EBITDA and the dividend agree? | No contradictions |

## FCF quality score (simple)

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| FCF ÷ net income (3-year) | Under 60% | 60–90% | Over 90% |
| Capex ÷ depreciation (5-year) | Under 0.7x or over 2.0x | 0.7–0.9x or 1.5–2.0x | 0.9–1.5x |
| SBC ÷ operating cash flow | Over 15% | 5–15% | Under 5% |
| Net debt ÷ EBITDA | Over 4x | 2–4x | Under 2x |
| Acquisition reliance | Spends more than FCF on deals | Some deals | Little or none |

8–10: FCF is a solid anchor. 6–7: usable with care. Below 6: lean on a scenario DCF or EV-based measures.

## From required return to a fair multiple (hypothetical)

The formula is the growing-perpetuity shortcut: if FCF per share grows at g forever and you require r, the value today is FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g). With r = 9%:

| Long-run growth (g) | Fair P/FCF | Matching FCF yield |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 0% | 11.1x | 9.0% |
| 2% | 14.6x | 6.9% |
| 3% | 17.2x | 5.8% |
| 4% | 20.8x | 4.8% |

## Turning it into a price range (hypothetical)

Normalized FCF per share is $2.00 and you require 9%.

| Scenario | g | Fair P/FCF | Fair price (× $2.00) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 0% | 11.1x | $22.22 |
| Base | 2% | 14.6x | $29.14 |
| Bull | 3% | 17.2x | $34.33 |

If the stock trades at $36.00 (18x normalized FCF), it is above the bull case: the market is assuming more than 3% perpetual growth, or a lower required return than yours. If it trades at $24.00 (12x), it only needs slightly positive growth to meet your return. The required return is your assumption, not a published rate; the growth rate should be lower than long-run nominal economic growth for most mature companies.

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| FCF relies on a working-capital release | Will reverse |
| Capex well below depreciation for years | Catch-up spending is coming |
| Dividend or buyback larger than normalized FCF | Funded with debt; see [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |
| Debt maturities ahead of FCF capacity | Refinancing risk; see [debt notes and covenant language](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) |
