Price to AFFO is the closest thing a REIT has to a cash earnings multiple, which makes it the natural number to compare across peers. The catch is that every REIT sets its own AFFO deductions. Two REITs can look two turns apart on reported P/AFFO and be level once their capex and leasing costs are put on the same footing.

This guide builds a fair P/AFFO peer table for TSX-listed REITs on **one capex basis**, then adds the columns that explain why multiples differ: AFFO payout, DRIP share and lease expiries. The general peer method is in [how to use peer tables without cherry-picking](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/); the rebuild of AFFO on actual spending is in [how to calculate price/AFFO in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-price-affo-in-cad-from-filings/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Compare REITs of the **same property type**, priced on the **same date** in the **same currency**, with AFFO per **diluted unit including exchangeable LP units**. Do not use each REIT's reported AFFO as given: **rebuild AFFO for every peer on a three-year average of actual sustaining capex and leasing costs**, then rank P/AFFO against the **peer median**. Explain the gaps with **AFFO payout on that basis**, **cash payout after the DRIP**, **lease expiries over the next two years**, **debt to gross book value** and **AFFO per unit growth**. A REIT that is cheap on reported P/AFFO only because it reserves less for capex is not cheap.

## Why one capex basis comes first

REALPAC's AFFO guidance leaves room for judgement on sustaining capex and leasing costs, and many REITs deduct a **normalized reserve** rather than actual spending. That is reasonable for smoothing lumpy spending, but reserves differ in how conservative they are. If you compare reported P/AFFO, you partly compare reserving policies.

## Alignment rules

| Rule | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Same property type (or similar mix) | Industrial, multi-family, retail, office and seniors housing have very different capex and leasing needs |
| AFFO rebuilt on 3-yr actual sustaining capex and leasing costs for every peer | Removes differences in reserving policy |
| Straight-line rent removed for everyone | Some REITs show it in FFO adjustments, some in AFFO |
| Diluted units incl. Class B LP units | Otherwise AFFO per unit is overstated |
| TTM for all, or forward for all | Mixing flatters growing REITs |
| Same price date and currency | Some TSX REITs report in US dollars |
| Canadian (REALPAC) and US peers kept apart or flagged | US REITs have no standard AFFO definition |

Read the cash payout column with care. A DRIP lowers the cash a REIT pays out, so Peer B's cash payout (74%) looks comfortable, but the units it issues through the DRIP dilute AFFO per unit later. The AFFO payout on your capex basis (98%) is the better test of whether the distribution is earned. ## Lease expiries: the column most often skipped

A heavy lease-expiry schedule cuts both ways. In a strong market it is a chance to re-lease space at higher rents; in a weak one it means vacancy, free rent and higher leasing costs. Either way, the next two years of leasing costs will probably run above the three-year average, so AFFO on your basis may still be generous for a REIT with 30% of its space rolling.

| Lease-expiry profile (2 yrs) | What to check in the MD&A | Adjustment to consider |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Under 15% of GLA | Weighted average lease term, renewal spreads | Usually none |
| 15% to 25% | Renewal spreads, leasing cost per square foot | Lift leasing costs toward recent highs |
| Over 25% | Top tenants rolling, market vacancy, free-rent periods | Model a bear case with lower occupancy |

## Canada-first issues

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| Class B LP units at many Canadian REITs | Include them in diluted units if AFFO adds back their distributions |
| REITs reporting in US dollars | Convert AFFO or price so both are in one currency |
| ACFO reported alongside AFFO | Use it as a cross-check; ACFO starts from operating cash flow |
| US-listed peers | No standard AFFO; rebuild from cash flow statements or keep separate |
| Units in a TFSA, RRSP or taxable account | Distribution tax character differs from dividends; see [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) |
