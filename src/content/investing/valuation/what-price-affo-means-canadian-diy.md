FFO tells you what a REIT earns from its buildings before revaluations. It does not tell you how much of that is left once the roofs, elevators and parking lots are maintained and the space is re-leased. **Adjusted funds from operations (AFFO)** subtracts those recurring costs, and **price to AFFO (P/AFFO)** is the REIT multiple closest to [P/FCF](/investing/valuation/what-p-fcf-means-canadian-diy/) for an ordinary company.

This post explains what P/AFFO tells you, how it differs from P/FFO, and why its biggest weakness is that AFFO has no single definition. The FFO side is in [what price/FFO means](/investing/valuation/what-price-ffo-means-canadian-diy/), and the full FFO-to-AFFO bridge in [valuation for REITs: NAV, FFO, AFFO explained](/investing/valuation/valuing-reits-nav-ffo-affo/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

P/AFFO = **unit price ÷ AFFO per unit**. Its inverse, **AFFO yield**, is the cash-like return on the unit price before any growth. AFFO starts from FFO and subtracts **sustaining capex**, **leasing costs** (tenant improvements and leasing commissions) and the **straight-line rent** adjustment, following REALPAC guidance. Because "sustaining" is management's estimate and many REITs use a **reserve** rather than actual spending, compare AFFO definitions before comparing P/AFFO. The **AFFO payout ratio** (distributions ÷ AFFO) is the standard check on whether a REIT's distribution is covered.

| Item | Value |

The retained part matters. A REIT that pays out nearly all its AFFO has to issue units or borrow to grow, which dilutes per-unit growth or adds leverage. A REIT that keeps some AFFO can fund part of its growth internally.

## P/AFFO vs P/FFO vs P/FCF

| Multiple | Numerator | Strength | Weakness |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| P/FFO | FFO per unit | REALPAC definition is widely followed | Before capex and leasing costs |
| **P/AFFO** | AFFO per unit | Closest to distributable cash | Capex reserve and items differ by REIT |
| P/ACFO | Adjusted cash flow from operations per unit (REALPAC) | Starts from IFRS operating cash flow | Less widely quoted; working-capital swings |
| P/FCF (non-REITs) | Free cash flow per share | Cash after capex | Rarely used for REITs: IFRS investing cash flow mixes sustaining capex with acquisitions and development |

## What goes into AFFO

| Deduction from FFO | Why | Variation between REITs |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Sustaining (maintenance) capex | Spending to keep buildings competitive | Actual vs a reserve; what counts as "value-add" |
| Tenant improvements and leasing commissions | Cost of re-leasing space | Actual vs normalized; lumpy in big expiry years |
| Straight-line rent adjustment | IFRS spreads rent increases evenly; cash rent differs | Usually consistent |
| Other items (some REITs) | Amortization of financing costs, unit-based compensation | Not standard |

Office and older retail properties need more capex and leasing spending per dollar of FFO than modern industrial or multi-family buildings. That is why P/AFFO, not P/FFO, is the better multiple when comparing REITs with different capex intensity.

## The AFFO payout ratio

| AFFO payout (hypothetical ranges) | Usual read |
|:--|:--|
| Well below 80% | Room to grow the distribution or fund growth internally |
| 80% to 95% | Typical for Canadian REITs; limited cushion |
| Near or above 100% | Distribution not covered by AFFO; funded by debt, asset sales or unit issuance |

These bands are a general rule of thumb, not a standard; check them against the REIT's capex basis. [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) covers distribution safety and tax character, and [payout ratio explained](/investing/dividends/payout-ratio-explained-canadian-companies/) the general idea.

## When P/AFFO is the right tool

| Use it for | Be careful with |
|:--|:--|
| Comparing REITs with different capex needs | REITs that use very different capex reserves |
| Judging distribution coverage | AFFO definitions that change year to year |
| Pricing cash-like earnings with the P/NAV view alongside | Development-heavy REITs, where AFFO lags the investment |
