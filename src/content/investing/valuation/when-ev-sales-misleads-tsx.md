EV/Sales fixes the biggest flaw in P/S by bringing debt and cash into the picture. That does not make it safe. The places where EV/Sales goes wrong on the TSX are different from the P/S traps: cash balances that are about to be spent, convertibles, gross margins hidden behind the same multiple, and takeover multiples treated as if they were market prices.

The traps EV/Sales shares with P/S (thin net margins, commodity pass-through revenue, acquired revenue, cyclical peaks) are covered in [when P/S misleads on TSX stocks](/investing/valuation/when-ps-misleads-tsx/). The bridge itself is in [what EV/Sales means](/investing/valuation/what-ev-sales-means-canadian-diy/) and the worksheet in [how to calculate EV/Sales in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-sales-in-cad-from-filings/). This post covers the rest. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

EV/Sales misleads when the **balance sheet is about to change** (a growth company burning its cash, a pending raise or acquisition), when **convertibles** are missed or double counted, when companies with very **different gross margins** are compared on the same multiple, and when **takeover comparables** are used as if they were trading multiples. Check cash runway, gross margin, the convertible notes and the source of any comparable before trusting the number.

## Trap 1: net cash that will not be there next year

For an unprofitable growth company, today's cash pile lowers EV and makes EV/Sales look modest. But the cash is going to be spent reaching breakeven.

| Hypothetical, share price unchanged | Today | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Market cap | C$800M | C$800M | C$800M |
| Net cash | C$300M | C$200M | C$100M |
| Enterprise value | C$500M | C$600M | C$700M |
| Revenue (flat, for clarity) | C$250M | C$250M | C$250M |
| **EV/Sales** | **2.0x** | **2.4x** | **2.8x** |

The fix is to subtract only the cash expected to be left at breakeven, or to run EV/Sales on forward revenue alongside the burn. Read the liquidity section of the MD&A and the going-concern note; see [how to read auditor opinions and going-concern language](/investing/analyze/read-auditor-opinions-going-concern-language/).

## Trap 2: the same multiple, very different gross margins

Revenue at a 75% gross margin is worth far more than revenue at a 30% gross margin. A quick way to see it is **EV ÷ gross profit** = EV/Sales ÷ gross margin.

| Hypothetical | Company X | Company Y |
|:--|--:|--:|
| EV/Sales | 3.0x | 3.0x |
| Gross margin | 75% | 30% |
| EV ÷ gross profit | 4.0x | 10.0x |

On EV/Sales they look identical. On gross profit, Company Y is two and a half times as expensive. When a peer set mixes software with hardware, services or distribution, add the gross margin column; see [how to analyze gross margin trends](/investing/analyze/analyze-gross-margin-trends/).

## Trap 3: convertibles missed or double counted

Convertible debentures are a common TSX and TSX Venture funding tool. Data screens handle them inconsistently: some leave them out of debt, some count both the debt and the diluted shares. Either error can move EV by 10% or more for a small issuer. Rebuild the treatment by moneyness, as in the worksheet, and check for conversions or redemptions announced after the balance-sheet date.

## Trap 4: takeover comps are not trading multiples

Deal values are usually quoted on an EV basis, so EV/Sales is the natural way to compare a stock with past acquisitions. But a deal multiple includes things a trading multiple does not.

| What is in a takeover EV/Sales | Why it inflates the comparison |
|:--|:--|
| Control premium | Buyers often pay well above the undisturbed price |
| Buyer-specific synergies | Cost savings the buyer can achieve and you cannot |
| Peak-cycle timing | Deals cluster when sentiment and prices are high |
| Different revenue date | Deal multiples often use the target's forward or last-reported revenue |

Keep takeover multiples in a separate column and never use them as the centre of a fair-value range. If the thesis is "someone will buy it", that is a catalyst, not a valuation.

## Trap 5: debt that is not worth face value

When a company's bonds or debentures trade far below par, EV built from face value overstates what the business is worth to its owners, and the equity starts behaving like an option. EV/Sales in that situation says more about credit risk than about value. See [how to read debt notes and covenant language](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) and [how to analyze interest coverage for levered TSX names](/investing/analyze/analyze-interest-coverage-levered-tsx/).

## 60-second check

| Question | If the answer is yes |
|:--|:--|
| Is the company losing money and holding net cash? | Subtract only cash left after expected burn |
| Has it raised equity, issued debt or closed a deal since the balance sheet? | Update EV on both sides |
| Are there convertible debentures? | One treatment, by moneyness |
| Do peers have gross margins more than 15 points apart? | Compare EV ÷ gross profit as well |
| Is any comparable a takeover price? | Move it to a separate column |
| Do the bonds trade well below par? | Treat EV/Sales as a credit question |
