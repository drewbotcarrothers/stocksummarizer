This is the one-page dividend-yield checklist. It assumes you know [what dividend yield means as a valuation multiple](/investing/valuation/what-dividend-yield-means-canadian-diy/), can [calculate it in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-dividend-yield-in-cad-from-filings/), know [when it misleads](/investing/valuation/when-dividend-yield-misleads-tsx/) and have a [peer comparison](/investing/valuation/dividend-yield-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). The checklist turns those into a price range with two simple methods: a **yield band** from the company's own history and a **yield plus growth** method from a required return. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Pass a **safety gate** first (payout, FCF coverage, balance sheet, dividend history), because a yield-based value is worthless if the dividend is cut. Then build a price range two ways: **indicated dividend ÷ the yield band** (low, average and high yields from a period the business still resembles) and **next year's dividend ÷ (required return − dividend growth)**. Where the two ranges overlap is your working range. Compare it with today's price and write down what has to stay true.

## The dividend-yield checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Safety: payout | Payout on the sector's basis (EPS, FCF, AFFO, DCF) sustainable? | Within the company's own target and peer norms |
| 2 | Safety: FCF cover | Dividends ÷ free cash flow over three years? | Below 100%, with room; see [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |
| 3 | Safety: balance sheet | Leverage and maturities manageable? | No refinancing wall that competes with the dividend |
| 4 | Safety: history | Cuts, suspensions or DRIP-funded payouts? | None recently, or explained |
| 5 | Right yield | Indicated regular dividend, same currency? | Built per the calculation post |
| 6 | Yield band | Own-history low, average, high from a comparable period | Period written down |
| 7 | Peer check | Relative yield vs the peer median | Gap explained |
| 8 | Yield + growth | Required return and sustainable dividend growth | Bear / base / bull written |
| 9 | Price range | Both methods compared with today's price | Overlap identified |

If any of steps 1–4 fails, stop. The [dividend trap checklist](/investing/dividends/avoid-dividend-traps-tsx/) is the place to go next, not a valuation.

## Method 1: the yield band (hypothetical)

Indicated annual dividend: **$2.60**. Share price today: **$50.00** (yield 5.2%). Over a comparable eight-year period, the yield ranged from 3.6% to 5.6% and averaged 4.4%.

| Scenario | Yield used | Price = $2.60 ÷ yield |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Bear (market stays pessimistic) | 5.6% (top of band) | $46.43 |
| Base (back to average) | 4.4% | $59.09 |
| Bull (back to the low-yield end) | 3.6% | $72.22 |

A band is only valid if the business, payout policy and interest-rate backdrop have not changed much. If rates sat much lower during the history than they do now, shift the band up or shorten the window.

## Method 2: yield plus growth (hypothetical)

For a steady payer, required return ≈ dividend yield + dividend growth. Rearranged, the **fair yield** is the required return minus growth, and the price is next year's dividend divided by that fair yield.

| Scenario | Required return (r) | Dividend growth (g) | Fair yield (r − g) | Next dividend | Price |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 9.5% | 3.0% | 6.5% | $2.68 | $41.20 |
| Base | 9.0% | 4.0% | 5.0% | $2.70 | $54.08 |
| Bull | 8.5% | 5.0% | 3.5% | $2.73 | $78.00 |

Next-year dividends are $2.60 grown at each scenario's rate (shown rounded). The bull case is very sensitive: a fair yield of 3.5% leaves little room for error, which is why it should never be the base case. Growth must be supported by earnings or cash-flow growth, not just a history of raises.

## Reading the result

| Outcome | What it means | Next step |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Price below both bear cases | The market expects a cut or much slower growth | Re-run the safety gate harder; find what you are missing |
| Price between bear and base | Possibly undervalued if the safety gate holds | Write down the one or two facts that must stay true |
| Price near or above both base cases | Yield offers little valuation support | Use other multiples; see [triangulating multiples, DCF and peer checks](/investing/valuation/triangulating-multiples-dcf-peer-checks/) |
| The two methods disagree a lot | The growth or required-return assumption is doing the work | Narrow the inputs before trusting either |

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| Dividend funded by new debt or equity | The yield is not paid from the business |
| Payout above the company's own target for several years | A reset is common when targets are breached |
| Yield band built on a different business | Acquisitions, spin-offs or a new payout policy break the history |
| Large special or variable component | Only the base dividend belongs in the yield |
| Return of capital is a big part of the distribution | Some of the "yield" is your own money; see [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) |
