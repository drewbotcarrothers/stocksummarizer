Most Canadian investors meet **dividend yield** as an income number: how much cash a stock pays each year as a percentage of its price. It is also a **valuation multiple**, the upside-down version of price-to-dividend, and it can be read the same way as P/E: against the company's own history, against peers, and next to growth.

This post covers yield as a valuation tool. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical. For why yield alone is a poor screen and how to judge whether a dividend is safe, see [why yield alone is a terrible screen](/investing/dividends/why-yield-alone-is-a-terrible-screen/), [payout ratio explained for Canadian companies](/investing/dividends/payout-ratio-explained-canadian-companies/) and [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/). Those posts are not repeated here.

## Direct answer

Dividend yield = **annual dividend per share ÷ share price**. As a valuation measure it is the inverse of price-to-dividend: a 4% yield means you pay 25 times the annual dividend. A yield **above the company's own history or its peers** can mean the shares are cheap relative to the dividend, **if** the dividend is safe and growing; often it means the market expects a cut. Add dividend growth to yield for a rough expected return (yield + growth), and always check which yield you are looking at: **trailing**, **indicated** or **forward**.

## Yield as an inverse multiple

| Dividend yield | Price-to-dividend | Read as |
|--:|--:|:--|
| 2% | 50x | Paying 50 years of today's dividend |
| 4% | 25x | Paying 25 years |
| 5% | 20x | Paying 20 years |
| 8% | 12.5x | Paying 12.5 years, if the dividend lasts |

Like a low P/E, a high yield is a price signal, not a quality signal. The [myth that a high dividend yield means safe income](/investing/frameworks/myth-high-dividend-yield-safe-income/) is the dividend version of the [myth that a low P/E is always cheap](/investing/frameworks/myth-low-pe-always-cheap/).

## Yield plus growth: a rough expected return

For a company with a stable payout policy, total return over long periods is roughly the dividend yield plus the dividend growth rate (the constant-growth logic in Gordon's model). That puts high-yield and fast-growing dividend stocks on one scale.

## Yield against the company's own history

A company with a stable business and payout policy tends to trade in a yield band. When the yield moves well above its average, the price has fallen relative to the dividend.

| Hypothetical | Value |
|:--|--:|
| Indicated annual dividend | $2.60 |

| Current yield | 5.2% |
| Eight-year average yield | 4.4% |
| Price at the average yield ($2.60 ÷ 4.4%) | $59.09 |

The implied $59.09 only means something if the dividend is as safe and as fast-growing as it was during those eight years. Interest rates matter too: when bond yields rise, dividend payers' yields tend to rise with them, so a band from a low-rate period may not apply.

## Which yield are you looking at?

| Hypothetical, price $50.00 | Dividends counted | Annual amount | Yield |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|
| Trailing | Last four quarterly payments: $0.50, $0.50, $0.50, $0.53 | $2.03 | 4.06% |
| Indicated | Latest quarterly × 4: $0.53 × 4 | $2.12 | 4.24% |
| Forward | Next four expected: $0.53, $0.53, $0.56, $0.56 | $2.18 | 4.36% |

Data providers use different conventions, which is why two websites can show different yields for the same stock on the same day. The worksheet is in [how to calculate dividend yield in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-dividend-yield-in-cad-from-filings/).

## Canada-first notes

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| Special dividends | Exclude from the yield; show separately |
| Dividends declared in US dollars by TSX-listed companies | Convert before dividing by the Canadian-dollar price |
| Monthly REIT distributions | Comparable as a yield, but taxed differently; see [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) |
| Eligible dividends and the dividend tax credit | After-tax yield in a taxable account is higher than the headline for many investors; see [eligible vs ordinary dividends](/investing/dividends/eligible-vs-ordinary-dividends/) |
| US dividend payers | Withholding tax can reduce the yield you keep, depending on the account; see [US dividend withholding tax by account](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/) |
| DRIPs | Reinvesting does not change the yield, but discounted DRIPs can dilute other holders |
