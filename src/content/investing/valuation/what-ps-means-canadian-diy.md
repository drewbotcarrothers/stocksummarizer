**Price-to-sales (P/S)** compares a company's market value with its revenue. Revenue is harder to manipulate than earnings and is positive even when a company is losing money, so P/S is often the first multiple people reach for with young growth companies or cyclicals at the bottom of a cycle. But revenue is not profit, and a dollar of sales is worth very different amounts in different businesses.

This post is educational; figures are hypothetical. For the earnings-based multiples it is often paired with, see [what P/E means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/what-pe-means-canadian-diy/) and [what EV/EBITDA means](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-for-canadian-diy-investors/).

## Direct answer

P/S = **market capitalization ÷ trailing twelve-month revenue** (or share price ÷ revenue per share). It tells you how many dollars investors pay for each dollar of sales. The key link is **P/S = P/E × net profit margin**: a low P/S often just means low margins, and a high P/S means investors expect high margins now or later. Use P/S to compare companies with **similar business models and margins**, or when earnings are temporarily missing. Because it uses market cap, it **ignores debt**; for levered companies, EV/Sales is the fairer version.

## The parts of P/S

| Part | What it is | Where it comes from |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Market capitalization | Share price × shares outstanding | Market quote and latest share count |
| Revenue (TTM) | Sales over the last four quarters | Income statements (IFRS 15 revenue) |
| P/S | Market cap ÷ revenue | Your calculation; see [how to calculate P/S in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-ps-in-cad-from-filings/) |
| Net margin | Net income ÷ revenue | Income statement |

## Reading P/S through margins (hypothetical)

| Business | Revenue | Net margin | Net income | Market cap | P/E | P/S |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Grocer-style retailer | $10,000M | 2% | $200M | $4,000M | 20x | 0.4x |
| Software company | $1,000M | 25% | $250M | $5,000M | 20x | 5.0x |

Both trade at 20 times earnings, yet one is "cheap" on P/S and the other "expensive". P/S only says something when you also know the margin, or the margin it can reach. See [how to analyze gross margin trends](/investing/analyze/analyze-gross-margin-trends/).

## Where P/S fits on the TSX

| Sector | P/S usefulness | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Software and tech (early stage) | High | Earnings often negative or reinvested |
| Retail and consumer | Moderate, within the group | Thin, comparable margins |
| Industrials | Moderate | Useful at cyclical troughs |
| Energy and miners | Low | Revenue swings with commodity prices |
| Banks and insurers | Not meaningful | "Revenue" is not comparable; use P/B and ROE |
| REITs and utilities | Low | Use price/AFFO, EV/EBITDA or rate base |
