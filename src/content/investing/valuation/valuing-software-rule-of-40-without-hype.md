The Rule of 40 is the most quoted shortcut in software investing: add revenue growth and profit margin, and a healthy company scores 40 or more. It is useful because it forces a trade-off into one number. A company growing 35% can afford thin margins; a company growing 10% needs strong ones. It is also easy to game, because "margin" can mean almost anything.

This post shows how to use the Rule of 40 as context for software valuation on the TSX without letting it become a hype machine. It does not repeat the method for [valuing growth stocks when earnings are negative](/investing/valuation/value-growth-stocks-negative-earnings/); it sits alongside it. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

**Rule of 40 = revenue growth % + profit margin %**, usually free cash flow (FCF) margin or adjusted EBITDA margin. Use it as a **quality screen, not a valuation**. Make it honest by using **organic growth**, an **FCF margin after stock-based compensation (SBC)**, and a **multi-year view**. Then compare it with EV/Sales across peers: a higher score can justify a higher multiple, but a company priced well above peers with a similar or lower honest score is pricing in hope.

SBC is added back in operating cash flow because it does not use cash, but it is paid for with dilution. Shareholders bear that cost through a growing share count. If you subtract it from FCF margin (or use FCF per diluted share), companies that pay heavily in stock lose much of their apparent advantage.

## Which inputs to use

| Input | Headline version | Honest version | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Growth | Reported revenue growth | Organic growth, constant currency | Acquisitions buy growth; see [serial software acquirers](/investing/analyze/analyze-serial-software-acquirers-tsx/) |
| Margin | Adjusted EBITDA margin | FCF margin after SBC and capitalized software | Adjusted EBITDA often excludes SBC and restructuring |
| Period | Latest quarter, annualized | Trailing twelve months and three-year average | Single quarters are noisy |
| Revenue type | All revenue | Recurring share disclosed | Services and hardware are less valuable |

## Context that the score misses

| Signal | Why it matters | Where to find it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Net revenue retention | Above 100% means existing customers grow; growth is cheaper | MD&A or investor presentations, when disclosed |
| Gross margin | High gross margin leaves room for future operating margin | Income statement |
| Customer concentration | A few large customers can swing growth | Segment and risk notes; see [customer concentration](/investing/analyze/analyze-customer-concentration-risk/) |
| Capitalized development costs | Moves spending out of the income statement and sometimes out of FCF | Intangibles note, cash flow investing section |
| Deferred revenue trend | Leading indicator of billings | Balance sheet |
| Share count growth | The real cost of SBC | Diluted share count over five years |

## Where the Rule of 40 fails

- **Early-stage companies.** Very high growth with deep losses can score well while burning cash; check runway first.
- **Mature, low-growth software.** A 5% grower with a 35% FCF margin scores 40 but may be shrinking in real terms; a cash-flow multiple such as [P/FCF](/investing/valuation/what-p-fcf-means-canadian-diy/) or [EV/FCF](/investing/valuation/what-ev-fcf-means-canadian-diy/) is more useful.
- **Acquisition-driven compounders.** Reported growth includes purchases; judge them on organic growth and return on acquired capital.
- **Cyclical tech hardware.** Margins swing with the cycle, so one year's score says little.

## A one-page software check

| Step | Question | Pass if |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Organic growth, three-year average? | Stable or rising |
| 2 | FCF margin after SBC? | Positive or clearly improving |
| 3 | Honest Rule of 40? | Comparable to peers you are measuring against |
| 4 | EV/Sales vs score? | Not far above the peer trend |
| 5 | Dilution? | Share count growth below about 2% a year (your own threshold) |
| 6 | Reverse check | The margin and growth implied by today's price look achievable |
