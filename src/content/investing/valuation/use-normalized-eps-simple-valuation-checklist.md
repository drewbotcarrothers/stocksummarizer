Normalized EPS is only useful if it ends in a decision you can defend: a price range set before you look at the quote, with the downside stress-tested. A checklist gets you there in order. First decide whether the company needs normalizing at all, then build the number two ways, make sure the balance sheet survives a trough, and only then apply a multiple.

This page pulls together the worksheet in [how to calculate normalized EPS in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-normalized-eps-in-cad-from-filings/), the traps in [when normalized EPS misleads](/investing/valuation/when-normalized-eps-misleads-tsx/) and the peer method in [normalized EPS vs peers](/investing/valuation/normalized-eps-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). It continues the hypothetical forest products company from the worksheet: normalized EPS of $1.27, a $14.00 share price, 120M diluted shares and C$700M of net debt. It is a framework for your own research, not a recommendation.

## Direct answer

(1) **Gate:** normalize only if earnings are cyclical or distorted by one-offs; otherwise use ordinary or forward P/E. (2) **Two methods:** one-off adjusted and mid-cycle (average or margin × revenue), on today's diluted shares. (3) **Reconcile:** if they differ by more than about 20%, explain why. (4) **Trough test:** leverage, interest cover and the dividend must survive a trough year. (5) **Fair band:** from the company's own history of P/E on normalized EPS, peers, and a 1 ÷ r cross-check. (6) **Price range:** bear, base and bull normalized EPS × the band.

## The checklist

| Step | Question | Pass if |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. Gate | Are earnings cyclical, or distorted by large one-offs? | Yes (otherwise use [P/E](/investing/valuation/use-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/) or [forward P/E](/investing/valuation/use-forward-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/)) |
| 2a. Method one | One-offs removed after tax, recurring "one-time" items kept | Done |
| 2b. Method two | Mid-cycle average or margin × revenue over a full cycle | Done |
| 2c. Shares, tax, interest | Today's diluted shares, statutory tax, interest on today's net debt | Yes |
| 3. Reconcile | Methods within about 20%, or the gap explained? | Yes |
| 4a. Trough leverage | Net debt ÷ trough EBITDA manageable (for example under 3x) | Yes |
| 4b. Trough dividend | Dividend covered by trough cash flow, or cut priced in? | Understood |
| 5. Fair band | History, peers and 1 ÷ r, written before checking the price | Done |
| 6. Range | Bear, base and bull normalized EPS × band | Done |
| 7. Cross-check | EV/EBITDA at mid-cycle and P/B or P/TBV consistent? | No contradiction, or explained |

## Step 3: reconcile the methods (hypothetical)

| Method | EPS | Gap vs base |
|:--|--:|--:|
| One-offs removed (current, soft year) | $0.93 | Not a normalization of the cycle; for reference |
| **Mid-cycle margin × revenue** | **$1.27** | Base |
| 8-yr average, today's shares | $1.38 | +9% |

The two cycle methods are within 9%. The one-off adjusted figure is lower because the latest year sits below mid-cycle (margin 7.9% against a 9.0% average), which is the point of normalizing.

## Step 4: the trough test (hypothetical)

| Measure | Mid-cycle | Trough (Y5 margin of 3% on Y5 revenue) |
|:--|--:|--:|
| EBIT | C$243M | C$66M |
| EBITDA (D&A of about C$224M) | C$467M | C$290M |
| Net debt ÷ EBITDA | 1.5x | 2.4x |
| EBIT ÷ interest (C$35M) | 6.9x | 1.9x |
| EPS | $1.27 | $0.19 |
| Dividend of $0.40 per share ÷ EPS | 31% | Over 200% |

The balance sheet passes, with thin interest cover at the bottom. The dividend does not pass on earnings in a trough; whether it would be cut depends on cash flow and management's policy, so treat a cut as possible in the bear case. [How to stress-test a stock thesis with a recession scenario](/investing/analyze/stress-test-stock-thesis-recession-scenario/) extends this test.

## Step 5: setting the fair band

| Input | Hypothetical result |
|:--|:--|
| The company's own P/E on normalized EPS over a full cycle | 9x to 13x |
| Peer median P/E on normalized EPS (same rules) | 11.25x |
| 1 ÷ r cross-check (below) | 10x to 12.5x |
| **Band used** | **9x (bear) to 12x (bull), 10.5x base** |

**The 1 ÷ r cross-check.** If a company reinvests its retained earnings at roughly its cost of capital, growth adds little value and a fair P/E on normalized earnings is about 1 ÷ r. At r = 10%, that is 10x; at 9%, about 11.1x; at 8%, 12.5x. A mature cyclical earning about its cost of capital should not usually trade far above this. The idea links to [what earnings yield means](/investing/valuation/what-earnings-yield-means-canadian-diy/): a normalized earnings yield of 1 ÷ P/E should at least match your required return.

## Step 6: worked example (hypothetical)

| Scenario | Mid-cycle EBIT margin | Normalized EPS | P/E | Price per share |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear (weaker pricing, higher costs) | 8.0% | $1.10 | 9.0x | $9.90 |
| Base | 9.0% | $1.27 | 10.5x | $13.34 |
| Bull (better mix after mill upgrades) | 9.8% | $1.40 | 12.0x | $16.80 |

## Cross-checks

| Check | Hypothetical result | Read |
|:--|:--|:--|
| EV ÷ mid-cycle EBITDA at the base price | (C$1,601M + C$700M) ÷ C$467M ≈ 4.9x | Typical of a commodity producer; compare with [EV/EBITDA vs peers](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/) |
| P/E on trailing EPS | 17.5x | Looks expensive only because the year is soft |
| Normalized earnings yield at today's price | 9.1% | Close to a 9% to 10% required return |
| Where in the cycle? | Current EPS about 0.73 × normalized | Below mid-cycle; trailing P/E overstates the valuation |

## Record it

Write down the window, margin, share count and band, and update them once a year or after a structural change (a mill closure, an acquisition, a new commodity price range). The [bull, bear, watch decision card](/investing/frameworks/bull-bear-watch-decision-card/) is a compact format.
