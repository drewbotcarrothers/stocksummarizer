This is the one-page FCF-yield checklist. The P/FCF checklist already turns a fair multiple, (1 + g) ÷ (r − g), into a price range; see [how to use P/FCF in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-p-fcf-simple-valuation-checklist/), and its unlevered twin in [how to use EV/FCF in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-ev-fcf-simple-valuation-checklist/). This version uses the same arithmetic from the yield side and adds the two questions the yield form is best at: **what growth is the market pricing in**, and **can the company fund its dividend and buybacks from owner FCF**. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Choose one basis (yield on price or on EV) and use **normalized owner FCF per share** (after stock-based compensation, latest diluted shares). Compare today's yield with a **required yield** = (r − g) ÷ (1 + g) for bear, base and bull cases. Turn it around to find the **growth the price implies**: g = (r − yield) ÷ (1 + yield). Then check that dividends plus buybacks fit inside owner FCF. A yield that clears the hurdle, implies modest growth and funds its cash returns is the profile to look for.

## The FCF-yield checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Steady, positive FCF; not a bank, insurer or REIT? | Yes |
| 2 | Basis | Yield on price or on EV, chosen and labelled? | One basis throughout |
| 3 | Normalize | Three-year average or mid-cycle FCF; capex near maintenance? | Not a working-capital or under-investment year |
| 4 | Owner FCF | SBC subtracted, latest diluted shares? | Built per the calculation post |
| 5 | Per-share trend | FCF per share growing with total FCF? | Share drift explained |
| 6 | Hurdle | Current yield vs required yield, bear / base / bull | Current at or above base, or explained |
| 7 | Implied growth | Growth the price implies at your required return | Below what the business has delivered per share |
| 8 | Cash returns | Dividend + buybacks ÷ owner FCF | Below 100% over three years |
| 9 | Debt check | Yield on EV and refinancing cost reviewed | No maturity wall that reprices the yield |

## Step 6: the hurdle test (hypothetical)

Normalized owner FCF per share: **$2.15**. Share price: **$40.00**. Current owner FCF yield: **5.4%**.

| Scenario | Required return (r) | Long-run growth (g) | Required yield (r − g) ÷ (1 + g) | Price at required yield |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 9.0% | 2.0% | 6.9% | $31.33 |
| Base | 8.5% | 3.0% | 5.3% | $40.26 |
| Bull | 8.0% | 4.0% | 3.8% | $55.90 |

Prices match the P/FCF checklist method because a required yield is just the inverse of a fair P/FCF. The yield form makes the comparison with today's price a single subtraction.

## Step 7: what growth is priced in?

Solve the same formula for g: implied growth = (r − current yield) ÷ (1 + current yield).

| Your required return | Implied long-run FCF-per-share growth at a 5.4% yield |
|--:|--:|
| 8.0% | 2.5% |
| 8.5% | 3.0% |
| 9.0% | 3.4% |

If FCF per share has grown faster than this over a full cycle, and the reasons still hold, the price asks for less than the business has delivered. If the per-share trend is flat because of dilution, even 2.5% may be too much. See [how to calculate FCF yield in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-fcf-yield-in-cad-from-filings/) for the per-share trend.

## Step 8: do cash returns fit? (hypothetical)

| Per share | Amount | Share of owner FCF |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Owner FCF (after SBC) | $2.15 | 100% |
| Dividend | $1.20 | 56% |
| Buybacks (net of shares issued) | $0.50 | 23% |
| Left for debt reduction or growth | $0.45 | 21% |

Dividend yield here is 3.0% and net buyback yield 1.25%, so shareholder yield is 4.25% against a 5.4% owner FCF yield. Cash returns use 79% of owner FCF, leaving room. If dividend plus buybacks exceed 100% for several years, the difference is coming from debt or asset sales. The dividend-only version of this check is [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/).

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| Yield on price far above yield on EV, with near-term maturities | Refinancing will lower the yield; see [when FCF yield misleads](/investing/valuation/when-fcf-yield-misleads-tsx/) |
| SBC large and rising; buybacks only offset it | Owner yield is lower than any headline |
| Capex below depreciation for years | FCF is borrowed from the future |
| Implied growth above the per-share track record | Price assumes improvement that has not happened |
| Cash returns above owner FCF for several years | Payouts funded by the balance sheet |
