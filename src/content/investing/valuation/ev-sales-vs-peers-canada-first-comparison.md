EV/Sales peer tables are where TSX growth stocks get judged, often by a screen that mixes companies with net cash and companies with heavy debt, software margins and hardware margins, and trading prices with takeover prices. The multiple only works when the peer set is rebuilt on one basis and read next to growth and margins.

The general peer steps (true peers, calendarizing fiscal years, median not mean) are in [P/E vs peers: a Canada-first comparison guide](/investing/valuation/pe-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/) and [how to use peer tables without cherry-picking comps](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/). The margin-based P/S version is [P/S vs peers](/investing/valuation/ps-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/); the EV build is in [how to calculate EV/EBITDA in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-ebitda-in-cad-from-filings/) and the concept in [what EV/Sales means](/investing/valuation/what-ev-sales-means-canadian-diy/). This post adds what EV/Sales peer sets need. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Rebuild EV for every peer on the **same balance-sheet date and claim list** (debt, leases, convertibles, preferreds, NCI, cash, equity investments) and revenue on the **same TTM window and gross-vs-net basis**. Compare to the **median**, then read every gap through three columns: **revenue growth**, **gross margin** (or EV ÷ gross profit) and **net debt or net cash as a share of EV**. Keep takeover multiples out of the median. A premium that lines up with faster growth and higher gross margins is usually explained.

## Alignment rules for EV/Sales peer tables

| Rule | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Same claim list in every EV | A screen may skip leases, NCI or convertibles for some names |
| Same balance-sheet date | Growth companies raise and burn cash quarter to quarter |
| Same revenue basis (gross vs net, IFRS 15 principal vs agent) | Agents and principals report very different revenue for the same activity |
| Same TTM window, calendarized | Fiscal years differ across TSX and US peers |
| Cash after expected burn for loss-makers | Otherwise cash-rich burners look cheap |
| Takeover multiples in their own column | They include a control premium |
| One currency per row | USD reporters converted at closing and average rates |

## Peer table (hypothetical)

| Company | EV/Sales | Revenue growth | Gross margin | EBITDA margin | EV ÷ gross profit | Net debt (net cash) ÷ EV |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Your company | 2.6x | 18% | 62% | 8% | 4.2x | 10% |
| Peer A | 1.4x | 6% | 45% | 12% | 3.1x | 25% |
| Peer B | 2.2x | 14% | 58% | 10% | 3.8x | (5%) |
| Peer C | 3.5x | 25% | 70% | −4% | 5.0x | (20%) |
| Peer D | 4.8x | 30% | 75% | 2% | 6.4x | (15%) |
| **Peer median (A–D)** | **2.85x** | 19.5% | 64% | 6% | 4.4x | |

Your company sits just below the median EV/Sales with growth and gross margin a little below the peer median too. On EV ÷ gross profit it is also slightly below. Nothing here says "mispriced"; it says "priced roughly in line with what it delivers". Peer D's 4.8x is the outlier, and its 30% growth and 75% gross margin explain much of it. Peer A's low multiple comes with the slowest growth and the most debt.

## Reading the gap through a mature margin

Because EV/Sales = EV/EBITDA × EBITDA margin, you can ask what each peer's multiple implies at maturity. Assume a hypothetical 12x EV/EBITDA for a mature company in this group.

| Company | EV/Sales | Implied mature EBITDA margin at 12x | Today's EBITDA margin | Gap to close |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Your company | 2.6x | 22% | 8% | 14 points |
| Peer A | 1.4x | 12% | 12% | 0 points |
| Peer C | 3.5x | 29% | −4% | 33 points |
| Peer D | 4.8x | 40% | 2% | 38 points |

Peer A is priced for no improvement at all. Peers C and D need large margin expansion to justify today's price. That is a sharper question than "which one is cheapest on EV/Sales".

## Canada-first issues for EV/Sales peer sets

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| Few TSX peers in software and tech | Add US peers, flagged; note that US GAAP leases and stock-based compensation norms differ; see [comparing a TSX stock to its US peer](/investing/valuation/compare-tsx-stock-to-us-peer/) |
| USD reporters on the TSX | Same currency for every column in a row |
| TSX Venture names with frequent financings | Check news releases for raises after the last balance sheet |
| Convertible debentures | One treatment per company, by moneyness |
| Recent takeover of a Canadian peer | Show the deal multiple separately and note the premium |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Peers chosen | Same business model and revenue type, reasons written |
| EV rebuilt | Same claim list and date for all |
| Revenue aligned | Same basis, TTM window and currency |
| Context columns added | Growth, gross margin, net debt or cash share of EV |
| Median used | Takeover multiples excluded |
| Gap explained | One sentence covering growth, margin and balance sheet |
