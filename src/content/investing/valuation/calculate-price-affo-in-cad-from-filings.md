Every Canadian REIT that reports AFFO shows a reconciliation from FFO in its MD&A. The headline number depends heavily on one line: how much the REIT deducts for sustaining capex and leasing costs, and whether that is the actual spending or a **reserve** management chose. This worksheet builds P/AFFO from the filings and then rebuilds it on your own capex basis.

The concept is in [what price/AFFO means](/investing/valuation/what-price-affo-means-canadian-diy/); the FFO starting point in [how to calculate price/FFO in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-price-ffo-in-cad-from-filings/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical and continue the REIT from the FFO worksheet (TTM FFO of C$144M, 120M diluted units including Class B LP units, unit price $15.00).

## Direct answer

(1) Start from **REALPAC FFO** for the trailing twelve months. (2) Subtract the **straight-line rent** adjustment, **sustaining capex** and **leasing costs** (tenant improvements and leasing commissions) as the REIT reports them. (3) Divide by the same **diluted units** used for FFO. (4) Divide the unit price by AFFO per unit. (5) Then **rebuild** AFFO using a three-year average of **actual** sustaining capex and leasing costs from the capex note or MD&A, and compare the two P/AFFOs. The gap tells you how conservative the REIT's reserve is.

## Step 5: rebuild on actual spending

Many REITs deduct a normalized amount (a reserve) rather than actual sustaining capex, and explain how the reserve is set in the MD&A. A reserve smooths lumpy spending, which is reasonable, but it can drift below what the buildings really need. Check it against the actual spending disclosed in the MD&A or capital expenditure tables.

A one-turn difference in P/AFFO and a five-point jump in payout ratio, from one line. Do the same for leasing costs if the REIT normalizes them and a large lease-expiry year is coming. Separating "sustaining" from "value-add" or "revenue-enhancing" capex is a judgement; read the MD&A description and treat recurring "value-add" spending on the existing portfolio with suspicion.

## Cross-check with ACFO and operating cash flow

| Measure (TTM, hypothetical) | Per unit | Use |
|:--|--:|:--|
| AFFO (REIT's basis) | $0.97 | Headline |
| AFFO (your basis) | $0.91 | Your valuation input |
| ACFO (REALPAC, if reported) | $0.93 | Cash-flow-statement-based cross-check |
| Distribution | $0.84 | Should sit below AFFO on your basis |

ACFO starts from IFRS cash flow from operations rather than from net income. If ACFO is consistently well below AFFO, look for working capital, interest timing or items AFFO leaves out. [How to read cash flow from operations vs net income](/investing/analyze/read-cfo-vs-net-income/) explains the general logic.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|:--|:--|
| Comparing P/AFFO across REITs with different capex methods | Rebuild on actual spending for each |
| Using a different unit count than for FFO | Same diluted count, incl. Class B LP units |
| Treating development spending as sustaining capex (or the reverse) | Read the capex classification note |
| Ignoring lease expiries | Normalize leasing costs for upcoming expiry years |
| Mixing currencies | Put price and AFFO in one currency at the same date |
