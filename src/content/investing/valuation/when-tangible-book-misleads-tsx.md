Price to tangible book has a reputation as the hard-nosed multiple: no goodwill, no fluffy intangibles, just the equity you could point to. That reputation is only half deserved. TBV still depends on how loans are reserved, where securities losses are parked, what the company calls "intangible" and, for insurers, on accounting that moves profit outside book value altogether.

This page lists the ways P/TBV misleads on TSX stocks and what to do about each. The build is in [how to calculate tangible book in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-tangible-book-in-cad-from-filings/); the ordinary P/B traps are in [when P/B misleads](/investing/valuation/when-pb-misleads-tsx/), and this page does not repeat them. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

P/TBV misleads when: (1) **TBV is tiny or negative** because of acquisitions or buybacks; (2) the removed **intangibles have real value**, such as spectrum or broadcast licences; (3) **loan loss allowances are thin**, which inflates TBV; (4) **unrealized losses** sit in different places at different companies; (5) the company is an **insurer**, where IFRS 17 holds future profit in a liability (the CSM); (6) **definitions differ** across peers; or (7) the share count or timing is off. Fix each before you compare.

## 1. Tiny or negative TBV

Serial acquirers, franchisors and companies that buy back a lot of stock can have tangible common equity close to zero or below it. P/TBV then goes to infinity or turns negative, and tells you nothing about value. It does tell you something about **risk**: there is little tangible cushion under creditors and shareholders.

| Situation | What P/TBV shows | Use instead |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Acquirer with goodwill above equity | Negative or very high | EV/EBITDA, P/FCF, ROIC on invested capital including goodwill |
| Heavy buybacks funded with debt | Negative | Net debt to EBITDA, interest coverage |
| Software or brand business | Tiny | Earnings, FCF and EV multiples |

The [serial acquirer analysis](/investing/analyze/analyze-ma-track-records-serial-acquirers/) explains how to judge whether that goodwill earned its keep.

## 2. Intangibles that are real assets

Telecoms carry **spectrum licences** as intangible assets, usually with indefinite lives under IAS 38. Broadcasters hold licences; some consumer companies own brands bought decades ago. Removing them treats a licence that can be sold at auction as worthless.

**Fix:** decide which intangibles a buyer would pay for, keep those, and say so. For telecoms, P/TBV is rarely the right tool at all; see [how telecoms make money](/investing/sectors/how-telecoms-make-money/) for the metrics that matter.

## 3. Thin loan loss allowances inflate TBV

TBV assumes loans are worth their carrying value net of the IFRS 9 expected credit loss allowance. A lender that reserves less than its peers for a similar loan book reports a higher TBV.

| Hypothetical lender (C$M) | As reported | At peer-level allowance |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Loans | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| Allowance for credit losses | 50 (0.5%) | 90 (0.9%) |
| Extra allowance, after tax at about 27% | | −29 |
| Tangible common equity | 600 | 571 |
| Change in TBV | | about −5% |

**Fix:** compare allowance as a percentage of loans, by loan type (residential mortgages need far less than credit cards or subprime auto), and watch the trend in provisions for credit losses (PCL). [Bank operating metrics to track](/investing/sectors/bank-operating-metrics-to-track/) covers the ratios.

## 4. Unrealized losses in different places

Securities a bank holds at **fair value through other comprehensive income** carry their unrealized gains and losses in AOCI, inside equity. Securities held at **amortized cost** do not. Two lenders with identical rate exposure can therefore show different TBV, and TBV can swing when rates move even if nothing is sold.

**Fix:** read the AOCI breakdown in the statement of changes in equity and the fair value note for amortized-cost securities. If one peer has large unrealized losses outside equity, adjust its TBV or flag it.

## 5. Insurers and IFRS 17

Since IFRS 17 took effect in 2023, an insurer's expected future profit on its in-force contracts is held in a liability called the **contractual service margin (CSM)** and released into earnings over time. It is not part of equity, so it is not part of TBV, even though for a life insurer it can be very large relative to book.

| Insurer view (hypothetical, per share) | Value | P at $40.00 |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Tangible book value | $20.00 | 2.0x |
| + after-tax CSM | $9.00 | |
| Tangible book + after-tax CSM | $29.00 | 1.4x |

**Fix:** for life insurers, read TBV alongside the CSM and any adjusted book value the company discloses, and check capital through the LICAT ratio (life) or MCT ratio (P&C), not CET1. [How to analyze insurers vs banks](/investing/analyze/analyze-insurers-vs-banks/) explains the differences.

## 6 and 7. Definitions, shares and timing

| Trap | Example | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Different deductions across peers | One bank deducts software and adds back DTL; another does neither | Rebuild every peer on your own rules |
| Preferred shares or LRCNs left in | TBV per share overstated | Remove all equity ranking ahead of common |
| Weighted-average shares used | Per-share figures off after buybacks or issuance | Use period-end shares |
| Stale balance sheet | Price today, TBV from four months ago | Note the date; adjust for big events since |
| NVCC instruments | Bank preferred shares and LRCNs can convert to common if OSFI declares non-viability | A tail risk for common holders, not a day-to-day adjustment; note it |
| Currency | US$ TBV against a C$ price | Convert at the period-end rate |

## A six-question check

| Question | If yes |
|:--|:--|
| Is TBV small relative to book, or negative? | Use other multiples; note the thin cushion |
| Would a buyer pay for the removed intangibles? | Keep them, or do not use P/TBV |
| Is the loan loss allowance below peers for a similar book? | Rebuild TBV at a peer-level allowance |
| Are large unrealized losses outside equity? | Adjust TBV or flag it |
| Is it an insurer? | Add CSM context and read LICAT or MCT |
| Are definitions, shares, date and currency consistent across peers? | Proceed to the peer table |
