**P/E** (price-to-earnings) divides a company’s share price by earnings per share. Canadian DIY investors see it everywhere — and misuse it just as often — especially across banks, resource cyclicals, and loss-making growth names.

Educational explanation only — not a buy/sell signal.

## Direct answer

Trailing P/E uses the last twelve months of EPS; forward P/E uses expected next-year EPS. A “cheap” P/E only means something **relative to history, peers, and earnings quality**. On the TSX, always ask which earnings (IFRS net income vs adjusted) and whether the cycle is flattering the denominator.

## The formula and the traps in the denominator

**P/E = share price ÷ earnings per share**

EPS can be diluted or basic, trailing or forward, GAAP/IFRS net income or “adjusted.” Canadian issuers often emphasize adjusted EPS — useful if reconciled, dangerous if you forget the adjustments.

## Trailing vs forward

| Flavour | Uses | Good for | Breaks when |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Trailing | Last ~12 months EPS | Sanity check vs history | Earnings just collapsed or spiked one-time |
| Forward | Consensus or company guide | Comparing expectations | Guidance is heroic or coverage is thin |

## Canada-first context

- **Banks:** P/E is used, but **P/B and ROE** often carry more weight in credit cycles.
- **Resource producers:** Mid-cycle earnings matter; peak-cycle P/E can look “cheap” before the commodity rolls over.
- **REITs / infrastructure:** Equity P/E is often the wrong primary lens vs FFO/AFFO or cash-flow multiples.
- **Software / growth:** Negative earnings make P/E useless until profitability; use other frameworks carefully.

## How DIY investors should use P/E

1. Pair with **peer median** in the same business model.
2. Check **earnings quality** (cash conversion, one-time items, dilution).
3. Compare to the company’s **own history** only if the business mix is stable.
4. Never use P/E alone for a thesis — triangulate with EV/EBITDA or P/FCF when relevant ([EV/EBITDA glossary](/investing/glossary/what-is-ev-ebitda/), [FCF glossary](/investing/glossary/what-is-free-cash-flow/)).

## When P/E misleads on TSX names

- Cyclicals at the top of the earnings cycle.
- Heavy one-time gains or tax items in the LTM.
- Rapid dilution (share count rising while EPS looks stable).
- Accounting differences vs a US peer without adjustments.
