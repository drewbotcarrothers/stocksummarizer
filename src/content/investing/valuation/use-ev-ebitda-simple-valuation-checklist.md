This is the one-page EV/EBITDA checklist. It assumes you can [calculate EV/EBITDA in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-ebitda-in-cad-from-filings/), know [when EV/EBITDA misleads](/investing/valuation/when-ev-ebitda-misleads-tsx/) and have a [peer comparison](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). The format mirrors [how to use P/E in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/). The new step here is the walk back from enterprise value to a **price per share**, because EV/EBITDA values the whole business, not your shares. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use EV/EBITDA for capital-heavy, consistently profitable businesses where debt differs across peers. Pick a **fair multiple range** from lease-consistent peers and the company's own history, apply it to normalized EBITDA to get a fair EV, then **subtract every claim ahead of common shareholders** (net debt, leases, preferreds, NCI) and add non-core investments to reach equity value per share. Compare that range with today's price, and remember that leverage widens it.

## The EV/EBITDA checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Positive, fairly stable EBITDA; not a bank or insurer? | Yes; capital-heavy or levered peers |
| 2 | Basis | EV and EBITDA on the same lease basis and scope? | Built per the calculation post |
| 3 | EBITDA quality | Reported vs adjusted gap under about 10%? | Yes, or you use reported |
| 4 | Normalize | Mid-cycle or 3-year average EBITDA? | Not a peak year |
| 5 | Capital intensity | Capex ÷ EBITDA similar to peers? | Within about 10 points, or multiple adjusted |
| 6 | Leverage | Net debt ÷ EBITDA within your limit? | Under about 3–4x for most non-utility businesses |
| 7 | Fair multiple | Bear, base, bull from peers and history | Written down, with reasons |
| 8 | Equity bridge | Fair EV − claims + non-core investments | Per-share value calculated |
| 9 | Cross-check | Do P/FCF and P/E agree? | No contradictions |

## Setting the fair multiple range

| Input | How to use it |
|:--|:--|
| Peer median (lease-consistent) | Centre of the base case |
| Company's own 5-10 year range | Only on the same lease basis (restate pre-2019) |
| Capex ÷ EBITDA vs peers | Higher capex → lower multiple |
| Growth vs peers | Faster durable growth → higher multiple |
| Leverage vs peers | Higher leverage → more conservative bear case |

## From fair multiple to price per share (hypothetical)

Normalized EBITDA is C$500M. Claims ahead of common shareholders, net of equity-accounted investments, total C$1,900M. There are 100M shares.

| Scenario | Fair EV/EBITDA | Fair EV | Less net claims | Equity value | Per share |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 7x | C$3,500M | C$1,900M | C$1,600M | $16.00 |
| Base | 8x | C$4,000M | C$1,900M | C$2,100M | $21.00 |
| Bull | 9x | C$4,500M | C$1,900M | C$2,600M | $26.00 |

A one-turn change in the multiple moves the per-share value by $5.00, about 24% of the base case, even though EV moves only 12.5%. That is leverage at work.

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| EBITDA − capex is negative or tiny | EV/EBITDA says nothing useful; use FCF or a DCF |
| Debt maturities exceed a few years of FCF | Refinancing can dilute equity; see [debt notes and covenants](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) |
| Adjusted EBITDA keeps rising faster than reported | Quality problem; see [non-GAAP metrics](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/) |
| Interest coverage weak | Equity is the shock absorber; see [interest coverage](/investing/analyze/analyze-interest-coverage-levered-tsx/) |
