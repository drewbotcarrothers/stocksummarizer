Canada's market is top-heavy. A handful of banks, pipelines, railways and resource giants make up a large share of the S&P/TSX Composite, and they trade millions of shares a day. Below them sits a long list of mid-caps (roughly C$2 billion to C$10 billion in market value) that are followed by fewer analysts, held by fewer funds and traded far less. Those differences show up in valuation: mid-caps often trade at lower multiples than mega-caps with similar growth, and the gap is not always a bargain.

This post explains the main valuation quirks of Canadian mid-caps compared with mega-caps, and how to tell a liquidity discount from a genuine opportunity. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Mega-caps tend to earn a **liquidity premium**: they are easier and cheaper to trade, included in more indexes and ETFs, covered by more analysts and often seen as lower risk, so investors accept a lower expected return and pay a higher multiple. Mid-caps often trade at a **discount** for thinner trading, wider spreads, fewer analysts, more concentrated ownership and more business concentration. Part of that discount is fair compensation for real risks; part can close over time if the company grows, gets added to an index or gets acquired. Judge the gap against **business quality, balance sheet and catalysts**, not just the multiple.

## Why the gap exists

| Factor | Mega-cap | Mid-cap |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Trading liquidity | Deep; large orders barely move the price | Thinner; big orders can move price |
| Index and ETF ownership | In the S&P/TSX 60 and most broad funds | May be in the Composite only, or in a few sector funds |
| Analyst coverage | Often 15+ analysts | Sometimes 3–8 |
| Business mix | Diversified across segments and regions | Often one product, region or customer group |
| Ownership | Widely held | Often a founder, family or anchor holder with a large stake |
| Takeover potential | Too big for most buyers | Realistic target for larger peers or private equity |

## Reading the discount (hypothetical)

| | Mega-cap peer | Mid-cap A | Mid-cap B |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Forward EV/EBITDA | 12.0x | 9.0x | 8.5x |
| Organic growth | 4% | 5% | 2% |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.5x | 1.5x | 4.0x |
| Top customer share of revenue | 5% | 8% | 35% |
| Analyst coverage | 18 | 6 | 3 |

Mid-cap A trades at a lower multiple despite faster growth and less debt. That looks like a liquidity discount that could narrow. Mid-cap B is cheaper still, but the balance sheet and customer concentration explain much of the gap; see [customer concentration risk](/investing/analyze/analyze-customer-concentration-risk/) and [net debt adjustments Canadians often miss](/investing/valuation/net-debt-adjustments-canadians-miss/).

## What can close the gap

| Catalyst | How it narrows the discount | Check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Index inclusion | New forced buyers, more liquidity | Float-adjusted market value vs index thresholds; see [what the S&P/TSX Composite measures](/investing/indexes-etfs/what-sp-tsx-composite-measures/) |
| Growth into a larger size tier | More funds can own it | Market value trend and share issuance |
| More analyst coverage | Better information, more buyers | New initiations after financings |
| Takeover | A buyer pays a premium | Ownership structure; see [dual-class share structures](/investing/valuation/value-dual-class-share-structures/) |
| US listing | Access to a larger investor base | Dual listing pros and cons; see [research dual-listed Canadian stocks](/investing/analyze/research-dual-listed-canadian-stocks/) |

Be wary of assuming any of these will happen on your timeline. Some mid-caps stay cheap for a decade because the reasons for the discount never go away.

## Practical points for DIY investors

- **Use limit orders.** With wider spreads, market orders can cost real money.
- **Size positions with liquidity in mind.** A position you can't exit in a few days of normal volume is riskier than it looks; see [position sizing for DIY stock pickers](/investing/process/position-sizing-diy-stock-pickers/).
- **Compare with the right peers.** A TSX mid-cap compared with a US mega-cap will always look cheap; see [how to compare a TSX stock to its US peer fairly](/investing/valuation/compare-tsx-stock-to-us-peer/).
- **Expect bigger reactions to news.** Fewer holders and analysts mean earnings surprises move price more.

## Common mistakes

- Calling every mid-cap discount a "mispricing".
- Using mega-cap multiples as the fair value target for a mid-cap.
- Ignoring how much of the float a founder or anchor holder controls.
- Forgetting that ETF-driven buying can inflate a mid-cap's multiple too; see [how ETF holdings can distort fair value narratives](/investing/valuation/etf-holdings-distort-fair-value-narratives/).
