This is the one-page P/S checklist. It assumes you can [calculate P/S in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-ps-in-cad-from-filings/), know [when P/S misleads](/investing/valuation/when-ps-misleads-tsx/), and have a [peer table](/investing/valuation/ps-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). The format mirrors [how to use P/E in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-pe-simple-valuation-checklist/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use P/S only where earnings are missing or temporarily depressed and margins can be reasoned about. Clean the revenue, then turn P/S into a valuation with one identity: **fair P/S ≈ target P/E × mature net margin**. Run bear, base and bull margin scenarios, multiply each fair P/S by revenue per share, and compare the range with today's price. If the current price needs the bull margin, the stock is not cheap on P/S.

## The P/S checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Are earnings negative, or below a normal level? | Yes, with a clear reason (early stage, trough, investment phase) |
| 2 | Margin comparability | Can you estimate a mature margin from peers or history? | At least 3 comparable data points |
| 3 | Revenue basis | Net of pass-throughs, continuing ops, TTM? | Built per the calculation post |
| 4 | Revenue quality | Recurring, organic, diversified? | Score of 6 or more (below) |
| 5 | Balance sheet | Is net debt small? | Under about 25% of market cap, otherwise use EV/Sales |
| 6 | Margin scenarios | Bear, base, bull mature net margin written down? | Each tied to evidence |
| 7 | Target P/E | What P/E would a mature version deserve? | Your assumption, recorded, near peer median |
| 8 | Price range | Fair P/S × revenue per share vs current price | Current price at or below the base value |
| 9 | Cross-check | Do cash burn, dilution and runway support the base case? | Funding needs are covered |

## Revenue quality score (simple)

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Recurring share of revenue | Under 25% | 25–60% | Over 60% |
| Organic share of growth | Under 50% | 50–80% | Over 80% |
| Customer concentration (top customer) | Over 25% | 10–25% | Under 10% |
| Gross margin trend (8 quarters) | Falling | Flat | Rising |
| Revenue basis stability | Changed in period | Minor reclassification | Unchanged |

8–10: revenue is a solid anchor. 6–7: usable with care. Below 6: P/S is not reliable; wait for earnings or use a scenario DCF. See [customer concentration risk](/investing/analyze/analyze-customer-concentration-risk/) and [retention clues in subscription businesses](/investing/analyze/evaluate-customer-retention-subscription-businesses/).

## Turning margin scenarios into a price range (hypothetical)

Revenue per share is $10.00. You assume a mature company in this group would trade near 18x earnings.

| Scenario | Mature net margin | Fair P/S (18 × margin) | Fair price (× $10.00) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 4% | 0.72x | $7.20 |
| Base | 8% | 1.44x | $14.40 |
| Bull | 12% | 2.16x | $21.60 |

If the stock trades at $16.00 (1.6x sales), it sits above the base case: the market expects a mature margin near 9%. That is not automatically wrong, but it is now the assumption to test. Two more adjustments keep this honest:

| Adjustment | Why | Simple approach |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Time to reach mature margin | A margin five years away is worth less today | Discount the fair price back at your required return, or haircut it |
| Dilution while losing money | Share count grows before margins arrive | Use a future share count; see [spotting dilution risk](/investing/analyze/spot-dilution-risk-canadian-issuers/) |

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| Gross margin falling as revenue grows | Scale is not helping; mature margin may never arrive |
| Cash runway under 18 months | Dilution or debt likely before profits |
| Revenue growth mostly acquired | P/S rewards buying sales |
| Going-concern language | See [auditor opinions and going-concern language](/investing/analyze/read-auditor-opinions-going-concern-language/) |
