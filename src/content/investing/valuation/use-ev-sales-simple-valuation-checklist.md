This is the one-page EV/Sales checklist. It assumes you can [calculate EV/Sales in CAD](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-sales-in-cad-from-filings/), know [when EV/Sales misleads](/investing/valuation/when-ev-sales-misleads-tsx/) and have a [peer comparison](/investing/valuation/ev-sales-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/). The structure mirrors [how to use P/S in a simple valuation checklist](/investing/valuation/use-ps-simple-valuation-checklist/), where fair P/S = target P/E × mature net margin. The EV/Sales version differs in two ways: the fair multiple comes from a **target EV/EBITDA × mature EBITDA margin**, and the walk back to a share price has to deal with **cash that will be burned** before the company gets there. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Use EV/Sales when a company has little or no profit yet, or is at a profit trough, and its balance sheet differs from its peers'. Set bear, base and bull **mature EBITDA margins**, multiply each by a **target EV/EBITDA** from mature peers to get a fair EV/Sales, apply it to forward revenue, then add **net cash after expected burn** (or subtract net debt and other claims) and divide by **diluted shares**. If the base case only works with today's full cash balance, the margin of safety is thinner than it looks.

## The EV/Sales checklist

| # | Step | Question | Pass if |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Eligibility | Losses or depressed margins, and a balance sheet unlike peers'? | Yes; otherwise use EV/EBITDA or P/FCF |
| 2 | EV basis | Full claim list, convertibles treated once? | Built per the calculation post |
| 3 | Cash runway | Cash covers the burn to breakeven? | Yes, without a new raise in the base case |
| 4 | Revenue quality | Mostly recurring, organic, gross-basis consistent? | Yes, or multiple cut |
| 5 | Gross margin | High enough to support the mature EBITDA margin? | Mature EBITDA margin well below gross margin |
| 6 | Mature margin | Bear, base, bull written down with reasons | Based on mature peers, not management's slide |
| 7 | Fair multiple | Target EV/EBITDA × mature margin | Target from mature peers, not growth peers |
| 8 | Per share | Fair EV + net cash after burn ÷ diluted shares | Calculated |
| 9 | Cross-check | Implied years to reach the mature margin | Plausible against history |

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Forward revenue is C$500M. Net cash today is C$200M, and the company expects to burn about C$120M before reaching breakeven, so net cash after burn is C$80M. There are 100M diluted shares. Mature peers trade around a 12x EV/EBITDA.

| Scenario | Mature EBITDA margin | Fair EV/Sales (12x × margin) | Fair EV | + Net cash after burn | Equity value | Per share |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Bear | 10% | 1.20x | C$600M | C$80M | C$680M | $6.80 |
| Base | 18% | 2.16x | C$1,080M | C$80M | C$1,160M | $11.60 |
| Bull | 25% | 3.00x | C$1,500M | C$80M | C$1,580M | $15.80 |

Using today's C$200M of net cash instead would add $1.20 to every scenario. That is the trap from the "misleads" post, turned into a number.

## Choosing the inputs

| Input | How to set it |
|:--|:--|
| Mature EBITDA margin | Median of established peers in the same business model; bear below, bull near the best |
| Target EV/EBITDA | Mature peers' median, on the same lease basis; see [EV/EBITDA vs peers](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/) |
| Forward revenue | Company guidance only if its track record is good; otherwise trailing growth faded |
| Expected burn | Recent quarterly operating cash outflow plus capex, times quarters to breakeven |
| Diluted shares | Include options, warrants and in-the-money convertibles |

## Red flags that override the checklist

| Red flag | Why it overrides |
|:--|:--|
| Cash runway shorter than the burn to breakeven | A raise is likely; model the dilution first; see [spotting dilution risk](/investing/analyze/spot-dilution-risk-canadian-issuers/) |
| Gross margin falling while revenue grows | The mature margin assumption is getting harder |
| Revenue growth mostly from acquisitions | Organic growth decides the multiple; see [organic vs acquisition growth](/investing/analyze/compare-organic-vs-acquisition-growth/) |
| Going-concern language in the audit report | EV/Sales is the wrong tool for now |
