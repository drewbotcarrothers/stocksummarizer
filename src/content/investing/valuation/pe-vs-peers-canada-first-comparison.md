"Cheaper than its peers" is one of the most common claims about a stock. It is only useful if the **peers are truly comparable** and the P/E figures are built **the same way**. On the TSX, where some sectors have only three or four listed companies and many issuers report in US dollars, that takes extra care.

This post is about P/E specifically. For the general method of building a peer group, see [how to use peer tables without cherry-picking comps](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/); for US comparisons, see [how to compare a TSX stock to its US peer fairly](/investing/valuation/compare-tsx-stock-to-us-peer/). It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Choose 4 to 8 peers with the **same business model** (not just the same sector label). Calculate every P/E on the **same basis**: same EPS type, same trailing or forward period, same currency, and calendarized if fiscal years differ. Compare with the **median**, not the average. Then explain any gap with fundamentals such as growth, return on equity, leverage and risk. A gap you cannot explain is a question, not a bargain.

## Canada-first peer set rules

| Rule | Why it matters on the TSX |
|:--|:--|
| Match the business model | "Industrials" mixes rails, waste, engineering and aerospace |
| Few Canadian peers? Add US or global peers, flagged | Some sectors have only 3–4 TSX names |
| Same reporting currency, or convert | Many TSX issuers report in USD |
| Calendarize fiscal years | Big Canadian banks have October 31 year ends; others use December |
| Same EPS basis | Mixing adjusted and IFRS EPS can move P/E by several turns |
| Exclude loss-makers or show them as "n/m" | Negative P/E is meaningless |
| Note size and liquidity | Small, thinly traded names often trade at a discount |

## Calendarizing a fiscal year (hypothetical)

A peer with an October 31 year end has EPS of $8.00 for fiscal 2025 and $8.80 expected for fiscal 2026. To compare with December year-end peers on a calendar 2025 basis, note that calendar 2025 includes 10 months of fiscal 2025 (January to October 2025) and 2 months of fiscal 2026 (November and December 2025):

| Piece | Calculation | EPS |
|:--|:--|--:|
| 10/12 of fiscal 2025 | 8.00 × 10 ÷ 12 | 6.67 |
| 2/12 of fiscal 2026 | 8.80 × 2 ÷ 12 | 1.47 |
| **Calendar 2025 EPS** | Sum | **8.13** |

The difference is small here, but it grows when earnings are changing fast.

Adding growth, ROE and leverage columns stops you from reading P/E alone. If you need the inputs, [calculate P/E in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-pe-in-cad-from-filings/) for each name.

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Peers chosen | Same business model, with reasons written down |
| Basis aligned | Same EPS type, period and currency for every name |
| Fiscal years aligned | Calendarized where year ends differ |
| Median used | Outliers shown but not driving the comparison |
| Gap explained | Growth, ROE, leverage, risk compared |
| Conclusion written | Discount or premium and whether it looks justified |
