**EV/Sales** (enterprise value to sales) compares the value of the whole business, including what it owes lenders and minus the cash it holds, with its revenue. It does the same job as [P/S](/investing/valuation/what-ps-means-canadian-diy/) for companies without reliable profits, but it fixes P/S's biggest blind spot: debt.

This post is educational; all figures are hypothetical. For the earnings-based cousin, see [what EV/EBITDA means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-for-canadian-diy-investors/).

## Direct answer

EV/Sales = **enterprise value ÷ trailing twelve-month revenue**, where EV = market cap + debt + lease liabilities + preferreds + minority interests − cash. Revenue belongs to all capital providers (lenders are paid out of it before shareholders), so EV is the matching numerator. Use EV/Sales instead of P/S when companies carry **different amounts of debt or cash**: a levered company always looks cheaper on P/S than it really is, and a cash-rich one looks more expensive. Like P/S, it says nothing without margins: **EV/Sales = EV/EBITDA × EBITDA margin**.

## The debt and cash bridge: why EV/Sales differs from P/S

| Hypothetical | Net cash company | No net debt | Levered company |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Revenue | C$1,000M | C$1,000M | C$1,000M |
| Market cap | C$2,000M | C$2,000M | C$2,000M |
| Debt (incl. leases, preferreds) | C$100M | C$300M | C$1,300M |
| Cash | C$500M | C$300M | C$300M |
| Net debt (net cash) | (C$400M) | C$0 | C$1,000M |
| Enterprise value | C$1,600M | C$2,000M | C$3,000M |
| P/S | 2.0x | 2.0x | 2.0x |
| **EV/Sales** | **1.6x** | **2.0x** | **3.0x** |

On P/S the three look identical. On EV/Sales the levered company costs almost twice as much per dollar of revenue as the cash-rich one, because a buyer of the whole business would also take on its debt. The full EV build (diluted shares, leases, preferreds, minority interests, equity-accounted investments) follows the same steps as [how to calculate EV/EBITDA in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-ev-ebitda-in-cad-from-filings/).

## Reading EV/Sales through margins

Just as P/S = P/E × net margin, **EV/Sales = EV/EBITDA × EBITDA margin**. That identity is the fastest way to tell whether an EV/Sales figure is demanding.

| Hypothetical business | EBITDA margin | EV/EBITDA | EV/Sales |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Food distributor | 5% | 10x | 0.5x |
| Industrial manufacturer | 15% | 10x | 1.5x |
| Software company | 30% | 10x | 3.0x |
| Unprofitable software company (today) | −5% | n/m | 4.0x |

The last row is where EV/Sales is used most: there is no EBITDA to divide by, so investors ask what EBITDA margin the company must reach to justify 4.0x sales. At a 10x EV/EBITDA a mature version would need a **40%** EBITDA margin. That is a high bar, and writing it down is the point.

## When EV/Sales is useful, and when it is not

| Situation | EV/Sales useful? | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Unprofitable growth companies with debt or large cash balances | Yes | Revenue is the only stable line and the balance sheet varies |
| Comparing a levered company with a debt-free peer | Yes | P/S would mislead |
| Cyclicals at a profit trough | Moderately | Revenue holds up better than EBITDA |
| Takeover and private-market comparisons | Yes | Deal values are usually quoted on an EV basis |
| Banks and insurers | No | Debt is their raw material; use [P/B and ROE](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/) |
| Commodity pass-through businesses | Low | Revenue swings with prices; see [when P/S misleads](/investing/valuation/when-ps-misleads-tsx/) |
| Mature, profitable companies | Low | EV/EBITDA and P/FCF say more |

## Canada-first notes

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| TSX tech names reporting in USD | Revenue at average rate, balance-sheet items at closing rate, market cap at the TSX quote |
| IFRS 16 lease liabilities | Include them in EV consistently for all peers (revenue is unaffected by IFRS 16) |
| Cash held abroad or restricted | Do not subtract trapped cash in full |
| Convertible debentures, common on the TSX and TSXV | Count as debt, or as shares if deep in the money, never both; see [cap tables and fully diluted share counts](/investing/analyze/read-cap-tables-fully-diluted-share-counts/) |
| Software companies using the "Rule of 40" | Growth % + profit margin % is often discussed next to EV/Sales; check which margin the company uses |
