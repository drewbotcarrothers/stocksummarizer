**Forward P/E** divides today's share price by **expected** earnings per share, usually for the next twelve months or the next fiscal year. It is the version most often quoted in market commentary because prices look ahead. But it swaps a fact (reported earnings) for a forecast, and that changes how you should read it.

This post goes deeper than the short trailing-vs-forward table in [what P/E means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/what-pe-means-canadian-diy/). It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Forward P/E = share price ÷ **estimated** EPS. "Forward" can mean the **next twelve months (NTM)**, the **current fiscal year (FY1)** or the **next fiscal year (FY2)**, so always check which. The estimate usually comes from **analyst consensus**, sometimes from **company guidance**, or from your own model. The gap between trailing and forward P/E shows how much **earnings growth** the estimate assumes. A low forward P/E is attractive only if that growth is realistic.

## Versions of forward P/E

| Version | EPS used | Best for | Watch for |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| NTM (next twelve months) | Blend of current and next fiscal year | Comparing companies with different year ends | Data providers blend differently |
| FY1 | Current fiscal year estimate | Simple, widely quoted | Partly already reported late in the year |
| FY2 | Next fiscal year estimate | Recovery or growth stories | Very uncertain; often revised |
| Guidance-based | Midpoint of company EPS guidance | Companies that guide EPS | Many Canadian issuers guide inputs, not EPS |

## Reading the gap between trailing and forward

| Trailing P/E | Forward P/E | What the estimate implies |
|--:|--:|:--|
| 15.0x | 13.0x | About 15% EPS growth |
| 15.0x | 15.0x | Flat earnings |
| 15.0x | 18.0x | EPS expected to fall about 17% |
| Negative | 25.0x | Return to profit expected |

The math: implied growth = trailing P/E ÷ forward P/E − 1. For the first row, 15.0 ÷ 13.0 − 1 ≈ 15%. If that growth looks too high, the forward P/E is too low to trust.

## Questions to ask about any forward EPS

| Question | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| How many analysts cover the stock? | One or two estimates on a small cap is thin evidence |
| How wide is the range of estimates? | A wide range means high uncertainty |
| Have estimates been rising or falling? | Revisions often continue for a while |
| Is it adjusted or IFRS EPS? | Consensus is usually on an adjusted basis |
| What commodity, rate or FX assumptions sit inside? | Resource and bank estimates depend on them |
| Does the company give guidance? | Under NI 51-102 Part 4A, forward-looking information needs a reasonable basis and stated assumptions |

## Where forward P/E fits on the TSX

| Sector | Usefulness | Note |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Banks | Useful alongside P/B and ROE | Fiscal years end October 31 |
| Energy and miners | Limited | Estimates move with commodity prices |
| Software and growth | Useful once profitable | Very sensitive to growth assumptions |
| Utilities and pipelines | Moderate | Cash flow multiples often matter more |
| REITs | Low | Use price/FFO or price/AFFO instead |
