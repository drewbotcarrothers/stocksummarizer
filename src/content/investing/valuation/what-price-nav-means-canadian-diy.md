For some Canadian companies, earnings and cash flow are not the best starting point, because the value sits in assets that are valued one by one: a gold miner's mines, a REIT's buildings, a holding company's stakes in other businesses, an oil producer's reserves. For these, investors compare the share price with **net asset value (NAV) per share**. The ratio, **price/NAV** (P/NAV), is one of the most quoted numbers in Canadian resource and real estate research, and one of the most assumption-heavy.

The REIT version of NAV (cap rates, the FFO and AFFO bridge) is covered in [valuation for REITs: NAV, FFO, AFFO explained](/investing/valuation/valuing-reits-nav-ffo-affo/), and the miner habits that sit around it in [how to analyze gold miners without only watching the spot price](/investing/analyze/analyze-gold-miners-beyond-spot-price/). This post explains P/NAV itself and how it differs across sectors. It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

P/NAV = **share price ÷ NAV per share**. NAV is the value of each asset, estimated separately, plus cash and investments, minus debt and other claims, divided by diluted shares. Below 1.0x, the market values the company at less than the sum of its assets on **your** (or an analyst's) assumptions; above 1.0x, at more. The ratio is only as good as the inputs: for miners, the **commodity price deck** and **discount rate**; for REITs, the **cap rate**; for holding companies, the value of private stakes. Always ask "NAV at what assumptions?" before reading the ratio.

## How NAV is built: a gold producer (hypothetical)

| Component | How it is valued | C$M |
|:--|:--|--:|
| Mine A (producing) | After-tax discounted cash flow over the mine life at a stated gold price | 1,800 |

| Exploration and land | Simple per-ounce or per-hectare value, or a fixed amount | 150 |
| Cash and investments | Balance sheet | 300 |

| Corporate G&A | Head-office costs over the mine lives, discounted | −250 |
| **NAV** | | **2,100** |

| Share price | | $11.20 |
| **P/NAV** | | **0.80x** |

The discount rate matters as much. Mining analysts often apply a single-digit **real** rate to producing assets by convention, with higher rates for development projects and riskier jurisdictions; write down the rate you use and test a higher one. Comparing P/NAV across sources is meaningless unless the decks and rates match.

## P/NAV across sectors

| Sector | What NAV means | Main assumption | Where to read more |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Gold and base-metal miners | Discounted cash flow of each mine, plus exploration, less net debt and G&A | Commodity price deck, discount rate, costs | [How gold miners make money](/investing/sectors/how-gold-miners-make-money/); [what AISC is](/investing/glossary/what-is-aisc/) |
| Royalty and streaming companies | Discounted value of royalty streams | Partner mine performance, price deck | Typically priced at a premium to NAV |
| Developers and juniors | NPV of a planned mine, risked for financing and permitting | Construction cost, timing, dilution | [Red flags in junior mining and energy filings](/investing/analyze/spot-red-flags-junior-mining-energy-filings/) |
| Oil and gas producers | Discounted value of reserves (NI 51-101 reserves reports) | Price forecast, reserve category | [How oil and gas producers make money](/investing/sectors/how-oil-gas-producers-make-money/) |
| REITs | Fair value of properties less debt | Cap rate | [Valuation for REITs](/investing/valuation/valuing-reits-nav-ffo-affo/) |
| Holding companies | Listed stakes at market, private stakes estimated, less holdco debt | Private-stake values, holdco costs | Persistent discounts are common |

## Why discounts and premiums persist

| Reason for a discount | Reason for a premium |
|:--|:--|
| Single-asset or high-risk jurisdiction | Diversified, low-cost, long-life assets |
| Development risk, financing and dilution ahead | Exploration upside not in NAV |
| Holding-company costs and complexity | Royalty model with no operating costs |
| Management with a poor capital-allocation record | Takeover interest in the sector |
| Leverage near covenant limits | Track record of beating guidance |

A discount is not a bargain by itself. It narrows only if something changes: the asset is de-risked, sold, or the company buys back shares at the discount.

## Where P/NAV fits

| Use it for | Avoid it for |
|:--|:--|
| Asset-heavy companies whose assets are valued one by one | Operating companies whose value is in their ongoing earnings |
| Comparing miners or REITs on the same assumptions | Comparing NAVs from different sources with different decks |
| Seeing how much of the value depends on the commodity price | As the only measure; pair it with cash flow multiples |
