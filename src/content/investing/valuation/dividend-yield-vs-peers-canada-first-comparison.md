Comparing dividend yields across peers looks easy because every quote page shows one. The trouble is that those yields are built on different conventions (trailing, indicated, forward), sometimes include specials, and sit on very different payout ratios and balance sheets. A peer table only says something about valuation when the yields are built the same way and read next to the things that decide whether the dividend lasts.

This post is the peer step of the dividend-yield set. The basics are in [what dividend yield means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/what-dividend-yield-means-canadian-diy/), the worksheet in [how to calculate dividend yield in CAD from filings](/investing/valuation/calculate-dividend-yield-in-cad-from-filings/) and the traps in [when dividend yield misleads on TSX stocks](/investing/valuation/when-dividend-yield-misleads-tsx/). General peer steps (true peers, calendarizing, the median) are in [P/E vs peers](/investing/valuation/pe-vs-peers-canada-first-comparison/) and [how to use peer tables without cherry-picking comps](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/). It is educational; all figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Rebuild every peer's yield on the same basis: **indicated regular dividend** (latest declared × payments per year), specials excluded, dividend and price in the **same currency**, prices from the same date. Compare each yield with the **peer median**, then read it next to **payout ratio**, **FCF payout** (dividends ÷ free cash flow), **dividend growth** and **leverage**. A yield above the median is only a valuation signal when coverage and the balance sheet are at least as good as the peers'. Otherwise it is the market pricing in a cut.

## Alignment rules for dividend-yield peer tables

| Rule | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Indicated regular dividend for all peers | Trailing yields lag recent raises and cuts |
| Specials and variable top-ups shown separately | One-off payouts inflate the yield |
| Same currency for dividend and price | US-dollar declared dividends on a CAD price understate the yield |
| Same price date | A week of market moves can shift yields by tenths of a point |
| Same payout basis per sector | EPS for operating companies, AFFO for REITs, distributable cash flow for pipelines |
| Note dividend reinvestment plan discounts | A discounted DRIP pays part of the dividend in new shares |

## Relative yield: one number for the comparison

**Relative yield** is the stock's yield divided by the peer median yield. In the table, your company's relative yield is 4.8% ÷ 4.7% ≈ 1.02, Peer C's is about 1.47. Tracking relative yield over time strips out moves that hit the whole group (for example, when interest rates change and every dividend payer reprices). A relative yield that drifts up while coverage and growth hold steady is the cleanest valuation signal a dividend peer table can give.

| Hypothetical | Your company | Peer C |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Relative yield today | 1.02 | 1.47 |
| Five-year average relative yield | 0.95 | 1.10 |
| Change | Slightly higher | Much higher |
| Coverage over the same period | Stable | Weakening |
| Read | Mildly cheaper on yield | Market expects a cut |

## Sector norms: compare like with like

Yields are only comparable inside a sector, because payout norms follow business models. The [dividend trap checklist](/investing/dividends/avoid-dividend-traps-tsx/) and [payout ratio explained](/investing/dividends/payout-ratio-explained-canadian-companies/) cover the safety side; here is the valuation side.

| Sector | Payout basis to use | Coverage column to add | Note |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Banks | Earnings (EPS) | Capital ratio from the shareholder report | See [valuation for banks](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/) |
| Pipelines and utilities | Distributable or free cash flow | Net debt ÷ EBITDA | Rate-sensitive group; compare relative yield; see [pipelines and utilities as bond proxies](/investing/analyze/analyze-pipelines-utilities-bond-proxies/) |
| REITs | AFFO | AFFO payout, debt to gross book value | See [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) |
| Telecoms | Free cash flow | Capex cycle, leverage | Payout can look high in heavy spectrum or network years |
| Resource producers | Free cash flow through the cycle | Base vs variable dividend split | Use the base dividend only |

## Canada-first issues

| Issue | What to do |
|:--|:--|
| TSX peers that declare in US dollars | Convert the dividend at a current rate before dividing; see [handling CAD vs USD results](/investing/analyze/handle-currency-cad-vs-usd-results/) |
| US-listed peers | Compare pre-tax yields, then note [US withholding by account](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/) separately |
| Eligible vs ordinary dividends | Same headline yield, different after-tax yield in a taxable account; see [eligible vs ordinary dividends](/investing/dividends/eligible-vs-ordinary-dividends/) |
| Monthly vs quarterly payers | Annualize both the same way |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Peers chosen | Same business model and payout basis, reasons written |
| Yields rebuilt | Indicated regular dividend, same currency, same date |
| Median used | Each yield compared with the peer median |
| Coverage added | Payout and FCF payout on the sector's basis |
| Growth added | Five-year dividend growth and yield + growth |
| Leverage added | Net debt ÷ EBITDA or sector equivalent |
| Gap explained | One sentence: cheaper, or riskier? |
