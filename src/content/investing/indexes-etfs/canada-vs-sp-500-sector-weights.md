Canadian investors often compare the TSX with the S&P 500 as if they were two versions of the same thing. They aren't. The two indexes are built from very different economies, and **sector mix** — not some national investing skill — explains most of why they rise and fall at different times.

Educational only — not financial advice. Sector weights change constantly; this post describes long-running *tendencies* and uses **hypothetical** numbers in charts. Check S&P Dow Jones Indices factsheets for current weights.

## Direct answer

The S&P/TSX Composite has long been dominated by **financials** (especially the big banks and insurers), **energy** and **materials** (miners, gold, fertilizer), with a relatively small technology weight. The S&P 500 has been led by **information technology** and other mega-cap growth businesses. So the TSX tends to do relatively well when commodity prices, interest margins and "value" stocks are in favour, and relatively poorly when big-tech growth leads. Neither is better by default; they're different bets.

## The structural differences

| Feature | S&P/TSX Composite (tendency) | S&P 500 (tendency) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Largest sector | Financials | Information technology |
| Resource exposure (energy + materials) | Large | Small |
| Technology weight | Small | Very large |
| Dividend yield | Higher | Lower |
| Valuation multiple (P/E) | Usually lower | Usually higher |
| Currency of earnings | Mix of CAD and USD | Mostly USD |
| Concentration | A handful of banks, energy and rail names matter a lot | A handful of mega-cap tech names matter a lot |

Both indexes are top-heavy. The difference is *which* companies sit at the top.

## Why the mix matters for returns

Different sectors respond to different forces. A rough map:

| Macro backdrop (illustrative) | Tends to help | Index that may benefit relatively |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Rising oil and gas prices | Producers, pipelines with commodity exposure | TSX |
| Rising gold and metal prices | Gold miners, base metals | TSX |
| Steeper yield curve, healthy credit | Banks, insurers | TSX |
| Falling long-term rates, growth in favour | Tech, long-duration growth | S&P 500 |
| AI/software investment boom | Mega-cap tech, semiconductors | S&P 500 |
| Housing or credit stress in Canada | Hurts banks | Neither — TSX more exposed |

This is why multi-year stretches of TSX underperformance or outperformance versus the S&P 500 are common. It's a sector story more than a country story.

## What it means for dividends and valuation

The TSX's higher dividend yield and lower P/E aren't "free value" — they mostly reflect its sector mix. Banks, pipelines, telecoms and utilities pay out more and trade at lower multiples than software companies almost everywhere. Comparing a TSX stock with the S&P 500's P/E is apples to oranges; compare it with its own peers instead — see [comparing a TSX stock with its US peer](/investing/valuation/compare-tsx-stock-to-us-peer/) and [when P/E misleads](/investing/valuation/when-pe-misleads-tsx/).

## Home bias, in plain terms

Canada is a small slice of global stock market value, yet many Canadians hold mostly Canadian stocks. Reasons people cite include familiarity, no currency risk, and the dividend tax credit on eligible Canadian dividends in taxable accounts. The cost is concentration: a TSX-only portfolio is effectively a big bet on banks, energy and materials.

## How stock pickers can use this

1. **Know your active bets.** Owning three banks in a sleeve is a large overweight versus a global index even if it looks "normal" versus the TSX.
2. **Use sector hubs** to understand the drivers: [banks](/sectors/banks/), [oil & gas producers](/sectors/oil-gas-producers/), [pipelines](/sectors/midstream-pipelines/), [gold](/sectors/gold-precious-metals/).
3. **Benchmark fairly.** Judge a Canadian stock sleeve against the Canadian total-return index, and the whole portfolio against your chosen mix.
4. **Account location matters** for US holdings — see [US dividend withholding tax by account](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/).
