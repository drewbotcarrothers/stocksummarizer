Two ETFs with nearly identical names can hold very different things, cost different amounts and trade very differently. The fastest way to tell them apart is the **ETF Facts** document — a short, standardized disclosure every Canadian ETF must file — plus the provider's marketing factsheet. Ten minutes with both will answer most of the questions that matter before you buy.

Educational only — not financial advice. No specific ETF is recommended here; numbers in examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Read an ETF's documents in this order: (1) **what index or strategy it follows**, (2) **what it actually holds** and how concentrated it is, (3) **what it costs** — MER plus TER, and the bid-ask spread you pay to trade, (4) **how it has tracked** its index, and (5) **distributions and tax**. The ETF Facts document is regulated and standardized in Canada, so the same headings appear for every fund; the provider's factsheet adds detail like sector weights and index methodology.

## ETF Facts vs factsheet

| Document | Who sets the format | Best for |
|:--|:--|:--|
| ETF Facts | Canadian securities regulators (standardized form) | Costs (MER, TER), risk rating, trading info, year-by-year returns |
| Provider factsheet | The ETF company | Index details, sector and country weights, holdings count, yield, duration (bond ETFs) |
| Prospectus / annual reports (MRFP) | Regulated, long-form | Full strategy, securities lending, detailed expenses |

| Section | Key fields | What to check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Quick facts | Fund manager, inception date, total value, MER, distribution frequency | Size and age — very small or new funds can close or trade thinly |
| What does the ETF invest in? | Top 10 holdings, investment mix | Top-10 share of the fund; overlap with stocks you already own |
| How risky is it? | Risk rating (low → high) | The rating is based on historical volatility — not a measure of losing money permanently |
| How has it performed? | Year-by-year returns, best/worst 3 months, average return | Returns are after expenses; compare with the index over the same years |
| Trading ETFs | Market price, NAV, average bid-ask spread | A wide spread is a hidden cost on every trade |
| Who is this ETF for? | Intended investor | Horizon and purpose match yours? |
| A word about tax | Distribution taxation | In taxable accounts, distributions are taxable even if reinvested |
| How much does it cost? | MER, TER, ETF expenses, trailing commission | ETF expenses = MER + TER, shown per $1,000 invested |

## Holdings and concentration

The top-10 table is the quickest truth test. A "Canadian equity" ETF whose top 10 are mostly banks and energy names will move with those sectors — see [what the S&P/TSX Composite measures](/investing/indexes-etfs/what-sp-tsx-composite-measures/).

| Check (hypothetical ETF) | Reading |
|:--|:--|
| Holdings count: 220 | Broad |
| Top 10 = 38% of fund | Moderately top-heavy |
| Largest sector = 31% | One sector drives a third of returns |
| Already own 4 of the top 10 directly | Your real exposure is higher than it looks |

- **MER** — management fee plus operating expenses (including sales tax), deducted inside the fund.
- **TER** — trading expense ratio: the fund's own trading costs.
- **Bid-ask spread** — paid by *you* when you trade; use limit orders and avoid the first and last minutes of the session.

Details in [MER, TER, and tracking difference in plain English](/investing/indexes-etfs/mer-ter-tracking-difference/). Broker commissions vary — see [how to compare Canadian discount brokers](/investing/brokers-tools/compare-canadian-discount-brokers/).

## Distributions and tax

- **Frequency** (monthly, quarterly, annually) is not the same as **yield**. Check the trailing distribution yield on the factsheet.
- **Return of capital** in distributions lowers your adjusted cost base in taxable accounts.
- **Foreign holdings** may lose withholding tax depending on structure and account — see [US dividend withholding tax by account](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/).

## A 10-minute pre-buy checklist

1. Write down the index or mandate in one sentence.
2. Note holdings count, top-10 share and largest sector.
3. Note MER, TER and the average bid-ask spread.
4. Compare 5-year returns with the index (difference ≈ tracking difference).
5. Check fund size and age.
6. Check overlap with stocks you already own.
7. Decide which account it belongs in.
