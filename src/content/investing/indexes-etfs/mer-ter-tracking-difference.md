ETF costs look tiny on paper — a fraction of a percent — so they're easy to ignore. But they're charged every year on your whole balance, and the number on the label isn't the whole story. Three terms cover almost everything you need: **MER**, **TER** and **tracking difference**.

Educational only — not financial advice. All figures are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

- **MER (management expense ratio):** the fund's management fee plus operating expenses (and sales taxes), as a yearly percentage of assets. It's taken inside the fund — you never see a bill.
- **TER (trading expense ratio):** in Canada, the fund's own **trading** costs (commissions it pays to buy and sell holdings) as a percentage of assets. Canadian ETF Facts show MER + TER together as "ETF expenses."
- **Tracking difference:** the ETF's actual return minus its index's return over a period. It's the best single measure of what owning the ETF really cost you.

## Watch the acronym trap

| Term | In Canada | In Europe / elsewhere |
|:--|:--|:--|
| MER | Management fee + operating expenses + taxes | Rarely used |
| TER | **Trading** expense ratio | **Total** expense ratio (closer to Canada's MER) |
| Expense ratio | Informal — usually means MER | US term, roughly comparable to MER |

If you read a US or UK article, "expense ratio" or "TER" may mean something different from the Canadian label.

## Tracking difference vs tracking error

| Measure | Question it answers | Good value |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Tracking difference | How much did the ETF lag (or beat) its index over the period? | Small and stable, close to −MER |
| Tracking error | How *consistently* did it follow the index (volatility of the gap)? | Low |

An ETF can have low tracking error (it follows the index closely day to day) but a steady negative tracking difference (it lags by its fees every year). For a long-term holder, **tracking difference matters more**.

## What drives tracking difference

| Driver | Effect (direction) |
|:--|:--|
| MER | Drag |
| TER (trading costs) | Drag |
| Cash held for distributions or flows | Small drag in rising markets |
| Sampling (not holding every index stock) | Either direction |
| Withholding tax on foreign dividends | Drag (depends on structure) |
| Securities lending revenue | Small boost |

You can estimate tracking difference yourself: take the ETF's annual returns from its ETF Facts or factsheet and subtract the index's total returns for the same years. See [how to read an ETF factsheet](/investing/indexes-etfs/how-to-read-etf-factsheet/).

## Your all-in cost, step by step

1. **MER + TER** from the ETF Facts ("ETF expenses").
2. **Bid-ask spread** each time you trade — half the spread on each buy and sell.
3. **Commissions**, if your broker charges them ([broker comparison](/investing/brokers-tools/compare-canadian-discount-brokers/)).
4. **Withholding tax** on foreign income where it isn't recoverable ([withholding tax by account](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/)).
5. Cross-check with **tracking difference** over 3–5 years.

Owning individual stocks has no MER, but it isn't free: spreads, commissions, FX conversion on US shares and — biggest of all — the risk of underperforming the index. Compare honestly in [ETFs vs individual TSX stocks](/investing/indexes-etfs/canadian-equity-etfs-vs-individual-stocks/).
