The **auditor's report** is easy to skip and dangerous to misunderstand. Most established issuers receive an unmodified opinion; that is not a gold star on the business. Modified opinions, key audit matters (KAMs), and **going-concern** uncertainty language are the parts DIY investors should actually parse.

Educational only — not financial advice. This is literacy, not an audit textbook. Pair with [Canadian annual report](/investing/analyze/read-canadian-annual-report/), [debt notes](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/), [interest coverage](/investing/analyze/analyze-interest-coverage-levered-tsx/), and [source hierarchy](/investing/frameworks/source-hierarchy-filings-transcripts-commentary/).

## Direct answer

Open the auditor's report in the annual financial statements. Note opinion type (unmodified vs qualified / adverse / disclaimer). Read KAMs for what was judgement-heavy. Search for going-concern / material uncertainty wording and management's plans. If present, immediately re-run liquidity, covenants, and kill criteria — do not wait for a dramatic headline.

## Quick reference

| Signal | Rough meaning for DIY readers | Next step |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Unmodified opinion | Fair presentation opinion obtained | Still read KAMs + notes |
| KAM / critical audit matter | Area of significant auditor judgement | Cross-read that note (rev, goodwill, etc.) |
| Emphasis of matter | Highlights a disclosure without modifying opinion | Read the referenced note |
| Qualified / adverse / disclaimer | Serious audit problem | High urgency — often avoid-new-money territory until understood |
| Material uncertainty related to going concern | Significant doubt on ability to continue as GC | Liquidity & covenant deep-dive now |

Canadian audits refer to Canadian Auditing Standards (CAS), aligned with ISAs; going-concern work commonly references CAS 570. Confirm current standard labels in the report you are reading.
