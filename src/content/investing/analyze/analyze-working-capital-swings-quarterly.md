Net income can look calm while cash from operations (CFO) wobbles. The usual culprit is **working capital**: receivables, inventory, and payables swinging quarter to quarter. DIY investors who only read the income statement miss the cash story.

Educational only — not financial advice. All worked numbers are **hypothetical**. Pair with [revenue quality](/investing/analyze/analyze-revenue-quality-canadian-issuers/) and [quarterly earnings literacy](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-quarterly-earnings-tsx/).

## Direct answer

From the balance sheet, compute quarter-over-quarter changes in trade receivables, inventories, and trade payables. Relate those deltas to sales growth. Then open the cash-flow statement (IAS 7 style) and confirm how working-capital movements bridge net income to CFO. Large AR/inventory builds without matching sales growth are a quality flag; aggressive payable stretch can temporarily boost CFO.

## Working-capital map

| Line | CFO effect when the balance **rises** | Common story |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Trade receivables | Uses cash (CFO down) | Sales booked before cash collected |
| Inventory | Uses cash | Build ahead of demand — or stuck stock |
| Trade payables | Provides cash (CFO up) | Paying suppliers later |
| Deferred revenue (where relevant) | Depends on classification | Cash in before revenue recognized |

## Hypothetical QoQ scorecard

| Item (hyp.) | Q2 | Q3 | Δ | Sales context | Reading |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Revenue | $100M | $105M | +5% | — | Modest growth |
| Trade AR | $22M | $40M | +$18M | AR grew faster than sales | Collection / channel lag risk |
| Inventory | $30M | $42M | +$12M | Build vs +5% sales | Demand miss or pre-buy |
| Trade AP | $18M | $10M | -$8M | Paying down AP | CFO headwind |
| Rough WC drag on CFO | — | — | ~$38M | — | Ask why before trusting EPS |

Always reconcile to the actual CFO working-capital lines — the table above is a **literacy sketch**, not a substitute for the filing.
