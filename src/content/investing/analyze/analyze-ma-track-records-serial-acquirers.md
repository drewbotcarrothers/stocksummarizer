**Serial acquirers** can compound brilliantly — or hide weak organic growth behind a deal treadmill. Judging the **track record** means logging what was promised, what was paid, and what showed up in cash, margins, and impairments years later.

Educational only — not financial advice. Scoreboard figures are **hypothetical**. Pair with [organic vs acquisition growth](/investing/analyze/compare-organic-vs-acquisition-growth/), [debt notes](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/), and [moat checklist](/investing/analyze/build-one-page-competitive-moat-checklist/).

## Direct answer

Build a multi-year deal log: target, size, cash vs stock, stated synergies, close date. Then score outcomes — retention, margin bridges, goodwill impairments, earnout true-ups, and leverage path. Prefer cash return / ROIC evidence over "strategic fit" language. Update the thesis: is management skilled at underwriting and integrating, or dependent on ever-larger deals?

## Deal-log columns (minimum)

| Column | Why |
|:--|:--|
| Target / close date | Perimeter timing |
| EV / consideration mix | Cash vs dilution |
| Stated synergy ($ / timing) | Promise to test |
| Multiple paid vs target peers | Underwriting discipline |
| Debt raised | Balance-sheet risk |
| Outcome notes (Y+1, Y+3) | Impairments, margins, exits |
