Junior mining and energy companies raise money on the promise of a discovery. Their filings follow special Canadian disclosure rules: **NI 43-101** for mineral projects and **NI 51-101** for oil and gas. Those rules exist because the gap between an exciting headline and an economic project can be very large. Learning a few red flags makes news releases much easier to read.

This guide teaches the reading skill. It is not a judgement on any company. All examples are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

For mining: check whether a number is a **reserve**, a **resource** (and which category) or just an **exploration result**, whether a **qualified person** signed off, and whether drill intervals are reported with **true widths** and alongside weaker holes. For oil and gas: check whether volumes are **reserves** evaluated by an independent evaluator or **contingent / prospective resources**, and how much of the story depends on undeveloped reserves. For both: follow the cash runway and dilution, because juniors rarely fund themselves.

## Mining red flags

| Red flag | Why it matters | What to check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| "Up to" grades or best-hole-only releases | Picks the best result out of many | Full drill table; holes with no significant results |
| Intervals without true widths | Core length can overstate thickness | Release should say true width or that it is unknown |
| Grab or selected samples headlined | Not representative of a deposit | Look for drill or channel sample data |
| Nearby-property comparisons | Another company's deposit says little about this one | NI 43-101 limits on adjacent-property disclosure |
| Historical estimates | Not current and not verified | Release must say a QP has not verified them |
| PEA economics built on inferred resources | Inferred tonnes are too uncertain for reserves | Cautionary statement and share of inferred tonnes |
| No current technical report | Material claims need support | SEDAR+ for a filed technical report |

## Oil and gas red flags

| Red flag | Why it matters | What to check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Prospective resources headlined | Undiscovered, risked volumes; may never be produced | Chance of discovery and development, cautionary language |
| Most value in proved undeveloped (PUD) | Needs capital that may not be raised | Future development costs vs cash and credit |
| BOE comparisons across oil and gas | 6:1 conversion is energy-based, not value-based | Price split between oil, liquids and gas |
| Evaluator not independent | Less assurance on reserves | Form 51-101F2 evaluator report |
| Flat production with rising debt | Treading water | Production per share, net debt trend |

## Financing and promotion signals

| Signal | What to look for |
|:--|:--|
| Frequent private placements with full warrants | Overhang and dilution; see [dilution risk](/investing/analyze/spot-dilution-risk-canadian-issuers/) |
| Flow-through shares | Tax-driven buyers who may sell quickly after closing |
| Going-concern note | Read [auditor opinion language](/investing/analyze/read-auditor-opinions-going-concern-language/) |
| Paid promotion or newsletter campaigns | Disclosed or not, a price run without new filings is a warning |
| Management paid well regardless of results | Read the [circular](/investing/analyze/read-management-information-circular/) |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Disclosure category identified | Reserve, resource (category) or exploration result |
| Qualified person or evaluator named | Name and independence checked |
| Full results read | All holes or wells, not only the headline |
| Technical report located | Date and key assumptions noted |
| Runway and dilution logged | Months of cash; warrant overhang |
| Commodity sensitivity noted | See [commodity price sensitivity](/investing/analyze/read-commodity-price-sensitivity-resource-issuers/) |
