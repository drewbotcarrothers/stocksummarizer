Many TSX names are **Canadian-listed multinationals**: reporting in CAD or USD while earning cash in several countries. Geographic revenue mix tells you where demand, regulation, and currency risk actually live — not where the head office sits.

Educational only — not financial advice. Mix percentages are **hypothetical**. Pair with [segment reporting](/investing/analyze/read-segment-reporting-canadian-filings/) and [customer concentration](/investing/analyze/analyze-customer-concentration-risk/). For trading FX costs in accounts see [commission-free trading and FX spreads](/investing/brokers-tools/commission-free-trading-fx-spreads/) (different topic — portfolio mechanics, not issuer geography).

## Direct answer

Open the geographic information in the notes (IFRS 8 entity-wide disclosures) and any MD&A geography discussion. Record revenue by country or region for at least two periods. Note the presentation currency and whether sales are local-currency. Layer qualitative country risks (policy, tax, trade, sanctions) from the AIF. Update your thesis when mix shifts — growth that is "all US" is a different risk stack than growth that is "all Canada."

## Hypothetical FX awareness sketch

| Scenario (hyp.) | CAD strengthens vs USD | Investor note |
|:--|:--|:--|
| US sales large, CAD reporting | Translated US revenue falls | Growth may look weaker without ops change |
| Costs in CAD, sales in USD | Margin pressure if unhedged | Check hedge disclosure |
| Natural hedge (costs in same FX) | Partial offset | Still read residual exposure |

This is literacy, not a hedge recommendation.
