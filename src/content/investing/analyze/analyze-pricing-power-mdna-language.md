Pricing power (the ability to raise prices without losing many customers) is one of the clearest signs of a strong business. Companies rarely state it as a number. Instead, it shows up in **how management describes revenue and margins** in the MD&A and on earnings calls: price realization, mix, surcharges, promotions, pass-through clauses and volume trade-offs.

This post shows how to read that language and check it against the numbers. It pairs with [how to analyze gross margin trends before you buy](/investing/analyze/analyze-gross-margin-trends/) and [how to evaluate management guidance language](/investing/analyze/evaluate-management-guidance-language/). It is educational and does not rate any company; examples are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Find the **revenue bridge** (price, volume, mix, currency and acquisitions) in the MD&A or press release. Strong pricing power looks like **price up, volume steady and gross margin up**. Then read the words: "price realization", "pricing actions" and "value-based pricing" point one way; "promotional intensity", "competitive pricing" and "price investments" point the other. Confirm the language with **gross margin** and **volume** over several quarters, and compare with a peer.

## Phrase guide

| MD&A phrase | What it can mean | Check with |
|:--|:--|:--|
| "Positive price realization" | Prices rose and stuck | Gross margin trend |
| "Pricing actions offset inflation" | Price raised to keep margin, not to expand it | Margin flat vs input costs |
| "Favourable mix" | Selling more of higher-margin products | Segment margins |
| "Surcharges" or "pass-through" | Costs passed on by contract | Revenue vs margin percentage |
| "Volume declines partially offset by pricing" | Price up but customers buying less | Volume trend over several quarters |
| "Promotional intensity" or "price investments" | Discounts to defend share | Gross margin falling |
| "Competitive pricing environment" | Rivals cutting price | Peer commentary |
| "Elasticity in line with expectations" | Some volume lost after price increases | Volume and churn data |

Words alone are weak evidence. Management chooses the framing. Treat each phrase as a question to check in the numbers; see [how to separate news from thesis](/investing/analyze/separate-news-from-thesis/).

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Revenue bridge found | Price, volume, mix, FX split for several quarters |
| Phrases logged | Key pricing words from MD&A and calls, by quarter |
| Gross margin checked | Direction matches the language |
| Volume checked | No hidden volume loss behind price gains |
| Peer compared | Same-period language and margins from a competitor |
| Verdict written | Strong, moderate, weak or unclear, with evidence |
