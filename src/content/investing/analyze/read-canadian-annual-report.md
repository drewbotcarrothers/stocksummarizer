A Canadian annual report can run 100–200 pages. You don't need an accounting degree to get value from it — you need a **reading order** and a short list of questions. This guide covers the financial statements and notes; the narrative sections have their own guides: [how to read an MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/) and [how to read an AIF](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-annual-information-form/).

Educational only — not financial advice. Example numbers are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Start with the MD&A for context, then read the **auditor's report** (any qualification or going-concern language?), the **balance sheet** (debt and liquidity), the **income statement** (revenue and margin trend), the **cash flow statement** (does profit turn into cash?), and finally the **notes** that explain segments, debt maturities and accounting choices. Compare two years side by side — every statement shows the prior year for exactly that reason.

## What's in a Canadian annual report

| Part | What it is | Time to spend |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Letter to shareholders | Management's story — marketing as much as information | 5 min |
| MD&A | Management's analysis of results, liquidity and risks | 30–45 min — [guide](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/) |
| Auditor's report | Independent opinion on the financial statements | 5 min |
| Financial statements | Balance sheet, income statement, comprehensive income, changes in equity, cash flows | 30 min |
| Notes to the statements | Accounting policies and detail behind every major number | 30+ min, selectively |

TSX-listed (non-venture) issuers must file annual audited statements and MD&A within **90 days** of year-end (120 days for venture issuers), so a December year-end company's annual report usually lands by the end of March. Find it on [SEDAR+](/investing/analyze/use-sedar-plus-for-stock-research/) or the company's investor relations page.

### 1. Auditor's report

Look for the word **"unmodified"** (clean) opinion. Red flags: a qualified or adverse opinion, a "material uncertainty related to going concern" paragraph, or a recent change of auditor. **Key audit matters**, where included, tell you which areas the auditor found hardest — often goodwill, revenue recognition or reserves.

### 2. Balance sheet (statement of financial position)

| Line | Question |
|:--|:--|
| Cash and current assets vs current liabilities | Can it pay the next 12 months of bills? |
| Total debt (short + long term + leases) | How big is debt relative to equity and cash flow? |
| Goodwill and intangibles | How much of "equity" is past acquisitions at book value? |
| Shareholders' equity | Rising from retained earnings, or from issuing shares? |

### 3. Income statement

Track revenue growth, gross margin and operating margin over two to five years. Watch for "other gains" or one-time items propping up net income — see [read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/). Earnings per share (basic and diluted) sits at the bottom; a widening gap signals [dilution](/investing/glossary/what-is-dilution/).

| Section | What to check |
|:--|:--|
| Operating | Is cash from operations close to, or above, net income over several years? |
| Investing | Capex vs depreciation — under-investing flatters free cash flow |
| Financing | Debt raised or repaid, shares issued or bought back, dividends paid |

This is where [free cash flow](/investing/glossary/what-is-free-cash-flow/) comes from; the deeper method is in [how to analyze free cash flow](/investing/analyze/free-cash-flow-analysis/).

### 5. The notes that matter most

| Note | Why |
|:--|:--|
| Segment information | Which divisions actually earn the profit |
| Debt / borrowings | Maturity schedule, interest rates, covenants |
| Revenue | How and when revenue is recognized |
| Share capital and share-based payments | Options, RSUs and share count changes |
| Commitments, contingencies, subsequent events | Obligations and surprises not yet on the balance sheet |
