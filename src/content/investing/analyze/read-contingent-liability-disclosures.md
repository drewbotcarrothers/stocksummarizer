A contingent liability is a possible obligation that depends on an uncertain future event, such as a lawsuit, a tax reassessment, an environmental claim or a guarantee. Most of it **does not appear on the balance sheet**. It sits in a note, often in careful legal language. For some companies, that note matters more than a quarter's earnings.

This post shows how to read those notes. It extends the IAS 37 overview in [how to read IFRS notes that trip up US-trained readers](/investing/analyze/read-ifrs-notes-us-trained-readers/) rather than repeating it. It is educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Find the **provisions note** and the **contingencies (or commitments and contingencies) note** in the annual financial statements, plus the legal proceedings section of the AIF. For each item, record: what it is, whether a **provision** was booked, the **amount or range** disclosed, the expected **timing**, and any **insurance or indemnity** recovery. Then compare the worst realistic outcome with cash, free cash flow and market cap. If the note says the company cannot estimate the effect, treat that as uncertainty to size, not as zero.

## The IAS 37 vocabulary

| Term in the note | What it means | How to read it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Provision | Liability recorded because an outflow is probable and can be estimated | Already in liabilities and earnings |
| Probable | More likely than not under IAS 37 | A lower bar than US GAAP's "probable" |
| Contingent liability | Possible obligation, or one that cannot be measured reliably | Disclosed only; not in liabilities |
| Remote | Outflow very unlikely | No disclosure required |
| Reimbursement | Expected recovery from insurance or another party | Recorded separately, only if virtually certain |
| "Seriously prejudicial" | Company withholds detail that could hurt its case | Allowed in rare cases; general nature still disclosed |

## Where to look

| Document | Section | What you get |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Annual financial statements | Provisions note | Opening balance, additions, uses, reversals, closing balance |
| Annual financial statements | Contingencies or commitments note | Lawsuits, tax disputes, guarantees, ranges if estimable |
| AIF | Legal proceedings and regulatory actions | Material claims, regulators involved, status |
| MD&A | Risks and uncertainties | Management's framing and any change in view |
| Interim reports (IAS 34) | Significant changes since year end | New claims or settlements during the year |
| Material change reports | Settlements or rulings | Dates and amounts once resolved |

## Types of contingencies and questions to ask

| Type | Common in | Questions |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Litigation and class actions | Consumer, financials, resource companies | Who is suing, for how much, and what stage is the case at? |
| Tax reassessments | Multinationals, insurers, resource companies | How much is disputed, and has a deposit been paid? |
| Environmental and reclamation | Miners, energy, industrials | Is it a provision (decommissioning) or a contingent claim? |
| Guarantees | Holding companies, parents of joint ventures | Whose debt is guaranteed, and up to what amount? |
| Regulatory penalties | Banks, telecoms, utilities | Is the regulator's process at an early or final stage? |
| Indemnities from divestitures | Serial sellers of businesses | How long do they last and are there caps? |

## Reading the provisions roll-forward

The provisions note usually shows a table that starts with the opening balance and adds new provisions, subtracts amounts used and reversed, and ends at the closing balance. Watch for:

- **Large reversals** that boost earnings in a quarter (and may be called a "one-time" gain; see [how to spot one-time items](/investing/analyze/spot-one-time-items-canadian-statements/)).
- **Repeated "unusual" additions** each year, which suggest a recurring cost.
- **Cash used** compared with the amount provided, which shows whether estimates were realistic.

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Notes found | Provisions and contingencies notes plus AIF legal section read |
| Items listed | Each claim recorded with type, status and amount or range |
| Provision vs disclosure split | You know what is already in liabilities |
| Recovery checked | Insurance and indemnities noted, with how certain they are |
| Downside sized | Worst realistic outcome compared with cash, FCF and market cap |
| Change tracked | Same items compared with the prior year's note |
