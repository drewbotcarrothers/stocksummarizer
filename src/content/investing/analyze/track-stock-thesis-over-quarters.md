A thesis that only lives in your head will quietly morph to match the stock price. Tracking it **on paper across quarters** is how you notice drift early — without inventing a new decision framework every time.

Educational only — not financial advice. Scores and examples are **hypothetical**. Use this with the [Thesis / Catalyst / Kill card](/investing/frameworks/thesis-catalyst-kill-criteria-card/) and [Bull / Bear / Watch brief](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/); do not replace those cards.

## Direct answer

After each earnings package, fill a one-page **QoQ scorecard**: restate the thesis in one sentence, score each pillar vs last quarter, note guidance and one-time items, check kill criteria, and write a one-line "what changed." Only then update catalysts or position sizing thoughts.

## What "changed" must mean

| Allowed change | Not a change |
|:--|:--|
| A pillar's evidence got stronger/weaker in filings | The stock is up/down |
| Guidance language shifted (raise/cut/narrow) | A broker target moved |
| A kill criterion tripped or nearly tripped | A sector ETF moved |
| Timeline for a catalyst slipped | Social media buzz |

## Go deeper

Store scorecards next to the brief you already use. If a pillar score falls for two quarters, that is a prompt to re-read the [MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/) and [earnings call transcript](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-earnings-call-transcript/) — not an automatic sell signal, and not advice.
