A great product sold mostly to one buyer is still a **fragile cash-flow machine**. Customer concentration shows up in AIF risk factors, revenue notes, and sometimes segment stories. DIY investors who skip it discover the risk when a contract non-renewal hits the press release.

Educational only — not financial advice. Concentration figures below are **hypothetical**. Pair with [revenue quality](/investing/analyze/analyze-revenue-quality-canadian-issuers/) and [geographic mix](/investing/analyze/analyze-geographic-revenue-mix-canadian-multinationals/).

## Direct answer

Read the AIF risk factors for customer-concentration language. Check the revenue note (and MD&A) for top-customer percentages when disclosed. If numbers are absent, treat qualitative "substantial portion" wording as a flag and estimate from contract / segment clues carefully. Stress a hypothetical loss of the largest account on revenue and margin. Put the result in your brief as watch or kill criteria — not as a vibe.

## Where concentration hides

| Source | What you may find | Limit |
|:--|:--|:--|
| AIF risk factors | Narrative on major customers | Often qualitative |
| Revenue note | % from major customers (IFRS 15 / entity-specific) | Disclosure thresholds vary |
| Segment note | One segment dominated by few buyers | May not name customers |
| MD&A | Contract wins/losses, renewals | Selective emphasis |
| Circular / RPT | Sales to related parties | Different risk flavour |

## Hypothetical stress

| Case (hyp.) | Revenue | Gross margin | Brief action |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Base | $100M | 40% | — |
| Lose Customer 1 (28%) | $72M | 36% (mix shift) | Revisit valuation & leverage |
| Lose top 3 (48%) | $52M | 32% | Likely kill / pass unless replaced |

Label stresses **hypothetical**. Real contract terms, switching costs, and replacement pipelines matter — read them when disclosed.
