Earnings are an opinion; cash is a fact — mostly. **Free cash flow (FCF)** is the cash a business generates after paying to keep itself running, and it is what ultimately funds dividends, buybacks and debt repayment. This hub goes beyond the [FCF glossary definition](/investing/glossary/what-is-free-cash-flow/) to show how to use FCF in actual stock decisions.

Educational only — not financial advice. Worked numbers below are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Build FCF yourself from the cash-flow statement: **cash from operations − capital expenditures − lease principal payments** (the IFRS 16 adjustment Canadian investors often miss). Look at it on a trailing-12-month and multi-year basis, **per share**, and compare it to what the company pays out. Use it to test dividend safety, earnings quality and valuation (P/FCF).

## Build FCF from the statement

| Line (cash-flow statement) | Where to find it | Note |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Cash from operating activities | Operating section total | Check whether interest paid sits here or in financing (IFRS allows a choice) |
| − Purchases of property, plant and equipment | Investing section | Some issuers split sustaining vs growth capex in the MD&A |
| − Purchases of intangibles / capitalized development | Investing section | Matters for software and telecom |
| − Principal portion of lease payments | **Financing** section | Under IFRS 16, lease costs move out of operating cash flow — subtract them to compare fairly |
| = Free cash flow (your definition) | | Write the definition down and use it consistently |

## Five questions FCF answers

### 1. Are earnings turning into cash?

FCF conversion = FCF ÷ net income. Over several years, a healthy, mature business tends to sit near or above 1. Persistently well below 1 suggests working-capital build-up, heavy reinvestment, or aggressive accounting — read the [MD&A liquidity section](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/).

### 2. Is the dividend safe?

| FCF payout (dividends ÷ FCF, trailing 12 months) | Typical reading |
|:--|:--|
| Below ~60% | Comfortable cushion for most businesses |
| ~60–90% | Covered, but check cyclicality and debt plans |
| ~90–100% | Thin; one bad year forces a choice |
| Above 100% | Funded by debt, asset sales or new shares — find out why |

*Rules of thumb only. Utilities, pipelines and REITs often use sector-specific cash measures (e.g. distributable cash flow, AFFO), so compare within sector.*

### 3. How much is maintenance vs growth?

Capital-heavy TSX sectors — producers, pipelines, utilities, telecom, rails — spend a lot on capex. Growth capex can depress FCF today and raise it later; sustaining capex never comes back. If the MD&A splits them, track both.

### 4. Is FCF per share growing?

Total FCF can rise while per-share FCF stagnates if the company keeps issuing shares. Divide by diluted shares every year.

| Year | FCF ($M, hypothetical) | Diluted shares (M) | FCF per share |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Year 1 | 240 | 100 | $2.40 |
| Year 2 | 270 | 108 | $2.50 |
| Year 3 | 290 | 118 | $2.46 |

*FCF grew 21% in total, but FCF per share barely moved — dilution ate the growth.*

### 5. What is the market paying for it?

Price ÷ FCF per share (or market cap ÷ FCF) gives **P/FCF**; its inverse is the FCF yield. Full guide: [what P/FCF means for Canadian DIY investors](/investing/valuation/what-p-fcf-means-canadian-diy/).

## Sector cautions

| Sector | FCF caveat |
|:--|:--|
| Banks and insurers | FCF isn't meaningful — use capital ratios, ROE and book value ([bank valuation](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/)) |
| Oil and gas, miners | Swings with commodity prices — look at a cycle, not a year |
| Pipelines, utilities | Large growth capex; check distributable cash flow and leverage |
| Software | Stock-based compensation boosts FCF; consider subtracting it |
| REITs | Use AFFO; property acquisitions aren't "capex" in the usual sense |

## Checklist

- [ ] FCF definition written down (including lease principal)
- [ ] 3–5 years of FCF and FCF per share in a table
- [ ] FCF payout ratio vs dividends + buybacks
- [ ] Sustaining vs growth capex noted where disclosed
- [ ] Compared with 3–5 [true peers](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/)
