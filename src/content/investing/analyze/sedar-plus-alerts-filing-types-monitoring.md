Most guides to SEDAR+ — including our own [step-by-step SEDAR+ research guide](/investing/analyze/use-sedar-plus-for-stock-research/) — focus on researching a company **before** you buy. This post is about the other 95% of the holding period: **monitoring** the stocks you already own, so you notice the filings that matter without reading everything a company files.

Educational only — not advice. SEDAR+ features change; check the [SEDAR+ help pages](https://systems.securities-administrators.ca/onlinehelp/general-help/getting-started/general-public-access/) for the current options.

## Direct answer

Set up SEDAR+'s free **financial document email alerts** for your holdings (up to 50 companies per email address; alerts cover documents such as annual and interim financial statements and MD&A, and arrive in a daily evening email). Those alerts **don't** cover news releases, material change reports, circulars or most other filings, so add the company's own investor-relations email list and do a short **weekly sweep** of each holding's recent SEDAR+ filings. Check **SEDI** separately for insider trades. Then triage by filing type: a material change report, change of auditor, late filing, or a large equity raise deserves a same-week read against your kill criteria; routine filings can wait for the quarterly review.

| Tool | What it catches | What it misses |
|:--|:--|:--|
| SEDAR+ financial document alerts | Annual and interim financial statements and MD&A for selected companies | News releases, material change reports, circulars, prospectuses |
| SEDAR+ cease trade order / disciplined list alerts | Cease trade orders (for example, for failing to file) | Normal filings |
| Company investor-relations email | News releases, sometimes presentations | Regulatory-only filings like early warning reports |
| Weekly SEDAR+ search by company | Everything filed publicly | Nothing, but it's manual |
| SEDI | Insider purchases and sales | Company filings |

## Which filing types matter for an existing holding

| Filing type | What it usually signals | Priority for a holder |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Interim / annual financial statements + MD&A | The scheduled update to your thesis numbers | **Review** at each release — see [how to read quarterly earnings](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-quarterly-earnings-tsx/) |
| Material change report | A significant change in the business (deal, CEO exit, major contract, guidance shock) | **High** — read within days |
| News release | Results, dividend changes, deals, guidance | Medium to high depending on content |
| Management information circular | Meeting agenda, executive pay, board changes, sometimes a deal vote | Medium; high if there's a transaction |
| Prospectus or shelf supplement | New shares or debt being issued | **High** if it means dilution — check size and price |
| Early warning report | Someone crossed 10% ownership (or changed a large stake) | Medium; can signal activism or a takeover |
| Business acquisition report | Details on a significant acquisition | Medium; useful for checking the price paid |
| Change of auditor notice | The auditor resigned or was replaced | **High** — read the reasons and any "reportable events" |
| Technical report (miners, NI 43-101) | Updated resources, reserves or mine plans | High for mining holdings |
| Annual Information Form | Risk factors and business description update | Annual skim — see [how to read an AIF](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-annual-information-form/) |

A company that files close to — or past — these deadlines is telling you something. A missed filing deadline can lead to a cease trade order, which is why the CTO alert is worth turning on for small caps.

## Triage: what to do with each alert

| Signal | Action |
|:--|:--|
| Routine quarterly filing, no surprises | Update your brief's numbers; check kill criteria; move on |
| Material change report or change of auditor | Same-week read; write down whether any [sell criterion](/investing/process/document-sell-criteria-before-you-buy/) is affected |
| Equity raise | Compute dilution: new shares ÷ existing shares, and the discount to market |
| Late filing or cease trade order | Treat as a thesis-level event; governance and liquidity risk rise |
| Insider buying or selling on SEDI | Context only; one sale rarely means much, clusters can |
| Restatement of prior results | High priority; revisit every number you relied on |

## A sample weekly sweep (15 minutes, hypothetical)

| Step | Time |
|:--|--:|
| Open the evening alert emails from the past week | 3 min |
| SEDAR+ search: each holding, filings from the last 7 days | 7 min |
| SEDI: holdings with insider activity this month | 3 min |
| Log anything material in each stock's brief | 2 min |

Keep a single log per holding — date, filing type, one-line takeaway, any kill-criterion impact. That log is also your evidence of an investing (not trading) process.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Relying only on the financial document alerts | Misses material change reports and deals | Add IR email and a weekly sweep |
| Reading every filing in full | Burnout, then nothing gets read | Triage by filing type |
| Ignoring circulars | Executive pay and dilution live there | Skim at least the summary and votes |
| Treating insider sales as a signal by themselves | Many are for taxes or diversification | Look for clusters and context |
| Not checking SEDI | Insider data isn't on SEDAR+ | Add SEDI to the monthly routine |
