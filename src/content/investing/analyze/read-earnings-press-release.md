On earnings day, most people see the stock move first and read the news second. That order makes you rationalize the chart. A better habit: **read the press release before you look at the price** — and read it from the tables up, not from the headline down.

Educational only — not financial advice. Examples are **hypothetical**. This post goes deeper on step 1 of [how to read quarterly earnings for TSX companies](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-quarterly-earnings-tsx/), which covers the full quarterly package (release, MD&A, cash flow) and the beat-or-miss trap.

## Direct answer

An earnings press release is the company's first, official, and most *framed* version of the quarter. Skip the headline and CEO quote at first. Read the **reported (IFRS) results table** against last year, then the **non-GAAP reconciliation** to see what "adjusted" removed, then the **segment table**, then **guidance** compared with what was said last quarter. Write a one-line verdict — and only then read the headline and look at the chart.

## Anatomy of a release

| Section | What it is | How much to trust it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Headline and bullet highlights | The numbers management wants quoted | Low — chosen for impact |
| CEO / CFO quote | Narrative and tone | Low — useful for tone only |
| Consolidated results table | Revenue, operating income, net income, EPS vs prior year | High — same figures as the filed statements |
| Non-GAAP measures and reconciliation | "Adjusted" EPS, EBITDA, FFO and how they're built | Medium — rules require reconciliation, but choices vary |
| Segment results | Revenue and profit by division | High |
| Outlook / guidance | Management's targets | Medium — forward-looking, compare to last quarter |
| Dividend and buyback | Declared dividend, buyback activity | High |
| Forward-looking statements caution | Legal boilerplate | Skim for new risk wording |
| Conference call details | Date and webcast link | Use for the [transcript](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-earnings-call-transcript/) |

| Headline says | Check | Where |
|:--|:--|:--|
| "Record adjusted EPS" | Reported EPS and the items removed | Reconciliation table |
| "Revenue up 15%" | Organic growth vs acquisitions and currency | Segment table, MD&A |
| "Strong free cash flow" | The company's own definition | Non-GAAP section |
| "Reaffirms guidance" | Whether the range quietly narrowed toward the low end | Outlook vs last quarter's release |
| "Beat expectations" | Whose expectations? Consensus isn't in the filing | Treat as noise — see the beat-or-miss trap in the [quarterly earnings guide](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-quarterly-earnings-tsx/) |

Canadian rules require non-GAAP measures in news releases to be shown alongside the most directly comparable IFRS measure with no less prominence, and reconciled. The details of what to check are in [read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/).
