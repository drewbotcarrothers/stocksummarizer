Most Canadian public companies report under **IFRS**. Many investing books, courses and screeners are built around **US GAAP**. The statements look alike, but some notes work differently, and a reader trained on US companies can draw the wrong conclusion from a familiar-looking number. This matters most when you compare a TSX company with a US peer.

This post lists the IFRS notes that most often trip up US-trained readers and what to check in each. It builds on [how to read a Canadian annual report](/investing/analyze/read-canadian-annual-report/) and [SEDAR+ vs EDGAR](/investing/analyze/sedar-plus-vs-edgar-us-stocks/). It is educational, not accounting or investment advice. Standards change; confirm current rules with the IFRS Foundation and FASB.

## Direct answer

Before comparing a Canadian IFRS reporter with a US GAAP peer, read six notes: **leases** (IFRS 16 lifts EBITDA and operating cash flow), **cash flow classification** (interest and dividends can sit in different sections), **impairment** (reversals are allowed under IFRS, except for goodwill), **development costs** (can be capitalized under IFRS), **property and investment property** (fair value or revaluation models are allowed), and **provisions** (a lower "probable" threshold). Also check which framework the company uses: some Canadian SEC registrants report under US GAAP.

## The notes that trip people up

| Area | IFRS | US GAAP | What to do |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Leases | IFRS 16: almost all leases on balance sheet; depreciation plus interest | ASC 842: operating leases on balance sheet, but one straight-line lease cost in operating expenses | Compare EBITDA and operating cash flow after leases; see [CFO vs net income](/investing/analyze/read-cfo-vs-net-income/) |
| Interest and dividends in cash flow | IAS 7 allows a choice of section (until IFRS 18 amendments take effect) | Interest paid is operating; dividends paid are financing | Read the cash flow policy note before comparing |
| Impairment reversals | Allowed for most assets except goodwill (IAS 36) | Not allowed for assets held and used | Look for reversal gains in "other income" |
| Development costs | Capitalized when IAS 38 criteria are met | Generally expensed (with exceptions such as some software) | Add back capitalized development when comparing margins |
| Investment property and PP&E | Fair value model (IAS 40) or revaluation model (IAS 16) allowed | Historical cost | Separate fair value gains from operating results |
| Inventory | LIFO not allowed (IAS 2) | LIFO allowed | Check US peers for a LIFO reserve |
| Provisions | Recognized when "probable" means more likely than not (IAS 37) | "Probable" means likely, a higher threshold | Expect earlier, larger provisions under IFRS |
| Expense presentation | By function or by nature | Generally by function | Map cost lines before comparing margins |

## Coming change: IFRS 18

IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027. It adds defined subtotals such as **operating profit**, sets new categories in the income statement and requires companies to explain **management-defined performance measures** in a note. It also removes the cash flow classification choice for interest and dividends for most companies. Expect restated comparatives in the first year; see [how to read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/).

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Framework confirmed | IFRS or US GAAP stated in the basis-of-preparation note |
| Lease effect noted | EBITDA and cash flow compared on a lease-adjusted basis |
| Cash flow policy read | Where interest and dividends are classified |
| Impairments checked | Any reversals, and goodwill tested separately |
| Capitalized development noted | Amount capitalized vs expensed |
| Fair value gains separated | Investment property or revaluation gains excluded from operating view |
