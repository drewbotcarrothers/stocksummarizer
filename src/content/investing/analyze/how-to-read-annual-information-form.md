The **Annual Information Form (AIF)** is the Canadian filing most DIY investors never open, and it is often the clearest description of what a company actually is. Where the MD&A explains the year, the AIF explains the **business, its structure, and what could break it**.

Educational only — not financial advice. Filing requirements can change; check the issuer's latest documents and the regulators' rules.

## Direct answer

The AIF is an annual disclosure document required of most non-venture Canadian reporting issuers under National Instrument 51-102 (Form 51-102F2), usually filed on SEDAR+ within 90 days of fiscal year-end. For stock analysis, read **the business description, risk factors, capital structure, dividend history, and (for resource companies) the reserves or mineral project summaries**. Skip the boilerplate on transfer agents and experts unless you need it.

## AIF vs MD&A vs financial statements

| | AIF | MD&A | Financial statements |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Frequency | Annual | Annual + each quarter | Annual (audited) + quarterly |
| Main job | Describe the business and its risks | Explain results and liquidity | Report the numbers |
| Best for | Business model, structure, risk inventory | Drivers, segments, guidance | Hard figures and notes |
| Changes slowly? | Yes — compare year to year | No | No |
| Venture (TSXV) issuers | Generally not required | Required | Required |

*Because the AIF changes slowly, the **differences** between two years' AIFs are often more informative than either document alone.*

### General development of the business

A three-year history of acquisitions, divestitures, financings and strategic shifts. Read this before the earnings headlines: it tells you whether the company you're looking at today is the same company that built the five-year chart.

### Description of the business

Segments, products, customers, competitive position, seasonality, employees, and regulatory environment. Write a plain-English answer to "how does this make money?" If you can't, you aren't ready for valuation.

### Risk factors

Long and lawyered, but useful if you read it **comparatively**:

1. Put last year's and this year's risk sections side by side.
2. Mark risks that are **new**, moved **up**, or got **longer**.
3. Separate company-specific risks from generic market risks.
4. Pick the two or three you think are mispriced and turn them into kill criteria.

### Capital structure and market for securities

Share classes, voting rights (dual-class structures are common on the TSX), preferred shares, convertibles, and trading history. Dual-class control matters for governance and takeover potential.

### Dividends and distributions

Historical dividends per share and any stated policy or restrictions (for example, debt covenants). Cross-check against free cash flow, not just earnings — see the [dividend investing hub](/investing/dividends/how-dividend-investing-works-canadian-diy/).

### Sector-specific disclosure

- **Oil and gas:** reserves data and related information under NI 51-101 (often in or referenced by the AIF). Note reserve life, proved vs probable mix, and future development costs.
- **Mining:** summaries of material mineral projects under NI 43-101. Note project stage, resource vs reserve classification, and key assumptions.
- **Banks and insurers:** less operational detail here; the capital and risk sections in the annual report do most of the work.

## A 45-minute AIF reading checklist

| Step | Question | Done |
|:--|:--|:--:|
| 1 | What changed in the business in the last three years? | ☐ |
| 2 | Can I explain segments and customers in two sentences? | ☐ |
| 3 | Which risk factors are new or moved up vs last year? | ☐ |
| 4 | Is there a dual-class or control shareholder? | ☐ |
| 5 | Any dividend restrictions or covenants? | ☐ |
| 6 | For resource names: reserve/resource basis and key assumptions noted? | ☐ |
| 7 | Material contracts or legal proceedings that could matter? | ☐ |
