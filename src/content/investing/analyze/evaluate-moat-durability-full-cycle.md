A competitive moat (lasting advantage that protects returns, such as low costs, switching costs, network effects, regulation or a strong brand) is easy to describe at a cycle peak. Almost every company looks protected when demand is strong. The real test is whether the advantage **holds through a downturn and recovers after it**.

This post goes beyond the [one-page competitive moat checklist](/investing/analyze/build-one-page-competitive-moat-checklist/). That checklist identifies the source of a moat. This one tests whether it **lasts**, using evidence from a full cycle. It pairs with [how to stress-test a stock thesis against a recession scenario](/investing/analyze/stress-test-stock-thesis-recession-scenario/). It is educational and does not rate any company; numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Judge moat durability with evidence from the **last full cycle**: (1) did **pricing and gross margin** hold up in the downturn; (2) did **customers stay** (retention, churn, renewals); (3) did the company **gain or lose market share**; and (4) how fast did **return on invested capital** recover to above the cost of capital? Then ask what has changed since: technology, regulation, new competitors or customer behaviour. A moat that held last time can still erode if its source is weakening.

## Durability tests and the evidence for each

| Test | Evidence to collect | Durable sign | Warning sign |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Pricing | Gross margin by year; price vs volume commentary | Margin dips modestly, recovers | Deep discounting, margin does not recover |
| Customers | Retention, churn, backlog, renewals | Retention stable | Customers switch or delay renewals |
| Market share | Industry data, competitor results | Share flat or rising | Share lost to cheaper rivals |
| Returns | ROIC vs cost of capital over the cycle | Stays above cost of capital | Falls below and stays there |
| Reinvestment | Capex and R&D through the downturn | Keeps investing | Cuts that weaken the product |
| Balance sheet | Leverage at the trough | Room to act | Forced asset sales or dilution |

## What erodes moats over time

| Moat source | What can erode it | Filing clue |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Cost advantage | New technology, cheaper imports | Margin compression vs peers |
| Switching costs | Easier data migration, open standards | Rising churn, shorter contracts |
| Network effects | A larger or niche rival network | Slowing user or volume growth |
| Regulation or licences | Policy changes, new entrants allowed | [Regulatory risk disclosures](/investing/analyze/analyze-regulatory-risk-disclosures-aif/) |
| Brand | Shifts in customer preference, private labels | Higher promotional spending |
| Scale | Demand falling below fixed-cost base | [Operating leverage](/investing/analyze/analyze-operating-leverage/) turning negative |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Last downturn identified | Years and cause noted |
| Margins and returns logged | Gross margin and ROIC through that period |
| Customer evidence logged | Retention or churn where disclosed |
| Share evidence noted | At least one industry source |
| Moat source re-checked | Changes since the last downturn listed |
| Verdict written | Durable, fading or unproven, with reasons |
