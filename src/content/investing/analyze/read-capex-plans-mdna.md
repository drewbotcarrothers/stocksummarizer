MD&A sections on liquidity and capital resources are where management tells you what they plan to **build, replace, and defer**. Reading those plans well is half of free-cash-flow analysis.

Educational only — not financial advice. Dollar figures are **hypothetical**. Pair with [How to read an MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/) and [free cash flow analysis](/investing/analyze/free-cash-flow-analysis/).

## Direct answer

In the MD&A, locate planned and historical capital expenditures, split maintenance vs growth as best you can, compare total capex to depreciation, and recompute simple FCF both with and without growth projects. Ask whether growth capex has a stated return logic — or is just "investing in the business."

## Maintenance vs growth (practical tells)

| Signal | Lean maintenance | Lean growth |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Language | Sustain, replace, integrity, sustaining capital | Expand, new facility, new market, growth capital |
| Vs D&A | Near depreciation over time | Sustained capex >> D&A |
| MD&A outlook | Flat sustaining budget | Multi-year project table with dates |
| Risk if cut | Asset integrity / safety | Delayed volume or share |

Canadian MD&A content expectations sit in NI 51-102 Form 51-102F1 (and related guidance). IFRS cash flow statements classify most PPE purchases as investing outflows.
