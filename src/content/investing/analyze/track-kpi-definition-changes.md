Key performance indicators (KPIs) such as subscribers, same-store sales, active users, backlog, production per day or "adjusted" margins are often the numbers that move a stock. Unlike IFRS line items, companies choose how to define them, and they **can change the definition**. A quiet change can make growth look faster, hide a slowdown or break a long history you rely on.

This post shows how to spot definition changes and keep your KPI history comparable. It builds on [how to read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/) and [how to track a stock thesis over quarters](/investing/analyze/track-stock-thesis-over-quarters/). It is educational; examples are hypothetical and no company is being rated.

## Direct answer

Each quarter, read the **definition note** for every KPI you track (usually in the MD&A "non-GAAP and other financial measures" section or a glossary at the back). Compare it with last year's wording. If the definition changed, look for the **company's explanation, a reconciliation or restated prior periods**. If there is no bridge, mark a **break** in your series and stop comparing across it. A KPI that is redefined often, always in a flattering direction, is itself a signal.

## Why definitions change (good and bad reasons)

| Reason given | Often legitimate when | Be careful when |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Acquisition or merger | Combined company needs one definition | Acquired volume is folded into "organic" metrics |
| New product or segment | Old KPI no longer covers the business | The new KPI starts right as the old one weakened |
| Alignment with peers | Makes comparisons easier | Only the flattering parts of the peer definition are copied |
| Regulator or standard change | NI 52-112 or IFRS changes force it | Change is used to restart a long history |
| "Better reflects how we manage the business" | Segment reporting also changed (IFRS 8) | Nothing else in the business changed |

## Where the change shows up

| Place | What to look for |
|:--|:--|
| MD&A non-GAAP and other measures section | Changed wording, new inclusions or exclusions, an explanation of why |
| KPI glossary or "key metrics" table | New footnotes, "revised" or "recast" labels |
| Segment note (IFRS 8) | Restated prior-period segment figures after a reorganization |
| Earnings call | Phrases like "on a comparable basis", "recast", "new methodology" |
| Investor day slides | A new headline metric replacing one used for years |

Canadian issuers that report non-GAAP measures, ratios and supplementary financial measures must follow **NI 52-112**. Among other things, it asks issuers to explain the composition of those measures and to explain changes from prior periods. For segments, **IFRS 8** generally requires prior-period segment information to be restated when the segment structure changes, unless the information is not available. Operating KPIs that are not financial measures (for example, wells drilled or stores opened) have fewer rules, so you need to read the footnotes yourself.

## Common definition changes to watch

| KPI type | Typical change | Effect on the trend |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Subscribers or users | Counting bundles, wholesale or inactive accounts | Higher count, lower revenue per user |
| Same-store sales | Changing how long a store must be open to count | Can drop weak new stores or add strong ones |
| Backlog or bookings | Including options, framework deals or unfunded orders | Larger backlog, lower conversion |
| Adjusted EBITDA or EPS | Adding new add-backs (restructuring, share-based pay) | Higher margins, weaker link to cash |
| Production | Switching from gross to net, or adding royalty volumes | Step change in volumes |
| Recurring revenue | Moving services or usage revenue into "recurring" | Higher recurring share |

## Keep a KPI definition log

| Column | Example entry |
|:--|:--|
| KPI | Active subscribers |
| Definition (verbatim) | Copy the exact wording from the filing |
| Source and date | Q3 MD&A, filed on SEDAR+ |
| Change vs last period | "Now includes wholesale lines" |
| Reason given | "Aligns with acquired business" |
| Bridge provided? | Yes, one overlap quarter / No |
| Your action | Restart series from Q4; compare only like-for-like |

Store the log with your research notes; see [how to keep stock research notes that compound over years](/investing/analyze/keep-stock-research-notes-that-compound/).

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Definitions copied | Exact wording saved for each KPI you track |
| Year-over-year compared | Current wording matched against the same quarter last year |
| Bridge found or not | Reconciliation, restatement or overlap period noted |
| Series marked | Breaks flagged in your spreadsheet or notes |
| Pattern judged | Number and direction of past changes recorded |
| Thesis updated | KPI-based thesis points re-checked on the new basis |
