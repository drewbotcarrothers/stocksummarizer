Industrials cover a wide group on the TSX: equipment dealers, engineering and construction firms, aerospace suppliers, waste haulers, trucking and logistics, building products and more. Many of them are **cyclical**: orders, pricing and margins rise and fall with capital spending, housing, freight volumes and government budgets. The operating-company default (margins, free cash flow, a P/E) still works, but it needs **timing clues**, because a cheap-looking P/E at a cycle peak can be the most expensive point to buy.

This post is an **analysis checklist** for industrials, like the sector checklists for [banks](/investing/analyze/analyze-banks-vs-operating-companies/) and [retail](/investing/analyze/analyze-retail-consumer-canadian-footprints/). It links our existing explainers rather than repeating them. It is educational and does not rate any company.

## Direct answer

For an industrial, add five habits to the default: (1) read **orders, backlog and book-to-bill** before revenue; (2) split revenue growth into **price and volume**; (3) check **margin in the backlog** (fixed-price vs cost-plus contracts); (4) watch **working capital** as the cycle turns; and (5) value the business on **mid-cycle earnings**, not the latest quarter. The disclosure mechanics are in [how to read order backlog and book-to-bill](/investing/analyze/read-order-backlog-book-to-bill/).

## Habit swap table

| Default habit | Why it misleads for industrials | Industrial replacement | Read this |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Trailing P/E | Peak earnings make the multiple look low | P/E on mid-cycle or average earnings | [When P/E misleads](/investing/valuation/when-pe-misleads-tsx/) |
| Revenue growth | Hides the order trend | Orders, book-to-bill, backlog months | [Backlog and book-to-bill](/investing/analyze/read-order-backlog-book-to-bill/) |
| Gross margin trend | Can lag backlog pricing | Margin on new orders vs backlog; contract type | [Gross margin trends](/investing/analyze/analyze-gross-margin-trends/) |
| Revenue as one number | Mixes price and volume | Price vs volume split, organic vs acquired | [Organic vs acquisition growth](/investing/analyze/compare-organic-vs-acquisition-growth/) |
| Free cash flow in one year | Working capital swings with the cycle | FCF over a full cycle; inventory and receivables | [Working capital swings](/investing/analyze/analyze-working-capital-swings-quarterly/) |
| Recurring revenue assumed | Equipment sales are lumpy | Share of service, parts and aftermarket | [Recurring vs cyclical revenue](/investing/analyze/identify-recurring-vs-cyclical-revenue/) |

## Cycle clues and where to find them

| Clue | What it can signal | Where to look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Book-to-bill under 1.0x for two or more quarters | Backlog shrinking; revenue may slow later | MD&A, quarterly press release |
| Cancellations or deferrals mentioned | Customers pulling back | MD&A outlook, earnings call |
| Inventory rising faster than sales | Demand slowing or supply buffers | Balance sheet, working capital note |
| Price increases slowing while volume is flat | Pricing power fading | MD&A revenue discussion |
| Aftermarket or service share rising | More stable revenue base | Segment or revenue disaggregation note |
| Guidance language shifting to "normalizing" | Management signalling a softer period | [Guidance language](/investing/analyze/evaluate-management-guidance-language/) |

## Contract type matters

| Contract type | Who carries cost risk | What to check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Fixed-price | The company | Cost overrun disclosures, provisions for loss-making contracts |
| Cost-plus or reimbursable | Mostly the customer | Fee margin, volume of work |
| Unit-rate or per-hour | Shared | Utilization and rate changes |
| Long-term service agreements | The company, over time | Renewal rates, inflation clauses |

IFRS 15 requires companies to disaggregate revenue and explain contract balances. That note is often the best place to see how much revenue is recognized over time versus at a point in time.

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Order trend logged | Book-to-bill and backlog for at least six quarters |
| Revenue split | Price, volume, FX and acquisitions separated where disclosed |
| Contract mix noted | Fixed-price share and any loss provisions |
| Working capital tracked | Inventory and receivables days vs sales growth |
| Mid-cycle earnings estimated | Average margin over a past cycle, not just the latest year |
| Aftermarket share noted | Service and parts share of revenue |
