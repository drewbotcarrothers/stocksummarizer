Peer tables are powerful — and easy to **rig**. Cherry-picking comps that make your target look cheap is not relative valuation; it is advocacy. This page is how to build peer sets with rules you could defend to a sceptical friend.

Educational only — not financial advice. Scatter points are **hypothetical**. Pair with [compare two TSX peers](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/), [triangulating multiples](/investing/valuation/triangulating-multiples-dcf-peer-checks/), and [when P/E misleads](/investing/valuation/when-pe-misleads-tsx/).

## Direct answer

Write the investment question first. Define peer-screen rules (size, model, geography, cycle) **before** listing tickers. Build the table on aligned periods and definitions. Keep inconvenient comps and explain structural differences. Show median and range, not only the flattering average. If you exclude a name, cite the rule.

## Minimum peer-table hygiene

| Hygiene item | Pass | Fail |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Screen written first | Rules in notes | "These are the comps management uses" alone |
| Period alignment | Same LTM / FY | Mix of stale years |
| Metric definition | IFRS or labelled non-GAAP | EBITDA soup |
| Exclusions logged | Rule cited | Silent deletions |
| Context | Sector primer linked | Pure multiple shopping |
