**Same-store sales (SSS)** — also called comparable-store sales — and related **volume / traffic** metrics are core for retailers, restaurants, and some consumer chains. They try to show growth from the ongoing store base, stripping new openings. DIY investors get hurt when they compare two SSS prints that do not share a definition.

Educational only — not financial advice. Figures are **hypothetical**. Pair with [recurring vs cyclical](/investing/analyze/identify-recurring-vs-cyclical-revenue/), [gross margin trends](/investing/analyze/analyze-gross-margin-trends/), and [peer tables without cherry-picking](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/).

## Direct answer

Copy the issuer's SSS definition (store age, what is excluded, whether e-commerce is inside). Split growth into **traffic/transactions**, **units**, and **price/mix** when disclosed. Normalize for openings, closures, renovations, calendar, and FX. Only then compare to peers on the same basis. Rising SSS on falling traffic is a different thesis than rising traffic with stable ticket.

## Definition traps

| Trap | Why it matters | What to write in notes |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Different age cutoffs (e.g. 12 vs 13 months) | Peer SSS not comparable | Note each cutoff |
| E-comm included vs excluded | Omnichannel shifts distort | Flag treatment |
| Store closures remove weak stores | SSS can "improve" while network shrinks | Track store count + SSS together |
| Easy prior-year lap | Soft comps inflate growth | Annotate lap difficulty |
| Fuel / tobacco / lottery carve-outs | Mix changes move headline | Use issuer bridge if given |
