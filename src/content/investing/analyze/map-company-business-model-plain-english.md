A stock thesis that starts with a multiple and never explains **how the company makes money** is a vibe, not a model. Mapping the business in plain English — customers, offer, delivery, monetization, constraints — is the cheapest diligence step you can take before SEDAR+ deep-dives or peer tables.

Educational only — not financial advice. Worked figures below are **hypothetical**. Pair with [segment reporting](/investing/analyze/read-segment-reporting-canadian-filings/), [revenue quality](/investing/analyze/analyze-revenue-quality-canadian-issuers/), and [Bull / Bear / Watch](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/).

## Direct answer

Write five sentences: (1) who the customer is and what job they hire the company for; (2) what is sold; (3) how it is delivered (assets, partners, channels); (4) how cash arrives (price metric, mix, recurring vs transactional); (5) what must stay true for margins (unit costs, regulation, cycle). If you cannot fill a box from filings, mark it **unknown** — do not invent it from a pitch deck.

## Filing places that feed the map

| Model box | Primary filing clues | Secondary clues |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Customers | AIF business description; MD&A customers / concentration | Segment note; customer % if disclosed |
| Offer | Products/services section; IP / brands | R&D note; marketing claims (treat lightly) |
| Delivery | Properties, plants, logistics, franchises | Capex plans; supplier concentration |
| Monetization | Revenue recognition note (IFRS 15); mix commentary | Contract liabilities / deferred revenue |
| Constraints | Risk factors; covenants; regulation | Pension, leases, commodity exposures |
