Four times a year every TSX company tells you how the business is going. Most investors read the headline ("beat by 3 cents") and the share-price reaction, then move on. A DIY investor can do much better in about 45 minutes with a fixed routine.

Educational only — not financial advice.

## Direct answer

For each quarterly report: read the **press release** for the headline and guidance, the **interim MD&A** for drivers and liquidity, and the **cash-flow statement** for reality. Compare against **your own prior notes** (what you expected and what management promised), not just against analyst consensus. Update your thesis only when a KPI that matters moves — not because the stock moved.

## Step 1 — The press release (10 minutes)

Capture these in your notes before you read any commentary:

- Revenue and IFRS net income/EPS, plus the company's adjusted figures
- The one or two KPIs that drive this business (see table below)
- **Guidance**: raised, maintained, cut, withdrawn — and on what
- Dividend or buyback changes

## Step 2 — The KPIs that actually matter by sector

| Sector | KPIs to track every quarter | Sector guide |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Banks | Net interest margin, provisions for credit losses, CET1 ratio, efficiency ratio, ROE | [Banks](/sectors/banks/) |
| Insurers | Core EPS, LICAT ratio, new business value, book value per share | [Insurers](/sectors/insurers/) |
| Oil and gas producers | Production per share, realized price vs benchmark, operating cost, FCF | [Oil and gas](/sectors/oil-gas-producers/) |
| Midstream and pipelines | Adjusted EBITDA, distributable cash flow, payout ratio, net debt/EBITDA | [Midstream](/sectors/midstream-pipelines/) |
| Gold miners | Production, all-in sustaining cost (AISC), FCF, reserves | [Gold](/sectors/gold-precious-metals/) |
| Rails and transport | Operating ratio, volumes (carloads/RTMs), fuel cost | [Transportation](/sectors/transportation/) |
| REITs | Occupancy, same-property NOI, FFO/AFFO per unit, payout | [REITs](/sectors/real-estate-reits/) |
| Software and tech | Organic growth, recurring revenue, margins, FCF | [Software and tech](/sectors/software-tech/) |

## Step 3 — The interim MD&A (15 minutes)

Use the reading order from [how to read an MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/): overview, segments, liquidity, non-GAAP, risks. In a quarter, focus on what **changed** since the annual MD&A.

## Step 4 — Cash flow reality check (10 minutes)

| Check | Healthy pattern | Worry pattern |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Operating cash flow vs net income | Similar or higher over a year | Persistently lower |
| Working capital | Moves with seasonality | Receivables/inventory growing faster than sales |
| Capex vs guidance | On plan | Rising with no matching growth |
| FCF vs dividends + buybacks | Covered over 12 months | Funded with debt or new shares |

Quarterly cash flow is noisy, so look at **trailing twelve months**. Method: [how to analyze free cash flow](/investing/analyze/free-cash-flow-analysis/).

## The "beat or miss" trap

A "beat" against consensus says as much about the analysts' model as about the company. Better comparisons, in order:

1. **Versus your thesis** — did the KPI you care about move the way you expected?
2. **Versus management's own guidance** — are they delivering what they said?
3. **Versus the same quarter last year** — especially for seasonal businesses.
4. **Versus consensus** — useful context for the price reaction, not for business quality.

A recent example of an earnings-quality story where EPS grew yet missed expectations is our [CPKC Q3 fuel-squeeze stock brief](/summaries/2026-10-29-cp-q3-fuel-squeeze/).
