"Revenue up 18%" is not yet a thesis. **Revenue quality** asks whether that growth is organic, collectible, concentrated, and recognized under policies you understand — especially on Canadian IFRS issuers where notes and MD&A carry the detail.

Educational only — not financial advice. All bridges and ratios below are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Read the revenue accounting policy note, bridge reported growth into organic / FX / acquisition pieces (MD&A often helps), check receivables and contract liabilities, and measure customer or geographic concentration. High growth with stretching receivables or heavy deferred revenue pull-forward deserves skepticism, not celebration.

## Quality scorecard (hypothetical worked view)

| Check | Healthy pattern (illustrative) | Watch pattern (illustrative) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Organic growth | Positive and explained by volume/price | Headline growth mostly FX or M&A |
| Receivables / DSO | Stable vs sales | DSO up while revenue "accelerates" |
| Deferred revenue / contract liabilities | Grows with bookings | Shrinks while revenue spikes |
| Returns / refunds / warranties | Stable provisions | Rising provisions vs sales |
| Top-customer share | Diversified or contracted | One customer >~20-30% without disclosure comfort |
| Policy changes | Stable IFRS 15 judgements | New estimates each year |

IFRS 15 *Revenue from Contracts with Customers* governs recognition. Entity-wide concentration disclosures often sit with IFRS 8. Canadian continuous disclosure is under NI 51-102.
