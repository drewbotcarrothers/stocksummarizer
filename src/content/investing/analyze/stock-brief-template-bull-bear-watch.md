A **stock brief** is one page that forces you to say what you believe about a company, what would prove you wrong, and what you are waiting for. It is the most useful habit a DIY investor can build, because it turns research into decisions you can audit later.

Educational only — not financial advice. The template below is a thinking tool, not a recommendation engine.

## Direct answer

Every brief has seven blocks: **snapshot, business model, bull case, bear case, watch list, kill criteria, and valuation range**. Keep it to one page, date it, cite sources, and update it after each quarterly report. If you can't fill the bear case honestly, you don't understand the stock yet.

## Copy-ready template

| Block | Prompt | Length |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Snapshot** | Ticker, exchange, date, price, market cap, sector, your position (none / starter / full) | 1 line |
| **Business model** | How it makes money; recurring vs cyclical; top 2 drivers | 2–3 sentences |
| **Bull case** | What has to be true for this to work from today's price? | 3 bullets |
| **Bear case** | What could go wrong, or what is the market already over-paying for? | 3 bullets |
| **Watch** | KPIs and dated catalysts that decide the next update | 3–5 bullets |
| **Kill criteria** | Specific, observable events that force an exit or rewrite | 2–3 bullets |
| **Valuation range** | Low / base / high, using sector-appropriate metrics | 1 small table |
| **Sources** | Filings and dates used | links |

## Writing each block well

### Bull and bear: symmetrical, specific, falsifiable

| Weak | Strong |
|:--|:--|
| "Great management" | "Has hit its leverage target three years in a row (per MD&A)" |
| "Rates might fall" | "Each 25 bp cut lowers interest expense by roughly X per the MD&A sensitivity" |
| "Competition" | "New entrant pricing 10–15% lower in segment Y, per Q2 call" |
| "Undervalued" | "Trades below peer median EV/EBITDA despite similar growth and lower leverage" |

### Watch list: dated and measurable

Tie each item to a filing or event: next quarterly report, investor day, regulatory decision, project start-up, debt maturity. Pull them from the [MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/) and the [earnings call](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-earnings-call-transcript/).

### Kill criteria: decided before you buy

Examples of the *form* (fill with your own numbers):

- Dividend not covered by trailing-12-month free cash flow for two consecutive quarters
- Net debt/EBITDA above the company's own stated ceiling with no plan
- Core KPI (e.g. same-store sales, production per share) negative for three quarters
- Management abandons a stated capital-allocation priority without explanation

### Valuation range

| Scenario | Key assumption | Metric used | Implied value range |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Low | e.g. margins revert to 5-year low | EV/EBITDA at peer low end | |
| Base | e.g. guidance met | Peer-median multiple | |
| High | e.g. growth project delivers on time | Upper-quartile multiple | |

Use metrics that fit the sector: [P/E](/investing/valuation/what-pe-means-canadian-diy/), [EV/EBITDA](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-for-canadian-diy-investors/), [P/FCF](/investing/valuation/what-p-fcf-means-canadian-diy/), or [P/B and ROE for banks](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/). A [simple DCF](/investing/valuation/simple-dcf-without-fake-precision/) can sanity-check the base case.

## The update loop

| When | What to update | Time |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Quarterly results | Snapshot, watch items, kill check | 30–45 min |
| Annual report + AIF | Business model, bear case, valuation range | 1–2 hours |
| Price move > 20% with no news | Valuation range only — is the thesis priced? | 15 min |
| Kill criterion triggered | Full rewrite or exit decision | as needed |
