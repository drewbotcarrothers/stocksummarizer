Buybacks can return cash or paper over a weak equity story. In Canada the disclosures differ for a **normal course issuer bid (NCIB)** versus a **substantial issuer bid (SIB)** — and the MD&A/financials tell you what was actually bought.

Educational only — not financial advice. Share figures are **hypothetical**. For the opposite capital-markets move see [spot dilution risk](/investing/analyze/spot-dilution-risk-canadian-issuers/).

## Direct answer

Identify whether the company announced an NCIB or an SIB, read the size and duration limits, then track shares purchased and dollars spent in subsequent disclosures. Reconcile to the basic and diluted share counts. In taxable accounts, note that buybacks can interact with ACB tracking at the portfolio level (light note only — see your tax resources).

## NCIB vs SIB (investor literacy map)

| Feature | NCIB (typical) | SIB (typical) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Intent | Ongoing repurchase in the market | Larger, time-bounded offer to shareholders |
| Scale | Limited % of public float over 12 months | Can be a much larger % |
| Disclosure | Press release + TSX forms; updates in MD&A/notes | Offer circular / formal bid documents |
| What to track | Daily/annual limits vs actual buys | Take-up, proration, funding source |

Exact percentage limits and exchange rules change; always read the issuer's announcement and the applicable TSX / securities-law documents rather than memorizing a number from a blog.
