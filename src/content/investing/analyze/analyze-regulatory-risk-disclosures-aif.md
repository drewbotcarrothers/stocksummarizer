Every Annual Information Form has a risk factors section, and most of it reads like legal boilerplate. Some regulatory risks, though, set the economics of the business: an allowed return on equity, a spectrum licence, a foreign-ownership cap, a capital requirement. This post shows how to find those and ignore the rest.

Educational only, not financial advice. Read it alongside [how to read an AIF](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-annual-information-form/). For sector context, link out to the [utilities](/investing/sectors/how-utilities-make-money/), [telecom](/investing/sectors/how-telecoms-make-money/) and [bank](/investing/sectors/how-canadian-banks-make-money/) explainers rather than repeating them here.

## Direct answer

Open the AIF's risk factors and any regulatory or "regulatory framework" section. Sort each item into **specific** (names a regulator, proceeding, date or number) or **boilerplate** (could apply to any company). For the specific items, compare against last year's AIF to see what's new, expanded or removed. Then map each one to a revenue or cost line and record the regulator, the next decision date and a kill criterion. Most of the signal is in changes from year to year.

## Year-over-year diff

| Change type | Example (hypothetical) | Signal |
|:--|:--|:--|
| New risk factor | First mention of a pending regulatory review | High: find the proceeding and the timeline |
| Expanded wording | Risk now quantifies a possible revenue impact | High: management sees it as more likely or material |
| Removed risk | A licence-renewal risk disappears after renewal | Positive, but confirm the terms |
| Unchanged boilerplate | "Changes in laws could adversely affect…" | Low: skim |

A simple approach is to paste both years' risk sections into a text-diff tool. Document changes in your [source hierarchy](/investing/frameworks/source-hierarchy-filings-transcripts-commentary/) notes as filings-level evidence.
