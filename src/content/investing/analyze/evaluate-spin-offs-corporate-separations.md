A spin-off splits one listed company into two (or more). Shareholders of the parent receive shares of the new company, often called **SpinCo**, in proportion to what they already own. Nothing is bought or sold on the day it happens. The questions that matter are what each piece is worth on its own, who will run it, how much debt goes with it, and whether anyone will be forced to sell.

This guide is a research checklist, not a call on any deal. All worked numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Read the **management information circular** for the arrangement before anything else. It contains the rationale, the pro forma financial statements for both companies, the debt allocation, the management and board line-ups, the tax treatment and the distribution ratio. Then check three things that often surprise people: (1) whether SpinCo is carrying more of the debt or the weaker assets, (2) whether index funds or income funds will have to sell SpinCo shares, and (3) how to split your adjusted cost base between the two holdings.

## What to pull from the circular

| Section | What to look for | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Background and reasons | Why now; alternatives considered | A clear strategic logic, or a way to shed a problem asset |
| Pro forma financials | Revenue, EBITDA, capex and debt for each company | Lets you build two simple [business-model maps](/investing/analyze/map-company-business-model-plain-english/) |
| Debt allocation | Who carries which bonds and credit facilities | SpinCo leverage can be set higher to send cash back to the parent |
| Separation agreements | Transition services, supply contracts, tax indemnities | Ongoing ties that can limit SpinCo's independence |
| Management and board | Who leads each company; incentive plans | Check [compensation alignment](/investing/analyze/evaluate-executive-compensation-alignment/) for the new team |
| Tax section | Whether the distribution is tax-deferred for Canadian holders (and US holders) | Affects your ACB and any taxable benefit |

## Common patterns around spin-offs

| Pattern | What can happen | How to check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Forced selling | Holders whose mandate does not fit SpinCo (index, dividend or size limits) sell early | Index eligibility, dividend policy, market cap at listing |
| Thin coverage | Few analysts cover SpinCo at first | Look for initiation reports after the quiet period |
| Messy first statements | Carve-out financials include allocated costs | Compare carve-out margins with the first standalone quarters |
| Parent re-rating | The remaining business may trade on a different multiple | [Peer tables](/investing/analyze/use-peer-tables-without-cherry-picking/) for each piece |

Academic studies of spin-offs, such as Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge (1993), reported positive average excess returns for spin-offs and parents in the years after separation, concentrated in companies later involved in takeovers. Averages are not a forecast for any single deal.

## ACB: split it the way the company tells you

After a tax-deferred spin-off, your adjusted cost base is usually split between the parent and SpinCo in proportion to their fair market values right after the distribution. The company normally publishes the allocation percentages in a tax information notice on its investor relations site. Record them in your [ACB tracker](/investing/accounts/adjusted-cost-base-tracking-diy-portfolios/) the day they come out.

| Hypothetical | Parent | SpinCo | Total |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| ACB before | $10,000 | — | $10,000 |
| FMV share after distribution | 70% | 30% | 100% |
| ACB after | $7,000 | $3,000 | $10,000 |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Circular read | Rationale, pro forma numbers and debt split noted |
| Two business maps | Each company has its own revenue drivers and risks |
| Leverage compared | Net debt / EBITDA for each piece |
| Forced-seller check | Index, dividend and size fit for SpinCo considered |
| Tax notice saved | ACB allocation percentages recorded |
| Review date set | Re-check after the first two standalone quarters |
