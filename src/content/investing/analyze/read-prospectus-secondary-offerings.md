When a listed Canadian company sells more shares after its IPO, the offering usually comes with a **short form prospectus**, or a **prospectus supplement** under an existing shelf. These documents look long, but a DIY investor only needs a handful of sections to answer the real questions: who is selling, how many shares, at what discount, and what the money is for.

This is a document-reading guide. It does not judge any offering. For counting shares, see [cap tables and fully diluted share counts](/investing/analyze/read-cap-tables-fully-diluted-share-counts/). For spotting a raise before it happens, see [dilution risk in Canadian issuers](/investing/analyze/spot-dilution-risk-canadian-issuers/). For the definition, see [What is dilution?](/investing/glossary/what-is-dilution/).

## Direct answer

Open the cover page first: it shows the offering price, the number of shares, the underwriters' fee and any over-allotment option. Then read **Use of Proceeds**, **Consolidated Capitalization**, **Plan of Distribution**, **Prior Sales** and **Risk Factors**. Confirm whether the shares come from the **company (treasury)**, which adds new shares, or from an **existing holder (secondary)**, which only changes who owns them. Last, compare the offering price with the recent trading price to see the discount.

## Treasury vs secondary

| Feature | Treasury offering | Secondary offering (selling shareholder) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Who gets the cash | The company | The selling holder |
| New shares created | Yes | No |
| Per-share dilution | Yes, unless the proceeds earn more than the cost of equity | None, but more shares may trade freely |
| What to ask | Is the use of proceeds productive? | Why is a large holder selling, and is there a lock-up? |
| Where to look | Cover page, Use of Proceeds | Cover page, Selling Shareholders section |

## The sections that matter

| Section | Read for | Red flag |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Cover page | Price, size, fees, over-allotment (often 15%, 30 days) | Large discount to the last close |
| Use of Proceeds | Debt repayment, acquisition, capex, general purposes | Mostly "general corporate purposes" with no plan |
| Consolidated Capitalization | Debt and equity before and after the deal | Leverage still high after the raise |
| Plan of Distribution | Bought deal vs marketed; underwriting fee; lock-ups | Short lock-up for insiders |
| Prior Sales | Shares and options issued in the past 12 months | Frequent raises at falling prices |
| Risk Factors | Offering-specific risks | New going-concern or financing language |
| Documents Incorporated by Reference | Which AIF, statements and MD&A apply | Old documents with no update on recent changes |

## Quick math (hypothetical)

| Item | Value |
|:--|--:|
| Shares before | 100.0M |
| New shares (base deal) | 10.0M at $18.00 |
| Last close before announcement | $20.00 |
| Discount to last close | 10% |
| Gross proceeds | $180M |
| Underwriting fee (4%) | $7.2M |
| Net proceeds | $172.8M |
| Shares after full over-allotment | 111.5M |

If the money pays down 8% debt, the interest saved can offset some of the extra share count. If it funds an acquisition, check the multiple paid and read [M&A track records](/investing/analyze/analyze-ma-track-records-serial-acquirers/).

## Your statutory rights (Canada)

Provincial securities laws give buyers in a prospectus offering a right to withdraw within two business days of receiving the prospectus, and rights of action if the prospectus contains a misrepresentation. These rights apply to buyers in the offering, not to people buying on the exchange afterward.

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Treasury or secondary identified | Seller and share source confirmed |
| Discount measured | Offering price vs last close logged |
| Use of proceeds rated | Specific, productive or vague |
| Pro forma leverage noted | Capitalization table read |
| Fully diluted count updated | New shares and over-allotment added |
| Lock-ups noted | Insider and selling-holder lock-up length recorded |
