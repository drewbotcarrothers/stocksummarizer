Your notes have one job: record what you knew and why you acted. When a stock falls 12% in a week, it's tempting to write "thesis weakening" even though nothing in the business changed. Over time those notes turn into a diary of price moves and stop being a record of evidence. The fix is structural. Keep **price action** and **fundamentals** in separate sections and connect them only through explicit rules.

Educational only, not financial advice. This is a note-keeping workflow. It builds on [separating news from thesis](/investing/analyze/separate-news-from-thesis/) and the [quarter-over-quarter thesis log](/investing/analyze/track-stock-thesis-over-quarters/) and doesn't add a new thesis card. Your decision rules still live on the [Thesis → Catalyst → Kill card](/investing/frameworks/thesis-catalyst-kill-criteria-card/).

## Direct answer

Split every stock note into three columns: **price log** (what the market did), **fundamental log** (what the business disclosed) and **decision log** (what you did and which rule triggered it). A price move goes only in the price log unless you can point to new information, such as a filing, KPI, guidance change or balance-sheet event. Only fundamental entries can change thesis pillars. Decisions must cite a pre-written rule, never just "it went down."

## Why mixing them hurts

| Mixed-note habit | What it does to you | Separated alternative |
|:--|:--|:--|
| "Down 15%, something must be wrong" | Lets price stand in for evidence | Log the move; search for new disclosures; if none, no thesis edit |
| "Up 30%, thesis confirmed" | Treats a rerating as proof the business improved | Ask which KPI moved; valuation change ≠ fundamental change |
| Writing targets next to KPIs | Anchors you to an entry price | Keep valuation ranges in a separate dated section |
| Editing old notes after a move | Hindsight bias erases your original reasoning | Append-only notes; date every entry |

Hindsight and anchoring both show up in [behavioural biases that hurt stock pickers](/investing/process/behavioural-biases-stock-pickers/). Separate notes are one of the cheapest ways to guard against them.

## What counts as "new information"

| Counts (fundamental log) | Doesn't count by itself (price log) |
|:--|:--|
| Quarterly results, MD&A, AIF or circular | Daily or weekly price moves |
| Guidance change in a press release | Analyst price-target changes |
| Material change report or news release | Social media sentiment |
| Covenant amendment, refinancing, equity raise | Sector ETF flows |
| Insider filings (weight lightly) | "Unusual volume" with no disclosure |

When a price move is large and you can't find a cause, check [SEDAR+ filing alerts](/investing/analyze/sedar-plus-alerts-filing-types-monitoring/) and the company's news releases before you assume anything.

## A simple template

| Date | Price log | Fundamental log | Decision log |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Hypothetical Day 1 | −8% on 2x volume; sector −3% | None found | No action; revisit after Q results |
| Hypothetical Day 22 | +2% | Q3: same-store sales below kill threshold for 2nd quarter | Kill rule #2 hit → review per card |
| Hypothetical Day 23 | −6% | None new | Already reviewing; no extra action |
