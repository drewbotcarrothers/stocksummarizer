"Energy" on the TSX covers businesses that make money in very different ways. A **producer** sells oil, gas and natural gas liquids, so its revenue moves with commodity prices. A **midstream** company moves and stores those products for fees. A **services** company rents rigs, crews and equipment, so it depends on how much drilling and completion work producers decide to do. Using one checklist for all three is a common mistake.

This post is an **analysis checklist** that sorts energy names into the right bucket and points to the explainers for each: [how oil and gas producers make money](/investing/sectors/how-oil-gas-producers-make-money/), [how midstream pipelines make money](/investing/sectors/how-midstream-pipelines-make-money/) and [how energy services make money](/investing/sectors/how-energy-services-make-money/). For company examples, see the [CNQ primer](/investing/primers/how-canadian-natural-resources-makes-money/) and the [Enbridge primer](/investing/primers/how-enbridge-makes-money/). It is educational and does not rate any company.

## Direct answer

First decide **which engine** the company runs. Then use the matching metrics: for producers, **netbacks, decline rates, reserve life and sustaining capex**; for midstream, **contracted share of cash flow, volume trends, payout of distributable cash flow and leverage**; for services, **utilization, pricing, backlog and fleet age**. Integrated companies (production plus upgrading or refining) need a segment-by-segment read.

## Which metrics fit which model

| Question | Producer | Midstream | Services |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| What drives revenue? | Commodity price x volume | Fees x volume (often take-or-pay) | Activity x price per job or day |
| Key operating metric | Netback per boe, production per share | Throughput, contracted percentage | Utilization, day rates |
| What keeps the business flat? | Sustaining capex to offset declines | Maintenance capex, recontracting | Fleet maintenance and replacement |
| Main balance-sheet check | Net debt to cash flow at a low price | Debt to EBITDA, credit rating | Net debt to EBITDA through a downturn |
| Typical shareholder return | Variable dividends and buybacks | Steady dividends | Buybacks, debt repayment |
| Read this | [Producers](/investing/sectors/how-oil-gas-producers-make-money/) | [Midstream](/investing/sectors/how-midstream-pipelines-make-money/) | [Services](/investing/sectors/how-energy-services-make-money/) |

## Habit swap table

| Habit | Why it misleads | Better approach |
|:--|:--|:--|
| One P/E for all energy names | Producer earnings swing with price | Cash flow at a stated price deck for producers; EV/EBITDA for midstream |
| Yield as the main midstream check | Payout can exceed cash flow | Payout of distributable cash flow plus leverage; see [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |
| Production growth as success | Growth can come from costly wells or acquisitions | Production and cash flow **per share** |
| Reserves as a fixed asset | Reserves depend on price and cost assumptions | Reserve life, proved vs probable, price deck used |
| Services revenue growth alone | Activity can fall quickly | Utilization and pricing trend; customer concentration |

## Where to look in filings

| Item | Where |
|:--|:--|
| Reserves and price deck | NI 51-101 statement (Form 51-101F1) in or with the AIF |
| Commodity sensitivity | MD&A sensitivity table; [how to read it](/investing/analyze/read-commodity-price-sensitivity-resource-issuers/) |
| Hedging | Financial statement notes on derivatives |
| Contract profile (midstream) | MD&A and investor presentation: take-or-pay and cost-of-service share |
| Utilization and fleet (services) | MD&A operating metrics |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Model identified | Producer, midstream, services or integrated (by segment) |
| Matching metrics logged | The three or four metrics from the table above |
| Price exposure noted | Sensitivity table read; hedges noted |
| Balance sheet tested | Leverage at a low price or activity level |
| Per-share view | Production or cash flow per share over several years |
