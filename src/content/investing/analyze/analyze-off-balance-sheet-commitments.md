Off-balance-sheet commitments are promises a company has made that are **not recorded as liabilities** but can still require cash: purchase contracts, take-or-pay agreements, guarantees, letters of credit, committed capital spending and arrangements with unconsolidated entities. They rarely sink a company alone, but they can turn a "low debt" story into a tighter one.

This post shows a simple way to find and size them. It connects to [how to research supply chain dependencies from filings](/investing/analyze/research-supply-chain-dependencies-filings/) (purchase commitments) and [how to read debt notes and covenant language](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/). It is educational; numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Read the MD&A sections on **liquidity, contractual obligations and off-balance-sheet arrangements**, then the **commitments** and **contingencies** notes in the financial statements. List each commitment with amount, timing and whether it can be cancelled. Add the firm, non-cancellable ones to a **stress view** of obligations and compare that total with operating cash flow. Large, inflexible commitments matter most when revenue is cyclical.

## What usually stays off the balance sheet

| Commitment | Typical example | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Purchase obligations | Multi-year supply contracts, minimum volumes | Cash goes out even if demand falls |
| Take-or-pay and capacity contracts | Pipeline capacity, rail cars, power | Pay for capacity whether used or not |
| Capital commitments | Contracts signed for plants, equipment, fleets | Committed capex limits flexibility |
| Guarantees | Parent guarantees of joint-venture or customer debt | Can become debt if a partner fails |
| Letters of credit and surety bonds | Construction, reclamation, insurance | Use credit capacity and can be called |
| Leases not yet started | Signed leases that begin next year | Will add lease liabilities later |
| Short-term and low-value leases | IFRS 16 exemptions | Small, but adds to fixed costs |
| Unconsolidated entities | Joint ventures and associates (IFRS 12 disclosures) | Debt may sit outside the group balance sheet |

Before **IFRS 16** (effective 2019), most operating leases were off the balance sheet. Now most leases are recorded as lease liabilities, so older comparisons need care. Supplier finance programs have their own disclosure under 2023 amendments to IAS 7 and IFRS 7.

## Where to find them

| Source | What to read |
|:--|:--|
| MD&A liquidity section | Contractual obligations table, maturities by year |
| MD&A off-balance-sheet arrangements | Description, business purpose, amounts (Form 51-102F1) |
| Commitments note | Purchase and capital commitments by year |
| Contingencies and guarantees note | Maximum potential payments under guarantees |
| Interests in other entities note (IFRS 12) | Joint ventures, associates and their commitments |
| Credit agreement summary | Letters of credit drawn against facilities |

## Questions to ask about each commitment

| Question | Low concern | Higher concern |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Can it be cancelled? | Cancellable with small penalty | Firm and non-cancellable |
| How long does it run? | Under 2 years | 10+ years |
| Is it linked to revenue? | Back-to-back with customer contracts | Fixed regardless of sales |
| Who is the other party? | Investment-grade counterparty | Weak partner or related party |
| Is it growing? | Stable versus revenue | Rising faster than revenue |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| MD&A read | Liquidity, obligations and off-balance-sheet sections reviewed |
| Notes read | Commitments, guarantees and IFRS 12 notes reviewed |
| List built | Each commitment with amount, timing, flexibility |
| Stress view | Firm commitments added to net debt as a separate figure |
| Cash compared | Total compared with operating cash flow in a weak year |
| Year compared | Changes from the prior year explained |
