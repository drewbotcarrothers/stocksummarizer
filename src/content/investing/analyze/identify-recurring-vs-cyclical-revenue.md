"Recurring revenue" is one of the most abused phrases in equity storytelling. Sometimes it means contracted subscriptions with high renewal. Sometimes it means "we hope customers come back." DIY investors need a **filing-based split** between revenue that is contractually or behaviourally sticky and revenue that moves with the cycle or one-off projects.

Educational only — not financial advice. Examples are **hypothetical**. Cross-link [revenue quality](/investing/analyze/analyze-revenue-quality-canadian-issuers/), [business-model map](/investing/analyze/map-company-business-model-plain-english/), and [operating leverage](/investing/analyze/analyze-operating-leverage/).

## Direct answer

Pull the revenue note (IFRS 15) and MD&A mix commentary. Classify disclosed buckets into **recurring-like** (subscriptions, contracted take-or-pay, regulated rates, multi-year service) vs **cyclical / transactional** (spot commodity, equipment deliveries, project milestones, discretionary retail). Then test stickiness with renewals, churn, deferred revenue / contract liabilities, backlog, and customer concentration — not with adjectives on the earnings call.

## Classification cheat sheet

| Clue in filings | Often more recurring | Often more cyclical / transactional |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Contract form | Multi-year, auto-renew, take-or-pay | Spot, purchase orders, short RFPs |
| Balance sheet | Rising deferred revenue / contract liabilities | Inventory / receivables spike with shipments |
| Volume driver | Seats, subscribers, contracted capacity | Commodity price × volume; housing starts; capex budgets |
| MD&A language | "Remaining performance obligations", ARR/MRR if defined | "Shipments", "realized price", "project awards" |
| Risk factor | Churn, price caps, regulation | Demand destruction, price crash, project deferrals |

If the issuer reports ARR or similar **non-GAAP** metrics, treat them under [non-GAAP literacy](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/) — definitions hop.
