Canadian utilities and pipelines are often called **bond proxies**: steady dividends, regulated or contracted cash flows, and share prices that tend to move inversely with bond yields. That description is useful but incomplete. Some of these companies behave like bonds most of the time; others carry volume, commodity, construction or regulatory risk that no bond does.

This is an **analysis checklist** for deciding when the bond-proxy lens fits and when it does not. It builds on our [utilities explainer](/investing/sectors/how-utilities-make-money/), [midstream explainer](/investing/sectors/how-midstream-pipelines-make-money/) and [Enbridge primer](/investing/primers/how-enbridge-makes-money/) without repeating them. It is educational only.

## Direct answer

Use the bond-proxy lens when most cash flow comes from **cost-of-service regulation or long take-or-pay contracts**, the balance sheet is investment grade, and dividend growth is funded by **rate base growth** without constant equity issues. Drop it, or add equity-style analysis, when results depend on **throughput volumes, commodity prices, merchant power or marketing**, when a large **capital project** dominates the plan, or when payouts exceed sustainable cash flow. In both cases, check how sensitive the share price and financing costs are to interest rates.

## What makes the cash flow bond-like

| Feature | Bond-like version | Equity-like version | Where to check |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Revenue model | Cost-of-service rates; take-or-pay contracts | Volume-based tolls, spot or merchant sales | Segment note; MD&A business overview |
| Regulator | Allowed ROE on rate base (OEB, AUC, BCUC, CER) | Unregulated or market-based | AIF regulatory section |
| Counterparties | Investment-grade shippers, many customers | Few customers, weaker credit | [Customer concentration](/investing/analyze/analyze-customer-concentration-risk/) |
| Growth | Rate base growth from approved projects | Large new builds, acquisitions | [Capex plans](/investing/analyze/read-capex-plans-mdna/) |
| Funding | Retained cash plus debt; DRIP modest | Frequent equity issues, hybrids | [Dilution risk](/investing/analyze/spot-dilution-risk-canadian-issuers/) |

## When not to treat it as a bond

| Warning sign | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Payout above distributable cash flow or AFFO-style measure | Dividend relies on debt or new shares |
| Net debt / EBITDA rising for several years | Credit rating pressure; higher refinancing costs |
| A single project bigger than a large share of the rate base | Cost overruns and delays fall on shareholders |
| Regulatory decision pending (rate case, allowed ROE cut) | Earnings reset risk |
| Heavy floating-rate or near-term maturities | Interest costs rise faster than allowed returns adjust |
| US or international exposure | Different regulators and currency; see [currency](/investing/analyze/handle-currency-cad-vs-usd-results/) |

## The rate check

| Question | Where to look |
|:--|:--|
| Dividend yield minus the 10-year GoC yield, now vs five-year history | Your own notes; bond yield from the Bank of Canada |
| Interest coverage trend | [Interest coverage guide](/investing/analyze/analyze-interest-coverage-levered-tsx/) |
| Debt maturity ladder and fixed vs floating mix | [Debt notes](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/) |
| Dividend coverage by cash flow | [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Cash flow mix classified | Share regulated or contracted vs volume or merchant |
| Regulator and allowed ROE noted | Next rate case date logged |
| Growth funding mapped | Capex plan vs retained cash, debt and equity |
| Payout coverage checked | Payout vs distributable cash flow |
| Rate sensitivity noted | Yield gap and debt structure |
| Lens chosen | Bond proxy, equity-like, or a mix, with reasons written down |
