"Moat" is a loaded word. Used carefully, it means a **durable advantage that protects returns** — switching costs, network effects, cost advantage, intangible assets, or efficient scale. Used carelessly, it is a slogan. This page is a **one-page DIY checklist**, not a duplicate of thesis or kill-criteria cards.

Educational only — not financial advice. Scores are subjective literacy tools. Feed results into [Bull / Bear / Watch](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/) and [Thesis → Catalyst → Kill](/investing/frameworks/thesis-catalyst-kill-criteria-card/). Sector structure primers (banks, rails, utilities, etc.) supply industry context — link them; do not paste them here.

## Direct answer

Write the moat claim in one sentence. For each of five boxes — switching costs, network effects, cost advantage, intangibles, efficient scale — give a 0–5 score and a one-line evidence note from filings or observable customer behaviour. Total is less important than **which boxes are empty**. Revisit after each major quarter or competitive event. If you cannot evidence a box, score it near zero.

## Evidence habits (not vibes)

| Moat box | Stronger evidence | Weaker evidence |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Retention, multi-year contracts, data portability friction | Marketing claim of "sticky customers" |
| Network effects | Liquidity / density metrics; two-sided growth | "Platform" label alone |
| Cost advantage | Unit costs vs peers over cycle | Temporary discounting |
| Intangibles | Patents, licences, brand pricing power with data | Logo recognition anecdotes |
| Efficient scale | Natural monopoly / regulated utility traits | "We're the biggest" without barriers |
