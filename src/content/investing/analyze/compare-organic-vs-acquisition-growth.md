Headline revenue growth that is mostly **bought** is a different business from growth that is mostly **organic**. DIY investors who skip the bridge end up paying a compounder multiple for a roll-up — sometimes fair, often not.

Educational only — not financial advice. Mix figures are **hypothetical**. Pair with [M&A track records](/investing/analyze/analyze-ma-track-records-serial-acquirers/), [revenue quality](/investing/analyze/analyze-revenue-quality-canadian-issuers/), and [one-time items](/investing/analyze/spot-one-time-items-canadian-statements/).

## Direct answer

Start from reported growth, strip FX and calendar effects when disclosed, then strip acquisition contributions (stub periods, pro forma bridges). The residual is your organic estimate. Judge whether organic is volume, price, or mix — and whether acquired growth came with margins, overlaps, and cash (not just stock) that fit the thesis.

## Bridge template (copy into notes)

| Step | Hyp. figure | Source |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Reported revenue growth | +18% | Income statement |
| FX / other | −2% | MD&A |
| Acquisitions (net of disposals) | −11% | MD&A / notes |
| Implied organic | +5% | Residual — label as estimate if not given |
| Organic volume vs price | +2% vol / +3% price | If disclosed |

If the issuer publishes an organic growth KPI, read its definition under [non-GAAP literacy](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/).
