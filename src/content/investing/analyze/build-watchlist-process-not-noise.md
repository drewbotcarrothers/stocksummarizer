Most DIY "watchlists" are anxiety feeds. A useful watchlist is a **small, rule-bound queue**: why a name is there, when you will look again, and what would remove it — without turning every tick into a decision.

Educational only — not financial advice. Pair with [separate news from thesis](/investing/analyze/separate-news-from-thesis/), [thesis tracking](/investing/analyze/track-stock-thesis-over-quarters/), [SEDAR+ alerts](/investing/analyze/sedar-plus-alerts-filing-types-monitoring/), and [Bull / Bear / Watch](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/).

## Direct answer

Split names into idea / active-watch / parked / dropped. Cap the active-watch count. Give each active name a thesis stub, one killer risk, a next review date, and filing alerts — not unbounded price obsession. Graduate to a full brief or drop with a logged reason. Turn off inputs that do not change filings, competitive facts, or your written catalysts.

## Sample operating rules (adapt, don't copy blindly)

| Rule | Example setting | Purpose |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Max active-watch | 10–15 names | Attention budget |
| Review cadence | Weekly 30 min | Prevents backlog guilt |
| Entry ticket | Thesis stub + risk + filing | Blocks tip-driven adds |
| Alert types | SEDAR+ + 1–2 price levels | Caps noise |
| Drop log | Date + one sentence | Stops zombie re-adds |
