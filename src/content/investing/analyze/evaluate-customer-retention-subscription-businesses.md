In a subscription business, the value is in customers who **stay and spend more**. Revenue growth can hide a leaky base if new sales replace lost customers. So retention clues, from churn and net revenue retention to deferred revenue and contract length, often tell you more than the headline growth rate.

This post shows what to look for and how to cross-check it. It builds on [how to analyze software business models on the TSX](/investing/analyze/analyze-software-business-models-tsx/) and the churn discussion in [how to analyze telecoms using ARPU, churn and capex cycles](/investing/analyze/analyze-telecoms-arpu-churn-capex/). It is educational; numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Look for a disclosed **churn rate**, **gross revenue retention** or **net revenue retention (NRR)** in the MD&A or investor materials and read the exact definition. If none is disclosed, use indirect clues: **recurring revenue share**, **deferred revenue (contract liabilities)**, **remaining performance obligations**, **customer counts**, and **sales and marketing spend per new dollar of revenue**. Strong retention looks like stable or rising deferred revenue, low churn, NRR above 100% and gross retention that does not slip.

## Retention metrics in plain English

| Metric | What it measures | Watch out for |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Customer churn | Share of customers lost in a period | Monthly vs annual; small vs large customers |
| Revenue churn | Share of recurring revenue lost | Can differ from customer churn |
| Gross revenue retention | Revenue kept from existing customers, before upsells | Capped at 100%; shows the leak |
| Net revenue retention | Revenue kept plus expansion from the same customers | Price increases can lift it temporarily |
| ARR or recurring revenue | Annualized subscription revenue | Definition varies by company |
| Customer lifetime | Average years a customer stays (about 1 / annual churn) | Sensitive to small churn changes |

These are not IFRS measures. In Canada, a ratio like NRR disclosed in an MD&A is typically a **supplementary financial measure** or non-GAAP ratio under **NI 52-112**, so the company should explain how it is calculated. If the definition changes, see [how to track KPI definitions when companies change them](/investing/analyze/track-kpi-definition-changes/).

## Indirect clues when retention is not disclosed

| Clue | Where | What it suggests |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Contract liabilities (deferred revenue) | Balance sheet and revenue note (IFRS 15) | Rising with revenue: customers prepaying and renewing |
| Remaining performance obligations | Revenue note (IFRS 15) | Contracted revenue not yet recognized; longer duration is stickier |
| Recurring share of revenue | MD&A or segment note | Higher share usually means more predictable revenue |
| Customer count vs revenue | MD&A KPIs | Revenue per customer rising may mean expansion |
| Sales and marketing as % of revenue | Income statement | High and rising spend may be filling a leaky bucket |
| Price increase commentary | MD&A, calls | Price can hide falling usage; see [pricing power](/investing/analyze/analyze-pricing-power-mdna-language/) |

## Sector differences

| Business type | Typical retention signal |
|:--|:--|
| Enterprise software | NRR, multi-year contracts, maintenance renewal rates |
| Vertical-market software | Very low churn, small annual price increases |
| Telecom and cable | Monthly postpaid churn, ARPU |
| Media and subscriptions | Paid subscribers, churn after price changes |
| Alarm monitoring and services | Attrition rate, recurring monthly revenue |

## Checklist

| Step | Done when |
|:--|:--|
| Disclosed metrics found | Churn, GRR or NRR recorded with exact definitions |
| Trend built | Several years or quarters on the same basis |
| Indirect clues checked | Deferred revenue, RPO, recurring share, S&M spend reviewed |
| Gross vs net split | You know how much comes from keeping vs upselling |
| Peer compared | Same metric from a similar business model |
| Risk noted | Price-driven retention or customer concentration flagged |
