Gross margin is often the first place a competitive problem shows up — and the first place one-time inventory charges pretend to be "just a blip." Before you buy, trend it, bridge it, and decide whether margin is even a thesis pillar.

Educational only — not financial advice. All percentages are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Compute gross margin from IFRS gross profit and revenue for at least eight quarters. Build a simple bridge (price, input costs, mix, FX, one-time COGS items). Compare definition and peers carefully. If margin is core to why you would own the stock, write a kill level in your thesis card.

## Hypothetical bridge

| Driver | Impact on GM (pp, hyp.) | Evidence location |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Selling price | +1.0 | MD&A price/volume |
| Input costs | -3.5 | MD&A, commodity notes |
| Product mix | -1.0 | Segment mix |
| FX | -0.5 | MD&A FX section |
| Inventory write-down | -2.0 | Notes / one-time items |
| **Net change** | **-6.0** | |
