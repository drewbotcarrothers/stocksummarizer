Executive pay tables look like HR paperwork. For investors they are a **behavioural contract**: what management gets paid for is what they are nudged to optimize. The circular's compensation discussion (CD&A-style tables in many Canadian packages) is the place to read that contract — not LinkedIn headlines about "total compensation."

Educational only — not financial advice and not HR advice. All pay figures below are **hypothetical**. Start from the [management information circular](/investing/analyze/read-management-information-circular/) and keep [insider ownership](/investing/analyze/analyze-insider-ownership/) as a separate, lighter input.

## Direct answer

Open the circular's compensation summary and CD&A-style narrative. Split cash salary, annual bonus, and equity (RSUs, options, PSUs). Write down the performance metrics that drive payouts. Check the peer group for relevance (size, sector, geography). Then ask whether those metrics match the risks and cash-flow drivers in **your** thesis — not whether the absolute dollar amount "feels high."

## Alignment reading grid

| Question | Why it matters | Where |
|:--|:--|:--|
| What % is at-risk vs fixed? | Skin in the game vs paycheck | Summary compensation table |
| Which metrics unlock the bonus? | What gets optimized | CD&A / incentive plan text |
| Are metrics one-year or multi-year? | Short-termism risk | Performance period footnotes |
| Peer group composition | Benchmark fairness | Peer-group list |
| Equity vs options mix | Dilution vs ownership | Equity plan / grant tables |
| Discretionary overrides | Board can soften misses | Narrative + footnotes |

## Hypothetical metric map

| Metric (hyp.) | Weight | Friendly to long-term owners? | Caution |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Adjusted EBITDA | 40% | Maybe — if definition is stable | Watch add-backs ([non-GAAP literacy](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/)) |
| Relative TSR vs peers | 30% | Directionally yes | Peer group quality drives the answer |
| Safety / ESG scorecard | 10% | Context | Confirm measurement auditability |
| Individual objectives | 20% | Soft | Highest discretion risk |

A package that pays heavily on short-dated adjusted EPS while leverage and FCF deteriorate is a **misalignment clue**, even if total pay looks "market."
