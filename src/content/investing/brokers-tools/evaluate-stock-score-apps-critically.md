Stock score apps promise to compress a company into a number — a 1–10 rating, a letter grade, a "fair value" with stars. Some are useful shortcuts; others are marketing wrapped around a backtest. For a DIY stock picker, the question isn't whether to look at scores, but how to **evaluate the score itself** before letting it influence a decision.

Educational only — not advice, and no app is named or recommended. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Before trusting any third-party stock score, check five things: (1) **method** — are the inputs and weights disclosed, and do they make sense for the sector? (2) **evidence** — is there a live, out-of-sample track record, or only backtests? (3) **data** — does it cover Canadian companies properly (currency, trusts, interlisted names) and how fresh is it? (4) **incentives** — how does the app make money, and does that reward exciting scores? (5) **use** — treat the score as a prompt for questions in your own research, never as the decision. A score that you can't explain in a sentence shouldn't move money.

## A scorecard you can apply (hypothetical weights)

| Criterion | Weight | Good sign | Red flag |
|:--|--:|:--|:--|
| Method transparency | 25% | Inputs, weights and update frequency published | "Proprietary AI" with no description |
| Evidence quality | 25% | Live track record since a stated date, with failures shown | Only backtests, or results start the day the product launched |
| Canadian data quality | 20% | Handles USD reporters, REITs/trusts, dual listings | TSX names with obviously wrong yields or P/Es |
| Sector awareness | 15% | Different models for banks, REITs, miners | One formula for everything |
| Incentives and conflicts | 15% | Subscription revenue, clear disclosures | Paid placements, broker kickbacks for trades |

Score each 1–5, weight, and compare apps on the same sheet. An app scoring under half on method and evidence is entertainment, not research.

| Backtest problem | What it does | Question to ask |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Data mining | Many rules tried; the best one is reported | How many variants were tested? |
| Look-ahead bias | Uses data that wasn't available on the date | Are point-in-time fundamentals used? |
| Survivorship bias | Delisted and bankrupt companies missing | Does the universe include dead companies? |
| No trading costs | Ignores spreads and turnover | Are returns shown after realistic costs? |
| Short or lucky window | One market regime | Does it work across different periods? |

## Canadian-specific checks

| Check | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| REITs and income trusts | Earnings-based scores misread flow-through entities |
| USD-reporting TSX companies | Currency mismatches distort valuation scores |
| Banks and insurers | Debt and EBITDA metrics don't apply |
| Small caps and venture issuers | Thin data, stale updates |
| Interlisted names | Duplicate listings may carry different scores |

## How to actually use a score

| Situation | Use the score as… |
|:--|:--|
| Generating ideas | One input to a [screen you designed](/investing/brokers-tools/screener-filters-that-match-your-process/), not the screen itself |
| Score disagrees with your thesis | A prompt: which input drives the difference? Check it in the filings |
| Score changes sharply | A review trigger, like a price move — not a sell signal |
| Two apps disagree | Normal; compare their inputs to learn something |

The real work still lives in the company's filings and your [stock brief](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/). Behavioural note: scores feel authoritative, which feeds [anchoring and overconfidence](/investing/process/behavioural-biases-stock-pickers/).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Buying because the score is high | Outsources the thesis | Write your own reasons first |
| Trusting a backtest chart | Most backtests decay live | Ask for out-of-sample and live results |
| Ignoring sector fit | Scores misread banks, REITs, miners | Check whether the model is sector-aware |
| Paying for several overlapping apps | Same underlying data, same blind spots | Pick one, audit it, use it sparingly |
