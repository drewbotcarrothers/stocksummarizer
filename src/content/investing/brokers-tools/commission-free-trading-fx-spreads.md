Most Canadian discount brokers now advertise $0 commissions on at least some stock and ETF trades. That's a real saving — but "commission-free" isn't "cost-free". For Canadian DIY investors the bigger costs are often **currency conversion** on US stocks and the **bid-ask spread** on less liquid names, and neither shows up as a line called "commission".

Educational only — not advice, and not a ranking of brokers. Fee levels below are **hypothetical**; check each broker's current fee schedule and order-routing disclosure.

## Direct answer

On a commission-free trade you still pay (1) the **FX markup** whenever Canadian dollars are converted to US dollars or back — commonly a percentage spread over the market rate, applied in each direction; (2) the **bid-ask spread**, which you cross whenever you buy at the ask and sell at the bid; and (3) any other fees (ECN or routing fees on some order types, account or transfer fees). For a US stock bought with Canadian dollars and later sold back to CAD, the FX cost can be many times any commission you saved. Holding USD cash, using **Norbert's gambit** for larger conversions, and using **limit orders** on thin stocks are the main ways to cut these costs.

## FX conversion: the hidden spread

When you buy a US stock in a Canadian-dollar account, the broker converts your CAD at its own rate, which includes a markup over the market rate. When you sell and the proceeds come back to CAD, it happens again. A hypothetical 1.5% markup each way costs about **3% per round trip** — more than a decade of withholding-tax savings on many stocks.

| Way to get USD | How it works | Cost profile (hypothetical) | Trade-off |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Automatic conversion on each trade | Broker converts at its rate | Highest | Convenient, expensive |
| Manual conversion into a USD account | Convert once, keep USD | Same markup, but only once | Need a USD sub-account, including in TFSA/RRSP where offered |
| Norbert's gambit | Buy an interlisted security in CAD, journal it to the US listing, sell in USD | Commissions plus small spreads | Takes a few days; journaling may need a call or form; price moves while you wait |
| Keep USD dividends in USD | Don't convert income | Zero | Requires USD account |

For US dividend stocks, also see [account location for US dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-us-dividend-stocks/) — saving 15% withholding in an RRSP isn't worth much if you pay 3% in FX to get there.

## Bid-ask spreads: the cost of immediacy

The spread is the difference between the best price buyers are bidding and the best price sellers are asking. A market order pays it; a patient limit order can sometimes avoid part of it.

| Stock type (hypothetical) | Typical spread | Cost on $10,000 round trip |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Large TSX bank | A cent on a ~$90 price | A few dollars |
| Mid-cap industrial | 0.1–0.3% | $10–$30 |
| Thinly traded small cap or venture stock | 1–3% | $100–$300 |
| ETF in the first or last minutes of the session | Wider than mid-day | Avoid the open and close |

Practical habits: use limit orders for anything that isn't highly liquid, check the quote depth before trading, and avoid market orders at the open, close, or around news.

## Payment for order flow and routing

In Canada, CIRO's Universal Market Integrity Rules (UMIR 7.5) have the effect of prohibiting dealers from receiving payment for order flow on Canadian-listed securities. Orders in US-listed securities may be routed through US intermediaries with different practices. Brokers must disclose their order-routing and execution policies — worth reading if you trade US stocks often.

## Other costs that commission-free doesn't cover

| Cost | When it shows up |
|:--|:--|
| ECN / routing fees | Some brokers charge for orders that remove liquidity, or for certain order types |
| Transfer-out fees | Moving accounts to another broker |
| Account or inactivity fees | Some accounts below balance thresholds |
| Options and other products | Per-contract fees often still apply |
| Fractional shares or "real-time FX" products | Spreads may be built into the price |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Judging a broker on commission alone | FX and spreads can dominate | Compare all-in round-trip cost |
| Converting currency on every US trade | Pays the markup twice per round trip | Keep a USD balance |
| Market orders on thin stocks | Pays the full spread or worse | Use limit orders |
| Frequent trading because it's "free" | Spreads, taxes and bad habits add up | Trade on thesis changes, not because you can — see [TFSA trading risk](/investing/accounts/tfsa-business-like-trading-risk/) |
