The taxable account usually gets described as "what's left after the TFSA and RRSP are full." For stock investors that undersells it. It is the only account where a capital loss has value, the only place the dividend tax credit applies, and the place where tax is deferred on gains until you sell anyway. It also carries the record-keeping burden.

Educational only — not tax advice. Rules below are from CRA and Finance Canada guidance as of November 2026. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

In a taxable (non-registered) account, **half of a capital gain is included in income** when you sell (the proposed increase to two-thirds was cancelled in 2025); **capital losses offset capital gains** and net losses can be **carried back three years or forward indefinitely**; eligible Canadian dividends get the **dividend tax credit**; US withholding is usually recoverable as a **foreign tax credit**; and you must track **adjusted cost base (ACB)** and watch the **superficial loss rule**. None of those features exist inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA.

## What only works in a taxable account

| Feature | Taxable account | TFSA / RRSP / FHSA |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Capital loss can offset gains | Yes | No |
| Net capital loss carryback (3 years) or carryforward | Yes | No |
| Dividend tax credit on eligible Canadian dividends | Yes | No |
| Foreign tax credit for US withholding | Usually | No |
| Tax on gains deferred until you sell | Yes | Not relevant (sheltered) |
| ACB tracking needed for tax | Yes | No |

The dividend tax credit calculation is covered in [the dividend tax credit and after-tax yield](/investing/dividends/dividend-tax-credit-after-tax-yield/); this post doesn't repeat it.

| Hypothetical (40% marginal rate) | Amount |
|:--|--:|
| Capital gain realized on Stock A | $12,000 |
| Capital loss realized on Stock B | −$8,000 |
| Net capital gain | $4,000 |
| Taxable portion (50%) | $2,000 |
| Tax at 40% | $800 |
| Tax without the loss ($12,000 × 50% × 40%) | $2,400 |
| Tax saved by the loss | $1,600 |

The same $8,000 loss inside a TFSA saves nothing. That is the core argument for holding higher-risk, higher-variance ideas in a taxable account, where a loss at least has some value.

## Carrybacks and carryforwards

If losses exceed gains in a year, the net capital loss can be applied against taxable capital gains in any of the three previous years, or carried forward with no expiry date. Net capital losses can only offset capital gains, not employment or dividend income (except in the year of death).

## The rules that catch stock investors

| Rule | What it does | Where to read more |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Superficial loss | Denies a loss if you (or an affiliated person, including your TFSA or RRSP) buy the same share within 30 days before or after the sale | [Superficial loss rule](/investing/accounts/superficial-loss-rule-stock-swaps/) |
| Average ACB | Identical shares bought at different prices share one average cost | [ACB tracking](/investing/accounts/adjusted-cost-base-tracking-diy-portfolios/) |
| DRIPs | Each reinvested dividend adds to ACB | [DRIPs in taxable accounts](/investing/dividends/drip-strategies-taxable-accounts-acb/) |
| Return of capital | Reduces ACB; can create a gain later | [Return of capital](/investing/dividends/return-of-capital-distributions-what-to-track/) |
| Foreign income | US dividends taxed as income; withholding creditable | [US withholding](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/) |

## Deferral is built in

A non-dividend stock held for 15 years in a taxable account isn't taxed until it's sold. That deferral is the reason the taxable account is a reasonable home for some growth stocks. See [account location for growth stocks you expect to compound](/investing/accounts/account-location-growth-stocks/).

## Which holdings fit here

| Holding type (hypothetical) | Why a taxable account can fit |
|:--|:--|
| Eligible Canadian dividend payers | Dividend tax credit applies only here |
| Higher-risk positions | A loss can offset gains |
| Low-dividend growth stocks | Gains are deferred until sale, half included |
| US dividend payers (after RRSP room) | Withholding is usually creditable |
| Positions you may want to harvest | Loss harvesting only works here |

## A taxable-account checklist

| Question | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Is ACB tracked for every holding, including DRIPs and ROC? | Required for accurate gains |
| Do I have unused net capital losses from prior years? | They can offset future gains |
| Am I about to sell at a loss and buy back within 30 days (in any account)? | Superficial loss |
| Are US dividends reported with the tax withheld? | Foreign tax credit |
| Are Canadian dividends eligible or other than eligible? | Different gross-up and credit |
