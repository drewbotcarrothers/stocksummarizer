US dividend stocks raise a placement question Canadian payers don't: a 15% US withholding tax that's **exempt** in some accounts, **creditable** in others and **lost** in the rest. The account-by-account rule is covered in [withholding tax on US dividends: TFSA vs RRSP vs taxable](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/). This post is the stock picker's follow-up: **which** US holdings deserve scarce RRSP room, which can live in a TFSA, and what changes in a taxable account.

Educational only — not tax advice. Treaty treatment depends on your residency and paperwork; check the IRS and CRA sources below or a cross-border professional. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Rank US holdings by how much withholding they'd lose. **High-yield US dividend payers** benefit most from the RRSP (or RRIF), where US-listed shares held directly are generally exempt from US withholding under Article XXI of the Canada–US tax treaty. Next best is a **taxable account**, where 15% is withheld (with a W-8BEN on file) but usually claimable as a foreign tax credit — though US dividends are then taxed as ordinary foreign income, with no dividend tax credit. **Low-yield US growth stocks** are the US names that suit a TFSA best, because the unrecoverable withholding is small relative to their expected price gains.

The withholding is a percentage of the **dividend**, not of the investment. A US growth stock yielding 0.5% loses less than a tenth of a percent a year in a TFSA — a reasonable price for tax-free capital gains. A 4–6% yielder loses roughly 0.7–0.9% every year, and that compounds.

## What changes in each account

| Issue | RRSP / RRIF | TFSA (and FHSA) | Taxable |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| US withholding on US-listed shares held directly | Generally exempt (treaty Art. XXI) | 15% withheld, not recoverable | 15% with W-8BEN; foreign tax credit usually available |
| Canadian tax on the dividend | None now; withdrawals taxed as income later | None | Taxed as foreign income at full rates (no dividend tax credit) |
| Currency | USD cash can often be held; avoids repeat conversions | Same | Same; also track FX in [ACB](/investing/accounts/adjusted-cost-base-tracking-diy-portfolios/) |
| A dividend cut and price drop | Loss not deductible | Loss not deductible, room gone | Capital loss usable (watch the [superficial loss rule](/investing/accounts/superficial-loss-rule-stock-swaps/)) |
| Paperwork | Broker handles exemption status | — | Make sure the W-8BEN is current, or withholding can be 30% |

Two things the RRSP exemption **doesn't** cover: US withholding inside a **Canadian-listed** fund that holds US stocks, and foreign withholding on non-US companies. For funds, see [US withholding tax inside Canadian-listed vs US-listed ETFs](/investing/indexes-etfs/us-withholding-tax-canadian-vs-us-listed-etfs/).

## Interlisted Canadian companies are not US dividends

Buying a Canadian bank, railway or pipeline on the NYSE doesn't create a US dividend — it's still a Canadian-source dividend, with no US withholding. Those names follow the logic in [account location for Canadian dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-canadian-dividend-stocks/), even if you hold them in USD.

## Other cross-border wrinkles

| Wrinkle | What to know |
|:--|:--|
| W-8BEN expiry | Brokers usually refresh it; if it lapses, withholding can jump to 30% |
| FX conversion costs | Converting CAD to USD at retail rates can cost more than the withholding you're trying to save; see [commission-free trading: FX and spreads](/investing/brokers-tools/commission-free-trading-fx-spreads/) |
| US estate tax | Larger holdings of US-situs assets (US stocks, including inside registered accounts) can raise US estate-tax questions for Canadians; check thresholds with a cross-border advisor |
| Foreign property reporting | If the total cost of specified foreign property in non-registered accounts exceeds $100,000 at any time in the year, Form T1135 is required |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Putting high-yield US payers in the TFSA by default | Loses 15% of every dividend, forever | RRSP first, taxable second |
| Assuming a Canadian-listed US ETF is exempt in the RRSP | Withholding happens inside the fund | Hold US-listed shares directly in the RRSP if exemption matters |
| Converting currency for every trade | FX costs can dwarf withholding savings | Keep USD in USD where your broker allows |
| Forgetting T1135 | Penalties for late filing | Track total foreign cost in taxable accounts |
