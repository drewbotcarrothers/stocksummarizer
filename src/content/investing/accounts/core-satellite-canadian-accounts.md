The [core-satellite approach](/investing/process/core-satellite-portfolio-basics/) — a cheap index core plus a capped sleeve of individual stocks — is simple on paper. In Canada it gets more complicated, because most DIY investors hold money in **several accounts with different tax rules**: a TFSA, an RRSP, possibly an FHSA, and a taxable (non-registered) account. Where you put the core and the satellites changes your after-tax result, what happens when a pick goes wrong, and how easy it is to rebalance.

Educational only — not financial or tax advice. Tax rules change and depend on your situation; check the [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html) or a qualified professional. All dollar amounts below are **hypothetical**. This post assumes you've already read the general concept in [Building a core-satellite portfolio](/investing/process/core-satellite-portfolio-basics/).

## Direct answer

Treat all your accounts as **one portfolio**. Set the satellite cap (say 10–20%, hypothetical) on the **household total**, not per account. Then place holdings by tax character and time horizon: money needed within a few years (often the FHSA) stays mostly in the core; US equity exposure in the core often fits best in the RRSP, where US-listed holdings can avoid US dividend withholding; long-term satellites with the most upside are often placed in the TFSA, accepting that losses there can't be claimed; and Canadian dividend-paying satellites, or positions you may want to sell at a loss, often fit the taxable account. Rebalance with new contributions and trades inside registered accounts to avoid triggering taxable gains.

A common mistake is running a separate core-satellite split inside each account. That multiplies the paperwork and usually ends with satellites everywhere. A single household-level cap is easier to track and lets each account do what it's best at.

## Step 2: what each account is good at

| Account | Tax on gains and dividends | If a satellite loses money | Horizon / access | Typical role (illustrative) |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| TFSA | Tax-free growth and withdrawals | Loss is not deductible, and that room is gone | Flexible; withdrawals re-added to room the next calendar year | Long-term satellites, Canadian holdings |
| RRSP | Tax-deferred; withdrawals taxed as income | Loss not deductible | Long-term (retirement) | Core, especially US equity exposure |
| FHSA | Contributions deductible; qualifying home withdrawals tax-free | Loss reduces your down payment | Limited participation period | Mostly core if the purchase is near |
| Taxable | Dividends and part of capital gains taxed annually | Capital losses can offset capital gains (watch the superficial loss rule) | Fully flexible | Canadian dividend payers, satellites you may need to trim |

Two account-specific details matter most for stock pickers:

- **US dividends.** US-listed stocks and ETFs held directly in an RRSP are generally exempt from the 15% US withholding tax under the Canada–US tax treaty; in a TFSA or FHSA that tax is withheld and can't be recovered. In a taxable account it can usually be claimed as a foreign tax credit. Details: [US dividend withholding tax by account](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/).
- **Losses.** A satellite that falls 50% inside a TFSA is a permanent loss of tax-free room; the same loss in a taxable account at least creates a capital loss. That asymmetry is why some investors put their most speculative ideas in the taxable account, not the TFSA.

## Step 4: rebalancing without tax friction

| Situation | Lower-friction fix |
|:--|:--|
| Satellites grow above the household cap | Trim winners inside the TFSA or RRSP first (no tax triggered) |
| Core needs topping up | Direct new contributions to the core rather than selling satellites |
| A taxable satellite has a loss | Consider realizing it to offset gains — but avoid buying it back within the superficial-loss window |
| A taxable satellite has a big gain | Rebalance around it with new money before selling and realizing the gain |
| FHSA purchase date is approaching | Shift remaining FHSA risk toward the core or cash-like holdings |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Per-account caps | Satellites creep into every account | One household cap, tracked in a single sheet |
| Speculative picks in the TFSA by default | Losses permanently shrink tax-free room | Keep the TFSA for long-term, high-conviction holdings |
| Frequent trading in a TFSA | CRA can treat business-like trading as taxable business income | Keep satellites long-term; see [TFSA for stock investors](/investing/accounts/tfsa-for-stock-investors/) |
| US dividend payers in the TFSA | 15% withholding is lost every year | Hold US dividend payers in the RRSP or taxable account where practical |
| Satellites in an FHSA you'll draw on soon | A drawdown can delay a home purchase | Keep short-horizon money in the core |
| Forgetting adjusted cost base in taxable | Mistakes at tax time | Track ACB for every taxable satellite |
