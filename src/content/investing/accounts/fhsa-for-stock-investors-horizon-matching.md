A self-directed first home savings account (FHSA) can hold the same TSX stocks and ETFs as a TFSA. For a stock picker that's tempting: a tax deduction on the way in and a tax-free withdrawal for a qualifying home purchase. The catch is that the money has a date attached. A stock portfolio that's fine over 20 years can be a problem over two, so the first decision in an FHSA isn't which ticker. It's how much equity risk the purchase date can take.

Educational only — not tax advice and not a recommendation on how to invest your FHSA. Rules below are from CRA guidance as of November 2026. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Decide the **risk budget from the purchase date first**, then pick holdings. The FHSA gives **$8,000 of participation room a year** (up to $40,000 lifetime, with up to $8,000 of unused room carried forward), contributions are **deductible**, and a **qualifying withdrawal is tax-free**. A self-directed FHSA can hold **securities listed on a designated stock exchange**. But a stock that falls 30% needs a **43% gain** to recover, and a home purchase may not wait for it. Losses inside an FHSA can't be claimed. If the home purchase doesn't happen, the balance can generally be transferred to an RRSP, which changes the horizon entirely.

The purchase date sets how long you can wait out a decline. A framework many investors use is to shrink the equity share as the date gets closer. The table below is an illustration of that idea, not a recommended allocation.

| Years until intended purchase | Illustrative equity share | Individual stock exposure | Reasoning |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 10+ | High | Possible, diversified | Time to recover from at least one bear market |
| 5–10 | Moderate | Limited, sized small | One deep decline could still delay the purchase |
| 2–5 | Low | Minimal | Little time to recover |
| Under 2 | Very low or none | None | The money is close to being spent |

The FHSA has a time limit too. Your maximum participation period ends on December 31 of the year of the earliest of: the 15th anniversary of opening your first FHSA, the year you turn 71, or the year after your first qualifying withdrawal. Fifteen years is a real horizon for equities. Eighteen months is not.

## What the account can hold, and what it can't fix

| Feature | What it means for a stock investor |
|:--|:--|
| Self-directed (trusteed) FHSA | Can hold qualified investments, including securities listed on a designated stock exchange |
| Deduction on contribution | Like an RRSP; you can claim it in the year or carry it forward |
| Qualifying withdrawal | Tax-free if it meets CRA's first-home conditions |
| Capital losses | Not claimable inside the account |
| Contribution room after a loss | Not restored by the loss |
| US dividends | Don't assume the treaty's retirement-plan exemption applies as it does in an RRSP |

For the US withholding side, see [how the Canada–US tax treaty affects DIY stock holders](/investing/accounts/canada-us-tax-treaty-diy-stock-holders/).

## A hypothetical: same stocks, different dates

Two investors each put $16,000 into the same mix of TSX stocks. In year two, the holdings fall 30%, to $11,200.

| | Investor A: buying in 18 months | Investor B: buying in 10 years |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Value after decline | $11,200 | $11,200 |
| Gain needed to recover | +42.9% | +42.9% |
| Time to recover before purchase | About 18 months | About 8 years |
| Practical effect | Down payment shrinks or purchase delayed | Room to wait |

Same stocks, same loss, very different consequence. That difference is the horizon, and it should come before the stock list.

## If the purchase doesn't happen

If you don't buy a qualifying home, CRA allows the FHSA balance to be transferred to an RRSP or RRIF on a tax-deferred basis without using RRSP room. At that point the money becomes retirement savings, and a longer-horizon stock approach can make sense. See [RRSP for stock investors](/investing/accounts/rrsp-for-stock-investors/).

## A stock investor's FHSA checklist

| Question | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| When do I realistically plan to buy? | Sets the risk budget |
| How much could I lose before the plan breaks? | Turns a percentage into a dollar answer |
| Is any single stock more than a small share? | Company risk on a short horizon |
| What happens if I don't buy? | RRSP transfer changes the horizon |
| Am I treating the FHSA like a trading account? | Short horizons and frequent trading add risk |
