Tax-loss selling is one of the few tax tools a DIY stock picker controls: sell a loser in a taxable account, realize a capital loss, and use it against capital gains. The catch is the **superficial loss rule**, which denies the loss if you effectively keep the same position. Getting the details right matters most when you want to **swap** into something similar rather than sit in cash.

Educational only — not tax advice. "Identical property" is a question of fact; confirm unusual situations with a tax professional. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

A capital loss is a **superficial loss** — and is denied — if you or a person **affiliated** with you (including your spouse or common-law partner, a corporation either of you controls, or a trust such as your TFSA or RRSP where you're a majority-interest beneficiary) **acquire identical property** during the period from **30 days before to 30 days after** the sale, and still own it at the end of that period. If the repurchase is in a taxable account, the denied loss is added to the ACB of the new shares, so it's deferred rather than lost. If the repurchase is in a TFSA or RRSP, the loss is effectively gone. Swapping into a **different company** (for example, one bank for another) generally avoids the rule; swapping into something that's arguably identical (a different class of the same shares, or an option to buy them) doesn't.

| Scenario (hypothetical) | Superficial? | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Sell 200 shares at a loss on day 0, buy 200 back on day 18 | Yes | Identical property acquired in window and held on day 30 |
| Bought 100 more on day −12, then sell the original 100 at a loss on day 0 | Yes | Acquisitions in the 30 days **before** count too |
| Sell on day 0, buy back on day 35 | No | Outside the window |
| Sell on day 0, buy back day 10, sell again day 25 (none held at day 30) | No for the first loss | Must still own identical property at the end of the period |
| Sell in taxable on day 0, spouse buys the same stock day 5 | Yes | Spouse is an affiliated person |
| Sell in taxable on day 0, buy same stock in your TFSA day 2 | Yes, and the loss is lost | Your TFSA counts; no ACB to add it to |
| Sell 200 at a loss, buy back 50 within the window | Partly | Loss denied proportionally to the shares reacquired |

| Where you reacquired | What happens to the denied loss | Practical result |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Your taxable account | Added to ACB of the new shares | Deferred: you'll get it back when you sell the new shares |
| Spouse's taxable account | Added to the spouse's ACB of the new shares | Shifts to your spouse |
| Your TFSA or RRSP | Not added anywhere useful | Permanently lost |
| A corporation you control | Generally deferred inside the corporation | Get advice before relying on it |

Worked example (hypothetical): you bought 100 shares at $60 (ACB $6,000) and sell at $45 (proceeds $4,500), a $1,500 loss. You buy 100 back at $46 nine days later in the same taxable account. The $1,500 loss is denied and added to the new shares' cost: new ACB = $4,600 + $1,500 = **$6,100**. Tracking this correctly is an [ACB ledger](/investing/accounts/adjusted-cost-base-tracking-diy-portfolios/) job.

## Swaps: what's identical and what isn't

| Swap (hypothetical) | Generally identical? | Notes |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Same company, same share class, any exchange (TSX vs NYSE listing) | Yes | Same security |
| Same company, different share class with essentially the same rights | Likely | Compare rights; often treated as identical |
| Call option or warrant on the same shares | Treated as identical | Rights to acquire identical property are caught |
| Bank A → Bank B | No | Different companies, different risks |
| One oil producer → another oil producer | No | Different businesses |
| ETF tracking index X → another provider's ETF tracking the same index X | Uncertain | CRA treats it as a question of fact; many investors switch to a different index to be safe |
| Individual stock → sector ETF holding it | Generally no | Different property, but consider how concentrated the ETF is |

For stock pickers, the cleanest swap is usually to a **different company in the same sector** whose thesis you'd actually own anyway — which is also a good test of whether the original thesis still holds. If you're not sure, the [peer comparison method](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/) helps pick a genuine alternative.

## Timing details that trip people up

| Detail | What to know |
|:--|:--|
| Settlement date | Tax treats the settlement date as the disposition date; Canadian and US stock trades now settle T+1, so check year-end deadlines |
| Year-end loss selling | Trades must settle in the calendar year to count for that year |
| DRIPs | An automatic reinvestment within the window is an acquisition of identical property — consider pausing the DRIP |
| Accounts at different brokers | Still you; still counted |
| Your spouse's trades | Coordinate before either of you sells at a loss |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Re-buying in the TFSA "because it's a different account" | Loss permanently denied | Wait 31 days or buy a different company |
| Forgetting the 30 days **before** | A recent top-up can taint the sale | Check purchases in the prior month |
| Leaving a DRIP on | Small reinvestments can deny part of the loss | Suspend DRIP around the sale |
| Assuming any ETF swap is safe | Same-index funds may be identical | Switch to a different index or wait |
| Not adjusting ACB after a denied loss | Overstates future gains | Add the denied loss to the new ACB |
