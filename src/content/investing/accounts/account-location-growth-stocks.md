A growth stock you expect to hold for a decade or more raises a different placement question from a dividend payer. There is little or no annual income to shelter, so the tax question is mostly about what happens on the day you sell, and what happens if the bet fails. The answer often isn't "put it in the TFSA and forget it."

Educational only — not tax advice. Examples are **hypothetical** and use simplified tax rates. For dividend payers, see [account location for Canadian dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-canadian-dividend-stocks/) and [account location for US dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-us-dividend-stocks/).

## Direct answer

For a low- or no-dividend growth stock, the **TFSA** gives the best result if the stock compounds, because the gain is never taxed. The **taxable account** is closer than many people think, because tax is **deferred until you sell** and only **half the gain is included** in income. And if the stock fails, only the taxable account lets you **use the loss**; a TFSA loss also means the contribution room spent on it isn't restored. An **RRSP** defers tax but turns the eventual gain into fully taxed income on withdrawal. US growth stocks that pay little or nothing lose little to US withholding, so the TFSA's main drawback for US stocks mostly disappears.

| Hypothetical: $10,000 at 10% a year for 20 years | Ending value | Tax on sale | After tax |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| TFSA | $67,275 | $0 | $67,275 |
| Taxable, held 20 years then sold | $67,275 | $12,887 | $54,388 |
| Taxable, gains realized every year | — | Paid yearly | $44,499 |

Assumes a 45% marginal rate, so a 22.5% effective rate on capital gains (half included). No dividends.

The TFSA wins. But the taxable account that holds and doesn't trade keeps about 81% of the TFSA result, while the one that realizes gains every year keeps about 66%. Holding period matters almost as much as account type. An RRSP is equivalent to a TFSA in after-tax terms only when your tax rate at withdrawal matches your rate when you contributed; if it's lower, the RRSP does better, and if it's higher, worse.

## If the stock fails

Growth stocks have a wider range of outcomes. Some compound, some go to zero.

| Hypothetical: $10,000 that falls to $0 | TFSA | Taxable | RRSP |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Loss claimable? | No | Yes, against capital gains | No |
| Tax value of the loss (22.5% effective) | $0 | $2,250 | $0 |
| Contribution room | Not restored | Not relevant | Not restored |

A TFSA is the best home for a winner and the worst home for a total loss. If you hold a basket of speculative growth names, the taxable account partly insures the losers.

| Growth holding (hypothetical) | Reasonable first home | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Established compounder, long holding period | TFSA | Large expected gain, tax-free |
| Speculative, wide range of outcomes | Taxable | Losses usable; deferral on winners |
| US growth stock, little or no dividend | TFSA or taxable | Withholding drag is small |
| US growth stock with a meaningful dividend | RRSP | Treaty withholding exemption |
| A position you might trade around | Taxable | Avoids TFSA business-income questions |

## US growth stocks and withholding

The 15% US withholding tax applies to dividends, not to gains. A US stock yielding 0.3% loses about 0.045 percentage points a year in a TFSA. The withholding cost that makes the RRSP the usual home for US dividend payers is close to nil for low-yield growth names. The treaty details are in [how the Canada–US tax treaty affects DIY stock holders](/investing/accounts/canada-us-tax-treaty-diy-stock-holders/).

## Behaviour is part of the location

The deferral benefit of a taxable account only works if you don't trade. Selling a winner to "lock in" gains, then buying something else, starts the tax clock. In a TFSA the same habit costs nothing in tax but can raise other questions; see [risk of business-like trading inside a TFSA](/investing/accounts/tfsa-business-like-trading-risk/).

| Habit | Cost in a taxable account | Cost in a TFSA |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Hold for 15+ years | Deferred tax, half inclusion | None |
| Trim winners every year | Tax every year | None, if not business-like |
| Average down into a falling stock | Higher ACB; loss later usable | Room spent on a possible loser |

## A growth-stock location checklist

| Question | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| How wide is the range of outcomes? | Wide ranges favour a taxable account for loss value |
| How long will I really hold it? | Long holds narrow the TFSA-vs-taxable gap |
| Does it pay a meaningful US dividend? | If yes, the RRSP becomes more attractive |
| Is my TFSA room better used elsewhere? | Room is limited; losses don't restore it |
| Will I trade around the position? | Trading erodes taxable deferral |
