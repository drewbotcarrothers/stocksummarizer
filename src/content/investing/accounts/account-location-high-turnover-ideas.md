Not every idea is a ten-year hold. Earnings plays, short-term momentum trades, and positions you expect to trim and add around all turn over quickly. Where you hold them changes how gains are taxed, whether losses count, and how much paperwork you create. Most of the risk comes from treating a fast-moving sleeve exactly like a long-term portfolio.

Educational only — not tax advice. Whether your activity is investing or trading is a question of facts that CRA decides case by case. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

For high-turnover ideas, the **taxable account** is usually the cleanest home: gains are taxed as you go, **losses are usable**, and nothing about the account itself creates a special risk. Two things change with frequency. First, if your activity looks like a **business**, gains can be taxed as **business income, fully included**, rather than as capital gains at 50%. Second, round-trip trading makes the **superficial loss rule** and **ACB tracking** harder. In a **TFSA**, frequent trading can lead CRA to tax the account itself; that risk is covered in [risk of business-like trading inside a TFSA](/investing/accounts/tfsa-business-like-trading-risk/) and isn't repeated here. In an **RRSP**, losses are wasted and there's no reason to accept that for speculative trades.

Business income does have one mirror-image benefit: losses become fully deductible business losses rather than capital losses. That doesn't make it a better outcome for a profitable trader.

Some investors use CRA's election on Form T123 to have gains and losses on Canadian securities treated as capital. Under the Income Tax Act, that election isn't available to traders or dealers in securities, so it isn't a fix for a trading pattern.

## Account by account

| Account | High-turnover fit | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Taxable | Usually best | Losses usable; no account-level risk; clear records |
| TFSA | Poor at high frequency | Account-level business income risk; losses wasted |
| RRSP | Poor | Losses wasted; room spent on speculation |
| FHSA | Poor | Short horizon plus losses wasted; see [FHSA for stock investors](/investing/accounts/fhsa-for-stock-investors-horizon-matching/) |

A separate taxable account for the fast-moving sleeve also makes records easier and keeps the long-term holdings in other accounts out of the picture.

## Round trips and the superficial loss rule

Frequent traders often sell a stock at a loss and buy it back soon after. If that happens within 30 days before or after the sale, in any of your accounts or your spouse's, the loss is denied and added to the ACB of the replacement shares.

| Hypothetical round trip | Shares | Price | Result |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|
| Buy (day 0) | 500 | $40 | ACB $20,000 |
| Sell (day 12) | 500 | $34 | Loss of $3,000 |
| Buy back (day 20) | 500 | $35 | Loss denied; new ACB $17,500 + $3,000 = $20,500 |

The loss isn't gone forever in a taxable account; it's deferred into the new ACB. Buy it back in your TFSA instead and it is gone. See [superficial loss rule for stock swaps and replacements](/investing/accounts/superficial-loss-rule-stock-swaps/).

## The record-keeping load

Each trade changes your ACB. With many partial buys and sells, an average-cost spreadsheet or broker report is essential, and broker ACB figures can be wrong after transfers or corporate actions. See [ACB tracking for DIY stock portfolios](/investing/accounts/adjusted-cost-base-tracking-diy-portfolios/).

| Record | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| Trade date and settlement date for every buy and sell | Superficial loss windows use settlement dates |
| Commissions and FX costs | Part of ACB and proceeds |
| Written reason for each position | Helps show intent if CRA asks |
| Separate account for short-term ideas | Cleaner records and fewer surprises |

## A high-turnover location checklist

| Question | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|
| How many trades a year, and how short are the holds? | Frequency drives the business-income question |
| Is this activity in a TFSA? | Account-level risk; read the TFSA post first |
| Will losses be usable? | Only in a taxable account |
| Am I buying back losers within 30 days anywhere? | Superficial loss |
| Can I rebuild ACB for every position? | Required at tax time |
