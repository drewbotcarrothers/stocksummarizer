Gold miners are not traditional dividend stocks, but several large Canadian-listed producers now pay meaningful dividends, and some investors own them partly for income. The trouble is that gold miner dividends are set in good years and tested in bad ones.

For how miner margins and dividend policies work, see [gold miner dividends: cyclical income reality check](/investing/dividends/gold-miner-dividends-cyclical-income-reality-check/). This post is the mistakes list. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The costly mistakes are judging a dividend at **today's gold price**, using **cash costs instead of AISC**, forgetting **growth capital and taxes**, ignoring that many miners **declare dividends in US dollars**, missing **jurisdiction and single-mine risk**, treating **royalty and streaming companies** as if they were miners, and assuming a long record protects the payout. In 2013, after gold fell from its 2011 high, Barrick cut its quarterly dividend 75% and Kinross suspended its dividend.

## Mistake 1: coverage at one gold price

A fixed dividend can be cheap at high gold prices and unaffordable at lower ones. Test it at several.

## The full mistakes table

| # | Mistake | Why it happens | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Coverage at today's gold price | Results look strong | Test at three prices, including a lower one |
| 2 | Using cash cost instead of AISC | Cash cost is lower | Use AISC, then subtract growth capital |
| 3 | Forgetting taxes and royalties | AISC excludes some | Check cash taxes and government royalties |
| 4 | Ignoring the US dollar | Shares trade in C$ | Check the currency the dividend is declared in |
| 5 | Ignoring single-mine risk | Company looks large | Check the largest mine's share of production |
| 6 | Ignoring jurisdiction | Risk feels far away | Read the country risk section of the AIF |
| 7 | Treating streamers like miners | Same sector | Different cost structure and dividend capacity |
| 8 | Counting on performance top-ups | Formulas sound automatic | Read the formula; it can shrink to zero |
| 9 | Ignoring reserve replacement | Production looks steady | Check reserve life and replacement each year |
| 10 | Buying for yield at a peak | Yield rises with payouts | Compare payout to a mid-cycle gold price |

## Mistake 4: the dividend is in US dollars

Many large Canadian-listed gold miners report in US dollars and declare dividends in US dollars. A Canadian investor's income then moves with the exchange rate.

| Hypothetical US$0.40 annual dividend | USD/CAD 1.25 | USD/CAD 1.35 | USD/CAD 1.45 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Income in C$ | $0.50 | $0.54 | $0.58 |

That isn't bad in itself, but it adds another moving part to a dividend that already moves with gold. See [how currency affects US dividend income for Canadians](/investing/dividends/currency-us-dividend-income-canadians/).

## Mistake 5 and 6: one mine, one country

A miner with one large mine supplying most of its cash flow can lose its dividend capacity overnight through a permit dispute, a pit wall failure or a change in government. Read the AIF's risk factors and look at production by mine. Count the jurisdictions you're exposed to across all your miners.

## Mistake 7: royalty and streaming companies

Royalty and streaming companies don't operate mines. They fund mines in return for a share of production or revenue, so they have much lower costs and capital needs. Their dividends behave differently and usually yield less. Don't compare a streamer's payout ratio with an operator's.

## Mistake 8: performance dividends

Some producers pay a base dividend plus a top-up tied to gold prices or net cash. Read the formula. If it depends on net cash above a threshold, a large acquisition or project can switch it off even with high gold prices.

## A gold miner dividend check

| Question | Healthy answer |
|:--|:--|
| Dividend ÷ FCF at a lower gold price | Below 100% |
| AISC trend | Stable or falling |
| Largest mine share of production | Not dominant |
| Net cash or low leverage | Yes |
| Dividend currency | Understood |
| Reserve life | Long enough to sustain production |
