Canadian telecoms have been dividend staples for a generation. Monthly bills feel like a utility, the market has few national carriers, and dividends have historically grown. But telecoms spend heavily on networks and spectrum, carry a lot of debt, and compete on price. The dividend has to fit around all of that.

This list goes with the [Canadian telecom dividends sustainability framework](/investing/dividends/canadian-telecom-dividends-sustainability-framework/), which sets out the five-part framework and the BCE 2025 example. Here the focus is on what investors get wrong. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The costly mistakes are measuring payout against **free cash flow before lease payments**, ignoring **spectrum spending** because it is lumpy, assuming the **dividend growth record** will continue, treating a **discounted DRIP** as free, ignoring **leverage**, missing changes in **competition and subscriber growth**, and confusing a stable monthly bill with a stable business. In May 2025 BCE cut its dividend by about 56%, a reminder that a long record isn't a guarantee.

## Mistake 1: the flattering payout ratio

Telecoms report free cash flow in more than one way. The same dividend can look comfortable or stretched depending on what you subtract.

## Mistake 2: ignoring spectrum because it is lumpy

Spectrum auctions happen every few years and the bills are large. Companies often present them as one-time items and fund them with debt. Over a decade, though, spectrum is a recurring cost of staying in business.

## The full mistakes table

| # | Mistake | Why it happens | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | FCF payout before leases | Companies headline the friendlier number | Use FCF after lease payments |
| 2 | Ignoring spectrum | It looks one-time | Average spectrum spending over ten years |
| 3 | Trusting the growth record | Years of raises build confidence | Re-check coverage each year; records end |
| 4 | Treating a discounted DRIP as free | Cash payout looks lower | Watch share count growth and the discount |
| 5 | Ignoring leverage | Bills are recurring, so debt feels safe | Track net debt to EBITDA against the company's target |
| 6 | Missing competition changes | Three national players feels permanent | Watch wireless pricing, ARPU and new entrants |
| 7 | Assuming subscriber growth continues | Population growth was strong for years | Check net additions and immigration-driven demand |
| 8 | Ignoring non-telecom segments | Media and other assets are small | Check impairments and capex in media or other units |
| 9 | Counting on asset sales | Announced deals sound certain | Treat proceeds as real only when closed |
| 10 | Owning all the big telecoms | Diversified by name | Same industry, same regulator, same rate sensitivity |

## Mistake 3: the record is not the forecast

A long run of raises tells you what management prioritized in the past, not what it can afford next year. When telecom free cash flow growth slowed and leverage rose, BCE cut its quarterly dividend in May 2025 from C$0.9975 to C$0.4375. The framework post covers what changed at the same time.

## Mistake 4: discounted DRIPs

Some telecoms have issued new shares at a discount to fund part of the dividend through their DRIP. The cash dividend looks better covered, but the share count grows and each share's future dividend is spread thinner. If the company stops the discount, more of the dividend has to come out of cash. Check the DRIP section of the dividend release and the share count trend.

## Mistake 6 and 7: market structure and subscriber growth

Telecom dividends depend on pricing discipline. When a stronger fourth carrier pushes prices down, or when immigration-driven subscriber growth slows, revenue per user and net additions can both weaken. That shows up first in wireless results, then in free cash flow. Use the [telecom ARPU, churn and capex analysis](/investing/analyze/analyze-telecoms-arpu-churn-capex/) habits each quarter.

## A quick telecom dividend check

| Question | Healthy answer |
|:--|:--|
| Dividend ÷ FCF after leases | Comfortably below 100% |
| Ten-year average spectrum included? | Still below 100% |
| Net debt to EBITDA | Within the company's target and falling |
| Share count growth | Low, no discounted DRIP |
| Wireless ARPU and net additions | Stable or improving |
