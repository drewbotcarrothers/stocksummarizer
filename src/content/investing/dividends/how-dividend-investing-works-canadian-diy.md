Dividend investing means owning stocks (or funds) that distribute cash to shareholders — then judging whether that cash is **sustainable**, after tax, and worth the opportunity cost versus total return.

For Canadians, the story is not just yield: **eligible dividend** tax treatment, TFSA/RRSP location, and Canada’s bank/utility/pipeline income culture all matter. Educational only — not advice.

## Direct answer

Treat dividend investing as **capital allocation literacy**: start with free-cash-flow coverage and payout sustainability, then compare after-tax outcomes by account type, and only then look at yield. Yield alone is a terrible screen.

## The cash-flow chain

1. Business earns cash.
2. Management allocates: reinvest, pay down debt, buy back shares, or pay dividends.
3. You receive a distribution (or distribution-like cash from REITs/trusts with different tax character).
4. Your account type changes what you keep after tax.

## Yield is not safety

High trailing yield can mean a falling price (value trap), an unsustainable payout, or a special dividend distorting the math. Prefer:

- Payout vs earnings **and** vs free cash flow
- Balance-sheet headroom
- Cycle awareness (energy producers ≠ utilities)

## Canada-specific lenses

- **Eligible dividends** in taxable accounts can be more tax-efficient than interest — still not a reason to ignore business quality.
- **US dividends in TFSA** generally face withholding that you may not recover — location literacy matters.
- **REIT distributions** are not the same as corporate dividends; character and ACB effects differ.
- **Aristocrat marketing** is a methodology story — read rules, not stickers.

## Building an income sleeve without abandoning total return

Many DIY investors keep an index core and add a dividend satellite of banks, utilities, pipelines, or select REITs they understand. Track total return (price + dividends) so you do not celebrate yield while underperforming a boring ETF after risk adjustment.
