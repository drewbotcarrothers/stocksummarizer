Midstream companies that process gas, fractionate NGLs and market products often yield as much as the big pipelines. Investors sometimes treat them as the same thing. The difference shows up when commodity margins fall, customers cut drilling or a growth project needs funding.

This list goes with [how to analyze dividends in Canadian midstream](/investing/dividends/analyze-dividends-canadian-midstream/), which sets out the fee-based coverage test. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The common mistakes are treating **midstream as pipeline-like**, counting **marketing income as recurring**, ignoring **volume risk** on fee-for-service contracts, overlooking **customer concentration** and **recontracting**, missing **frac spread** and **export** sensitivity, and underestimating how **big growth programs and leverage** can force a reset. AltaGas cut its monthly dividend by about 56% in December 2018 as part of a plan to cut debt, a reminder that midstream dividends depend on the balance sheet.

## Mistake 1: calling a good marketing year "normal"

Marketing segments can swing by hundreds of millions a year. If the dividend was set or raised after a strong marketing year, it may depend on that income repeating.

## Mistake 2: volume risk on "fee-based" contracts

"Fee-based" doesn't always mean guaranteed. A fee per gigajoule processed earns nothing if producers send less gas. Check how much fee-based EBITDA has take-or-pay or minimum volume commitments, and how much depends on actual throughput.

| # | Mistake | Why it happens | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Treating marketing income as recurring | Recent year looks normal | Average it over five years |
| 2 | Assuming all fees are guaranteed | "Fee-based" label | Separate take-or-pay from volume-based fees |
| 3 | Treating midstream like long-haul pipelines | Same sector name | Compare contracted share of EBITDA |
| 4 | Ignoring customer concentration | Customers aren't named in quotes | Check top-customer share and credit quality |
| 5 | Ignoring recontracting | Contracts look long | Note when major contracts expire and at what rates |
| 6 | Missing frac spread exposure | It's buried in sensitivities | Read the NGL / propane sensitivity |
| 7 | Treating export growth as low risk | Terminals sound like infrastructure | Check shipping, tariff and price exposure |
| 8 | Ignoring leverage during growth | Projects promise future cash | Track debt to EBITDA vs target through construction |
| 9 | Missing equity and hybrid funding | Raised in separate releases | Watch share count and hybrid issuance |
| 10 | Pairing midstream with producers as diversification | One is a "service" | Both depend on Western Canadian drilling |

## Mistake 5: recontracting

When a long contract expires, the new rate may be lower if competing capacity has been built. A company can lose part of a facility's EBITDA without anything breaking. Look for contract expiry disclosures and any comments about rates on renewal.

## Mistake 8: leverage during a growth phase

A midstream company can look comfortable on paper while debt rises to fund new facilities that haven't started earning yet. AltaGas is the Canadian example. In December 2018, with its share price falling and debt elevated after a large US acquisition, it cut its monthly dividend from C$0.1825 to C$0.08 and announced asset sales to cut debt.

## A midstream warning-sign list

| Sign | What to check next |
|:--|:--|
| Dividend raised after a record marketing year | Fee-based payout in a weak year |
| Fee-based share of EBITDA falling | Which segment is growing and why |
| Top customer cutting capex | Volume commitments in that contract |
| Leverage above target for two years | Funding plan and asset sale progress |
| New hybrid or equity issue | Use of proceeds and per-share impact |
