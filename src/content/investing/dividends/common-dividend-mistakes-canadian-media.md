Media dividends have produced some of the TSX's clearest warnings and some of its steadiest growers. The difference usually shows up in the revenue mix and the balance sheet long before the dividend changes. Most mistakes come from looking at a yield and a payout ratio while ignoring where revenue is heading and what lenders allow.

This list goes with [how to analyze dividends in Canadian media](/investing/dividends/analyze-dividends-canadian-media/). Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The common mistakes are **trusting free cash flow cover when leverage is the binding limit**, **counting asset-sale proceeds** as dividend capacity, ignoring **cord-cutting and advertising cycles**, treating a **high yield on a shrinking business** as income, missing **credit agreement restrictions**, assuming **dual-class shares pay the same**, and buying a **telecom for its media** or a media stock for telecom-like stability. Corus reported C$106.8M of free cash flow in fiscal 2023 and still suspended its dividend, because its lenders and its leverage, not cash cover, set the limit.

## Mistake 1: trusting cover when leverage is the constraint

A dividend can be covered by free cash flow and still be cut if debt is too high relative to profit. Boards and lenders then prefer debt repayment.

| Hypothetical broadcaster | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Segment profit (C$M) | 400 | 330 | 280 |
| Net debt (C$M) | 1,100 | 1,100 | 1,050 |
| Net debt ÷ segment profit | 2.8x | 3.3x | 3.8x |
| Free cash flow (C$M) | 150 | 120 | 100 |
| Dividend (C$M) | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| FCF cover | 3.0x | 2.4x | 2.0x |

Cover looks fine every year. Leverage is what crosses a covenant. Corus reported pro forma net debt of 3.62 times segment profit at August 31, 2023, and suspended its dividend the same October.

## Mistake 2: counting asset sales as capacity

Selling a division raises cash once. If it goes to debt repayment, it doesn't support the dividend. Corus said its fiscal 2023 bank debt repayment of C$172M included C$141M of net proceeds from selling its Toon Boom animation software business. That repayment helped leverage, but the cash couldn't repeat.

## Mistake 3: ignoring the direction of revenue

A yield can look generous because the stock has fallen with the business. Compare the trend in each revenue line, not just the total.

| Hypothetical channel group, C$M | 3 years ago | Today | Annual change |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Subscriber fees | 520 | 450 | −4.7% |
| TV advertising | 700 | 560 | −7.2% |
| Digital and streaming | 60 | 110 | +22.4% |
| Total | 1,280 | 1,120 | −4.4% |

Digital growth is real but too small to offset the decline. A dividend set on the old total is set too high.

| # | Mistake | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|
| 1 | Trusting FCF cover when leverage binds | Track net debt ÷ segment profit vs covenant |
| 2 | Counting asset-sale cash | Exclude it; check where it went |
| 3 | Ignoring revenue direction | Read each revenue line's trend |
| 4 | Treating a high yield on a shrinking business as income | Ask what revenue looks like in three years |
| 5 | Missing credit agreement restrictions | Read AIF and credit amendments on SEDAR+ |
| 6 | Assuming all share classes pay the same | Check the rate per class |
| 7 | Buying a telecom for its media or the reverse | Analyze the segment that drives the dividend |
| 8 | Ignoring strikes and content supply | Read commentary on program supply |
| 9 | Forgetting a US-dollar dividend | Translate income at several exchange rates |
| 10 | Assuming a long growth streak means no risk | Check what drives the growth |

## Mistake 5: missing lender restrictions

Credit agreements can limit dividends when leverage rises. Corus's October 2023 credit amendment, as described in its Annual Information Form, raised the maximum allowed debt-to-cash-flow ratio and introduced additional restrictions on distributions to shareholders. Those amendments are filed on SEDAR+ and are worth reading before buying any leveraged media stock for income. See [how to read debt notes and covenant language](/investing/analyze/read-debt-notes-covenant-language/).

## Mistake 6: one dividend, two classes

Dual-class companies can pay different amounts on each class. After its March 2023 reset, Corus's annual rate was C$0.12 per Class B non-voting share and C$0.115 per Class A voting share. Check which class you own and which class trades.

## Mistake 10: assuming a streak is the whole story

Thomson Reuters has raised its dividend for 33 consecutive years, including a 10% increase to US$2.62 for 2026. The streak rests on subscription revenue from legal, tax and accounting professionals. The lesson is to know what drives the growth, so you notice if that driver changes. See [how to read a dividend history chart beyond the streak](/investing/dividends/read-dividend-history-chart-beyond-streak/).

## A media warning-sign list

| Sign | What to check next |
|:--|:--|
| Net debt ÷ segment profit rising for 2+ years | Covenant level and amendments |
| Asset sales used to repay debt | Recurring FCF without them |
| Advertising and subscriber fees both falling | Whether digital growth can offset |
| Yield far above peers | Whether the market expects a cut |
| Credit agreement amended | Distribution restrictions |
| Writers' or actors' strikes | Program supply and ad revenue next quarters |
