Healthcare sounds defensive, and some of it is. But the Canadian healthcare dividends most investors can buy sit on businesses with thin margins, heavy debt and government-set revenue. The mistakes come from assuming "healthcare" means "safe."

This list goes with [how to analyze dividends in Canadian healthcare](/investing/dividends/analyze-dividends-canadian-healthcare/), which covers business models, occupancy, labour and funding. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The common mistakes are assuming **demographics guarantee dividends**, treating **government funding as a guarantee**, ignoring **interest rate hedges that expire**, judging payout at **target occupancy instead of current occupancy**, missing **wage and agency staffing costs**, chasing **monthly high yields**, and overlooking **international or tenant concentration**. Northwest Healthcare Properties REIT is the example on debt: in September 2023 it cut its monthly distribution 55%, from C$0.06667 to C$0.03 a unit, citing expected 2024 results including the maturity of interest rate caps.

## Mistake 1: demographics are not a dividend policy

An aging population raises demand for care over decades. It doesn't decide this year's occupancy, wage settlements or interest costs. Treat demographics as a long-run tailwind, not a reason to skip the payout check.

Northwest said the maturity of its interest rate caps in early 2024 was expected to reduce AFFO per unit by about C$0.16 to C$0.20 a year. Against an annual distribution of C$0.80, that was a large hit. Read the debt note for the expiry dates of hedges, not just the current average rate.

| Hedge check | Where to find it |
|:--|:--|
| Share of debt that is floating rate | Debt note |
| Hedge type and expiry dates | Financial instruments note |
| Management estimate of impact | MD&A outlook |

## Mistake 3: government funding is not a guarantee

Long-term care revenue is largely set by provincial funding. Funding can rise, but not always as fast as costs, and policy can change with each budget.

## The full mistakes table

| # | Mistake | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|
| 1 | Treating demographics as a dividend policy | Check this year's payout |
| 2 | Ignoring expiring interest rate hedges | Read hedge expiry dates |
| 3 | Treating government funding as a guarantee | Compare funding and wage growth |
| 4 | Judging payout at target occupancy | Use current occupancy |
| 5 | Missing agency staffing costs | Read labour cost commentary |
| 6 | Chasing a monthly high yield | Check AFFO per unit cover |
| 7 | Overlooking tenant or country concentration | Read the property and tenant mix |
| 8 | Ignoring redevelopment funding needs | Read capex plans and funding sources |
| 9 | Confusing healthcare property with healthcare operations | Know whether you collect rent or run the care |
| 10 | Assuming a cut is the end of the risk | Check leverage after the cut |

## Mistake 4: target occupancy vs current occupancy

Retirement operators publish occupancy targets. The distribution is paid from today's occupancy.

| Hypothetical | Current 88% | Target 95% |
|:--|--:|--:|
| AFFO per unit | C$0.50 | C$0.68 |
| Distribution per unit | C$0.60 | C$0.60 |
| Payout | 120% | 88% |

## Mistake 5: monthly high yields

Many healthcare property and seniors care issuers pay monthly, which feels like a paycheque. A high monthly yield with AFFO cover above 100% is borrowing from the future. See [how to avoid dividend traps on the TSX](/investing/dividends/avoid-dividend-traps-tsx/).

## Mistake 6: concentration abroad or in one tenant

Some Canadian-listed healthcare property owners hold assets in several countries, adding currency and local rate risk. Others depend on a few large hospital or clinic tenants. Read the geographic and tenant breakdown.

## Mistake 7: rent collector or care operator?

Owning a healthcare REIT that collects rent is different from owning an operator that runs the homes and bears the wage costs. The first is a property business, the second an operating business with property attached. Know which one you hold.

## A healthcare warning-sign list

| Sign | What to check next |
|:--|:--|
| AFFO payout above 100% | Time to recover and funding of the gap |
| Interest rate hedges expiring within a year | Estimated AFFO impact |
| Wage settlement above funding increase | Margin outlook |
| Occupancy below the level that covers the distribution | Recovery pace |
| Asset sales to fund debt repayment | Lost income vs debt saved |
