Insurance stocks sit next to the banks in most Canadian dividend portfolios, but they don't behave like banks. Life insurers carry long-dated promises that are sensitive to interest rates and equity markets; property and casualty insurers carry the weather. The mistakes below come from treating them as interchangeable income stocks.

This is a mistakes list. For the full method, see [how to analyze dividends in Canadian insurance](/investing/dividends/analyze-dividends-canadian-insurance/). Educational only; numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

The most common errors are measuring the payout on **IFRS 17 net income** instead of core or operating earnings, judging a P&C insurer on **one catastrophe-heavy quarter**, using **one set of metrics for life and P&C insurers**, ignoring **capital ratios and holding-company cash**, and assuming the 2008–09 experience can't repeat. Manulife halved its dividend in 2009 after market declines hit its capital. Life insurer dividends are well supported today by most measures, but they aren't immune to market shocks.

## The mistakes table

| # | Mistake | Why it happens | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Payout ratio on IFRS 17 net income | Net income is the headline number | Use core or operating EPS and read the reconciliation |
| 2 | Judging a P&C dividend on one quarter | Catastrophe losses cluster seasonally | Use trailing four quarters and multi-year combined ratios |
| 3 | Same checklist for lifecos and P&C | Both are "insurance" | Lifeco: LICAT, capital generation. P&C: combined ratio, MCT |
| 4 | Ignoring LICAT or MCT | Capital ratios feel technical | Track the ratio each quarter against the company's own range |
| 5 | Ignoring holding-company cash | Dividends look like they come from earnings | Check that holdco cash covers dividends and debt service |
| 6 | Dismissing a low yield | Some P&C insurers yield less than banks | Look at dividend growth and payout headroom, not yield alone |
| 7 | Missing market sensitivity | It's buried in the MD&A | Read the equity and interest rate sensitivity tables |
| 8 | Overlooking foreign exposure | Head offices are Canadian | Check segment earnings by region and the currency effect |
| 9 | Treating reserve releases as recurring | They boost reported earnings | Strip favourable development when judging run-rate coverage |
| 10 | Assuming "it can't happen again" | Strong recent record | Remember 2009; stress-test against a market drawdown |

Q3 looks alarming on its own. Across the year the dividend is covered 2.3 times. The question to ask is whether catastrophe losses are rising **year after year**, not whether one quarter was bad.

## Mistake 10: remember 2009

In the 2008–09 financial crisis, falling equity markets and interest rates increased the reserves and capital that some life insurers had to hold against products with guarantees. Manulife cut its quarterly dividend in half in August 2009. Since then the industry has changed products, hedging and capital rules, but life insurer dividends still depend on markets more than many income investors assume. Read each company's **sensitivity tables** to see how a 10% or 20% equity drop or a fall in interest rates would affect earnings and capital.

## Mistake 6: low yield isn't low quality

A P&C insurer with a modest yield and a low payout ratio may have more room to grow its dividend, and more cushion in a bad catastrophe year, than a higher-yielding name. Compare dividend growth and payout headroom, not just starting yield. [Dividend growth vs high yield](/investing/dividends/dividend-growth-vs-high-yield/) covers the trade-off.

## Quick checklist before buying for income

| Check | Lifeco | P&C |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Payout on the right earnings | Core / underlying EPS | Operating EPS, trailing four quarters |
| Capital ratio | LICAT vs company range | MCT vs company range |
| Cash behind the dividend | Holdco cash, remittances | Operating cash, reserve trend |
| Shock sensitivity | Equity and rate tables | Catastrophe loss history |
| Concentration | Combined weight with banks | Combined weight with banks |
