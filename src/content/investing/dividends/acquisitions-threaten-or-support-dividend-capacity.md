When a dividend payer announces a big acquisition, management usually talks about growth, synergies and accretion. Dividend investors should ask a narrower question: after the deal, is the dividend per share better or worse covered, and is the balance sheet strong enough to keep paying it through a bad year?

This post shows how to answer that question from the deal announcement. For judging management's broader M&A track record, see [how to analyze M&A track records of serial acquirers](/investing/analyze/analyze-ma-track-records-serial-acquirers/) and [organic vs acquisition growth](/investing/analyze/compare-organic-vs-acquisition-growth/). It's educational; figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

An acquisition **supports** dividend capacity when it raises **free cash flow per share** (not just total FCF), keeps **leverage** within the company's target, and the acquired cash flows are at least as stable as the existing business. It **threatens** dividend capacity when it's paid for with **heavily discounted new shares** that dilute FCF per share, pushes **debt above target** for years, depends on **synergies** that haven't happened yet, or swaps stable cash flow for cyclical cash flow. Recalculate the payout ratio on pro forma FCF per share.

## Reading the accretion claim

"Accretive" can mean many things. Ask which metric and when:

| Claim | How strong |
|:--|:--|
| Accretive to EBITDA | Weak: ignores interest, capex, taxes and share count |
| Accretive to adjusted EPS, year 3 | Moderate: depends on synergies and adjustments |
| Accretive to FCF per share, year 1 | Strong: closest to dividend capacity |
| Leverage-neutral by year 2 | Strong when paired with per-share accretion |

## Signals in the announcement

- **Dividend raise announced with the deal.** Can reflect confidence, or a way to soften a dilutive equity issue. Check the pro forma payout ratio.
- **Asset sales to fund it.** Selling stable assets to buy riskier ones changes the quality of what backs the dividend.
- **Hybrid or preferred financing.** Adds fixed claims ahead of common dividends; see [preferred shares and hybrid capital](/investing/analyze/analyze-preferred-shares-hybrid-capital/).
- **Credit rating agency comments.** A negative outlook after a deal is a useful warning.

## When acquisitions support dividends

Acquisitions can genuinely help: a regulated utility buying another regulated utility at a fair price, funded within its leverage target, can add stable rate base and lift per-share cash flow for years. The tests above don't assume deals are bad; they force the question back to per-share coverage and balance sheet risk.
