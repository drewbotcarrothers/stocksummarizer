Utilities are the stocks many Canadians buy when they want a dividend they don't have to think about. That comfort is where the mistakes start. Regulated utilities are reliable businesses, but their dividends depend on regulators, capital markets and interest rates in ways that a quick look at the yield won't show.

This is a mistakes list that goes deeper than the short section in [how utilities fund and sustain dividends](/investing/dividends/how-utilities-fund-sustain-dividends/), which explains the rate base model. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

The costly mistakes are judging coverage on **FCF after growth capex** (negative by design), watching **total dividends instead of dividend per share** while the share count grows, treating utilities as **bond substitutes** without accepting their **interest rate sensitivity**, assuming **allowed returns** are fixed, reading **dividend growth guidance** as a promise, and ignoring **wildfire, regulatory and currency risk** at US-heavy utilities. Owning utilities, pipelines and telecoms together can also mean holding the same interest rate bet three times.

## Mistake 1: treating utilities as bonds without the bond math

A utility's price often moves with long-term bond yields because many investors own utilities for income relative to bonds.

## Mistake 2: total dividends vs dividend per share

Utilities fund growth partly with new shares, including DRIP issuance. Total dividends paid can grow quickly while each share's dividend grows more slowly.

## The full mistakes table

| # | Mistake | Why it happens | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Treating utilities as bonds without rate risk | Steady dividends feel bond-like | Expect price swings when long bond yields move |
| 2 | Watching total dividends, not DPS | Headlines quote total growth | Track dividend per share and share count |
| 3 | FCF coverage after growth capex | Standard dividend-safety habit | Look at earnings payout and the funding plan instead |
| 4 | Assuming allowed ROE is fixed | Rate cases sound routine | Read rate case outcomes; a lower allowed ROE cuts earnings growth |
| 5 | Reading growth guidance as a promise | Multi-year ranges sound firm | Treat guidance as a target that depends on rate base growth and funding |
| 6 | Ignoring regulated vs unregulated mix | Everything is called a "utility" | Check how much earnings come from merchant power or contracts |
| 7 | Ignoring wildfire and climate liability | Seen as a US issue | Check service territory and liability regime; some US utilities suspended dividends after wildfires |
| 8 | Ignoring hybrid and preferred costs | They aren't common dividends | Count hybrid interest and preferred dividends ahead of common |
| 9 | Overlooking currency at US-heavy utilities | Listed in Toronto, reported in C$ | Check the share of earnings from US operations and FX sensitivity |
| 10 | Stacking utilities, pipelines and telecom | Diversified by name, not by risk | Add up rate-sensitive income weight across all three |

## Mistake 4: allowed returns change

Regulators set an allowed return on equity and an equity share of the rate base in periodic rate cases. A cut of half a percentage point in allowed ROE sounds small, but on a large rate base it can remove years of earnings growth. Read the regulatory section of the MD&A and [regulatory risk disclosures in the AIF](/investing/analyze/analyze-regulatory-risk-disclosures-aif/). The [how utilities make money](/investing/sectors/how-utilities-make-money/) primer explains the rate base formula.

## Mistake 7: wildfire and liability risk is real

Utility dividends are not immune to catastrophic liability. PG&E suspended its dividend after the 2017 California wildfires and filed for bankruptcy protection in 2019; Hawaiian Electric suspended its dividend in 2023 after the Maui wildfires. Several Canadian-listed utilities own US operations, so check where they operate and how liability is handled there.

## Mistake 10: the same bet three times

| Hypothetical income portfolio | Weight | Main shared risk |
|:--|--:|:--|
| Two utilities | 15% | Long bond yields, regulation |
| Two pipelines | 15% | Long bond yields, leverage |
| One telecom | 8% | Leverage, regulation |
| Rate-sensitive total | 38% | One factor drives a large slice of income and price |
