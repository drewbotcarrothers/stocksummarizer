Canadians often hold US dividend stocks for sectors that are thin on the TSX, like health care and consumer brands. The dividends arrive in US dollars, though, and most Canadians spend in Canadian dollars. That means your income in CAD moves with the exchange rate, gets trimmed by conversion costs, and has to be reported in CAD at tax time.

This post explains how currency affects US dividend income for Canadian investors. It is educational; figures are hypothetical. For withholding tax by account type, see [withholding tax on US dividends: TFSA vs RRSP vs taxable](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/); this post focuses on the currency side.

## Direct answer

A US dividend is paid in **US dollars**, usually after **15% US withholding** (except in RRSPs and RRIFs holding US-listed shares, where the tax treaty generally exempts it). Its value to you in Canadian dollars then depends on the **USD/CAD exchange rate** and any **conversion spread** your broker charges. A stronger US dollar raises your CAD income; a weaker one lowers it, even if the company raises its dividend. Reduce friction by **holding USD in a USD account** rather than converting each payment, and report the income in CAD using the **Bank of Canada rate** on the day received or the annual average rate, as CRA allows.

## Dividend growth vs currency moves

Currency can overwhelm a company's dividend increase in either direction.

| Hypothetical | Year 1 | Year 2 | Change |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Dividend per share (US$) | 4.00 | 4.20 | +5.0% |
| USD/CAD | 1.40 | 1.30 | −7.1% |
| Dividend per share (C$) | 5.60 | 5.46 | −2.5% |

The company raised its dividend 5%, yet your CAD income fell. The reverse happens when the US dollar strengthens. Over long periods these swings can partly even out, but there's no guarantee, and they matter most if you're **spending** the income. For how currency affects company results and valuation, see [handle currency: CAD vs USD results](/investing/analyze/handle-currency-cad-vs-usd-results/) and [how currency affects valuation when peers report in USD](/investing/valuation/currency-valuation-peers-report-usd/).

## The conversion cost leak

Every time USD is converted to CAD (or back), your broker typically applies a spread to the exchange rate. Spreads differ by broker and can be well over 1% on small conversions; check your broker's fee schedule.

| Hypothetical, C$2,000 of US dividends a year | Annual cost |
|:--|--:|
| Converted at a 1.5% spread | $30 |
| Converted at a 0.5% spread | $10 |
| Kept in USD and reinvested in US holdings | $0 conversion cost |

Ways investors reduce the leak:

- **Hold a USD account** (many brokers offer USD sides of TFSAs, RRSPs and taxable accounts) and reinvest US dividends in US holdings.
- **Convert in larger, less frequent batches** if your broker's spread shrinks with size.
- **Norbert's gambit**: buying a security listed in both currencies (for example, an ETF with CAD and USD units on the TSX, or an interlisted stock), then asking your broker to journal it to the other currency side and selling. It can cut costs on large conversions but involves trading steps and timing risk; check your broker's process.

## Tax reporting in Canadian dollars

| Item | How it works |
|:--|:--|
| Currency for reporting | Report foreign dividends in CAD |
| Exchange rate | Bank of Canada rate on the day received, or the annual average rate if received throughout the year (CRA accepts either, consistently applied) |
| Withholding in a taxable account | Generally claimable as a foreign tax credit, subject to limits |
| Withholding in a TFSA | Not recoverable |
| RRSP / RRIF holding US-listed shares | Treaty exemption generally applies; no withholding |
| Dividend tax credit | Doesn't apply to foreign dividends; they're taxed like interest |

Your broker's T5 usually reports foreign income in CAD already, but if you hold through a USD account, confirm which rate was used. See [account location for Canadian dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-canadian-dividend-stocks/).

## Planning your income mix

| If you... | Consider |
|:--|:--|
| Spend dividends in CAD | Expect CAD income to swing with the exchange rate; keep a cash buffer |
| Reinvest US dividends | Keep them in USD to avoid round-trip conversions |
| Want CAD-stable income | A higher share of Canadian payers, accepting sector concentration |
| Hold US stocks in a TFSA | Accept the 15% withholding drag, or hold them in an RRSP instead |

## Common mistakes

- Judging US dividend growth only in USD when you spend in CAD.
- Converting every small dividend at a retail spread.
- Assuming the dividend tax credit applies to US dividends.
- Holding US-listed dividend stocks in a TFSA without accounting for unrecoverable withholding.
