When two companies in the same sector both pay dividends, the higher yield is usually the first thing people compare. It's also the least informative. A dividend comparison should ask which payout is better covered, better funded and more likely to grow, using the same data basis for both.

This post is a dividend-specific scorecard. For a general framework to compare two TSX peers on business quality and valuation, use [how to compare two TSX peers](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/); this one only covers the dividend side. It's educational; companies X and Y are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Put both companies on the **same basis** (trailing 12 months, Canadian dollars, per share, same definitions), then score five dividend tests: **coverage** (payout ratio on free cash flow and the sector's own metric, like AFFO or distributable cash flow), **balance sheet** (leverage vs sector norms), **growth** (DPS and FCF per share growth), **record** (how the board behaved in bad years) and **yield vs history** (is today's yield high because the price fell?). Weight coverage and balance sheet most. The stock with the higher yield often scores lower.

## The scorecard

Score each test from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), then apply weights. The weights below are one reasonable starting point; adjust them to your own process.

| Test | Weight | What earns a 5 | X score | Y score |
|:--|--:|:--|--:|--:|
| Coverage | 30% | Payout well below 100% of FCF and the sector metric | 2 | 4 |
| Balance sheet | 25% | Leverage at or below sector norms with room to spare | 2 | 4 |
| Growth | 20% | DPS growth matched by FCF per share growth | 1 | 4 |
| Record | 15% | Held or raised through past downturns | 3 | 4 |
| Yield vs history | 10% | Yield near its normal range (not stretched by a price drop) | 2 | 4 |
| **Weighted total (out of 5)** | | | **2.0** | **4.0** |

## Common comparison mistakes

- Comparing a REIT's distribution with a corporation's dividend without adjusting for structure.
- Mixing a fiscal-year figure for one company with a trailing figure for the other.
- Comparing a US-dollar dividend with a Canadian-dollar one without converting.
- Ignoring that one company may pay part of its dividend in DRIP shares, which dilutes per-share growth.
