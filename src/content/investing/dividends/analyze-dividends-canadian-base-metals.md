Base metals miners produce copper, zinc, nickel and related metals. Their dividends tend to be small and sometimes erratic, because their cash flow depends on volatile metal prices and big, lumpy capital programs. That doesn't make their dividends meaningless. It means you analyze them differently from a bank or utility.

Educational only; worked numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named. For how these companies earn money, see [how base metals miners make money](/investing/sectors/how-base-metals-miners-make-money/).

## Direct answer

Analyze a base metals dividend by following the chain from **metal price** to **by-product credits**, **unit costs (C1 and AISC)**, **growth capital** and the **balance sheet**. Test free cash flow at several metal prices, compare it with the dividend, and check whether the company's policy ties payouts to free cash flow or sets a small fixed amount. Then look at **concentration** in one mine or country and the **debt maturity** schedule. First Quantum's January 2024 dividend suspension, after its largest mine was halted, shows why concentration matters.

## Step 2: test free cash flow at three prices

Worked example: a copper miner producing 300 million pounds a year at a US$2.75/lb AISC, with US$200 million of growth capital, paying a fixed US$150 million dividend.

| Hypothetical, US$M | Copper US$3.50/lb | US$4.25/lb | US$5.00/lb |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Margin over AISC | 225 | 450 | 675 |
| Growth capital | −200 | −200 | −200 |
| FCF after growth capital | 25 | 250 | 475 |
| Dividend | 150 | 150 | 150 |
| Coverage | 0.17x | 1.67x | 3.17x |

This is why many base metals miners keep fixed dividends small and return extra cash through buybacks or special dividends when prices are high.

## Step 3: read the dividend policy

| Policy type | How it works | What to watch |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Small fixed dividend | Token payout, rarely changed | Yield is low; total return depends on the business |
| Percentage of FCF | Payout moves with free cash flow | Income varies a lot year to year |
| Base plus top-up | Fixed base plus specials or buybacks | Plan on the base only |
| No dividend | Cash goes to growth or debt | Not an income holding |

The policy tells you what management is promising. A small fixed dividend is easy to keep; a large one set at the top of a cycle is not.

## Step 4: check concentration

Many base metals miners depend on a few large mines. If one stops, the dividend can stop with it. In November 2023, Panama's Supreme Court ruled First Quantum's contract for the Cobre Panama copper mine unconstitutional, and production was halted. On January 15, 2024, the company said it was suspending its dividend, cutting capital spending and exploring asset sales to protect its balance sheet.

| Question | Where to look |
|:--|:--|
| Largest mine's share of production or EBITDA | MD&A segment table |
| Countries of operation | AIF, risk factors |
| Permits and contract renewals due | AIF, legal proceedings |
| Single-commodity exposure | Revenue by metal |

## Step 5: check the balance sheet and maturities

Base metals companies borrow to build mines. When a project runs late or prices fall, debt repayment competes with the dividend. Look at net debt to EBITDA at a mid-cycle price, not just today's, and at when major bonds and loans fall due. Lenders have a claim before shareholders do.

## Step 6: by-products and costs

Copper miners often report costs net of by-product credits. If gold or molybdenum prices fall, reported copper costs rise even if nothing changed at the mine. Check how much of the cost advantage comes from by-products. Also check smelter treatment and refining charges, which affect what miners receive for concentrate.

## A base metals dividend checklist

| Question | Healthy answer |
|:--|:--|
| Dividend ÷ FCF at a lower metal price | Below 100%, or covered by net cash |
| Policy type | Clear and realistic for the cycle |
| Largest mine's share of EBITDA | Not dominant |
| Net debt at mid-cycle prices | Moderate |
| Major growth project | Funded without stretching the balance sheet |
| By-product reliance | Understood |
