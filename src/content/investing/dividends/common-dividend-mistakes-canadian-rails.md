Railway dividends rarely get into trouble. The mistakes investors make with CN and CPKC are usually about expectations: dismissing them for low yields, overpaying for safety, or missing how acquisitions and operating changes affect dividend growth.

This list goes with [how to analyze dividends in Canadian rails](/investing/dividends/analyze-dividends-canadian-rails/), which covers free cash flow after capex, the operating ratio and leverage. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The common mistakes are **dismissing a low starting yield** without considering growth, **buying for yield** when the stock is really a total-return holding, ignoring the **operating ratio**, forgetting that **acquisitions pause dividend growth**, counting **buybacks as income**, comparing to US railways without adjusting for **currency and structure**, and underestimating **labour, trade and weather** disruptions.

## Mistake 1: dismissing a low yield

A railway yielding 2% looks poor next to a utility at 5%. But if the railway's dividend grows much faster, the yield on your original cost can catch up over time.

## Mistake 2: buying rails for income

Railways are often better understood as total-return holdings with a growing dividend. If you need a set income level now, a 1% to 2% yield won't get you there, and stretching your portfolio toward railways for income will leave you short. Use them for growth and stability, and judge them on total return.

| 2 | Buying rails for current income | They feel as safe as utilities | Treat as total-return holdings |

## Mistake 4: acquisitions pause dividend growth

After a big acquisition, a railway's leverage rises and debt repayment comes first. CPKC held its quarterly dividend at C$0.19 from 2022 into 2025 while paying down debt from the Kansas City Southern deal, then raised it 20% to C$0.228 in April 2025. Investors who expected raises every year during that period were disappointed, though the dividend was never at risk.

## Mistake 8: disruptions

Railways face labour disputes, wildfires, floods, avalanches and trade policy changes. In August 2024 both CN and CPKC stopped operations in a labour dispute that ended with federal referral to binding arbitration. Wildfires and floods in British Columbia have also closed lines in recent years. These events rarely threaten the dividend, but they affect quarterly cash flow and can slow buybacks.

## Mistake 9: paying any price

Railways are high-quality businesses, and the market usually prices them that way. A great company bought at a very high valuation can still produce weak returns for years. Compare the current P/E and dividend yield with the company's own history. See [historical multiple ranges without anchoring](/investing/valuation/historical-multiple-ranges-without-anchoring/).

## A rails warning-sign list

| Sign | What to check next |
|:--|:--|
| Operating ratio rising for several quarters | Cause: labour, fuel, volumes, weather |
| Leverage above target | Expected time to return to target |
| Buybacks suspended | Free cash flow and capex plans |
| Volumes falling in a key commodity | Exposure and expected duration |
| Dividend growth well below EPS growth | Capital priorities, acquisitions |
