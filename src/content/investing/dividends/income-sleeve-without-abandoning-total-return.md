Some investors want a portfolio that pays them steady cash. Others want the highest long-run return. You don't have to pick one. An **income sleeve** is a deliberately sized slice of the portfolio chosen for dividend income, sitting next to a core that is still built for total return.

This post shows how to size and run a sleeve without letting the hunt for yield take over the whole portfolio. It's educational, not advice; all portfolios and figures are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

Decide the sleeve's **job** (for example, "cover a known cash need" or "keep me invested through bad markets"), cap its **size** (often a minority of the portfolio), diversify it **across sectors**, and judge it on **income growth and total return**, not yield alone. Keep the core in index funds or quality compounders whatever their yield. Rebalance with rules written in advance so the sleeve can't quietly become the whole portfolio.

This is a variation on [core-satellite portfolio basics](/investing/process/core-satellite-portfolio-basics/): the income sleeve is a satellite with a specific job.

## What the trade-off looks like

Compare three hypothetical C$300,000 portfolios.

| Hypothetical | All total-return core | 70% core / 30% income sleeve | All high-yield |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Core yield | 2.0% | 2.0% | — |
| Sleeve yield | — | 5.0% | 5.0% |
| Blended yield | 2.0% | 2.9% | 5.0% |
| Annual income | $6,000 | $8,700 | $15,000 |
| Sectors with 10%+ weight | Several, broad | Broad core, sleeve tilted | 4 rate- and credit-sensitive sectors |
| Biggest risk | Selling shares for cash in a bad year | Sleeve drifts beyond its cap | One sector shock hits most of the income |

The middle option raises income by about 45% over the core-only portfolio while keeping most of the money in the total-return engine. The all-high-yield portfolio lifts income by roughly another 70%, but look at what it owns.

That concentration is why [yield alone is a terrible screen](/investing/dividends/why-yield-alone-is-a-terrible-screen/) and why high-yield-only portfolios can see income and capital fall together. See also [dividend growth vs high yield](/investing/dividends/dividend-growth-vs-high-yield/).

## Rules that keep the sleeve honest

| Rule | Hypothetical setting | Why it helps |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Size cap | Sleeve at most 30% of the portfolio | Stops yield-chasing from taking over |
| Sector cap inside the sleeve | No sector above 30% of sleeve income | Limits one shock hitting most of the income |
| Safety floor | Each holding passes an FCF coverage check | Keeps traps out; see [FCF coverage](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |
| Scoreboard | Income growth and total return, reviewed yearly | Prevents a high yield from hiding a falling price |
| Rebalance band | Trim back to cap when the sleeve exceeds it by 5 points | Turns drift into a rule, not a mood |
| Exit rule | Dividend cut or coverage below 1.0x for two years triggers a review | Decided before emotion arrives |

## How the sleeve and core interact

- **Cash flow.** Dividends from the sleeve can cover a known need, so you're less likely to sell core holdings in a falling market.
- **Rebalancing.** If the core falls, sleeve dividends can be directed into the core rather than reinvested in the sleeve.
- **Overlap.** Broad Canadian index funds already hold the big banks, utilities and pipelines. Check that your sleeve doesn't double that exposure; [Canadian equity ETFs vs individual stocks](/investing/indexes-etfs/canadian-equity-etfs-vs-individual-stocks/) shows how to look through the holdings.
- **Account location.** Where the sleeve sits affects after-tax income; see [account location for Canadian dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-canadian-dividend-stocks/).

## What doesn't belong in the sleeve

| Candidate | Why it is a poor fit for this job |
|:--|:--|
| Highest yield in a sector with coverage below 1.0x | Income at risk; likely trap |
| Covered call ETF chosen only for its distribution | Payout may include option premium and ROC; see [covered call ETFs vs dividend stocks](/investing/dividends/covered-call-etfs-vs-dividend-stocks/) |
| Stock with a recent special dividend inflating trailing yield | Forward income is lower than it looks |
| A fifth bank when the core index already holds six | Adds concentration, not diversification |

## Common mistakes

- Defining the sleeve by yield target alone ("everything must yield 5%").
- Never rebalancing, so the sleeve grows past its cap after a strong run for income stocks.
- Counting dividends as return while ignoring price declines in the same holdings.
- Duplicating index fund exposure without noticing.
