A **dividend trap** is a stock that looks like a generous income holding right before the income — and often the share price — falls. Lobster traps work because the way in is easy and the way out isn't. Dividend traps are similar: a high yield draws you in, and by the time the cut is announced, the price has usually already dropped.

Educational only — not financial advice. No specific stock is being called a trap here; examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Most dividend traps share a pattern: **a yield well above peers, payouts above free cash flow, rising debt, a deteriorating business, and management language that shifts from "growing" to "sustainable" to "reviewing."** You can catch most of them by checking five-year free-cash-flow coverage, the payout ratio on the right metric for the sector, the debt trend and maturities, and the latest MD&A language — *before* you look at the yield.

## Ten warning signs you can check

| # | Warning sign | Where to look |
|--:|:--|:--|
| 1 | Yield far above sector peers (not just the market) | Sector comparison — see [why yield alone is a terrible screen](/investing/dividends/why-yield-alone-is-a-terrible-screen/) |
| 2 | FCF coverage below 1.0× for two or more years | Cash-flow statement — [FCF coverage](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |
| 3 | Payout ratio above the company's own target | MD&A; [payout ratio explained](/investing/dividends/payout-ratio-explained-canadian-companies/) |
| 4 | Net debt rising faster than cash flow | Balance sheet; debt-to-EBITDA trend |
| 5 | Big maturities in the next 24 months at higher rates | Liquidity section of the [MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/) |
| 6 | Dividend growth slowing to token increases | Dividend history |
| 7 | Large DRIP participation or discount propping up cash coverage | Financing section; shares issued under the DRIP |
| 8 | Language change: "grow" → "sustain" → "review" | MD&A outlook and [earnings call](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-earnings-call-transcript/) |
| 9 | Credit rating outlook moved to negative | Company news releases; rating agency announcements disclosed by the company |
| 10 | Insider selling or reduced buybacks alongside a "safe" dividend message | Insider filings (SEDI) and capital-allocation commentary |

One sign alone isn't a verdict. Three or more together deserve real caution.

## TSX sector-specific traps

| Sector | Typical trap pattern | Better metric | Sector page |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| REITs | High distribution, AFFO payout near or above 100%, refinancing at higher rates | AFFO payout, debt maturities, NAV | [REITs](/sectors/real-estate-reits/) |
| Telecom | Dividend growth continued while capex, leverage and competition rise | FCF after capex and spectrum, debt-to-EBITDA | [Telecom](/sectors/telecom/) |
| Pipelines / midstream | Growth capex funded with debt while dividend grows | DCF coverage, leverage target | [Midstream](/sectors/midstream-pipelines/) |
| Utilities | Rate-base growth needing heavy equity issuance | Payout vs target, FFO-to-debt, share count | [Utilities](/sectors/utilities/) |

## What to do if you already own one

1. **Re-underwrite from scratch.** Would you buy it today at this price, knowing what you know? If not, why hold?
2. **Separate the dividend from the thesis.** A cut can be healthy if it funds debt reduction — some stocks re-rate after a well-explained reset.
3. **Check your position size.** A stock you're nervous about shouldn't be a top holding; see [position sizing](/investing/process/position-sizing-diy-stock-pickers/).
4. **Write kill criteria in advance** — for example, "exit if FCF coverage stays below 0.9× for another year." The [stock brief template](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/) has a slot for them.
5. **Mind tax.** In a taxable account, selling at a loss and buying the same stock within 30 days before or after the sale can trigger the superficial loss rule — check with a tax professional before swapping.

## Common mistakes

- Buying *because* the yield jumped.
- Trusting "the dividend is safe" from management without checking coverage.
- Averaging down repeatedly into a falling yield story.
- Assuming an index label (like "Aristocrat") rules out traps — see [Canadian Dividend Aristocrats explained](/investing/dividends/canadian-dividend-aristocrats-explained/).
