Insurance companies pay dividends out of a business that is harder to see than a bank's or a utility's. Profit depends on actuarial assumptions, investment markets and, for property and casualty insurers, the weather. Since IFRS 17 took effect in 2023, reported net income can also swing for accounting reasons that have little to do with dividend capacity.

This post shows how to analyze an insurer's dividend specifically. For the broader business model, see [how insurers make money](/investing/sectors/how-insurers-make-money/); for valuation, [valuing insurers with book value and combined ratio](/investing/valuation/valuing-insurers-book-value-combined-ratio/). Educational only; worked numbers are hypothetical.

## Direct answer

For **life insurers**, measure the payout against **core or underlying EPS** (the company's own non-IFRS measure), check the **LICAT ratio** against OSFI's supervisory target, and follow **capital generation and holding-company cash**, because the dividend is paid from cash the operating subsidiaries send up. For **property and casualty insurers**, use **operating EPS**, the **combined ratio** through a full year of catastrophe seasons, and the **MCT ratio**. In both cases, the dividend is safest when capital generated comfortably exceeds dividends plus the capital needed for growth.

## Lifeco vs P&C: what to measure

| Lens | Life insurer | P&C insurer |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Earnings base for payout | Core or underlying EPS | Operating or adjusted EPS |
| Capital test (OSFI) | LICAT: supervisory target total ratio 100% | MCT: supervisory target 150% |
| Typical reported capital | Well above the supervisory target | Well above the supervisory target |
| Key cash measure | Capital generation; holdco cash and remittances | Operating cash, reserve development |
| Main swing factors | Interest rates, equity markets, credit, policyholder behaviour | Catastrophes, claims inflation, reserve releases |
| Payout pattern | Stated payout range of core earnings | Often lower payout with steady raises |

Many life insurers state a target payout range of core or underlying earnings, commonly somewhere between roughly 30% and 55%. Check each company's stated range in its investor presentation; ranges differ and change.

The operating insurers must hold capital to meet regulators in each country. Only the excess can be paid up (remitted) to the parent. A lifeco with strong reported earnings but weak remittances, for example because a fast-growing business needs capital, has less dividend capacity than the EPS suggests.

Buybacks are usually the flexible item. If capital generation falls, a disciplined insurer cuts buybacks first; the dividend is the last thing to go. A company already using all its capital generation before buybacks has much less room.

## P&C insurers: think in full years

Catastrophe losses cluster in certain seasons (in Canada, summer storms, hail and wildfires), so a single quarter's earnings can look weak against the dividend. Judge coverage on **trailing four quarters** of operating EPS and look at the **combined ratio** over several years. A combined ratio below 100% means underwriting is profitable before investment income. See [how to analyze insurers vs banks](/investing/analyze/analyze-insurers-vs-banks/) for the underwriting checklist.

## The dividend checklist

| Check | Where to find it | Comfortable sign |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Payout on core / operating EPS | Results release, supplementary package | Inside or below the stated range |
| LICAT or MCT ratio | Quarterly report, capital section | Well above the supervisory target and stable |
| Capital generation vs dividends | Investor presentation, MD&A | Dividends a minority of capital generated |
| Holdco cash | MD&A, investor day | Covers at least a year of dividends plus debt service |
| Financial leverage ratio | MD&A | At or below the company's target |
| Market sensitivity | MD&A sensitivity tables | Moderate exposure to equity and rate shocks |
| P&C: combined ratio, 5 years | Annual reports | Consistently below 100% |

## Accounting traps

- **IFRS 17 net income** can move with market-driven items. That's why insurers report core or underlying earnings; read the reconciliation. [Adjusting multiples for IFRS quirks](/investing/valuation/adjust-multiples-ifrs-quirks/) explains the basics.
- **Reserve releases** at P&C insurers can lift earnings for a while; they aren't a permanent income stream.
- **Currency.** Large Asian and US operations mean the Canadian-dollar earnings base moves with exchange rates.
