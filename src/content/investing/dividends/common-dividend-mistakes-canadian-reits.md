Canadian REITs pay monthly, own buildings you can visit, and often yield more than banks or utilities. Those features make them easy to buy for the wrong reasons. Most REIT distribution cuts were visible in the numbers months ahead; investors missed them because they were watching the yield.

This list goes with [how to analyze dividends in Canadian REITs](/investing/dividends/analyze-dividends-canadian-reits/), which covers the per-unit method. Educational only; numbers are hypothetical unless a source is named.

## Direct answer

The common mistakes are reading a **rising yield as a bargain**, judging coverage on **FFO instead of AFFO**, watching **total AFFO instead of AFFO per unit**, ignoring the **debt maturity ladder**, assuming **occupancy and leasing costs** stay put, misreading **return of capital** and **special distributions paid in units**, and owning several REITs that share the **same property type or city**. When several small pressures arrive together, an 80% payout can become 100% quickly.

## Mistake 1: the rising yield

A REIT's yield rises when its unit price falls. Sometimes that is an opportunity. Often it's the market pricing in a cut before management announces one.

RioCan is a well-known example. Its units yielded about 8% in late 2020 after a steep fall in price. On December 3, 2020 it cut its monthly distribution by one-third, from C$0.12 to C$0.08 a unit, effective January 2021.

## The full mistakes table

| # | Mistake | Why it happens | Better habit |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Buying the highest yield | Looks like a discount | Compare with peers and ask what the market is pricing |
| 2 | FFO payout instead of AFFO | FFO is the headline number | Use AFFO, and check the maintenance capex assumption |
| 3 | Total AFFO, not AFFO per unit | "Record AFFO" headlines | Divide by weighted average units every year |
| 4 | Ignoring debt maturities | Debt is in the notes | Read the maturity ladder and rates on maturing debt |
| 5 | Assuming occupancy holds | Leases feel long | Check lease expiries for the next two years and top tenants |
| 6 | Ignoring leasing costs | They're lumpy | Average tenant incentives and leasing commissions over several years |
| 7 | Misreading return of capital | ROC sounds like a warning | Learn the tax character; judge safety on AFFO |
| 8 | Counting special distributions as income | They appear in the yield | Check if paid in units and consolidated; strip from yield |
| 9 | Trusting monthly payments as a sign of safety | Regular deposits feel stable | Frequency says nothing about coverage |
| 10 | Owning REITs with the same exposure | Different tickers | Check property type and city overlap |

## Mistake 4: the debt maturity ladder

REIT debt is usually spread over many years, but each year a slice is refinanced at current rates. If the slice maturing next year was borrowed at 3% and new debt costs 5%, AFFO per unit falls even if rents rise. Find the maturity table in the MD&A and note the weighted average rate on each year's maturities.

## Mistake 7 and 8: return of capital and special distributions

Many REIT distributions are partly **return of capital** for tax purposes. That is normal and is not, by itself, a safety signal. It lowers your adjusted cost base, which matters in a taxable account. See [return of capital: what to track](/investing/dividends/return-of-capital-distributions-what-to-track/).

Some REITs also declare a year-end **special distribution paid in units**, followed immediately by a unit consolidation so the unit count doesn't change. It is mostly a tax allocation, not extra cash. Don't add it to the yield.

## Mistake 10: concentration you can't see on the label

| Hypothetical REIT sleeve | Weight | Main exposure |
|:--|--:|:--|
| Office REIT | 5% | Downtown office, refinancing |
| Diversified REIT | 5% | Majority office by NOI |
| Retail REIT | 5% | Grocery-anchored plazas |
| Office-like exposure | 10% | Two "different" REITs, one property type |

Check the NOI split by property type in each REIT's MD&A, not just the name.

## A REIT warning-sign list

| Sign | What to check next |
|:--|:--|
| Yield well above peers | Analyst questions on the last call; AFFO per unit trend |
| AFFO payout above 90% | Maintenance capex assumption; next year's maturities |
| Units issued below NAV | AFFO yield on the units vs the acquisition yield |
| Occupancy falling two quarters running | Lease expiry schedule; largest tenants |
| Distribution "under review" | Treat as a likely cut and re-check your thesis |
