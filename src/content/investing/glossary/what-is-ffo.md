**Funds from operations (FFO)** is the headline earnings measure for real estate investment trusts (REITs). It starts from net income and removes items — mainly property revaluations and one-off gains — that say little about the rent a REIT collects year after year.

Educational glossary entry — not a recommendation. Examples are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

FFO = IFRS net income, adjusted to remove unrealized fair-value changes on investment properties, gains or losses on property sales, deferred taxes and certain other non-recurring or non-cash items. In Canada, most REITs follow the industry association **REALPAC**'s white paper on FFO and AFFO for IFRS. FFO per unit is the REIT equivalent of earnings per share, and **P/FFO** is the REIT equivalent of P/E. FFO is a **non-GAAP measure**, so each REIT must reconcile it to net income in the MD&A — always read that reconciliation.

## Why REITs need FFO at all

Under IFRS, Canadian REITs usually carry investment properties at **fair value**, and changes in that value run through net income. A good appraisal year can double reported profit without one more dollar of rent; a cap-rate shock can wipe it out. FFO strips those swings so you can see the recurring operating result.

## FFO vs AFFO vs net income

| Measure | What it includes | Best used for | Watch out for |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Net income (IFRS) | Everything, including property revaluations | Statutory picture; NAV direction | Swings with appraisals and cap rates |
| FFO | Net income minus fair-value changes, disposal gains and other listed items | Comparing operating earnings across REITs (P/FFO) | Ignores the capex needed to keep buildings competitive |
| AFFO | FFO minus sustaining capex, leasing costs and straight-line rent | Judging whether the **distribution** is covered | No single definition — reserves for capex vary widely |
| Cash flow from operations (IFRS) | Actual operating cash, including working capital | Reality check on FFO and AFFO | Lumpy quarter to quarter |

## Common FFO adjustments (Canadian practice)

| Typical adjustment | Direction | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Unrealized fair-value gains/losses on investment properties | Removed | Non-cash appraisal changes |
| Gains/losses on disposal of properties | Removed | Not recurring rent |
| Deferred income taxes | Removed | Non-cash |
| Fair-value changes on exchangeable units or financial instruments | Removed | Accounting classification, not operations |
| Transaction costs on business combinations | Added back | One-off |
| Equity-accounted joint ventures | Adjusted to the same FFO basis | Consistency |

Lists vary — that's the point of reading the reconciliation. **US REITs** use the Nareit definition under US GAAP, where real estate depreciation is the big add-back, so P/FFO comparisons across the border need care (see [comparing a TSX stock to its US peer](/investing/valuation/compare-tsx-stock-to-us-peer/)).

## How to use FFO without fooling yourself

| Check | Rule of thumb |
|:--|:--|
| Payout ratio | Compare distributions to **AFFO**, not FFO; above ~100% of AFFO leaves no cushion |
| Per-unit trend | Track FFO **per unit** — total FFO can grow through dilutive unit issuance |
| Same-property NOI | Separate organic rent growth from acquisitions |
| Definition drift | Check whether the REIT changed what it adjusts out |
| Debt | FFO can look stable while interest costs reset higher on refinancing |

A distribution cut usually shows up in the AFFO payout ratio long before the announcement — see [REIT distributions vs corporate dividends](/investing/dividends/reit-distributions-vs-corporate-dividends/) and our dated brief on [Allied Properties' distribution cut](/summaries/2026-11-02-ap-allied-distribution-cut/).
