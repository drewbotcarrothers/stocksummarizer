Most DIY investors who enjoy researching stocks face a quiet problem: concentrated stock picking is risky, but owning only index funds leaves no room for their own ideas. The **core-satellite** structure solves that by giving each job its own bucket — a broad, cheap **core** that delivers market returns, and a smaller **satellite** sleeve where your stock picks live under clear limits.

Educational only — not financial advice. All allocations below are **hypothetical** examples, not recommendations. This post covers the general concept; account-specific setups (TFSA, RRSP, taxable) are a separate topic.

## Direct answer

A core-satellite portfolio puts most of your money (often described as roughly 70–90%) in diversified, low-cost index ETFs and a smaller share in individual stocks or targeted positions. The core keeps your overall result close to the market; the satellites express your research without letting any single mistake sink the plan. The key is writing down the satellite's **size cap, position limits and benchmark** before you buy anything.

## How big should the satellite be?

There's no correct number. The point is to **choose it deliberately** and only raise it after your results earn it — the same idea as in the [ETF investor to stock picker playbook](/investing/pathways/etf-investor-to-stock-picker/).

## Rules that keep the satellite honest

1. **Cap per stock** — for example, no single satellite above 5% of the total portfolio (hypothetical). See [position sizing](/investing/process/position-sizing-diy-stock-pickers/).
2. **Written thesis for every buy** — use a [bull / bear / watch brief](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/).
3. **Check overlap** — buying a bank stock when your core already holds all the banks is an *added* overweight, not diversification. See [why Canada is not the S&P 500](/investing/indexes-etfs/canada-vs-sp-500-sector-weights/).
4. **Rebalance on a schedule** — if satellites grow past the cap, trim back to the core; if they shrink, don't automatically top them up.
5. **Benchmark with dividends** — compare the satellite with a total-return index, not the price index ([what the Composite measures](/investing/indexes-etfs/what-sp-tsx-composite-measures/)).

If after three to five years the satellite hasn't beaten (or at least matched) a simple index after costs, that's useful information: the core could absorb the satellite money, and you'd keep the research as a hobby.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Satellite creep | One winner grows to 30% of the portfolio | Hard cap + scheduled trims |
| Doubling the core | Satellites duplicate the index's biggest names | Check look-through overlap |
| Too many satellites | 25 tiny positions become an expensive index | Fewer, higher-conviction picks within caps |
| No benchmark | You can't tell skill from luck | Track total return vs index yearly |
| Raiding the core | Selling core ETFs to fund a hot idea | Fund satellites only from new money or the sleeve itself |
