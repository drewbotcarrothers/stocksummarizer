**Position sizing** is how much of your portfolio one idea is allowed to become — before emotions negotiate. For Canadian DIY stock pickers, sizing collides with an already concentrated domestic market (financials and energy weight in common benchmarks).

Educational process literacy — not advice. No one-size formula fits every household.

## Direct answer

Decide a **maximum single-name weight**, a **satellite sleeve budget** versus your index core, and stricter caps into earnings or speculative ideas. Write the rules before you buy. Review them when concentration creeps via dividends, DRIP, or winners that grew.

## Why Canada context matters

Even a “diversified” TSX-heavy book can be a banks-and-energy bet. Adding three more bank stocks as “diversification” may not reduce risk the way a global ETF would. Size individual Canadian names with that correlation in mind.

## A simple rule stack (illustrative)

These are **examples of rule shapes**, not recommendations:

1. **Core vs satellite:** keep most equity in broad ETFs; limit total individual-stock sleeve.
2. **Single-name cap:** e.g. hard ceiling as a % of total equity for any one stock.
3. **Speculation tuition cap:** smaller ceiling for venture, binary catalysts, or names you barely understand.
4. **Into-earnings size:** reduce size when a binary print can gap overnight.
5. **Add rules:** adds only after thesis confirmation — not because price fell alone.

## Process beats precision

A crude written rule you follow beats an elegant Kelly-formula cosplay you abandon. Pair sizing with **kill criteria** and a quarterly review ritual.
