The Canadian market is already concentrated — heavy in banks, energy and materials, light in technology and health care compared with the US. We covered that at the **index** level in [Banks and energy weight: why Canada is not the S&P 500](/investing/indexes-etfs/canada-vs-sp-500-sector-weights/). This post is about the next layer: **your** portfolio. When a Canadian investor owns a Canadian index ETF, adds a few favourite bank and pipeline stocks, works in the same industry and owns a home in a Canadian city, the real concentration can be far higher than any single holding suggests.

Educational only — not financial advice. All weights and portfolios below are **hypothetical** and do not describe any real index or fund.

## Direct answer

Concentration risk is the chance that one shared driver — a sector, an interest-rate move, a country's economy, your employer — hurts many of your holdings at once. To measure it, **look through** your ETFs to their holdings and add your individual stocks, then add exposures outside the brokerage account (employer shares, pension, job, home). Track a few numbers: your largest single-stock weight, your look-through weight in each sector, your **effective number of holdings**, and how many positions share the same factor (for example, rate sensitivity). Then set a written concentration budget and check it at least once a year.

## Effective number of holdings

Counting positions overstates diversification when weights are uneven. A simple measure is the **effective number of holdings**: 1 ÷ (sum of squared weights).

| Portfolio (hypothetical) | Positions | Weights | Effective number |
|:--|--:|:--|--:|
| A — equal weight | 20 | 20 × 5% | 20.0 |
| B — top-heavy | 20 | 2 × 20%, 6 × 5%, 12 × 2.5% | 9.8 |
| C — concentrated | 8 | 8 × 12.5% | 8.0 |

## Factor concentration

Different tickers can share one driver. Group your holdings by their **main sensitivity**:

| Shared driver | TSX areas that often share it | What a shock looks like |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Interest rates | Utilities, telecoms, REITs, pipelines, preferred shares | Rising rates hit all of them together |
| Oil and gas prices | Producers, services, some midstream, the C$ | An oil slump hits income and currency at once |
| Canadian housing and credit | Banks, lenders, REITs, home-improvement retailers | A housing slowdown hits banks and your home equity |
| Metal prices | Gold, copper, base-metal miners | Commodity downturns hit miners together |

The sector explainers — [utilities](/investing/sectors/how-utilities-make-money/), [telecom](/investing/sectors/how-telecoms-make-money/), [REITs](/investing/sectors/how-reits-make-money/), [midstream](/investing/sectors/how-midstream-pipelines-make-money/) — show why so many Canadian income stocks share rate sensitivity.

## A simple concentration budget

| Rule (illustrative — set your own numbers) | Example limit |
|:--|:--|
| Single stock, as % of total portfolio | 5% |
| Look-through sector total | No more than 1.5× the sector's weight in a broad global index |
| Holdings sharing one main driver | No more than one-third of the satellite sleeve |
| Employer exposure (shares, options, plan holdings) | 5% or less, and sell plan shares on a schedule |
| Review frequency | Annually, and after any position doubles |

These numbers are examples, not recommendations. What matters is writing them down before markets test them — the same discipline as [position sizing](/investing/process/position-sizing-diy-stock-pickers/). Placement across accounts is covered in [core-satellite design for Canadian accounts](/investing/accounts/core-satellite-canadian-accounts/).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Counting tickers, not exposures | 15 stocks can be three bets | Group by sector and main driver |
| Ignoring the ETF's holdings | Satellites double the index's biggest names | Look through before buying |
| Ignoring employer and job risk | A downturn can cost both your job and your savings | Cap employer shares |
| "Home bias is safe" | Familiar is not the same as diversified | Compare your Canada weight with global market weights deliberately |
| Never trimming winners | One position quietly grows to 20% | Rebalance rules triggered by weight |
