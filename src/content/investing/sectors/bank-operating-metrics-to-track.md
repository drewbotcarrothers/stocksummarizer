Bank earnings releases run to dozens of pages of tables. You don't need all of them. A handful of operating metrics tell you whether a bank is growing profitably, controlling costs, staying ahead of credit problems and holding enough capital to keep paying you. Learn these once and every quarterly report gets faster to read.

Educational only — not financial advice. All figures are **hypothetical**. For how banks earn money in the first place, start with [banks: industry structure and how money is made](/investing/sectors/how-canadian-banks-make-money/).

## Direct answer

Track eight things each quarter: **net interest margin (NIM)**, **efficiency ratio**, **operating leverage**, **provisions for credit losses (PCL) ratio**, **gross impaired loans**, **CET1 ratio**, **return on equity (ROE)**, and **loan and deposit growth**. Together they cover the four questions that matter: Is the core engine earning? Are costs under control? Is credit deteriorating? Is there enough capital?

## The metrics at a glance

| Metric | Formula (simplified) | What it tells you | Watch for |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Net interest margin | Net interest income ÷ average earning assets | Profitability of the lending spread | Compression from deposit competition or rate cuts |
| Efficiency ratio | Non-interest expenses ÷ revenue | Cents of cost per dollar of revenue (lower is better) | A rising ratio over several quarters |
| Operating leverage | Revenue growth − expense growth | Whether revenue outgrows costs | Persistent negative readings |
| PCL ratio | PCL ÷ average loans (in basis points) | Cost of expected credit losses | Sharp jumps; rising impaired provisions |
| Gross impaired loans (GIL) | Loans classified as impaired | Actual credit stress, not just expectations | Steady upward trend |
| CET1 ratio | Common equity tier 1 ÷ risk-weighted assets | Loss-absorbing capital | Cushion over the regulatory minimum shrinking |
| ROE | Net income to common ÷ average common equity | Returns on shareholders' money | Falling ROE with rising P/B |
| Loan & deposit growth | Year-over-year change | Franchise momentum and funding | Loans growing much faster than deposits |

Banks often report "adjusted" versions that remove one-time items. Read both — see [how to read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/).

## Worked example (hypothetical bank)

| Input (hypothetical) | This year | Last year |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Revenue | $110B | $104B |
| Non-interest expenses | $62B | $60B |
| Net interest income | $60B | $57B |
| Average earning assets | $2,600B | $2,500B |
| PCL | $10B | $7B |
| Average loans | $1,000B | $950B |

| Metric | Calculation | Result |
|:--|:--|--:|
| Efficiency ratio | 62 ÷ 110 | 56.4% |
| Operating leverage | +5.8% revenue − 3.3% expense growth | +2.4 pts |
| NIM | 60 ÷ 2,600 | 2.31% |
| PCL ratio | 10 ÷ 1,000 | 100 bps |
| PCL ratio last year | 7 ÷ 950 | ~74 bps |

The bank is growing and controlling costs, but credit costs rose meaningfully. That's the line to dig into in the MD&A: are new provisions for **performing** loans (models anticipating weaker conditions) or for **impaired** loans (actual defaults)? Guide: [how to read an MD&A](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-mdna/).

Under IFRS 9, banks book provisions for **expected** losses, so PCL can rise before defaults actually appear. A jump in performing-loan provisions is a forecast; a rise in gross impaired loans is the reality catching up.

Regulatory minimums and buffers are set by OSFI and can change; check the bank's latest report for the requirement it states.

## A 15-minute quarterly bank check

1. Open the supplementary financial package or investor presentation.
2. Record NIM, efficiency ratio, PCL ratio, GIL, CET1 and ROE for this quarter and the same quarter last year.
3. Note operating leverage (revenue growth minus expense growth).
4. Read the credit section of the MD&A for performing vs impaired provisions.
5. Update your [stock brief](/investing/analyze/stock-brief-template-bull-bear-watch/) and compare with peers ([peer comparison](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/)).

## Linking metrics to valuation

ROE and capital drive bank valuation. A bank sustaining a higher ROE with a solid CET1 cushion generally earns a higher price-to-book — the method is in [valuation for banks: P/B, ROE](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/).
