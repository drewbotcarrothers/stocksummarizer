Banks and insurers get most of the attention in Canadian financials, but the TSX also lists a varied group of **diversified financials**: asset managers, alternative-investment managers, an exchange operator, financial holding companies and specialty lenders. They share a sector label, not a business model. Some earn steady fees on other people's money; others take credit risk on their own balance sheet. Sorting them correctly is the first step.

Educational only — not financial advice. This is an evergreen concept explainer; for bank and insurer specifics see the living [banks](/sectors/banks/) and [insurers](/sectors/insurers/) deep dives. All figures here are **hypothetical**.

## Direct answer

Most diversified financials make money in one of four ways: **fees on assets under management (AUM)** (traditional and alternative asset managers), **transaction, listing and data fees** (exchanges and market infrastructure), **returns on a portfolio of owned businesses** (financial holding companies), or a **lending spread minus credit losses** (specialty and consumer lenders). Fee businesses are driven by markets and net flows; lenders by credit quality and funding costs; holding companies by the value of what they own and the discount the market applies to it.

## The sub-industries

| Sub-industry | How it earns | Key metric | Main risk |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Traditional asset / wealth managers | Management fees on mutual funds, ETFs, advice | AUM, net flows, fee rate | Outflows, fee compression to low-cost ETFs |
| Alternative asset managers | Management fees + performance fees (carried interest) on private funds | Fee-bearing capital, fee-related earnings | Fundraising slowdowns, valuation marks |
| Exchanges and market data | Trading, clearing, listing and data subscriptions | Trading volumes, recurring data revenue | Low trading and new-issue activity |
| Financial holding companies | Earnings and value of owned insurers, managers, other businesses | Net asset value per share, discount to NAV | Persistent holding-company discount |
| Specialty / consumer lenders | Interest spread on loans minus credit losses | Loan growth, net charge-offs, funding cost | Recession-driven credit losses, regulation |
| Investment dealers / brokers | Commissions, underwriting, advisory fees | Deal activity, advisor assets | Cyclical capital-markets activity |

Alternative managers often highlight **fee-related earnings (FRE)** — profit from recurring management fees — separately from **performance fees** or **carried interest**, which are lumpy and depend on selling investments at a gain. Investors usually pay a higher multiple for FRE than for performance income.

## Lenders: spread minus losses

Specialty lenders earn the gap between what they charge borrowers and what they pay for funding, minus operating costs and **credit losses**. High-yield consumer loans can produce attractive returns in good times, but losses rise quickly in a downturn. Track **net charge-off rate**, **provision for credit losses**, **funding mix** and **allowance coverage** — the same logic as in [bank operating metrics](/investing/sectors/bank-operating-metrics-to-track/).

## Holding companies: sum of the parts

A holding company's value is the sum of its stakes (listed and unlisted), minus holding-company debt and costs. These shares often trade at a **discount to net asset value**. The discount can narrow if management buys back stock or simplifies the structure — or persist for decades. Valuation is a [sum-of-the-parts](/investing/valuation/triangulating-multiples-dcf-peer-checks/) exercise, not a single multiple.

## Metrics to track

| Metric | Applies to | Watch for |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Net flows as % of starting AUM | Asset managers | Persistent outflows |
| Average fee rate (bps) | Asset managers | Steady decline without volume offset |
| Fee-related earnings and margin | Alternative managers | FRE growth slowing as fundraising dries up |
| Recurring revenue share | Exchanges / data | Falling share, heavier reliance on trading volumes |
| Net charge-offs, provisions | Lenders | Rising losses ahead of guidance |
| Discount to NAV | Holding companies | Discount widening with no catalyst |

## What helps and what hurts

| Driver | Helps | Hurts |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Equity and bond markets | Rising markets lift AUM and fees | Bear markets cut AUM and performance fees |
| Interest rates | Steady rates support lending and fundraising | Rapid rate rises hit borrowers and deal activity |
| Capital-markets activity | Strong IPO and trading volumes | Quiet new-issue markets |
| Product trends | Growth in private markets, recurring data | Shift to low-fee products |
| Credit cycle | Low unemployment | Recession-driven defaults |

## Valuation basics

Fee-based managers and exchanges are usually valued on **P/E** or **EV/EBITDA**, with premiums for recurring revenue; alternative managers on multiples of **fee-related earnings**; lenders on **P/B vs ROE**; holding companies on **price vs NAV**. Using one yardstick across the whole group is the most common mistake — see [relative vs intrinsic valuation](/investing/valuation/relative-vs-intrinsic-valuation/).
