Canadian Natural Resources (TSX: CNQ) is Canada's largest oil and natural gas producer. This primer explains **how CNQ makes money** — a portfolio built around long-life, low-decline oil sands assets, plus conventional oil, natural gas and a small international business — and which numbers to watch.

Educational primer — not a recommendation to buy or sell CNQ. Figures are from Canadian Natural's **2025** year-end results; check the latest filings for current numbers.

## Direct answer

Canadian Natural sells crude oil, synthetic crude oil (SCO), bitumen, natural gas liquids and natural gas. Its profit per barrel is the realized price minus royalties, operating costs and transport, and its cash flow is unusually durable because most of its reserves are **long life, low decline**: oil sands mines and thermal projects that keep producing for decades with relatively little capital to hold volumes flat. In 2025 it produced a record average **1,571 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day (MBOE/d)**, and the company says about **73%** of its total proved reserves are long life, low decline.

## The business segments

| Segment / asset group | What it is | How it makes money | Key sensitivity |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Oil Sands Mining and Upgrading | Horizon and the Albian mines (100% owned since a November 2025 asset swap with Shell); bitumen mined and upgraded to SCO | Sells SCO at prices close to light crude benchmarks | WTI price, upgrader reliability, turnarounds |
| Thermal in situ | Steam-assisted bitumen projects | Sells blended bitumen at heavy-oil prices | WTI and the WCS heavy-oil differential, natural gas cost for steam |
| Conventional E&P — North America | Primary heavy crude, Pelican Lake, light crude, NGLs and natural gas | Sells oil, liquids and gas at market prices | Oil and AECO gas prices, drilling results |
| International E&P | North Sea and Offshore Africa | Sells light crude at Brent-linked prices | Brent price, decommissioning costs |
| Midstream and Refining | Pipeline interests and a 50% interest in the North West Redwater refinery | Fees and refining margins | Throughput and refining spreads |

Operating cost is only part of the picture — royalties rise with oil prices, and the Alberta oil sands royalty regime steps up once a project has paid out. But low and stable operating costs are what let long-life assets generate cash through low-price periods.

## How the cash reaches shareholders

| Lever | What to look for | Where |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Dividend | 26 consecutive years of increases through the 2026 raise | Year-end news release |
| Buybacks | Share repurchases under a normal course issuer bid | Quarterly reports |
| Free cash flow allocation policy | A published policy linking the share of free cash flow returned to shareholders to the net debt level | Year-end release and MD&A |
| Net debt | Debt minus cash; determines the allocation tier | MD&A |
| Capital budget | Sustaining vs growth capital and production guidance | Annual budget release (usually December) |

## What moves the stock

| Driver | Direction | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| WTI oil price | Up = good | Most production is oil and SCO priced off WTI |
| WCS heavy-oil differential | Narrower = good | Thermal and heavy crude are priced at a discount to WTI; pipeline capacity matters |
| AECO natural gas price | Mixed | Revenue on gas sales, but gas is also a fuel cost for oil sands |
| CAD/USD | Weaker CAD = good | Oil is priced in US dollars, costs are mostly in Canadian dollars |
| Turnarounds and outages | Unplanned = bad | Large single assets mean outages move quarterly volumes |
| Carbon and emissions policy | Uncertain | Affects long-term costs and growth decisions |
