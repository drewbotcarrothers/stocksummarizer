Enbridge (TSX: ENB) is one of North America's largest energy infrastructure companies — and, for many Canadian dividend investors, a core holding. This primer explains **how Enbridge makes money**: four segments, mostly fee-based cash flows, and the handful of numbers that tell you whether the dividend story is intact.

Educational primer — not a recommendation to buy or sell Enbridge. Figures are from Enbridge's **2025** year-end results; check the latest filings for current numbers.

## Direct answer

Enbridge earns tolls and regulated rates for moving and delivering energy, not from the price of oil or gas itself. It reports four segments: **Liquids Pipelines** (crude oil and liquids pipelines and terminals in Canada and the US, including the Mainline system), **Gas Transmission** (natural gas pipelines, gathering, processing and storage), **Gas Distribution and Storage** (regulated gas utilities in Ontario, Québec, Ohio, North Carolina, Utah, Wyoming and Idaho) and **Renewable Power Generation** (wind, solar and geothermal interests). In 2025 Enbridge reported adjusted EBITDA of C$19.95 billion, about half from Liquids Pipelines.

## The four segments

| Segment | What it owns | How it gets paid | Main risks |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Liquids Pipelines | Mainline, regional oil sands, Gulf Coast and Mid-Continent systems, terminals | Negotiated and regulated tolls on volumes shipped; some contracted capacity | Western Canadian supply, competing pipelines, toll renegotiations, permitting and legal challenges |
| Gas Transmission | Interstate and Canadian gas pipelines, storage, gathering and processing | Mostly long-term contracted capacity and regulated rates | Rate cases, recontracting, project costs |
| Gas Distribution and Storage | Enbridge Gas Ontario plus US utilities (Ohio, North Carolina, Utah, Wyoming, Idaho) | Regulated cost-of-service rates set by provincial and state regulators | Rate decisions, weather, energy-transition policy |
| Renewable Power Generation | Onshore wind and solar in North America; offshore wind interests in Europe | Long-term power purchase agreements and contracted revenue | Wind resource, construction costs, tax credit rules |

| Metric | What it tells you | Where to find it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Adjusted EBITDA by segment | Operating earnings power and mix | Quarterly news release, MD&A |
| Distributable cash flow (DCF) per share | Cash available for dividends and reinvestment (non-GAAP) | News release; Enbridge's dividend policy is framed as a share of DCF |
| Debt-to-EBITDA | Balance-sheet capacity; Enbridge targets 4.5–5.0× | Investor presentations, MD&A |
| Secured capital program | Growth projects already sanctioned and their in-service dates | Investor day and quarterly updates |
| Mainline throughput | Volume on the core liquids system | Quarterly operating data |

## Why investors treat it like a utility (and where that breaks)

| Utility-like | Not utility-like |
|:--|:--|
| Most EBITDA comes from contracts and regulated rates | Large debt load makes it sensitive to interest rates |
| Long-lived assets with high barriers to entry | Big projects carry cost-overrun and permitting risk |
| Dividend set as a share of predictable cash flow | Some US and Canadian pipelines face legal and political challenges |
| Gas utilities earn allowed returns on rate base | Liquids volumes depend on Western Canadian production and egress competition |
