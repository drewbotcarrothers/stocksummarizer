Shopify (TSX: SHOP) is Canada's largest technology company by market value and builds the software that millions of merchants use to sell online and in person. This primer explains **how Shopify makes money** — two revenue lines, why gross merchandise volume (GMV) matters more than subscriptions, and the metrics to watch.

Educational primer — not a recommendation to buy or sell Shopify. Shopify reports in **US dollars**. Figures are from its **2025** full-year results; check the latest filings for current numbers.

## Direct answer

Shopify earns revenue two ways. **Subscription Solutions** is the monthly fee merchants pay for a Shopify plan (from Basic up to Shopify Plus for large brands), plus sales of themes, apps and domains. **Merchant Solutions** is revenue tied to what merchants sell — mainly payment-processing fees through Shopify Payments, plus currency conversion, Shop Pay Installments, Shopify Capital lending, shipping labels and other services. In 2025 Shopify reported revenue of **US$11.56 billion** (up 30%), of which about 76% came from Merchant Solutions, on **US$378.4 billion of GMV** (up 29%).

## The two revenue lines

| | Subscription Solutions | Merchant Solutions |
|:--|:--|:--|
| What it is | Plan fees, themes, apps, domains | Payments, Shop Pay Installments, Capital, shipping, Markets and other services |
| What drives it | Number of merchants × plan price; pricing changes | GMV × share processed by Shopify Payments × fee rate |
| 2025 revenue | US$2.75B | US$8.80B |
| Margin profile | Higher gross margin (software) | Lower gross margin (card-network and processing costs are passed through) |
| Main sensitivity | Merchant churn, plan mix | Consumer spending, payments penetration, competition on processing fees |

| Metric | Formula / definition | Why watch it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| GMV | Total value of orders processed on the platform | Revenue engine for Merchant Solutions |
| Gross Payments Volume (GPV) and penetration | GMV processed through Shopify Payments ÷ total GMV | Higher penetration = more fee revenue per dollar of GMV |
| Take rate | Revenue ÷ GMV (about 3.0% in 2024 and 2025) | Whether Shopify is capturing more of each sale |
| Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) | Subscription revenue run-rate | Merchant base and pricing power |
| Free cash flow margin | FCF ÷ revenue | Converting growth into cash — Shopify discloses it each quarter |
| Operating expenses as % of revenue | Sales, marketing, R&D, G&A | Discipline vs reinvestment |

## Reading Shopify like a TSX investor

- **Currency:** results are in US dollars while the TSX shares trade in Canadian dollars, so the CAD/USD rate affects your return. See [comparing a TSX stock to its US peer fairly](/investing/valuation/compare-tsx-stock-to-us-peer/).
- **Valuation:** classic P/E and dividend screens don't fit a high-growth platform; growth-adjusted multiples and free cash flow are more common — see [when P/E misleads on TSX stocks](/investing/valuation/when-pe-misleads-tsx/).
- **Stock-based compensation:** check how much adjusted metrics exclude — see [how to read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/) and [what is dilution?](/investing/glossary/what-is-dilution/).
- **Concentration:** Shopify can be a large weight in Canadian tech and in the S&P/TSX Composite — see [concentration risk when Canada is already concentrated](/investing/process/concentration-risk-canadian-portfolios/).

## Bull / bear / watch (evergreen framing)

| Bull case themes | Bear case themes | Watch |
|:--|:--|:--|
| GMV growth well above e-commerce overall | Consumer spending slowdown hits GMV | GMV growth vs prior quarters |
| Rising payments penetration and new services | Competition from Amazon, other platforms and payment processors | GPV penetration and take rate |
| Expansion into enterprise, offline and international | Valuation sensitive to any growth slowdown | Free cash flow margin |
| Software-like subscription base | Lending (Capital) adds credit risk | Loss provisions on merchant cash advances and loans |
