Canada has exactly two railways big enough to be "Class I": **Canadian National (TSX: CNR)** and **Canadian Pacific Kansas City (TSX: CP)**. Both are among the most widely held industrial stocks on the TSX. This primer is company- and regulation-specific: how the two networks differ, which rules cap or shape their revenue, and the labour and regulatory history that occasionally jolts both stocks at once.

For the generic railroad business model — operating ratio, pricing power, carload mix — read [Industrials – Rails: how money is made](/investing/sectors/how-railroads-make-money/) first. Educational primer — not a recommendation.

## Direct answer

"Class I" is a US Surface Transportation Board (STB) category for freight railroads with annual operating revenue above an inflation-indexed threshold — about **US$1.09 billion for 2025**. CN and CPKC are the only Canadian-based Class I railroads, and both run large US networks under STB oversight. In Canada they're federally regulated under the **Canada Transportation Act**, which includes a revenue cap on western grain (the **maximum revenue entitlement**, MRE), shipper remedies such as long-haul interswitching, and federal labour law under the Canada Labour Code. CN's network reaches three coasts (Atlantic, Pacific and the US Gulf); CPKC, created when Canadian Pacific combined with Kansas City Southern in April 2023, is the only single-line network linking Canada, the US and Mexico.

## CN vs CPKC: the networks

| | CN (CNR) | CPKC (CP) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Footprint | Canada coast to coast, plus a north–south US line to the Gulf of Mexico | Canada coast to coast, US Midwest, and Kansas City Southern's lines to the Gulf and into Mexico |
| Signature advantage | Access to Prince Rupert and Halifax as well as Vancouver; the "three coasts" network | Single-line service Canada–US–Mexico since the 2023 combination |
| Key ports | Vancouver, Prince Rupert, Montreal, Halifax, US Gulf | Vancouver, Montreal, US Gulf, Lázaro Cárdenas (Mexico) |
| Main growth story | Intermodal via West Coast ports, bulk exports | Cross-border synergies and new Mexico–US–Canada service |
| Main extra risk | Canadian bulk volumes, port and weather disruptions | Integration, Mexico concession and cross-border trade policy |

Both carry similar Canadian freight — grain, potash, coal, energy products, forest products, intermodal containers and autos — so policy shocks in Canada tend to hit both. Recent dated briefs: [CN downgrade](/summaries/2026-10-20-cnr-cn-rail-downgrade/) and [CPKC Q3 fuel squeeze](/summaries/2026-10-29-cp-q3-fuel-squeeze/).

## Regulation that shapes revenue

| Rule | What it does | Why investors care |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Maximum revenue entitlement (MRE) | Caps each railway's total revenue from regulated western grain per crop year; the Canadian Transportation Agency sets a cost index (VRCPI) each spring and determines the final MRE | Limits pricing on a large Prairie commodity; exceeding the cap means repaying the excess plus a penalty |
| Long-haul interswitching | Lets captive shippers ask the Agency to set a rate to move traffic to a competing railway's interchange | Caps pricing power at captive locations |
| Level-of-service complaints and final offer arbitration | Shippers can ask the Agency to rule on service or rates | Periodic rulings and political pressure on service |
| Canada Labour Code | Federal labour law for interprovincial railways; the Minister can refer disputes under s.107 | Strikes or lockouts can halt both networks at once |
| STB (US) | US rate and merger oversight | Merger approvals and US service rules |

## Labour: a shared event risk

In **August 2024**, CN and CPKC both locked out Teamsters Canada Rail Conference members on August 22, halting most freight rail in Canada. The federal Labour Minister used section 107 of the Canada Labour Code to direct the Canada Industrial Relations Board to order a return to work and binding arbitration, and operations resumed within days. The lesson for investors: because the two railways negotiate with the same union on similar timelines, labour risk is **correlated** — owning both doesn't diversify it away.

## What to watch

| Metric | Why |
|:--|:--|
| Operating ratio (operating expenses ÷ revenue) | Core efficiency measure; lower is better |
| Revenue ton-miles and carloads by commodity | Volume trend by business line |
| Fuel surcharge revenue vs fuel costs | Lags can squeeze margins when fuel moves fast |
| Grain volumes vs MRE | Regulated revenue headroom |
| Labour contract expiry dates | Event-risk calendar |
| Capital spending and free cash flow | Track and equipment are heavy, recurring costs — see [what is owner earnings?](/investing/glossary/what-is-owner-earnings/) |
