Royal Bank of Canada (TSX: RY) is Canada's largest bank by market capitalization and one of the most widely held stocks on the TSX. This primer explains **how RBC makes money** — its five business segments, what drives each, and which numbers to watch — so you can read any dated news about the stock in context.

Educational primer — not a recommendation to buy or sell RBC. Segment figures are from RBC's **fiscal 2025** annual report (year ended October 31, 2025); check the latest annual and quarterly reports for current numbers.

## Direct answer

RBC earns money in five segments: **Personal Banking** (Canadian mortgages, deposits, cards and personal lending, plus Caribbean and US cross-border retail), **Commercial Banking** (business loans and deposits), **Wealth Management** (Canadian and US advice and asset management, including City National Bank in the US), **Insurance** (life, health and other insurance products) and **Capital Markets** (corporate and investment banking plus trading). In fiscal 2025 RBC reported total revenue of C$66.6 billion and net income of C$20.4 billion. The two banking segments together produced about half of segment profit; Capital Markets and Wealth Management produced most of the rest.

## The five segments at a glance

| Segment | How it makes money | Main drivers | FY2025 net income |
|:--|:--|:--|--:|
| Personal Banking | Spread on mortgages, cards and loans vs deposit costs; card and account fees | Mortgage and deposit growth, net interest margin, credit losses on cards and unsecured loans | C$7,105M |
| Commercial Banking | Spread on business loans and deposits; fees | Business loan growth, deposit mix, commercial credit quality | C$3,020M |
| Wealth Management | Fee-based asset charges, advisor commissions, net interest income at City National | Market levels (AUA/AUM), net new assets, advisor payouts, US banking spreads | C$4,289M |
| Insurance | Insurance service results and investment income | Claims experience, new business, interest rates | C$828M |
| Capital Markets | Advisory and underwriting fees, lending, trading revenue | Deal activity, market volatility, corporate lending, risk appetite | C$5,393M |

## Why the mix matters for the stock

- **Diversification.** When rate cuts squeeze lending spreads, strong markets can lift Wealth Management fees and Capital Markets trading — and vice versa. That diversification is a key reason investors pay attention to the segment mix, not just the headline EPS.
- **Capital Markets is the swing factor.** Trading and deal revenue is volatile. A record trading quarter can't be extrapolated.
- **Wealth Management is revenue-heavy, margin-light.** Advisor compensation absorbs a large share of revenue, so its profit share is much smaller than its revenue share.
- **HSBC Canada.** RBC completed its acquisition of HSBC Bank Canada in March 2024, adding clients, loans and deposits mainly in the banking segments. When comparing years, check how much growth came from the acquisition versus organic.

## Numbers to watch each quarter

| Metric | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Net interest margin (all-bank and Canadian banking) | Quarterly report | Spread income on the largest segments — see [what is NIM?](/investing/glossary/what-is-net-interest-margin/) |
| Provision for credit losses (PCL) ratio | Quarterly report, credit section | Mortgage, card and commercial credit stress |
| CET1 ratio | Capital management section | Room for buybacks, dividends and growth versus OSFI's expectation |
| ROE (reported and adjusted) | Highlights table | Drives the P/B multiple — see [valuing banks with P/B and ROE](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/) |
| Efficiency ratio / operating leverage | Highlights and segment pages | Whether revenue grows faster than costs |
| Wealth AUA/AUM | Wealth Management segment | Fee base for the next quarter |
| Capital Markets revenue split | Capital Markets segment | How much is trading vs fee-based |

RBC's fiscal year ends **October 31**, so full-year results arrive in late November or early December, with the annual report and AIF on SEDAR+.
