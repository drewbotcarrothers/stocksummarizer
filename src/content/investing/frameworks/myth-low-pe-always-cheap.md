A low price-to-earnings ratio is the most common reason people call a stock "cheap." Sometimes it is. Often the market is pricing in something the trailing number hasn't caught yet.

Educational myth-buster — not financial advice. All numbers below are **hypothetical**. For the full list of situations where P/E breaks down, read [when P/E misleads on TSX stocks](/investing/valuation/when-pe-misleads-tsx/); this post is the short version.

## The myth

> "This stock trades at 6x earnings while the market is at 18x. It's cheap."

## The reality in one line

P/E is **price ÷ earnings per share**. A low ratio means either the price is low relative to *durable* earnings (cheap) — or the earnings in the denominator are temporarily high, low quality, or about to fall (a value trap). The ratio alone can't tell you which.

## Five reasons a low P/E isn't cheap

| Reason | What's going on | What to check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Peak-cycle earnings** | Commodity, steel, chemicals or homebuilders earning record margins | Earnings vs a 5–10 year average; see [how oil & gas producers make money](/investing/sectors/how-oil-gas-producers-make-money/) |
| **One-time gains** | Asset sale or tax recovery inflates EPS | Reported vs adjusted EPS; [read non-GAAP metrics critically](/investing/analyze/read-non-gaap-metrics-critically/) |
| **Heavy debt** | Equity is a thin slice of the enterprise | Compare on [EV/EBITDA](/investing/valuation/ev-ebitda-for-canadian-diy-investors/), not just P/E |
| **Shrinking business** | Earnings falling every year; the multiple prices the decline | Revenue and EPS trend over 5 years |
| **Dilution or claims ahead** | Convertibles, warrants or an equity raise coming | Diluted share count; [what is dilution?](/investing/glossary/what-is-dilution/) |

## When a low P/E *can* mean cheap

| Signal | Why it helps |
|:--|:--|
| Earnings are mid-cycle or below, not at a peak | The denominator isn't flattering |
| Free cash flow roughly matches earnings | Earnings are real cash — check [P/FCF](/investing/valuation/what-p-fcf-means-canadian-diy/) |
| Low debt and stable share count | No hidden claims ahead of you |
| A specific, temporary reason for pessimism | The market may be over-extrapolating one bad patch |
| Peers with similar economics trade higher | Relative gap, not just a low absolute number — see [compare two TSX peers](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/) |

**Rule of thumb:** before you call a stock cheap, finish the sentence "*the P/E is low because…*". If you can't, the market probably knows something you haven't read yet.
