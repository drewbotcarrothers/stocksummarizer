The TFSA is many Canadians' favourite account, so it becomes the default home for everything — including US dividend stocks. That default has a small, permanent, and easy-to-avoid cost.

Educational only — not tax advice. Numbers are **hypothetical**; confirm your own situation with a tax professional. The rules are covered in depth in [account location for US dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-us-dividend-stocks/) and [withholding tax on US dividends: TFSA vs RRSP vs taxable](/investing/dividends/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-rrsp-taxable/). This post is the short symptom → cost → fix version.

## Symptom

You hold US-listed dividend payers (or a US-listed dividend ETF) in your TFSA, and your statement shows "US non-resident tax" deducted from every dividend.

## Cost

The Canada–US tax treaty sets a 15% withholding rate on dividends for Canadian residents. The treaty generally **exempts** trusts operated for retirement income — which covers RRSPs and RRIFs — but **not TFSAs**. Inside a TFSA, nothing is taxable in Canada, so there's no Canadian tax to offset with a foreign tax credit. The 15% is simply gone.

## Fix

| Step | Action |
|:--|:--|
| 1 | List US-listed holdings with meaningful yields across all accounts |
| 2 | Where you have RRSP room, put the **highest-yield US payers there first** |
| 3 | Use the TFSA for Canadian dividend payers and low- or no-yield US growth stocks, where withholding barely matters — see [account location for Canadian dividend stocks](/investing/accounts/account-location-canadian-dividend-stocks/) |
| 4 | Remember that Canadian-listed ETFs holding US stocks add their own layer — see [US withholding inside Canadian- vs US-listed ETFs](/investing/indexes-etfs/us-withholding-tax-canadian-vs-us-listed-etfs/) |
| 5 | Don't sell at a loss and rebuy in another account without checking the [superficial loss rule](/investing/accounts/superficial-loss-rule-stock-swaps/) |

**Keep it in proportion.** On a 1–2% yield the drag is small. Don't let a tax detail override diversification, your [core-satellite plan](/investing/accounts/core-satellite-canadian-accounts/), or the simple fact that tax-free growth in a TFSA can still beat holding nothing.
