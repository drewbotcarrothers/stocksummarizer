Every StockSummarizer [stock summary](/summaries/) is written about a specific moment — an earnings print, a dividend raise, a downgrade. That makes briefs go stale as *news*. But used the right way, they're excellent **learning objects**: worked examples of how to read a company, with the sources attached.

Educational only — not financial advice, and briefs are not recommendations. This guide is about **brief anatomy**: which parts are dated, which are durable, and which evergreen "parent" posts each part plugs into. For a full study schedule, see the [30-day literacy plan](/investing/pathways/30-day-literacy-plan-before-individual-stocks/) and the [Start Here pathway](/investing/pathways/canadian-diy-stock-investing-start-here/).

## Direct answer

Read the evergreen parent (company primer or sector explainer) first, then the brief. In the brief, separate the **dated catalyst** (what just happened, today's numbers) from the **durable lessons** (how the business makes money, which metrics matter, what the bull and bear debate is about). Rewrite the bull / bear / watch in your own words, check one claim against a filing, and revisit after the next quarterly report to see what changed.

| Brief section | Dated or durable? | What it teaches | Evergreen parent |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| The short version / What happened | **Dated** | How to summarize a catalyst in two sentences | [How to read quarterly earnings](/investing/analyze/how-to-read-quarterly-earnings-tsx/) |
| Business overview vs peers | Mostly durable | Who the real peers are and why | Company primers, e.g. [Royal Bank](/investing/primers/how-royal-bank-rbc-makes-money/), [Enbridge](/investing/primers/how-enbridge-makes-money/) |
| Performance table | **Dated** | How returns compare across a peer set | [Compare two TSX peers](/investing/analyze/compare-two-tsx-peers/) |
| Valuation table | **Dated numbers**, durable method | Which multiples suit which sector | [When P/E misleads](/investing/valuation/when-pe-misleads-tsx/), [bank valuation](/investing/valuation/valuing-canadian-banks-pb-roe/) |
| Dividends table | **Dated numbers**, durable method | Yield vs coverage vs growth | [FCF coverage of dividends](/investing/dividends/fcf-coverage-of-dividends/) |
| Analyst view | **Dated** | Consensus is an input, not an answer | [Evaluate stock score apps critically](/investing/brokers-tools/evaluate-stock-score-apps-critically/) |
| Bull / bear / what to watch | Judgment (dated) | How to frame both sides of a debate | [Bull / bear / watch decision card](/investing/frameworks/bull-bear-watch-decision-card/) |
| Data notes / Sources | Durable habit | Where every number came from | [Source hierarchy card](/investing/frameworks/source-hierarchy-filings-transcripts-commentary/) |

| Step | Output you keep |
|:--|:--|
| Parent first | Two sentences: how the company makes money |
| Mark dated vs durable | A highlighted copy, or a two-column note |
| Rebuild bull / bear / watch | Your own [decision card](/investing/frameworks/bull-bear-watch-decision-card/), without peeking |
| Check one claim | One number traced to the MD&A or results release on [SEDAR+](/investing/analyze/use-sedar-plus-for-stock-research/) |
| Revisit | What the next quarter confirmed or broke |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|:--|:--|
| Treating a brief as a buy or sell signal | It's a worked example dated to one moment |
| Copying the bull case | Write your own before reading ours |
| Trusting old table numbers | Check the date; re-pull current data from filings |
| Reading ten briefs shallowly | Read two in the same sector deeply |
