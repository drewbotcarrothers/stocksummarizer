Canadian DIY stock investing starts with **process**, not a ticker tip. If you already have a TFSA or RRSP and own broad ETFs, this pathway shows how to graduate into individual TSX names without abandoning diversification or inventing a day-trading habit.

This guide is educational literacy for Canadian brokerage accounts — **not financial advice**. It sequences free StockSummarizer hubs so you build skills in order: accounts and risk framing → filings literacy → valuation without fake precision → dividends and location → then sector and company primers.

## Who this pathway is for

- ETF-first investors who want **one or a few** stock satellites, not forty half-understood names.
- Beginners who can log into a discount broker but freeze on SEDAR+, MD&A language, or P/E screens.
- Intermediate DIYers who already pick stocks but want a **repeatable** brief template (bull / bear / watch) instead of headline chasing.

If you have zero emergency cash, unresolved high-interest debt, or a horizon under three years for money you might need, pause stock picking and fix that first. StockSummarizer does not cover budgeting or debt products — stay on-pillar with stocks and sectors once the basics of investable capital are honest.

## The five checkpoints (in order)

### 1. Map accounts before tickers

Know which dollars live in **TFSA**, **RRSP**, **FHSA**, and **taxable**. Location changes after-tax outcomes for US dividends, eligible Canadian dividends, and capital gains. Read the accounts hubs next, especially TFSA-for-stock-investors and US withholding comparisons — then decide *where* a future buy would sit before you fall in love with a story.

### 2. Keep a core; add satellites on purpose

Canada’s index is already concentrated in financials and energy. A sensible default for many DIY investors is an **index core** (one-ticket or Canadian equity ETF) plus a small satellite sleeve for individual names you can research. The core-satellite pathway and indexes literacy posts explain the building blocks without ranking products.

### 3. Learn filings before screeners

SEDAR+ (issuer filings), the MD&A, and the Annual Information Form beat any score app. Practice on one liquid name — a Big Six bank or a large pipeline — using our analyze hubs: research-from-scratch, SEDAR+ step-by-step, and reading quarterly earnings. Write a one-page brief with bull / bear / watch before you buy anything.

### 4. Valuation as a range, not a target

P/E, EV/EBITDA, and P/FCF are tools, not oracles. Learn relative vs intrinsic framing, when bank P/B matters more than P/E, and how to triangulate without spreadsheet theatre. Soft CTA: these hubs will eventually feed a paid **Valuation Without Fake Precision** ebook; the free layer stays complete.

### 5. Income and risk rules before size

If you care about dividends, learn coverage and traps before yield screens. If you care about growth, write **kill criteria** and position-size rules first. Process hubs on position sizing and documenting sell criteria belong here — not after a 40% drawdown forces the lesson.

## Suggested 30-day literacy sprint

| Week | Focus | Outputs |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Accounts + core-satellite framing | One-page account map; decide satellite budget % |
| 2 | Filings on one liquid TSX name | SEDAR+ folder; MD&A notes; earnings one-pager |
| 3 | Valuation + peer table | Peer table of 3–5 comps; valuation checklist |
| 4 | Brief + risk rules | Bull/bear/watch card; position-size and sell rules |
