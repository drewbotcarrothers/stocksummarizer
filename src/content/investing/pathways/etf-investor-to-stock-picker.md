Most Canadian DIY investors are told — correctly — to start with **broad equity ETFs**. The minority who still want individual stocks need a transition plan: keep the core, shrink the ego, and add only names you can research like a part-time analyst.

This playbook is for ETF holders who are curious about stock picking, not for people who want to replace XEQT with forty tips from a forum. Educational only — not advice.

## What you keep from the ETF habit

- **Automatic diversification** across hundreds of names (even if Canada is bank- and energy-heavy).
- **Low decision fatigue** — rebalancing rules beat impulse.
- **Honest benchmarking** — your stock sleeve should beat *or clearly serve a purpose versus* the ETF you already own; otherwise the ETF was enough.

## What changes when you add stocks

Individual names introduce **idiosyncratic risk**, earnings-gap risk, and the temptation to overtrade in commission-free apps. You also inherit homework: filings, peer tables, and thesis updates. If you will not schedule that work, stay ETF-only.

## A clean transition structure

1. **Freeze the core.** Leave your one-ticket or Canadian equity ETF alone while you learn. Do not sell the core to fund experiments in week one.
2. **Define a satellite budget.** Example framing (illustrative, not advice): 5–15% of equity in individual names, funded by new contributions — not by raiding the core.
3. **Pick a sector you will actually study.** Banks, pipelines, utilities, or a liquid producer you already understand from the news. Use our [sector snapshots](/sectors/) as living context.
4. **One name, one brief.** Write bull / bear / watch before the order ticket. Link to the research-from-scratch and brief-template analyze hubs.
5. **Review cadence.** Quarterly thesis check tied to earnings; annual “should this still be a stock or an ETF?” offsite.

## Skills you must earn before size increases

| Skill | Proof |
|:--|:--|
| Filings | You can find the latest MD&A and AIF on SEDAR+ without a tutorial open |
| Peers | You can build a 4–6 name peer table with shared metrics |
| Valuation | You can explain why a multiple is high/low *for that sector* |
| Location | You know whether the next buy belongs in TFSA, RRSP, or taxable |
| Exit | Kill criteria written *before* entry |

## Common transition mistakes

- **Yield chasing** as the first stock idea — read dividend trap and aristocrats hubs first.
- **US dividend payers in TFSA by default** — withholding tax literacy matters.
- **Copying a concentrated finfluencer book** — their risk capacity is not yours.
- **Turning the TFSA into a trading account** — process and CRA narrative risk are real educational topics in our process hubs.
