## The short version: a Buy rating on a stock that now follows Emera

Canadian Utilities (TSX: CU), the ATCO-controlled Alberta electricity and natural gas utility, closed at **CA$49.85** on October 7, 2026, after rising about **29%** over twelve months but slipping about **1%** in the past month. That same day, **TD Securities upgraded the stock from Hold to Buy** and lifted its target from C$52 to **C$57**, about 14% above the price. The twist: one day earlier, Canadian Utilities agreed to be bought by Emera in an all-share deal, so the new story is **a ticket into Emera**, not a standalone Alberta utility.

The one number that matters is the exchange ratio: each Class A share becomes **0.755 of an Emera share**, worth about **C$49.35** at Emera's C$65.36 screener price. Canadian Utilities trades right on top of that value, so a buyer today is mostly buying Emera stock through a side door, with a bigger dividend attached. Below we go through the deal math, the dividend bump, and how Canadian Utilities compares with Emera, Fortis, Hydro One, AltaGas and ATCO on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the Emera deal and TD's upgrade

On **October 6, 2026**, Emera, ATCO and Canadian Utilities announced a definitive agreement for Emera to acquire all of Canadian Utilities, valued at about **C$14.3B** and paid entirely in Emera shares. The terms that matter for Canadian Utilities holders:

- **Class A non-voting shares** (other than ATCO's) convert into **0.755 of an Emera share** each; Class B shares get **0.819**.
- At Emera's screener price of **C$65.36**, 0.755 shares are worth about **C$49.35**. Canadian Utilities closed at **C$49.85** on October 7, so there is **almost no premium left on the table** (the companies' implied premium at announcement was under 1%).
- **ATCO**, which controls Canadian Utilities, supports the deal. Its own holders get Emera shares plus shares of a spin-off ("New ATCO") holding housing, defence, ports and retail energy.
- The deal needs shareholder votes, an Alberta court and a long list of regulators (Alberta Utilities Commission, FERC, CFIUS and others). **Closing is targeted for the second half of 2027.**

The market was lukewarm. By the time the Globe and Mail took stock, Canadian Utilities was down about 2.6% since the announcement and Emera about 4.3%, while ATCO had jumped. Fitch also flagged the deal as possibly a slight negative for Canadian Utilities, which carries the stronger credit rating of the two.

### The TD upgrade behind the headline

On **October 7**, TD Securities analyst John Mould upgraded Canadian Utilities from Hold to **Buy** (MarketBeat lists it as Strong-Buy) and raised the target from **C$52 to C$57**. Mould was not uncritical: he noted that the combined company has less relative exposure to Florida, a high-quality utility jurisdiction, and that Alberta authorizes lower utility returns. Read together, the upgrade is a view that the combined Emera is worth more than today's price, rather than a call on Canadian Utilities' own results. Its last standalone print was second-quarter adjusted earnings of **C$0.51 per share** on **C$914M** of revenue (July 29, 2026).

## Business overview: Canadian Utilities vs Canadian utility peers

Canadian Utilities owns regulated electricity transmission and distribution and natural gas distribution and transmission, mostly in Alberta, plus smaller energy infrastructure, retail energy and international operations, including a gas utility in Australia. The regulated businesses earn an allowed return on a growing rate base, which is why the company built one of the longest dividend-growth records in Canada, more than fifty consecutive years of increases. The trade-off is Alberta's allowed returns, which sit below those of some US jurisdictions.

On the TSX screener it sits in the **Utilities** sector. Emera (EMA) is the acquirer and the stock Canadian Utilities now tracks. Fortis (FTS) and Hydro One (H) are the two largest Canadian regulated utilities. AltaGas (ALA) mixes regulated gas utilities with midstream. ATCO (ACO.X) is the parent and a deal party. Capital Power was left out because it is a power producer rather than a regulated utility, and to keep the table at six rows.

## Why it matters: from steady Alberta utility to a ticket into Emera

The old narrative was **a steady Alberta utility with more than fifty years of dividend increases**, valued on its own rate base and allowed returns. The new narrative is **a ticket into Emera**: until the deal closes or breaks, the stock should move almost one for one with 0.755 Emera shares, not on its own quarterly results.

That reframes the TD upgrade. It is really a vote on the combined company and on Emera's share price, so anyone weighing Canadian Utilities now has to weigh Emera's Florida-plus-Alberta growth plan, its share issuance and its payout as well.

## Bull case

1. **More income.** Holders get about **20% more dividend income** (C$2.20 vs C$1.83 a share) once the deal closes.
2. **A bigger platform.** They swap into a larger, more diversified utility, with Florida next to Alberta, about 95% regulated earnings and a **C$32B** capital plan through 2030.
3. **Emera upside.** If Emera recovers toward its C$76.67 consensus target, Canadian Utilities rides along, which is the core of TD's case.

## Bear case and risks

1. **No deal pop.** There is almost no takeover premium, so the deal itself adds little to the price.
2. **A long road.** Approvals run more than a year, through Alberta and US regulators, a court and shareholder votes. A break would leave the stock on its own again after a 29% run.
3. **Credit and payout.** Fitch flagged the deal as a possible slight negative for Canadian Utilities' stronger credit rating, and Emera's payout is already above 90%.

## What to watch

1. **The spread.** Track Canadian Utilities' price against 0.755 Emera shares; a widening gap would signal doubts about the deal.
2. **More analyst revisions.** Watch whether other brokers follow TD or reset targets to the exchange ratio.
3. **The joint circular and shareholder votes.** Meetings are expected in **early 2027**.
4. **Third-quarter results.** Both companies report this fall; Emera's update on funding the larger plan matters most.

## Bottom line

Canadian Utilities has earned its reputation as one of Canada's steadiest dividend payers, and TD's upgrade adds a C$57 target to a stock that already rose about 29% in a year. But since October 6 its price has been anchored to 0.755 Emera shares, and it trades right at that value with almost no premium. On forward earnings it is in line with Emera, while it looks expensive on sales and book. The real questions are whether Emera's shares recover and whether the deal clears more than a year of approvals.
