## The short version: a downgrade for a stock that still looks cheap

Barrick Mining (TSX: ABX) was **CA$55.84** in our October 8 table and closed Friday, **October 16**, at **C$57.74** in Toronto. Over the twelve months to October 8 the shares were up about **18%**, ahead of most of the big TSX gold names. That run is why **CIBC downgraded the stock to Neutral** on October 15: in its view Barrick has already outperformed its peers, and the cash from its planned IPO is still months away.

The numbers in our peer tables tell a slightly different story. At about **10x trailing earnings**, Barrick is still one of the cheapest gold majors on the TSX, with the **lowest price-to-sales and price-to-book** in its group and a dividend yield more than twice the group average. The swing factor is the planned **initial public offering of its North American gold business**, targeted for year-end. Below we go through the downgrade, the second-quarter print behind the rally, the Newmont settlement, and how Barrick compares with Agnico Eagle, Wheaton Precious Metals, Franco-Nevada, Kinross and Alamos Gold on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: CIBC steps back after a year of outperformance

On **October 15, 2026**, CIBC analyst **Anita Soni** cut Barrick to **Neutral from Outperformer** and lowered her target on the New York listing to **US$49 from US$57**. Her argument was about price, not quality. After a year of beating its peers, she said, Barrick's valuation looks less attractive. She also noted that the IPO will not put proceeds in shareholders' hands for a few months, and that Barrick still has to show the listing lifts the value of the whole company.

CIBC trimmed its gold forecasts at the same time, to **US$4,500 an ounce for the fourth quarter of 2026**, US$5,200 for 2027 and US$5,500 for 2028. Spot gold was near US$4,190 on October 16, well below the levels earlier in the year (the first-quarter average was close to US$4,900). Other brokers have not followed CIBC yet. National Bank's target is C$70 and Canaccord's is C$73.

### The Q2 print behind the rally

The last full report came on **August 10, 2026**, and it was a clean beat:

- **Gold production** rose **11%** from the first quarter to **796,000 ounces**, above the 730,000–770,000 ounce guidance range.
- **Revenue** jumped **44%** from a year earlier to **US$5.3 billion**. Earnings per share were **US$0.73** (+55%) and **adjusted EPS** was **US$0.82**, up **74%**.
- **Operating cash flow** was **US$1.70 billion**, up 28%. **Attributable free cash flow**, though, was only **US$141 million**, held back by heavy spending.
- Barrick **returned US$1.5 billion** in the quarter, mostly through **US$1.2 billion of buybacks**, and still ended with **net cash of about US$1.25 billion**.
- **Capital spending guidance** was cut to **US$3.8–4.2 billion**.

The quarter also closed a long-running fight. Barrick **settled its disputes with Newmont**, its partner in Nevada Gold Mines. Newmont agreed to pay Barrick **US$1.95 billion**, folded its **Fourmile** project into Nevada Gold Mines (creating a Nevada complex of nearly **100 million ounces**), and consented to the planned IPO. Barrick said **Mark Hill** would lead the new company. At an investor event in early October, management said the listing is still targeted for year-end but could slip into **January**.

## Business overview: Barrick vs the TSX gold majors

Barrick is worth about **C$92 billion**, second in this group only to **Agnico Eagle** at about **C$131 billion**. The two large streaming and royalty companies, **Wheaton Precious Metals** and **Franco-Nevada**, follow at about C$86 billion and C$65 billion, with **Kinross** and **Alamos Gold** near C$39 billion and C$19 billion.

On the income statement, though, Barrick is the biggest business here. Over the last twelve months it booked about **C$28.6 billion of revenue** and **C$9.0 billion of net income**, the most in the group on both lines. Agnico earns slightly less on about two-thirds of the revenue, which shows its higher margins. The streamers are much smaller businesses with very high margins, because they buy metal at fixed prices instead of running mines.

## Performance: up about 18% in a year, behind only Wheaton

Over the twelve months to October 8, Barrick was up about **18%**. That trails only **Wheaton** (about +24%) and beats Franco-Nevada (+9.5%) and Agnico Eagle (+7.9%), while Kinross and Alamos Gold are both **down about 7%**. That relative strength is exactly what CIBC flagged.

The month to October 8 was rough for the whole group as gold cooled from its highs. Barrick fell about **10%**, close to the group average, while Kinross dropped about **22%**. Over five and ten years Barrick is the laggard here, up about +142% and +163%, a reminder of how long it traded as the sector's problem child before the recent turnaround.

## Valuation: about 10x earnings in a sector that pays 18x

On trailing earnings Barrick trades at about **10.4x**, against a group average near **18x**. Only **Kinross**, at about 9x, is cheaper. Barrick also has the **lowest price-to-sales (3.4x)** and **lowest price-to-book (2.4x)** in the table, and it trades near **10x forward earnings**. At the October 16 close of C$57.74 the trailing multiple is closer to **10.7x**, so it still looks cheap.

The streamers sit at the other end of the table. Wheaton and Franco-Nevada trade above **30x** trailing earnings, because investors pay up for a royalty model with no direct exposure to mining costs. Agnico Eagle, the premium operator, is near 16x.

Kinross is the closest like-for-like comparison and is slightly cheaper still.

## Dividends: the highest yield in the group, and well covered

Barrick's dividend policy aims to return **half of its attributable free cash flow**. It pays a **base dividend of US$0.175 a quarter**, plus a **top-up at year-end**. In our table that works out to about **C$1.16 a share** and an indicated yield of about **2.1%**, the highest in this group, where the average is under 1%. The payout ratio is about **24%** of trailing earnings, so the dividend is well covered.

## Why it matters: from messy miner to a cleaner story

The old story was Barrick as a **messy global miner**: disputes with partners and host governments, a mine shutdown in Mali, and patchy delivery on guidance. The new story is a **cleaner company**, with three straight strong quarters, net cash on the balance sheet, the Newmont fight settled, and a plan to list its North American gold business separately.

The open question is whether the IPO **unlocks value for Barrick shareholders**, or whether the market keeps valuing the parent, and its higher-risk assets, at a discount. CIBC's downgrade does not argue the turnaround is fake. It argues that much of it is already in the price until the IPO proves otherwise.

## Bull case

1. **Cheap on almost every measure.** About 10x trailing and forward earnings, the lowest price-to-sales and price-to-book in the group, and net cash on the balance sheet.
2. **The IPO could put a market price on the best assets.** A separate listing of the North American gold business, including a Nevada complex of nearly 100 million ounces, could show what the market will pay for low-risk ounces.
3. **Returns and growth.** Big buybacks, a covered dividend, and copper growth from the **Lumwana** expansion in Zambia, with first copper targeted for early 2028.

## Bear case and risks

1. **CIBC's point.** After a year of outperformance, the easy re-rating may be done, and IPO proceeds are months away.
2. **Costs are climbing.** All-in sustaining costs rose 11% from a year earlier to about **US$1,866 an ounce**, and guidance assumes oil at **US$70 a barrel**, far below where oil trades now. Barrick says every US$10 a barrel adds about US$12 an ounce to costs.
3. **Thin free cash flow and political risk.** Attributable free cash flow was just **US$141 million** in the second quarter, and the Mali dispute showed the political risk in its African mines.

## What to watch

1. **Third-quarter results on November 9, 2026**, and whether Barrick meets guidance for a fourth straight quarter.
2. **The North American IPO**, targeted for year-end, though it could slip into January.
3. **The gold price**, after its pullback from the highs earlier this year, and whether CIBC's lower forecasts prove right.
4. **The C$68 average target.** Whether the stock closes the gap, and whether more brokers follow CIBC.

## Bottom line

Barrick has done much of what investors asked: steady production, net cash, big buybacks and a settled fight with Newmont. At about 10x earnings it is still one of the cheapest gold majors on the TSX, and it pays the highest yield in its peer group. CIBC's downgrade is a call on timing more than quality. After an 18% year, the next leg probably depends on two things outside the current numbers: the gold price, and whether the North American IPO shows the market what Barrick's best assets are worth. The November 9 third-quarter report is the next test.
