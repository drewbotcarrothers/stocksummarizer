## What happened

On **August 27, 2026**, Toronto-Dominion Bank reported **fiscal Q3** (ended July 31, 2026):

- **Reported** net income **CA$4.6B** / diluted EPS **CA$2.74**.
- **Adjusted** net income **CA$4.7B** / diluted EPS **CA$2.77** — up about **21%** and **26%** year over year; management called out **record** earnings and EPS.
- **Adjusted revenue** up about **8%**; **adjusted ROE ~16%** (reported ROE **15.8%**).
- **CET1 14.3%** after buying back about **14.5M** shares in the quarter.
- Credit: impaired PCLs softer quarter-over-quarter; total PCLs guided toward the **low end** of the prior **40–50 bps** FY26 range.
- Fifth consecutive quarter of positive operating leverage.

**Capital return (earnings call):** management said that with continued organic capital accretion and RWA growth in line with FY26 year-to-date, TD **could return over CA$13B** of capital in **fiscal 2027** (primarily buybacks, **in addition to** the dividend) to migrate CET1 toward about **13%**. Organic growth remains priority #1; selective fee-income M&A second; excess capital return third.

**U.S. franchise:** plan for about **100 new U.S. branches by end-2028** (most openings weighted to 2028), densifying East Coast markets and expanding Florida / Carolinas — **subject to regulatory approval**. AML remediation remains priority number one.

**Street color:** National Bank Financial lifted its target toward **CA$196** (Outperform); other Buy/Outperform targets cluster in the high-180s; Barclays stays more cautious (Underweight / mid-160s). MarketBeat-style consensus sits around **Moderate Buy** with an average 12-month target near **CA$171** — roughly flat to the September 18 screen price after a strong re-rating.

**Tape (TSX screener 2026-09-18):** TD **+59.2%** over 1Y (peer-leading among the Big Five on that window), **−1.1%** over 1M; indicated dividend yield ~**2.58%** on annual DPS **CA$4.20**; payout ~**46%** TTM.

## Why it matters

The old TD story was cleanup / remediation overhang and a softer U.S. print. The new story is **record earnings + CET1 flexibility + explicit FY27 capital-return math**, with a multi-year **U.S. branch densification** option — while the stock has already run ~**60%** in a year. Income investors still get a mid-2% indicated yield with a sub-50% payout; total-return buyers care whether the **>$13B** FY27 return illustration and 100-branch plan survive credit, RWA growth, and regulatory gates.

## Bull case

1. **Record Q3 / run-rate:** adj. NI CA$4.7B, EPS CA$2.77 (+26% YoY), revenue +8%, ROE ~16%, fifth straight quarter of positive operating leverage.
2. **Capital return optionality:** CET1 14.3% + organic accretion → management illustration of **>$13B** FY27 returns toward ~13% CET1 — plus the ongoing dividend.
3. **U.S. reinvestment + Street targets to CA$196:** 100-branch plan by 2028 (regulatory-dependent) and multiple Outperform/Buy raises if earnings power holds.

## Bear case

1. **Tape already priced a lot:** +59% 1Y; consensus ~CA$171 is roughly flat vs CA$170 screen — limited “easy” upside to the average target.
2. **>$13B / 100 branches are conditional:** capital return needs continued accretion + contained RWA; U.S. branches need **regulatory approval**; AML remediation remains priority #1.
3. **Credit / U.S. execution:** PCLs guided better but not zero; U.S. ROE still trails Canadian P&C; any re-tightening of credit or slower U.S. volumes hits the narrative.

## What to watch next

1. FY27 capital-return cadence (buyback pace vs CET1 path to ~13%).
2. Regulatory progress on U.S. branch openings / densification.
3. PCL trajectory vs “low end of 40–50 bps” guide.
4. Whether adj. ROE holds near mid-teens through FY26/FY27.
5. Dividend growth vs payout staying disciplined under heavier buybacks.
