## The short version: record earnings, already in the price?

Toronto-Dominion Bank (TSX: TD), Canada's second-largest bank by market value, traded at **CA$170.37** on the September 18, 2026 screener, after a run of about **59%** over twelve months — the best one-year move among the Big Five. Over the last month it slipped about **1%**. The story has flipped from remediation overhang to **record earnings and capital return**: a record fiscal third quarter, a path to hand back more than C$13B in fiscal 2027, and a plan for about 100 new U.S. branches by 2028.

At about **18x trailing earnings** and with the average analyst target near **C$171**, roughly level with the share price, the question for a new buyer is how much of that good news the re-rating has already captured. Below we go through the Q3 print, the capital-return math and the U.S. plan, and how TD compares with its Big Five peers — RBC, BMO, Scotiabank and CIBC — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: a record Q3 and a C$13B capital-return path

On **August 27, 2026**, TD reported fiscal Q3 (the quarter ended July 31, 2026). The headline numbers:

- **Adjusted net income about C$4.7B** and adjusted diluted EPS of **C$2.77**, up about **21%** and **26%** year over year — a record on both lines.
- **Reported net income C$4.6B** and EPS of **C$2.74**, up from C$3.3B and C$1.89 a year earlier.
- **Adjusted revenue up about 8%** and **adjusted ROE near 16%** (reported ROE 15.8%).
- **CET1 ratio of 14.3%** after buying back about **14.5M shares** in the quarter.
- Provisions for credit losses guided toward the **low end of the 40–50 basis-point** fiscal 2026 range, and a **fifth straight quarter** of positive operating leverage.

On the call, CEO Raymond Chun said that, with continued organic capital accretion and risk-weighted asset growth in line with fiscal 2026 so far, TD **could return over C$13B of capital in fiscal 2027**, mostly through buybacks and on top of the dividend, to bring CET1 down toward about **13%**. Management's order of priorities stays the same: organic growth first, selective fee-income acquisitions second, and returning excess capital third. The C$13B figure is an **illustration, not a commitment** — it depends on how much capital the bank keeps generating and how fast its balance sheet grows.

### The U.S. branch plan and the remediation backdrop

U.S. Banking outlined a plan to open about **100 new U.S. branches by the end of 2028**, with most openings weighted to 2028, densifying East Coast markets and expanding in Florida and the Carolinas. The plan is **subject to regulatory approval**, and management repeated that anti-money-laundering (AML) remediation remains **priority number one**. In Q3 the U.S. segment earned about **C$1.07B** (US$771M) on a reported basis, with adjusted net income up about 12% and a reported segment ROE of 10.2%.

That last figure is the weak spot. A U.S. return on equity near 10% still trails TD's Canadian business by a wide margin, and the branch build adds costs before it adds revenue. The capital-return story is strong, but the U.S. franchise is where execution and regulatory risk sit — and where the valuation discussion below gets more nuanced.

## Business overview: TD vs the Big Five banks

TD runs four main businesses: Canadian personal and commercial banking (its biggest earner), U.S. retail banking along the East Coast, wealth management and insurance, and wholesale banking through TD Securities. The Canadian bank and wealth arms provide steady, fee-rich earnings; the U.S. bank, credit costs and capital markets are the swing factors.

On the TSX screener, TD sits in the **Finance** sector, and the natural comparison is the rest of the Big Five. **RBC (RY)** is the largest Canadian bank and the benchmark on profitability. **BMO (BMO)** has the other big U.S. footprint. **Scotiabank (BNS)** is the international-focused lender, and **CIBC (CM)** is the most Canada-centred of the group. Insurers, smaller banks and asset managers in the sector were left out because their business mix is not comparable.

## Why it matters: from remediation overhang to capital return

The old narrative was TD as a **bank in the penalty box**: AML remediation and a softer U.S. print, priced at a discount to its peers. The new narrative is **record earnings, CET1 flexibility and an explicit capital-return illustration**, plus a multi-year plan to densify the U.S. branch network.

Income investors still collect a mid-2% yield with a sub-50% payout. Total-return investors care whether the C$13B-plus return math and the 100-branch plan survive credit costs, balance-sheet growth and regulatory gates. The open question is whether TD can turn its excess capital into faster per-share growth now that the market no longer prices it at a discount.

## Bull case

1. **Earnings power.** A record Q3 — adjusted net income of about C$4.7B, EPS up 26%, revenue up 8% and ROE near 16% — gives cover for big capital returns.
2. **Excess capital.** CET1 at 14.3% and a path to return over C$13B in fiscal 2027 point to heavy buybacks on top of the dividend.
3. **U.S. reinvestment and Street support.** The 100-branch plan adds a growth leg, and the most bullish targets reach C$196, about 15% above the price.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Already priced.** After a 59% one-year run, the average target of about C$171 sits roughly at the share price.
2. **Conditional plans.** The C$13B return depends on capital accretion and contained risk-weighted asset growth, and the U.S. branches need regulatory approval while AML remediation continues.
3. **Credit and U.S. execution.** Provisions are guided lower but not to zero, and a U.S. ROE near 10% still trails the Canadian bank.

## What to watch

1. **Fiscal Q4 and full-year results.** Whether adjusted ROE holds near the mid-teens and provisions stay at the low end of the 40–50 basis-point guide.
2. **Fiscal 2027 buyback pace** versus the CET1 path toward about 13%.
3. **Regulatory progress** on the U.S. branch openings and AML remediation milestones.
4. **Analyst targets.** Whether the consensus moves up toward the C$183–C$196 bullish cluster or the stock drifts back toward the Underweight view in the mid-C$160s.

## Bottom line

TD has earned its re-rating: record adjusted earnings, a fifth straight quarter of positive operating leverage, a 14.3% CET1 ratio and a credible plan to return more than C$13B in fiscal 2027. The U.S. branch plan adds a longer-term growth leg, but it comes with regulatory strings and a remediation programme still under way. The issue is price. At about 18x trailing earnings, above the Big Five average, and level with the consensus target after a 59% run, much of the turnaround is already in the stock. The next test is whether buybacks and U.S. execution deliver the per-share growth the multiple now assumes.
