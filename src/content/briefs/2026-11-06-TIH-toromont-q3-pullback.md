## The short version: a Q3 crush, then a 15% slide

Toromont Industries (TSX: TIH), one of Canada's largest Caterpillar dealers (plus CIMCO refrigeration and AVL power enclosures), **crushed its third quarter** — then the stock dropped about **15%** over the next month. Revenue jumped **26%** and operating income jumped **46%**, helped by power systems and a sharp ramp in AVL enclosure sales. Bookings rose **50%** and backlog reached **C$2.9 billion**, including about **C$1 billion** of firm power-system orders for delivery mostly in **2027**.

The shares closed near **C$227** at September 30 and have since pulled back to about **C$193**. On the TSX screener Toromont still trades at about **31 times trailing earnings**, the **richest** multiple in this Cat-dealer / industrial distribution set, and yields only about **1.2%**. So the question is whether this pullback is a buying chance after a Strong-Buy upgrade, or whether the premium multiple still has further to fall. Below we go through the quarter, the power backlog, and how Toromont compares with Finning, Wajax, Russel Metals and ADENTRA on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: a Q3 beat and a power backlog into 2027

Toromont reported on **November 2, 2026**, for the quarter ended September 30. The headline numbers:

- **Revenue of C$1,652.1 million**, up **26%**,
- **operating income of C$279.3 million**, up **46%**,
- **net earnings of C$192.1 million**, up **37%**, or **C$2.35** a share,
- Equipment Group up **26%**, with new equipment sales up **49%** on power systems plus mining and construction deliveries.

### AVL, bookings and the balance sheet

**AVL**, the power-enclosure business Toromont bought in 2025, booked **C$218.5 million** of revenue in the quarter, versus **C$77.9 million** a year earlier. Bookings rose **50%** (Equipment Group +59%; power-system orders +84%). Backlog sat at **C$2.9 billion**, versus C$1.3 billion a year ago, and includes about **C$1.0 billion** of firm Power Systems orders for delivery substantially in **2027** (announced June 17).

Accounting for the AVL purchase cut about **C$0.19** from EPS in the quarter; excluding those items, EPS rose **28%**. The company is in a **net-cash** position, with net debt to total capitalization at **minus 12%**. The board kept the quarterly dividend at **C$0.56**.

After the quarter, **CIBC** upgraded the stock from Hold to **Strong-Buy**, and the MarketBeat consensus sits at **Buy** with an average target of about **C$246.60** (~28% upside vs C$193.06).

## Business overview: Toromont vs TSX distribution peers

Toromont sells and rents Caterpillar equipment, runs product support, builds power systems and enclosures (AVL), and owns CIMCO refrigeration. The growth story that used to be a classic dealer cycle — construction, mining, product support — is now also a **power-systems** story tied to data-center and industrial demand.

On the TSX screener, Toromont sits in **Distribution services**. **Finning (FTT)** is the closest Caterpillar peer. **Wajax (WJX)** is a smaller industrial distributor. **Russel Metals (RUS)** distributes metals. **ADENTRA (ADEN)** distributes architectural building products. That set keeps the table inside industrial distribution rather than pulling in unrelated wholesalers.

## Why it matters: from dealer cycle to power backlog

Toromont's story used to be a classic Caterpillar dealer cycle: construction, mining, product support. Now it is also a **power-systems** story. Data-center and industrial power demand is filling a backlog that stretches into **2027**, and AVL is converting that into revenue.

The open question is whether that growth is already priced into a **31-times** earnings multiple after a sharp pullback — especially when Finning offers a similar Caterpillar footprint at a clear discount on earnings, sales and cash flow. The **~28%** consensus upside and the Strong-Buy upgrade argue the Street still sees room; the peer tables argue investors are still paying up for the backlog.

## Bull case

1. **Power orders are showing up.** Power-system orders and the AVL enclosure ramp are in the revenue line and in a **C$2.9 billion** backlog, including about **C$1 billion** for 2027.
2. **Net cash.** The balance sheet gives Toromont room to invest and keep paying the dividend.
3. **Upside after the slide.** After a **15%** one-month drop, the average analyst target still implies about **28%** upside, and a major bank just moved to Strong-Buy.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Richest multiples.** Toromont still trades at the richest earnings and book multiples in this peer set, even after the slide.
2. **2027 execution risk.** A large share of the backlog is power systems due in 2027 — execution, customer timing and capacity all have to land.
3. **Power-helped growth.** Equipment Group growth was helped by power; if that cools, mining and construction alone may not keep this pace. Dealer stocks can re-rate quickly when industrial demand softens.

## What to watch

1. **Power-system order intake and 2027 delivery execution** on the ~C$1 billion of firm orders already in backlog.
2. **AVL capacity expansion**, including the planned Waterdown build-out that has to support the enclosure ramp.
3. **Mining and construction bookings** once the power surge normalizes — whether the classic dealer cycle can carry growth on its own.
4. **Whether the premium multiple compresses toward Finning** (near ~22x trailing) as the shares settle after the 15% slide.

## Bottom line

Toromont delivered a strong third quarter and a backlog that stretches into 2027, then gave back about 15% of its share price in a month. The pullback is real, and a Strong-Buy upgrade plus ~28% consensus upside make the question tempting — but the stock remains the richest name in this peer set on earnings and book value. Whether this is a chance depends on 2027 power delivery and on whether investors keep paying a premium for that backlog.
