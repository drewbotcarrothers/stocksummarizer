## The short version: a record margin after a rough year

Stantec (TSX: STN), the Edmonton-based engineering and design firm behind water systems, buildings, energy projects and infrastructure, reported a **record 20.2% adjusted EBITDA margin** for the third quarter on November 12, 2026, and **raised its 2026 profit targets**. The stock jumped about **14% on November 13** to **C$122.20**, one of the biggest gains on the TSX that day.

The jump came after a hard year. On the November 9 screener, Stantec was **down about 34% over twelve months** at C$105.02, with WSP Global down almost as much. Before the jump it also had the **lowest trailing P/E in its peer group, about 24x**, against a group average of about 35x. The question for a new buyer is whether this quarter marks the turning point, or whether the good news is now in the price. Below we go through the quarter, the backlog, and how Stantec compares with WSP Global, AtkinsRéalis, Aecon, Bird Construction and Badger Infrastructure on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 12

On **Thursday, November 12, 2026**, after the market closed, Stantec reported results for the quarter ended September 30. All figures are in Canadian dollars.

- **Net revenue of C$1.86B, up 9.4%**, with **5.3% organic growth** and 2.6% from acquisitions. Every region and business grew organically, led by double-digit organic growth in Water.
- **Adjusted EBITDA of C$377.1M, up 16.6%**, for a **record 20.2% margin**, 120 basis points higher than a year earlier.
- **Adjusted earnings of C$1.89 a share, up 23.5%**; diluted EPS rose 21.2% to C$1.60, and net income rose 19.7% to C$179.6M.
- **Project margin of 55.0%**, up 60 basis points, helped by solid execution and favourable project recoveries. Administrative and marketing costs fell to 35.5% of net revenue.
- **Operating cash flow of C$392M**, up C$76M from a year earlier, and net debt of about **1.2 times adjusted EBITDA**, at the low end of its 1–2x target range.

### The US and the backlog

The **United States**, Stantec's biggest market, grew net revenue **10.9% to nearly C$1.1B**, with 4.9% organic growth as project delays from the first half of the year cleared. Canada grew 4.1% organically and the Global region 7.5%. **Contract backlog reached C$9.2B, up 11.6%** (9.9% organic), or about **13 months of work**, with double-digit organic backlog growth in Canada and Global and in Buildings, Water, and Energy & Resources.

### Raised targets and buybacks

Stantec raised its 2026 targets: **adjusted EPS growth of 17–19%** (from 15–18%), an **adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.3–18.5%** (from 17.8–18.3%), and an adjusted net income margin at or above 9.8%. It narrowed expected net revenue growth to 9–11%. So far in 2026 it has **bought back 3.39 million shares for C$350.4M**, about 3% of the company.

## Business overview: Stantec vs engineering and infrastructure peers

Stantec is a consulting business: it is paid for engineering, architecture, environmental and project-management work, rather than for building projects itself. That gives it high project margins and a light balance sheet, but it depends on a steady flow of public and private project spending, especially in water, buildings, energy and transportation.

On the TSX screener, Stantec sits in the **Industrial services** sector. **WSP Global (WSP)** is its closest peer, a larger Montreal-based engineering consultancy. **AtkinsRéalis (ATRL)** combines engineering services with nuclear work. **Aecon (ARE)** and **Bird Construction (BDT)** are infrastructure and construction contractors, and **Badger Infrastructure (BDGI)** provides non-destructive excavation services. Pipelines (Enbridge, TC Energy, Pembina, Keyera, South Bow), Waste Connections and Precision Drilling share the screener sector but are not business-comparable, so they were left out.

## Why it matters: from delays to margins

For most of 2026, the Stantec story was **project delays in the US and a falling share price**. This quarter shifts it toward **wider margins, steady growth and a fuller backlog**. Margins rose because projects ran well and overhead fell as a share of revenue, not because of a single big contract, and management raised its full-year profit targets with one quarter left.

That matters most for investors who want **steady compounding rather than a big dividend**: a consulting firm with low debt, a 13-month backlog and ongoing buybacks can grow earnings per share faster than revenue if margins hold.

## Bull case

1. **Broad-based demand.** Water, buildings and energy work is growing, and backlog grew organically in every region and business, which supports revenue into 2027.
2. **Room to keep buying back stock.** With net debt at about 1.2 times adjusted EBITDA, Stantec can keep funding buybacks and small acquisitions that add to earnings per share.
3. **Still below the peer average.** Even after the jump, a trailing P/E near 28x sits below the roughly 35x group average, and the forward P/E was about 16x before the move.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Some of the margin may not repeat.** Part of the project-margin gain came from favourable project recoveries, which are not guaranteed in future quarters.
2. **Backlog restatement.** Stantec revised its previously published backlog for the last four quarters down by about 2–2.6% after identifying reporting adjustments; US organic backlog growth was 5.8% year over year.
3. **US spending risk.** Growth in the United States depends on public infrastructure spending, and the first-half delays could return. After a 14% day, some of the good news is already in the price.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter results**, and whether the margin stays near 20%.
2. **Stantec's 2027 targets**, likely published with year-end results early in the new year.
3. **Organic growth in the US** and new backlog wins, especially in Water and Buildings.
4. **More buybacks and acquisitions**, and whether analysts move targets toward the C$146–C$169 end of the range.

## Bottom line

Stantec's third quarter was strong across the board: 9.4% net revenue growth, a record 20.2% adjusted EBITDA margin, 23.5% higher adjusted EPS, a C$9.2B backlog and raised 2026 targets. The market rewarded it with a 14% jump after a year in which the stock fell about a third alongside WSP. Before the move it had the lowest trailing P/E in its peer group; after it, the stock is less cheap but still below the average. The slide looks overdone on these numbers, but the next leg depends on margins holding.
