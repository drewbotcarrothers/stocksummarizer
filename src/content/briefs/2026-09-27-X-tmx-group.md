## The short version: a growth plan priced at a premium

TMX Group (TSX: X), the operator of the Toronto Stock Exchange, TSX Venture, the Montréal Exchange and a growing data business, closed at **CA$54.49** on September 24, 2026. The stock is up about **20%** over three months but only about **1.5%** over twelve months. Management's message is that TMX is **"well on track"** toward its **TM2X** goal: doubling revenue from **C$1B to C$2B**, faster than it took to get from C$500M to C$1B.

At about **25x trailing earnings** and **21x forward**, TMX trades at a premium to its financial peers, so the question for a new buyer is how much of the C$2B plan is already in the price. Below we go through the CIBC presentation, the deal slate, and how TMX compares with IGM Financial, Onex, Intact, Sun Life and Great-West on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the CIBC update on TM2X

On **September 24, 2026**, CFO **David Arnold** spoke at **CIBC's Eastern Institutional Investor Conference**, and coverage of his remarks circulated on September 26–27. The main points:

- TMX is **"well on track"** to grow revenue from **C$1B to C$2B** in half the time it took to grow from C$500M to C$1B.
- **Aspirational mix targets:** recurring revenue about **two-thirds** of the total, roughly **half** of revenue from outside Canada, and Global Solutions, Insights and Analytics (GSIA) near **50%**.
- Recurring revenue is about **53%** today, and management said it will **not grow that mix by starving the transactional businesses** (the TSX, TSX Venture and Montréal Exchange).

None of this was new guidance in the form of a number for next quarter. It was a **progress report on a long-term plan**, and its value is in the mix targets: a business that is two-thirds recurring and half international would deserve a different multiple from a domestic exchange that depends on trading volumes and listings.

### Hot capital markets and a busy deal slate

The backdrop is strong. Arnold pointed to **more IPOs in the first half of 2026 than in all of 2025**, Canadian capital raising **up about 15%** year over year through August, and financings **up nearly 90%**. Those transactional revenues are exactly the part of TMX that swings with markets.

The 2026 deal slate is also in motion:

- **Cboe Australia closed** and has been rebranded as **TMX Australia Exchange**.
- **Refi closed** and was folded into **VettaFi**, TMX's index and data business.
- **Cboe Canada** is still pending **Competition Bureau and OSC** review.
- The **BOX + MEMX** combination still needs **SEC approval**.

Two closings push TMX further outside Canada and deeper into data; two pending deals carry regulatory and timing risk. That split matters for the valuation: investors are paying today for deals that are not all done.

## Business overview: TMX vs financial peers

TMX runs Canada's main equity exchanges (TSX and TSX Venture), the Montréal Exchange for derivatives, clearing and depository services, and a data and analytics arm that includes Trayport (energy trading software), Datalinx and VettaFi. The steadier part is recurring revenue from data, index licensing, listings fees and technology; the cyclical part is trading, clearing and new-issue activity.

On the TSX screener, TMX sits in the **Finance** sector, which is dominated by the Big Six banks. The banks were **deliberately excluded**, since a bank's balance sheet is a poor comparison for an exchange-and-data operator. The peer set uses liquid capital-markets and diversified financial names: **IGM Financial (IGM)**, a wealth and asset manager; **Onex (ONEX)**, an alternative-asset manager and investor; and three large diversified financials, **Intact (IFC)**, **Sun Life (SLF)** and **Great-West (GWO)**. CI Financial was missing from the screener merge.

## Why it matters: from exchange utility to global data platform

The old narrative was TMX as **Canada's exchange utility**: listings, trading and clearing, tied to the domestic market cycle. The new narrative is **a global market-infrastructure and data platform**, with Trayport, Datalinx and VettaFi alongside the exchanges and a stated path from C$1B to C$2B in revenue.

The CIBC update and the Australian and Refi closings fit that new narrative. Growth investors care whether the deal slate and recurring-mix targets stick. Income investors care whether the dividend keeps growing while management funds technology and M&A. The open question is how long the market will pay a premium before the pending deals close.

## Bull case

1. **TM2X on track.** Management says the C$1B to C$2B plan is on schedule, with 2026 capital-markets activity already strong.
2. **Closed deals widen the moat.** TMX Australia Exchange and Refi inside VettaFi expand data and international reach.
3. **Street upside.** Targets of about C$65 to C$71 imply meaningful upside from C$54.49 if the mix shift lands.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Premium already paid.** About 25x trailing earnings against an 18x peer average means a lot of progress is priced in.
2. **Regulatory risk.** Cboe Canada and BOX + MEMX still need the Competition Bureau, the OSC and the SEC; timing and dis-synergies are real risks.
3. **Cyclical revenue.** Recurring revenue is still only about 53%, and transactional volumes can swing with markets.

## What to watch

1. **Competition Bureau and OSC decisions on Cboe Canada.** Approval would add a second Canadian equity venue to TMX.
2. **SEC approval on BOX + MEMX.** The biggest test of the U.S. strategy.
3. **Q3 2026 results, expected around October 26.** Recurring revenue share and the strength of listings and trading.
4. **Mix progress.** Whether recurring and international revenue keep climbing toward the two-thirds and one-half targets.

## Bottom line

TMX has earned a premium: the dominant position in Canadian listings, a growing data business, two closed deals in 2026 and a decade of compounding that tops this peer table. Management's TM2X update says the C$2B revenue plan is on track. The issue is price: at about 25x trailing earnings and 26x free cash flow, TMX already costs more than its financial peers, and two important deals still sit with regulators. The next test is whether the Q3 results show the recurring mix moving toward two-thirds while markets stay busy.
