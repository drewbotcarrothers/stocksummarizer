## The short version: hyper-growth at a premium price

Celestica (TSX: CLS), the Toronto-based electronics manufacturer that builds hardware for cloud and AI data centres, closed at **CA$480.23** on the September 12, 2026 TSX screener after jumping about **6.6%** in a day. That capped a week of about **+11%** and a month of about **+8%**, and leaves the stock up about **38%** over twelve months. The story the market is paying for is **AI-driven growth**: trailing revenue up about 46% and earnings per share roughly doubled.

The price already reflects a lot of that. At about **36x trailing earnings** and **23x forward**, with a price to free cash flow near **78x**, the question for a new buyer is whether cash generation can catch up with the earnings story. Below we go through the move, the fundamentals underneath it, and how Celestica compares with a set of large TSX technology names — Constellation Software, CGI, OpenText and Evertz — on size, returns, valuation and analyst ratings.

## What happened: a 6.6% jump inside a bigger swing

On the **September 12, 2026** screener, Celestica was one of the louder large-cap movers on the TSX:

- a one-day gain of about **6.6%**, on roughly average volume (relative volume about **1.1x**),
- a one-week gain of about **10.8%** and a one-month gain of about **8.2%**,
- but a three-month change of about **−11.9%**, so the stock was still below where it traded in June.

That pattern looks more like a **bounce or continuation inside a bigger swing** than a fresh breakout from calm water. Celestica's weekly volatility runs near **6%**, far above a typical large-cap, and its 14-day RSI near **58** shows momentum without the stretched readings of a blow-off top. The daily technical rating on the screener was **Buy**, with moving averages at **Strong buy**. No single company announcement is attached to the move in the data used here, so treat it as a **market re-rating of the AI-hardware trade** rather than a news-driven jump.

### What the screener shows underneath

The fundamentals behind the run are strong on almost every growth measure (trailing twelve months, CAD):

- **Revenue about C$21.6B, up about 46%** year over year.
- **Diluted EPS of C$13.31, up about 108%.**
- **Gross margin about 11.5%, operating margin about 8.4%** and net margin about 7.2%.
- **Return on equity about 52%.**
- **Free cash flow about C$717M**, a margin of only about **3.3%**, after about C$863M of capital spending.
- Forward estimates of about **C$15.70** of annual EPS and about **C$28.8B** of annual revenue.

The weak spot is cash conversion. Free cash flow is less than half of net income, and the screener's **next-twelve-month free cash flow estimate is negative** (about −C$195M) as capital spending stays elevated. The balance sheet is workable for a grower — debt to equity near **0.40**, a current ratio of about **1.23** and net debt of about C$632M — but the gap between earnings and cash matters for the valuation discussion below.

## Business overview: Celestica vs TSX tech peers

Celestica is an electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company: it designs, builds and supplies hardware for other companies' brands. The fast-growing part of the business serves cloud and data-centre customers with servers, networking and storage equipment, which is why it rides the AI infrastructure build-out. A more diversified advanced-technology business serves aerospace and defence, industrial and health-tech customers. Margins in contract manufacturing are thin — an 11.5% gross margin is typical of hardware assembly rather than software — so growth and scale do most of the work.

On the TSX screener, Celestica sits in the **Electronic technology** sector, but there is no other TSX-listed EMS company to compare it with; the sector's other large names are aerospace companies such as Bombardier and MDA Space. So the peer set mixes the large TSX technology names with one same-sector hardware maker. **Constellation Software (CSU)** is the only TSX tech name of similar market value. **CGI (GIB.A)** is the largest IT-services firm, with revenue in the same range. **OpenText (OTEX)** is a mature enterprise-software company, and **Evertz (ET)** is a same-sector Canadian maker of broadcast and video hardware.

## Why it matters: from contract manufacturer to AI infrastructure play

The old narrative was Celestica as a **low-margin contract manufacturer**, priced at a modest multiple and exposed to the hardware cycle. The new narrative is an **AI infrastructure supplier**, riding the data-centre build-out with mid-40s revenue growth, triple-digit EPS growth and a return on equity above 50%.

That rerating explains the multi-year gains, and it also explains the risk. The market now prices Celestica more like a growth technology company than a manufacturer, so it needs the AI and cloud spending cycle to stay strong. The open question is whether cash generation catches up with earnings before the market rotates away from paying any multiple for growth.

## Bull case

1. **Growth is not subtle.** Revenue up about 46% and EPS up about 108% over the trailing year, with forward estimates still pointing higher (about C$15.70 of EPS on C$28.8B of revenue).
2. **The forward multiple is less stretched.** About 23x forward earnings versus 36x trailing, with a trailing PEG near 0.31.
3. **Momentum and quality.** A Strong buy rating, moving averages at Strong buy, RSI near 58 rather than overbought, and return on equity above 50%.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Premium metrics.** Price to book near 15.7x and price to free cash flow near 78x leave little room for disappointment.
2. **Thin cash conversion.** A 3.3% free cash flow margin and a negative next-twelve-month free cash flow estimate are red flags if capital spending stays high.
3. **Volatility.** The stock is still about 12% below its level three months earlier, weekly volatility is near 6%, and a sector de-rating in AI hardware would hit hard.

## What to watch

1. **Follow-through after the jump.** Whether Celestica holds above its pre-spike level or fades back into its three-month range.
2. **Next quarterly results.** Revenue and EPS against the roughly C$28.8B and C$15.70 annual estimates.
3. **Capital spending and free cash flow.** Whether cash generation starts to catch up with reported earnings.
4. **AI-hardware sentiment.** How the wider electronics and AI-supplier group trades, since a peer de-rating would pull Celestica with it.

## Bottom line

Celestica has earned its re-rating: revenue up about 46%, EPS roughly doubled, a return on equity above 50% and the best one-year record among large TSX technology names. The 6.6% jump on the September 12 screener adds momentum to that story without changing the numbers. The issue is cash and price. At about 36x trailing earnings, 23x forward and nearly 78x free cash flow, with a negative cash flow estimate for the next twelve months, a lot of growth is already in the stock. The next test is whether the coming quarters turn earnings into cash.
