## What happened (news + tape)

On **October 1, 2026**, WSP Global announced an agreement to acquire **GCM Corpo Inc.** — a Canadian provider of engineering consulting and specialist services for the energy and industrial sectors:

- GCM Corpo brings about **600 professionals** across Québec, Western Canada and Atlantic Canada, serving energy, industrial, and mining and metals clients.
- The deal expands WSP’s Canadian platform in process engineering, industrial digitalization, OT cybersecurity, project and construction management, procurement, detailed engineering and environmental services, aligned with the **2025–2027 Global Strategic Action Plan**.
- A portion of consideration will be paid in **WSP common shares**, including to **Fonds de solidarité FTQ**, GCM’s largest shareholder. The **TSX** gave conditional approval for the share issuance.
- Close is expected in **Q4 2026**, subject to customary conditions including regulatory approval.
- **Critical:** the official release **does not disclose a purchase price**. Ignore AI headlines that invent a C$600 million deal figure from the ~600-professional headcount.

Recent Street notes stay constructive even after late-September target trims: National Bank near **C$216**, BMO near **C$275**, CIBC near **C$270**, Stifel near **C$230**, and RBC near **C$303**. Consensus from **14 analysts** sits near **C$283.57** (S&P Global via stockanalysis, pulled October 2, 2026). The October 1 close was **CA$171.04**, about **4.8%** soft versus the screener print of **CA$179.74**.

## Why it matters

The old story: a premium global engineering consultant that had already sold off hard over the past year. The new story asks whether a tuck-in of about six hundred Canadian energy and industrial professionals, paid partly in shares, strengthens the platform without a disclosed price tag investors can model. Growth investors care whether Q4 close and integration lift Canada energy bookings. Income investors should know the indicated yield is still under one percent.

## Bull case

1. About **600** energy and industrial professionals deepen Canada’s platform under the **2025–2027** plan, with **TSX** conditional share approval already in hand.
2. Street still clusters near **C$284** on a Strong Buy screen after the one-year slide.
3. Trailing P/E near **26x** with forward near **15x** screens cleaner than several construction peers, and LTM net income near **C$931M** is real scale.

## Bear case

1. **Purchase price undisclosed** means dilution and return-on-invested-capital math stay opaque until filings.
2. The one-year tape is still down about **34%** with Stantec, so a tuck-in may not re-rate the stock.
3. **Q4** close still needs regulatory approval, and share consideration means existing holders fund part of the deal.

## What to watch next

1. **Q4 2026** close timing and any disclosure of final consideration.
2. The next earnings print (stockanalysis flags early **November 2026**).
3. Canada energy and industrial booking trends after integration.
4. Whether Street targets hold near **C$284** after the deal.
