## The short version: a tuck-in after a hard year

WSP Global (TSX: WSP), the Montréal-based engineering and professional services firm with about **83,000** people worldwide, closed at **CA$179.74** on the September 24, 2026 screener print, down about **34%** over twelve months and about **6.5%** over the last month. On **October 1**, it agreed to buy **GCM Corpo**, a Canadian engineering consultancy with about **600 professionals** focused on energy, industrial and mining work. The purchase price was not disclosed. The new story is **a deeper Canadian energy and industrial platform**.

WSP trades at about **26x trailing earnings** but only about **15x forward**, and the Street's consensus target near **C$284** sits far above the share price. The question for a new buyer is whether a small tuck-in, paid partly in shares, changes anything after a hard year. Below we go through the deal, what the release leaves out, and how WSP compares with AtkinsRéalis, Stantec, Bird, Badger and Aecon on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the GCM Corpo acquisition

On **October 1, 2026**, WSP announced an agreement to acquire **GCM Corpo Inc.**, a Canadian provider of engineering consulting and specialist services to the energy and industrial sectors. The key points:

- GCM brings about **600 professionals** across **Québec, Western Canada and Atlantic Canada**, serving energy, industrial, and mining and metals clients.
- It adds capabilities in **process engineering, industrial digitalization, OT cybersecurity, project and construction management, procurement, detailed engineering and environmental services**.
- Part of the consideration will be paid in **WSP common shares**, including to **Fonds de solidarité FTQ**, GCM's largest shareholder; the **TSX** has given conditional approval for the share issuance.
- Closing is expected in **Q4 2026**, subject to customary conditions including regulatory approval.

CEO **Alexandre L'Heureux** called GCM highly complementary to WSP's **2025–2027 Global Strategic Action Plan**. This is the kind of deal WSP has done many times: buy a specialist team, fold it into the regional platform, and sell its skills across a much larger client base. Against a firm of about 83,000 people, 600 professionals is **a tuck-in, not a transformation**.

### What the release doesn't say

The official release **does not disclose a purchase price**. Some AI-generated headlines turned the ~600-professional headcount into a "C$600M" deal value; that figure does not come from WSP and should be ignored.

The missing price matters. Without it, investors can't model **how many new shares will be issued**, how much dilution existing holders absorb, or what return on invested capital WSP expects. Given the deal's size relative to WSP's C$24B market value, the dilution is likely small, but the details won't be clear until closing filings.

## Business overview: WSP vs engineering and construction peers

WSP designs and advises on transportation, buildings, water, environment, power and energy, and resource projects, mostly under fee-based professional services contracts rather than fixed-price construction. Its biggest markets are the U.S., Canada, the U.K., Australia and the Nordics. Fee-based work is steadier than construction, but WSP's growth has relied heavily on acquisitions, which makes integration and pricing discipline part of the story.

On the TSX screener, WSP sits in the **Industrial services** sector, which is dominated by midstream pipelines and waste management. As a documented exception, the peers are **business-comparable engineering and industrial-services names**: **AtkinsRéalis (ATRL)** and **Stantec (STN)**, the other large Canadian engineering consultancies; **Bird (BDT)** and **Aecon (ARE)**, Canadian contractors; and **Badger (BDGI)**, a hydrovac excavation services company. Enbridge, TC Energy, Pembina, Keyera, South Bow and Waste Connections were left out.

## Why it matters: from premium consultant to energy-platform builder

The old narrative was WSP as **a premium global engineering consultant**, priced for steady acquisition-led growth, which had already sold off hard over the past year. The new narrative asks whether **a tuck-in of about 600 Canadian energy and industrial professionals**, paid partly in shares, strengthens the platform without a disclosed price tag for investors to model.

Growth investors care whether the Q4 close and integration lift Canadian energy bookings. Income investors should know the indicated yield is still under 1%. The open question is what will reverse the derating: deals like GCM, or simply better organic growth numbers.

## Bull case

1. **A deeper energy platform.** About 600 energy and industrial professionals strengthen WSP's Canadian business under the 2025–2027 plan, with TSX share approval already in hand.
2. **Street conviction.** Consensus near C$284 and a Strong buy screen point to large upside after the one-year slide.
3. **Real scale at a fair forward price.** About 15x forward earnings and C$931M of trailing net income screen cleaner than several construction peers.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Opaque terms.** With no disclosed price, dilution and return-on-capital math stay unclear until filings.
2. **No re-rating guarantee.** The stock is still down about 34% over the year alongside Stantec, and a tuck-in may not change sentiment.
3. **Closing and dilution.** The Q4 close still needs regulatory approval, and share consideration means existing holders fund part of the deal.

## What to watch

1. **Q4 2026 close.** Timing, and any disclosure of the final consideration and share count.
2. **Q3 results, expected in early November 2026.** Organic growth, margins and backlog.
3. **Canadian energy and industrial bookings** after integration.
4. **Analyst targets.** Whether the Street holds near C$284 or keeps trimming toward the share price.

## Bottom line

WSP has built a global engineering franchise of about 83,000 people, earns nearly as much profit as AtkinsRéalis and Stantec combined and trades at about 15x forward earnings after a 34% slide. The GCM Corpo deal adds a useful Canadian energy and industrial team, but it is too small to change the numbers, and its price is undisclosed. The issue is sentiment: the market has turned away from engineering consultancies even as analysts see about 58% upside. The next test is whether the November results show the earnings growth that the forward multiple assumes.
