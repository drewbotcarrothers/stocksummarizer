## The short version: AI's checkout rail, priced like it

Shopify (TSX: SHOP), the Canadian commerce platform behind millions of online and in-person stores, closed at **CA$205.30** on September 24, 2026, up about **14%** in a week from CA$180.01 on September 17. Over twelve months the stock is roughly flat (about **−2%**), but it has climbed about **29%** in three months. The new story is **agentic commerce**: Meta's new AI agent, Muse, now checks out with Shop Pay on every Shopify store, days after Google switched on native checkout for Shopify merchants in AI Mode and Gemini.

The business underneath is growing more than 30% a year with expanding cash margins. But at about **100x trailing earnings** and **70x forward**, the question for a new buyer is how much of the AI checkout story is already in the price. Below we go through the Meta deal, the second-quarter print behind it, and how Shopify compares with five Canadian tech names — Constellation Software, Descartes, OpenText, Kinaxis and Lightspeed — on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Meta's Muse checks out with Shop Pay

On **September 21, 2026**, Shopify and Meta announced a deep partnership around Muse, the personal AI agent Meta launched on September 8. The deal brings:

- **agentic checkout with Shop Pay on all Shopify stores** inside Muse, so a shopper can go from a question to a completed order without leaving the agent,
- a second big AI distribution channel in a week: **Google** began rolling out native checkout in **AI Mode and Gemini** for eligible Shopify merchants from September 18,
- a contrast with **Amazon**, which went the other way and started **blocking Muse**.

Deutsche Bank said the deal should ease fears that AI agents will route shoppers around Shopify. Earlier in the month, at Goldman Sachs Communacopia on **September 10**, CFO Jeff Hoffmeister said shoppers referred by large language models show roughly an **80% conversion uplift**. None of this has a disclosed revenue figure yet, so treat it as **distribution, not a number in the model**: Shopify gets placed inside the agents rather than being bypassed by them.

The rally also came off a low base. Early in September the stock sold off (September 8–9) on oil and Middle East worries, rate-hike fears, profit taking after an August rally, and pre-planned 10b5-1 share sales by CEO Tobi Lütke (Form 144 filed September 4).

### The Q2 print behind the story

The deal lands on top of a strong second quarter, reported **August 5, 2026** (US dollars):

- **Revenue US$3.58B, up 34%** year over year (33% in constant currency); subscription revenue up 22% and merchant solutions up 37%.
- **Gross merchandise volume US$115.6B, up 32%**, with Shopify Payments penetration at **68%** (from 64%).
- **Operating income US$488M, up 68%** — profit growing about twice as fast as revenue.
- **Free cash flow US$654M, an 18% margin**, up from 16% a year earlier.
- **Buybacks of US$1.42B** in the quarter.

The quarter also showed why the trailing P/E needs care. GAAP net income of **US$1.50B** included **US$1.06B** of mark-to-market gains on equity investments; excluding them, net income was about **US$439M**. For the third quarter, management guided revenue growth in the **low thirties**, gross profit growth in the mid-to-high twenties, and a free cash flow margin in the **high teens to low twenties**. That keeps the growth case intact, but it also sets a high bar for a stock priced at these multiples.

## Business overview: Shopify vs Canadian tech peers

Shopify sells the software and payments stack that merchants use to run online and in-person stores: subscriptions for the platform itself, plus merchant solutions such as Shop Pay, Shopify Payments, shipping and capital. Merchant solutions, the faster-growing part (up 37% in Q2 vs 22% for subscriptions), scale with the GMV flowing through the platform. That makes Shopify more cyclical than a pure subscription business — it rides consumer spending and small-merchant health — but it also means every new channel, including AI agents, can feed straight into revenue.

On the TSX screener, Shopify sits in the **Commercial services** sector, but its only large same-sector neighbour is CAE (aerospace training), and the rest are sub-C$1B names, so neither is a useful comparison. Instead the peer set is Canada's software and commerce names. **Constellation Software (CSU)** is the closest in revenue scale. **Descartes (DSG)** sells logistics and supply-chain software used by merchants and shippers. **OpenText (OTEX)** is a mature enterprise-software company. **Kinaxis (KXS)** sells supply-chain planning software, and **Lightspeed (LSPD)** is the most direct business comparable, selling point-of-sale and commerce software to retailers and restaurants.

## Why it matters: from AI victim to AI checkout rail

The old worry was Shopify as an **AI casualty**: if shopping agents answer questions and place orders, they could route shoppers around merchant storefronts and the platform behind them. The new narrative is Shopify as the **checkout rail for AI agents** — Shop Pay, its product catalogue and its merchant base plugged into Meta and Google rather than being bypassed by them.

That matters for both kinds of investor. Growth investors want proof that agent channels add measurable GMV. Valuation-minded investors want to know whether a 30%-plus grower can hold a P/E near 100 if rates stay high. There is no dividend, so returns depend entirely on growth and buybacks. The open question is whether agentic checkout becomes a real revenue line before the market loses patience with the multiple.

## Bull case

1. **Neutral checkout infrastructure.** Meta (Muse) and Google (AI Mode and Gemini) are plugging into Shop Pay, giving Shopify distribution inside AI agents without having to own one.
2. **Growth with operating leverage.** Revenue grew 34% in Q2 while operating income rose 68% and the free cash flow margin widened to 18% from 16%.
3. **Net-cash balance sheet.** About C$7.0B of cash and investments against roughly C$0.25B of debt funds buybacks (US$1.42B in Q2) and AI investment without leverage.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Priced for perfection.** About 100x trailing earnings, 70x forward and 82x free cash flow, with a beta near 2.6, leaves little room for a miss.
2. **Agentic commerce is unproven at scale.** Conversion and take rates from AI channels are early, and Amazon blocking Muse shows platforms can also shut agents out.
3. **Macro and sentiment.** Higher rates, oil and softer consumer spending can compress the multiple and slow small-merchant GMV; pre-planned CEO share sales and noisy GAAP earnings add pressure.

## What to watch

1. **Q3 results, expected in early November (date unconfirmed).** Does revenue growth hold in the low thirties, as guided?
2. **Free cash flow margin.** Whether it lands in the high-teens to low-twenties range management guided.
3. **Agent-channel disclosure.** Any early numbers on orders or GMV through Muse, Google AI Mode and other agents.
4. **Rates and consumer spending**, which drive the multiple — plus the buyback pace after US$1.42B in Q2.

## Bottom line

Shopify has earned its premium more than most: growth above 30%, operating income rising faster than revenue, an 18% free cash flow margin, and now a seat inside the AI agents that were supposed to threaten it. The Meta and Google integrations strengthen the long-term story without yet changing the numbers. The issue is price. At about 100x trailing earnings, 70x forward and roughly 4% above its small TSX consensus target, a great deal of good news is already in the stock. The next test is whether Q3 growth and cash margins hold up to the guide.
