## The short version: record high, growth multiple

Finning International (TSX: FTT), the world's largest Caterpillar dealer, closed at a record **CA$112.30** on October 2, 2026, after a run of about **73%** over twelve months and about **30%** in the last month alone. The market is no longer pricing Finning like a plain mining-and-construction dealer. The new story is **power**: rental generators, backup power for data centres and industry, and a record equipment backlog.

That shift is real. But at about **27x trailing earnings** and **22x forward**, the question for a new buyer is simple: how much of the power story is already in the price? Below we go through the new deal, the backlog behind it, and how Finning compares with its closest TSX peers — Toromont, Russel Metals and Wajax — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the John F Hunt Power deal

On **October 1, 2026**, Finning acquired **John F Hunt Power**, one of the UK's larger specialist generator rental companies. The deal brings:

- about **2,500** generator units,
- **6** depots and **145** employees, with a 24/7 nationwide service and a fuel-as-a-service offering,
- a business Finning expects to **more than triple** its power rental revenue in the UK and Ireland.

The purchase price wasn't disclosed. Finning funded it from existing liquidity and said it won't materially affect consolidated revenue, earnings or the balance sheet. So treat this as a **strategic step, not an earnings jolt**. It makes Finning a bigger player in UK temporary and backup power, a market where data-centre demand is strong even while UK construction is soft.

### The Q2 print behind the rally

The deal builds on a strong second quarter, reported **August 5, 2026**:

- **Revenue C$3.13B, up 20%** — Finning's first quarter above C$3 billion.
- **Record EPS of C$1.22**, up 21% from adjusted C$1.01 a year earlier.
- **Product support (parts and service) up 11%**, the ninth quarter in a row of year-over-year growth.
- **Adjusted ROIC of 19.0%.**
- A **record equipment backlog of C$3.8B**, up 22% since December 31. Power & energy was about **a third** of it, led by backup power demand including data centres; mining orders were also strong.

The weaker spots: free cash flow was only about **C$15M** in the quarter as working capital absorbed cash, and net debt sat at about **1.6x** adjusted EBITDA. Both matter for the valuation discussion below.

## Business overview: Finning vs distribution peers

Finning sells, rents and services Caterpillar equipment. Its biggest markets in the story above are copper mining in Chile, resource projects in Western Canada, the UK and Ireland, and Argentina, where government policy is a swing factor. The steady part of the business is product support — parts and service on a large installed fleet — which tends to hold up better than new equipment sales.

On the TSX screener, Finning sits in the **Distribution services** sector. The closest comparable is **Toromont (TIH)**, the Caterpillar dealer for Eastern Canada. **Russel Metals (RUS)** is an industrial and metals distributor and **Wajax (WJX)** is an industrial equipment dealer. Smaller or non-comparable names in the sector were left out.

## Why it matters: from mining dealer to power business

The old narrative was Finning as a **cyclical mining and construction dealer**, priced like one. The new narrative is a **power and energy business**, riding backup-generation demand (including data centres), copper mining in Chile and Western Canadian resource projects, with recurring parts and service revenue underneath.

The John F Hunt Power deal fits that new narrative. It isn't big enough to move earnings on its own, but it signals where management wants to grow. The open question is how long the market will keep paying a growth multiple for a dealer whose cash flow still swings with working capital.

## Bull case

1. **Visibility.** A record C$3.8B backlog, with orders outpacing deliveries, gives visibility into 2027.
2. **A new growth leg.** Power rentals and backup-power demand add growth that is less tied to the mining cycle, and the UK deal more than triples Finning's UK & Ireland rental revenue.
3. **Quality of earnings.** Product support has grown nine quarters in a row, and adjusted return on invested capital is about 19%.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Priced for growth.** At about 27x earnings after a 30% one-month jump, there is little room for a miss.
2. **Thin cash flow.** Free cash flow is thin while working capital grows (P/FCF ~69x), and net debt is about 1.6x EBITDA.
3. **Macro exposure.** Copper prices, Argentina policy and soft UK construction can all hit orders quickly.

## What to watch

1. **Q3 results, expected in early November.** Does the backlog turn into deliveries — and, more importantly, into cash?
2. **John F Hunt Power integration.** How fast it lifts UK power rental revenue.
3. **Data-centre power orders in Canada**, where Finning says talks are active.
4. **Analyst targets.** Whether the Street lifts its targets above the current share price, or the stock drifts back toward the ~C$118 consensus.

## Bottom line

Finning has earned its rerating: record revenue and EPS, a record backlog, a credible power-and-energy growth leg, and a steadily growing parts and service business. The John F Hunt Power deal adds to that story without changing the numbers. The issue is price. At ~27x trailing earnings, ~69x free cash flow and only about 5% below the consensus target, a lot of good news is already in the stock. The next test is whether Q3 turns that backlog into cash.
