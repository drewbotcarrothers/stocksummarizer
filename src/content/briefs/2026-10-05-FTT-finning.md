## What happened (news + tape)

On **October 1, 2026**, Finning — the world's largest Caterpillar dealer — acquired **John F Hunt Power**, one of the UK's larger specialist generator rental companies (~**2,500** generator units, **6** depots, **145** employees). Finning expects the deal to **more than triple** its UK & Ireland power rental revenue. It was funded from existing liquidity and is not expected to materially affect consolidated revenue, earnings or the balance sheet — a strategic step rather than an earnings jolt.

It builds on the **August 5, 2026** second-quarter print: revenue of **C$3.13B** (+**20%**), the first quarter above C$3B; record EPS of **C$1.22**; adjusted ROIC of **19.0%**; and a record equipment backlog of **C$3.8B**, with power & energy about a third of it, led by backup power demand including data centres.

The stock closed at a record **CA$112.30** on October 2 (TSX screener), up about **30%** in a month and **73%** in a year. Stifel initiated coverage at **Buy** with a **C$127** target on September 29. MarketBeat consensus is **C$117.80** from **10 analysts** — only about 5% above the share price. Screener rating: **Strong buy**.

## Bull / Bear / Watch

**Bull:** A record backlog with orders outpacing deliveries gives visibility into 2027; power rentals and backup-power demand add a growth leg less tied to the mining cycle; product support has grown nine quarters in a row and adjusted ROIC is about 19%.

**Bear:** At ~27x trailing earnings after a +30% month, there's little room for a miss; free cash flow is thin while working capital grows (P/FCF ~69x) and net debt is ~1.6x EBITDA; copper prices, Argentina policy and soft UK construction can hit orders quickly.

**Watch:** Q3 results (expected early November) — does the backlog turn into deliveries and cash? How fast John F Hunt Power lifts UK power rental revenue; data-centre power orders in Canada; whether analysts lift targets above the share price.
