## The short version: a good quarter with one broken network

TFI International (TSX: TFII), the Montreal-based trucking and logistics group that is Canada's largest trucking company, closed at **CA$157.13** on October 23, 2026, up about **19%** over twelve months. A week later it reported higher third-quarter profit, and the stock still fell **17%** in a single session, to **C$130.05** on October 30. Investors looked past the quarter to a **fourth-quarter outlook far below expectations** and to the U.S. less-than-truckload (LTL) business that TFI bought from UPS in 2021.

The question now is whether the drop has priced in a problem that management admits it has not solved. Below we go through the quarter, the outlook, the end of the UPS freight partnership, and how TFI compares with its TSX transport peers — CN Rail, CPKC, Exchange Income, Mullen Group and Cargojet — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 2026 results and a weak outlook

On **October 29, 2026**, after the market close, TFI reported its third-quarter results. TFI reports in U.S. dollars:

- **Revenue of US$2.23B, up 13%**, and revenue before fuel surcharge of US$1.86B, up 8%, helped by US$59.3M from acquisitions.
- **Net income of US$105.7M, up 25%**, or US$1.28 a diluted share, from US$1.02.
- **Adjusted EPS of US$1.67, up 39%** from US$1.20, but short of the roughly **US$1.74–1.76** analysts expected.
- **Free cash flow of US$116.4M**, down from US$199.4M a year earlier.
- A quarterly dividend of **US$0.47** a share, up 4% from a year ago.

The bigger shock was the outlook. TFI guided **fourth-quarter adjusted EPS to US$1.30–1.40**, against analyst estimates near **US$1.65**. It expects its Truckload operating ratio to improve by 400–500 basis points year over year and Logistics by 100–200, but its LTL operating ratio to get **0–100 basis points worse**.

### The U.S. LTL problem behind the drop

TFI has three main segments. **Truckload** had a strong quarter: operating income nearly doubled to **US$100.2M** and its operating ratio improved to 86.7%. **Logistics** operating income rose **59%** to US$49.4M, helped by freight for data centers and battery storage projects. The weak spot was **LTL**, where operating income fell **43%** to US$44.4M (including a US$14.4M non-cash impairment on an office building) and the operating ratio rose to 91.3% from 88.8%.

The operating ratio is costs divided by revenue, so lower is better. On the call, CEO Alain Bédard said the U.S. LTL network, TForce Freight, runs **closer to 97%**, while the Canadian LTL business is **under 75%**. He blamed thin route density, outside linehaul costs on the spot market and the network's culture, and said a new U.S. LTL leader, a retired executive from a peer, started on October 1. He also said TFI has **no intention of selling** the business.

TFI also confirmed that its six-year **freight partnership with UPS ends early in 2027**. RBC analyst Walter Spracklin put the lost revenue at about **US$300M** and said that "explains the market reaction." The stock fell **17.2%** on the TSX on October 30 and about 17% on the New York listing, on heavy volume, while the TSX itself fell 1.7%.

## Business overview: TFI International vs TSX transport peers

TFI is a holding company of trucking businesses across Canada, the U.S. and Mexico: package and courier delivery, LTL (shared loads with many stops), truckload (one customer per trailer, including specialized flatbed work) and asset-light logistics and brokerage. It has grown largely through acquisitions, including UPS Freight in 2021 and flatbed carrier Daseke in 2024.

On the TSX screener, TFI sits in the **Transportation** sector. **CN Rail (CNR)** and **CPKC (CP)** are the two Class I railways and TFI's main rivals for long-haul freight. **Exchange Income (EIF)** owns aviation and manufacturing businesses. **Mullen Group (MTL)** is the closest Canadian trucking and logistics peer, and **Cargojet (CJT)** is the overnight air cargo carrier. Air Canada and Westshore Terminals are left out as an airline and a coal port.

The screener rates TFI a **Strong buy**, but only four analysts are in the TSX-listing consensus, so the broader **U.S.-listing consensus** is more useful: an average target of about **US$167.94** from **19 analysts** (14 Buy, 5 Hold), with targets from US$130 to US$263. After the call, RBC's Spracklin wrote that "inadequate pricing and lack of density" in U.S. LTL "do not in our view sound like easy fixes," while Desjardins analyst Benoit Poirier said LTL weakness "overshadowed solid execution" in truckload and logistics. More target cuts are likely in the coming days.

## Why it matters: from fixer to turnaround

The old narrative was TFI as **a disciplined buyer that fixes every business it touches**, priced as one of the best operators in North American trucking. The new narrative is **a U.S. turnaround it has not cracked**: five years after buying UPS Freight, the U.S. LTL network is still close to break-even, and the CEO says the problem is people and culture rather than trucks or systems.

That shifts what drives the stock. The trucking cycle and the strong truckload and logistics units still matter, but the next leg depends on a new U.S. LTL leader, a slow build of a non-union network through Hercules Forwarding, and how much freight TFI keeps when the UPS partnership ends.

## Bull case

1. **Truckload and logistics are booming.** Truckload operating income nearly doubled to US$100.2M and logistics rose 59%, with demand from data center and battery storage projects. Management expects both to improve again in the fourth quarter.
2. **The drop made it cheap.** At about 13x forward earnings, TFI trades at the lowest multiple in the group and well below the railways, so even trimmed estimates could leave it reasonably valued.
3. **Cash keeps flowing.** Year-to-date free cash flow was US$442.2M, TFI bought back shares in October, raised its dividend 4%, and keeps funded debt at about 2.1x EBITDA.

## Bear case and risks

1. **The U.S. fix is people and culture.** Bédard described the U.S. LTL network as "a rock in my shoe," and changing a network's culture usually takes years, not quarters.
2. **The UPS partnership ends in early 2027.** About US$300M of revenue is at risk, and some customers may move to other carriers during the transition.
3. **Costs keep rising.** Driver wages and outside linehaul costs are climbing, and the fourth-quarter outlook already points to lower earnings than the third quarter.

## What to watch

1. **Analyst target cuts.** How far the US$167.94 average target falls once brokers update for the outlook.
2. **The new U.S. LTL leader.** TFI has not named the executive yet; the first changes to pricing, density and linehaul will show whether the plan is working.
3. **Fourth-quarter results, expected in February.** Whether TFI lands inside its US$1.30–1.40 adjusted EPS range, and what it says about 2027.
4. **UPS freight after 2027.** How much of the partnership's volume TFI can keep or replace.

## Bottom line

TFI International has earned its reputation: truckload and logistics are growing fast, cash flow funds buybacks and a rising dividend, and the stock now trades at about **13x forward earnings** after a **17%** one-day drop. The issue is the U.S. LTL network it bought from UPS, which runs near break-even five years later and is about to lose its UPS freight partnership. A fourth-quarter outlook of **US$1.30–1.40** a share, about 18% below expectations, shows how much that weighs on the whole company. The next test is whether a new U.S. leader can move the operating ratio before the UPS volume goes away in 2027.
