## The short version: the strike is over, the recovery is not in the numbers yet

Metro Inc. (TSX: MRU) runs grocery stores (Metro, Super C, Food Basics) and pharmacies (Jean Coutu, Brunet) across Quebec and Ontario. For six months, a strike at its **produce distribution centre in Laval, Quebec** disrupted fresh produce in its home market, and shoppers went elsewhere. The strike ended on **September 24, 2026**, two days before the fiscal year closed, and Metro estimates it cost about **C$97M after tax** for the year.

On **November 18, 2026**, Metro reported fourth-quarter results: **adjusted EPS fell 14% to C$0.97** and **food same-store sales fell 1.9%**. The shares fell **3.3% to C$97.58**, a day after touching a 52-week high. Below we go through the quarter, how Metro compares with Loblaw, Empire, George Weston, Couche-Tard, Dollarama and North West on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets, and what has to go right from here.

## What happened: Q4 fiscal 2026 (November 18)

Metro reported for the **12 weeks ended September 26, 2026**, its fiscal fourth quarter. It was also the first earnings call for **Marc Giroux**, who took over as chief executive on September 27.

### The Q4 print behind the drop

- **Sales of C$5,179.5M, up 1.4%**, helped by new stores but held back by the strike in Quebec.
- **Food same-store sales down 1.9%** (a year earlier: up 1.6%). Online food sales rose 3.3%, and Metro's food basket inflation ran below the 2.4% food-from-stores CPI.
- **Pharmacy same-store sales up 5.5%**, with prescription drugs up 6.5% and front-store sales up 3.1%.
- **Gross margin of 19.2%**, down from 20.0%, with about C$40M of lost profit and direct strike costs in gross profit.
- **Net earnings of C$201.9M (C$0.97 a share), down 7.0%**, and **adjusted EPS of C$0.97 vs C$1.13**, down 14.2%.
- **Strike impact (Metro's estimate, not adjusted out of results): C$31M after tax, or 16¢ a share, in the quarter; C$97M, or 48¢ a share, for fiscal 2026.**
- **C$164.7M of share buybacks** in the quarter, and four discount stores opened, including one conversion.

For the full fiscal year, sales rose **2.5% to C$22.55B** and adjusted EPS fell **6.1% to C$4.48**.

### Outlook and the stock reaction

Metro says the Laval centre is back to normal service and the priority is **winning back customer traffic**, with more marketing and promotions. **Four weeks into fiscal 2027, food same-store sales are still negative but improving.** It plans seven new discount stores this year and nine more Metro-to-Food Basics conversions in Ontario (ten in total, the first completed in Q4), plus three new pharmacies and 31 renovations.

The stock had climbed into the print, touching a **52-week intraday high of C$101.62 on November 17**. On November 18 it closed **down 3.31% at C$97.58**, on a day the S&P/TSX Composite fell 2.09%.

## Why it matters: from a strike story to a share-recovery story

The old narrative was **a steady grocer disrupted by a long strike**. The new narrative is **a grocer that has to win back shoppers it lost**, under a new chief executive, while budgets are tight and shoppers hunt for deals. The strike hit Metro's home market of Quebec, where some customers found new stores during six months of thin produce shelves.

That is why Metro is leaning on **discount banners and pharmacy**. Super C and Food Basics fit value-focused shoppers, and pharmacy keeps growing faster than food. The next few quarters will show whether lost traffic returns, and how much margin Metro gives up in promotions to bring it back.

## Bull case

1. **The strike is over.** Next year compares against a weak, strike-hit base, and the Laval centre is back to normal service.
2. **Earnings rebound expected.** Analysts expect fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS of about C$5.32, up about 19% from C$4.48.
3. **Pharmacy and discount.** Pharmacy same-store sales grew 5.5%, and ten Ontario stores are moving to the Food Basics banner.
4. **Valuation and buybacks.** The P/E is below the group average, and buybacks keep shrinking the share count.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Lost shoppers.** Food same-store sales are still negative four weeks into the new year, and some customers may not return.
2. **Competition.** Loblaw, Empire, Dollarama and Walmart are all chasing value-focused shoppers.
3. **Margins.** Heavier promotions to win customers back could squeeze gross margin further.
4. **Modest yield.** At about 1.6%, returns lean on earnings growth more than income.

## What to watch

1. **Q1 fiscal 2027 results** in late January, and whether food same-store sales turn positive.
2. **Gross margin** as promotions ramp up.
3. **The buyback renewal**, since the current bid expires on November 26.
4. **Discount openings and Ontario conversions**, and the dividend increase Metro has typically announced with Q1.

## Bottom line

The strike cost Metro about C$97M after tax and pushed fourth-quarter adjusted EPS down 14%, but it is now over, and the company is already seeing improvement in food same-store sales, even if they are still negative. At about 19.6 times forward earnings, below the group average, and about 5% below the average target, the market is pricing a recovery but not a quick one. The strike is behind Metro, but the recovery still has to show up in the numbers.
