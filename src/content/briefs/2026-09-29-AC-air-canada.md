## The short version: a big buyback, a weak reaction

Air Canada (TSX: AC), Canada's largest airline, closed at **CA$28.96** on the September 24, 2026 screener print, up about **56%** over twelve months but down about **1.5%** over the last month. On **September 28**, it completed a **C$800M** substantial issuer bid, retiring about **9.8%** of its shares at **C$29**. The Dutch auction cleared at the bottom of its range, and the stock sold off to about **C$26.15** after the preliminary results. The new story is **capital return**, funded by selling a minority slice of Aeroplan.

On the screener, Air Canada trades at about **20x trailing earnings** and only **0.33x sales**, the cheapest sales multiple in its peer group. The question for a new buyer is whether C$29 was a bargain price for the company to buy back its own stock, or a warning that the market sees less value. Below we go through the bid, its funding, and how Air Canada compares with CPKC, CN, TFI International, Exchange Income and Chorus Aviation on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: the C$800M issuer bid completes

On **September 28, 2026**, Air Canada said it had taken up and paid for shares under its **C$800M substantial issuer bid**, a modified Dutch auction with a price range of **C$29–C$33**. The terms:

- **27,586,206** Class A and Class B shares bought at **C$29.00**, the bottom of the range.
- That is about **9.8%** of the shares outstanding as of September 24, leaving about **252.7M** shares.
- About **66.8M** shares were tendered, so the offer was **oversubscribed**, and about **41%** of successfully tendered shares were bought (odd lots excluded).
- Settlement by TSX Trust is expected **on or before October 2, 2026**.

Retiring nearly a tenth of the company in one step is a large move for an airline. Management says it brings the **share count back below pre-pandemic levels**. Because the shares were bought at the low end of the range, Air Canada got more shares for its C$800M than it would have at a higher clearing price. For holders who did not tender, the math is simple: **each remaining share owns a bigger slice** of future earnings, as long as those earnings hold up.

### The Aeroplan funding and the market's verdict

The bid was funded partly with proceeds from a **minority equity investment in Aeroplan**, Air Canada's loyalty program, by funds managed by **Blackstone** and **La Caisse**, alongside other Canadian institutions. That matters: the buyback was paid for by **monetizing a slice of a valuable asset**, not by draining operating cash or adding debt.

The market's reaction was cooler. The preliminary results on **September 25** showed the auction would clear at C$29, the floor of the range, and the stock slid. By the **September 28 close**, it traded near **C$26.15**, about 10% below the screener price used in the tables and below the price Air Canada had just paid. The tables below keep the September 24 screener values, but the post-bid price is the more relevant starting point for anyone looking at the stock afterward.

## Business overview: Air Canada vs transport peers

Air Canada flies domestic, U.S. and international routes from hubs in Toronto, Montréal, Vancouver and Calgary, runs a leisure airline (Air Canada Rouge) and a cargo business, and controls Aeroplan, one of Canada's largest loyalty programs. Loyalty and premium cabins are the steadier parts of revenue; fuel, labour and travel demand make airline earnings among the most cyclical on the TSX.

On the TSX screener, Air Canada sits in the **Transportation** sector. The peers are the liquid names in the same sector: the two railways, **CPKC (CP)** and **CN Railway (CNR)**; trucking and logistics company **TFI International (TFII)**; **Exchange Income (EIF)**, which owns regional airlines and aviation services alongside manufacturing; and **Chorus Aviation (CHR)**, a regional aviation and leasing company. Chorus is included as an aviation peer despite being under C$1B in market value. CAE was left out because the screener classifies it in Commercial services.

## Why it matters: from pandemic rebuild to capital return

The old narrative was Air Canada as **a leveraged airline rebuilding after the pandemic**. The new narrative is **capital return**: an oversubscribed issuer bid funded by monetizing a slice of Aeroplan, with the share count back below pre-pandemic levels.

Value investors care whether C$29 was a gift or a warning that the clearing price sat at the floor. Growth investors care whether the New Frontiers strategy and premium demand can thicken a thin net-income print on about C$24B of revenue. The open question is whether earnings per share actually rise now that there are fewer shares.

## Bull case

1. **Per-share accretion.** Air Canada retired nearly 10% of its shares at C$29 in an oversubscribed offer; if earnings hold, earnings per share rise.
2. **Smart funding.** The cash came from the Aeroplan minority stake sold to Blackstone and La Caisse funds, not from a stretched balance sheet.
3. **Cheap screen, bullish Street.** Targets cluster in the mid-to-high C$30s, and at 0.33x sales Air Canada is the clear cheap name among transport peers.

## Bear case and risks

1. **No floor at C$29.** The auction cleared at the bottom of the range and the stock fell toward C$26, so the market is not treating C$29 as support.
2. **Thin margins.** Net income of about C$430M on about C$24B of revenue leaves little room; fuel, labour and demand swings can erase the buyback benefit quickly.
3. **No dividend.** Income buyers get nothing while they wait; the thesis rides on earnings power and a recovery in the multiple.

## What to watch

1. **Settlement on or before October 2.** The final share count should land near 253M.
2. **Q3 results, expected in late October.** Whether strong revenue finally shows up in net income.
3. **Aeroplan milestones.** How the loyalty partnership develops after the minority sale.
4. **Fuel costs and travel mix into winter**, especially leisure versus business demand, and whether the stock moves back toward the ~C$35 consensus.

## Bottom line

Air Canada has used the value inside Aeroplan to shrink its share count by about 10% in one transaction, bringing it back below pre-pandemic levels. On the screener it is the cheapest name in its transport group on sales, and the Street sees about 20% upside even from the pre-bid price. The issue is earnings quality: margins under 2% and a forward P/E above the trailing one suggest profits may soften, and the market's selloff after the auction shows doubt. The next test is whether the late-October results turn about C$24B of revenue into per-share earnings growth.
