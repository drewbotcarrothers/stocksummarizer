## What happened (news + tape)

On **September 28, 2026**, Air Canada completed its **C$800 million** substantial issuer bid:

- Took up **27,586,206** Class A/B shares at **C$29.00** — about **9.8%** of shares outstanding as of September 24, leaving about **252.7 million** shares.
- About **66.8 million** shares were tendered; the offer was **oversubscribed** with roughly **41%** proration (odd lots excluded).
- Settlement by TSX Trust is expected on or before **October 2, 2026**.
- Funded partly with proceeds from the minority equity investment in **Aeroplan** by funds managed by **Blackstone** and **La Caisse**.
- The Dutch auction cleared at the **bottom** of the **$29–$33** range. After the preliminary results, the tape slid — **September 28 close near CA$26.15**.

Street notes (Aug–Sep 2026) include Scotiabank Sector Outperform (target lifts toward **C$37.50**), BMO Outperform **C$37**, Stifel Buy **C$38**, CIBC **C$40**, and Jefferies Hold **C$31**. Consensus from **15 analysts** sits near **C$34.89** (S&P Global via stockanalysis, pulled September 29, 2026).

## Why it matters

The old story: a leveraged airline rebuilding after the pandemic. The new story: capital return — an oversubscribed issuer bid funded by monetizing a slice of Aeroplan — with the share count below pre-pandemic levels. Value investors care whether $29 was a gift or a warning that the clearing price sat at the floor. Growth investors care whether New Frontiers and premium demand can thicken a thin net-income print on ~$24 billion of revenue.

## Bull case

1. **Retired ~10% of the float at $29** in an oversubscribed offer — classic per-share accretion math if earnings hold.
2. Funding came from the **Aeroplan minority stake** (Blackstone / La Caisse), not a stressed operating-cash scramble.
3. Street targets still cluster in the **mid-to-high thirties**, and on price to sales Air Canada is the clear cheap screen versus transport peers.

## Bear case

1. The Dutch auction cleared at the **bottom of the range**, and the stock sold off toward **~$26** — the market is not treating $29 as a floor.
2. Last-twelve-months net income is only about **$430 million** on **~$24 billion** of revenue — airline cyclicality, fuel, and labour can erase the buyback benefit fast.
3. **No dividend** for income buyers — the thesis rides on earnings power and multiple recovery.

## What to watch next

1. Settlement on or before **October 2** and the final share count near **253 million**.
2. The next earnings print — Street is watching **late October** — for whether revenue strength shows up in net income.
3. Aeroplan partnership milestones after the minority sale.
4. Fuel costs and leisure versus business mix into winter.
