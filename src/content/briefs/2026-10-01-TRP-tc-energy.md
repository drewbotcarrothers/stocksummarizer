## The short version: recycling a mature Mexico asset

TC Energy (TSX: TRP), one of North America's largest natural gas pipeline operators, closed at **CA$84.26** on the September 24, 2026 screener print, up about **14.5%** over twelve months but down about **15%** over three months. On **September 21**, it agreed to sell its **Guadalajara–Manzanillo** gas pipeline in Mexico to ESENTIA affiliates for about **C$560M (US$400M)**. The new story is **capital recycling**: selling a mature asset to fund higher-return growth across North America.

At about **25x trailing earnings** and **22x forward**, TC Energy trades near its peer average, with a **4.2%** indicated yield, the lowest in its group. The question for a new buyer is whether a deal this small changes anything, and whether Street targets near **C$100** still have room. Below we go through the sale, what TC Energy keeps in Mexico, and how it compares with Enbridge, Pembina, Keyera and South Bow on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the Guadalajara–Manzanillo sale

On **September 21, 2026**, TC Energy announced an agreement to sell **Energía Occidente de México (EOM)**, the entity that owns the Guadalajara–Manzanillo natural gas pipeline, to affiliates of **ESENTIA Energy Development**. The terms:

- Gross purchase price of about **C$560M (US$400M)**.
- The pipeline runs **313 km** and moves up to **500 MMcf/d** of gas.
- Closing is anticipated in the **first half of 2027**, subject to customary conditions, regulatory approvals and consents.
- CEO **François Poirier** framed it as **portfolio optimization**: cash from a mature asset to fund high-value growth across North America.

The sale is **small relative to the company**. At about C$560M, it is roughly **0.7%** of TC Energy's market value. It doesn't change the earnings picture or the dividend in any meaningful way; its value is in **what the cash is redeployed into** and in the signal that management is willing to sell mature assets rather than issue shares to fund growth.

### The asset, and what TC Energy keeps in Mexico

The Guadalajara–Manzanillo line connects imported LNG near the port of Manzanillo and continental gas near Guadalajara to **power and industrial customers in Colima and Jalisco**. It is a mature, contracted lateral: steady, but not a growth asset.

After closing, TC Energy still owns and operates about **3,300 km** of pipelines in Mexico and about **8.7 Bcf/d** of installed natural gas transportation capacity. So Mexico remains a core part of the portfolio. The timing risk is regulatory: the close is not expected until the first half of 2027, and Mexican approvals and consents can slip, which would delay the cash.

## Business overview: TC Energy vs midstream peers

TC Energy runs natural gas transmission pipelines across Canada, the U.S. and Mexico, owns gas storage, and has a power business that includes a stake in Ontario's Bruce Power nuclear plant. Most of its cash flow comes from **regulated and long-term contracted pipelines**, which makes it more utility-like than commodity-driven. Since spinning off its liquids pipelines as South Bow, it has been a **pure natural gas and power company**.

On the TSX screener, TC Energy sits in the **Industrial services** sector. The peers are the liquid midstream names in the same sector: **Enbridge (ENB)**, the largest pipeline company, with crude, gas and utilities; **Pembina (PPL)**, a Western Canadian pipeline and processing operator; **Keyera (KEY)**, a gas processing and liquids business; and **South Bow (SOBO)**, the liquids pipeline company spun out of TC Energy. Waste Connections was left out as not a midstream business.

## Why it matters: from steady gas midstream to capital recycler

The old narrative was TC Energy as **a steady Canadian gas midstream with Mexico exposure and a mid-four percent yield**. The new narrative asks whether **selling mature assets** frees capital for higher-return North American projects without shrinking the core franchise.

Income investors care that the indicated yield still clears 4% after the three-month slide. Growth investors care whether redeployment shows up in the next growth slate before the first-half 2027 close. The open question is whether more asset sales follow, and whether they are big enough to matter.

## Bull case

1. **Cash to redeploy.** About C$560M can go into accretive North American growth, while TC Energy keeps about 3,300 km and 8.7 Bcf/d in Mexico.
2. **Street support.** Targets cluster near C$100, with Scotiabank at C$105 and Morgan Stanley at C$108.
3. **Steadier record.** A 4.2% yield and a 14.5% one-year gain look steadier than Enbridge's flat year after its own deal week.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Too small to matter.** At about 0.7% of market value, the sale may not re-rate the stock.
2. **Weak tape and payout optics.** The stock is down about 15% over three months, and the trailing GAAP payout prints above 100%.
3. **Timing and valuation.** The close isn't expected until 2027, with Mexican regulatory and consent risk, and price to sales near 5.5x is the richest in the group.

## What to watch

1. **First-half 2027 close.** Timing, and where the proceeds are redeployed.
2. **Q3 results, expected in early November 2026.** Updates on the capital program and leverage.
3. **Leverage and the dividend growth path** after the sale.
4. **Follow-on deals.** Any further Mexico or Canadian asset sales or growth announcements, and whether the stock closes the gap to the ~C$100 consensus.

## Bottom line

TC Energy has earned its reputation as a disciplined gas pipeline operator: high margins, a solid one-year record and a Street that sees about 18% upside. The Guadalajara–Manzanillo sale fits a sensible strategy of recycling mature assets into growth, while leaving most of the Mexico network in place. The issue is scale: at about 0.7% of market value, the deal is a signal, not a catalyst, and the stock still trades near its peers on earnings with the lowest yield in the group. The next test is where the proceeds go, and whether the November results show growth that justifies the targets.
