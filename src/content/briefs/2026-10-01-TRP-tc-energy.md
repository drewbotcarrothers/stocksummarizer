## What happened (news + tape)

On **September 21, 2026**, TC Energy announced an agreement to sell **Energía Occidente de México (EOM)** — the entity that owns the **313-kilometre Guadalajara–Manzanillo** natural gas pipeline — to affiliates of **ESENTIA Energy Development** for a gross purchase price of about **C$560 million (US$400 million)**:

- The line transports up to **500 MMcf/d**, connecting imported LNG near Manzanillo and continental gas near Guadalajara to power and industrial customers in Colima and Jalisco.
- Close is anticipated in **H1 2027**, subject to customary conditions, regulatory approvals and consents.
- CEO **François Poirier** framed the deal as portfolio optimization — redeploying proceeds from a mature asset toward high-value growth across North America.
- After closing, TC Energy still owns and operates about **3,300 km** of Mexico pipelines and about **8.7 Bcf/d** of installed natural gas transportation capacity.

Recent Street notes stay constructive: Scotiabank Buy at **C$105**, TD Cowen Buy upgrade at **C$102**, BMO Buy at **C$99**, Raymond James Hold at **C$92**, and Morgan Stanley Overweight at **C$108**. Consensus from **24 analysts** sits near **C$99.57** (S&P Global via stockanalysis, pulled October 1, 2026). The September 30 close was **CA$82.39**, about **2.2%** soft versus the screener print of **CA$84.26**.

## Why it matters

The old story: a steady Canadian gas midstream with Mexico exposure and a mid-four percent yield. The new story asks whether selling a mature Mexico lateral for half a billion frees capital for higher-return North American projects without shrinking the core franchise. Income investors care that the indicated yield still clears four percent after the three-month slide. Growth investors care whether redeployment shows up in the next growth slate before the H1 2027 close.

## Bull case

1. **C$560M of cash to redeploy** into accretive North American growth, while Mexico still keeps about **3,300 km** and **8.7 Bcf/d**.
2. Street targets cluster near **C$100**, with Scotiabank at **C$105** and Morgan Stanley at **C$108**.
3. A **~4.2%** indicated yield with a **~14.5%** one-year gain still looks steadier than Enbridge’s roughly flat year after its own deal week.

## Bear case

1. At roughly **0.7% of market cap**, the deal may be too small to re-rate the stock.
2. The three-month tape is still down about **15%** with Enbridge, and trailing GAAP payout prints over **100%**.
3. Close slips to **2027** with Mexico regulatory and consent risk, and price to sales near **5.5x** is the richest in this set.

## What to watch next

1. **H1 2027** close timing and where proceeds are redeployed.
2. The next earnings print (stockanalysis flags early **November 2026**).
3. Leverage and the dividend growth path after the sale.
4. Any follow-on Mexico or Canadian growth announcements.
