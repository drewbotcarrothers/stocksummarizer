## The short version: a guide cut, then a 19% wipe

Gildan Activewear (TSX/NYSE: GIL), the Montreal-based maker of blank tees, underwear and socks — now combined with HanesBrands — **missed its own third-quarter sales target and cut full-year 2026 guidance hard**. Motley Fool Canada reported the shares **crashed nearly 19% to about C$49.04** on the November 5–6 session after the release.

On the November 3 TSX screener (still pre-crash), Gildan closed at **CA$57.27**, with a market cap of about **CA$10.6 billion**, down roughly **31% over twelve months** and **4.5% over the prior month**. Trailing earnings look meaningless after Hanes purchase accounting (about **78x** on thin GAAP EPS); **forward P/E is about 8x**, the cheapest reading in our peer set. Indicated dividend yield is about **2.2%**. So the question is whether the new, lower earnings power is already in the price — or whether soft apparel demand keeps the multiple cheap. Below we go through the print, the guide cut, and how Gildan compares with Canada Goose, Saputo, Lassonde, Aritzia and Dollarama on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 miss and a sharp FY guide cut

Gildan reported on **November 5, 2026**, for the quarter ended September 27. Results are in **U.S. dollars** and on a continuing-operations basis (Hanes Australia was held for sale and closed October 26).

Headline numbers:

- **Net sales US$1.47 billion**, up **61.1%** year over year on the Hanes deal, but well short of prior guide of about **US$1.65 billion**,
- **Adjusted diluted EPS US$1.54** (+54%); **GAAP diluted EPS from continuing ops US$1.37**,
- **Adjusted operating margin 27.0%** (ahead of the ~26% guide for the quarter),
- **Free cash flow US$606 million** in Q3 and about **US$622 million** year to date.

Management pointed to soft demand in both **Wholesale and Retail** — about **US$100 million** of greater-than-expected demand softness versus the prior outlook — plus product-availability and service constraints (mainly Retail), mix, and lower net selling prices. Versus Hanes-inclusive pro forma prior-year sales, both channels were down.

### FY26 outlook cut and long-term roadmap withdrawn

Full-year 2026 guidance was reset:

| Metric | Prior guide | New guide |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Revenue | US$6.0–6.2B | **US$5.4–5.5B** |
| Adj. diluted EPS | US$4.65–4.75 | **US$3.70–3.80** |
| Free cash flow | ~US$1.0B | **US$700–750M** |
| Adj. operating margin | ~21.8% | **~20%** |

At the midpoints, the **adj EPS guide is down about 20%**. Gildan also **withdrew its previously communicated 2026–2028 objectives** and said it expects to provide an updated long-term outlook at an **Investor Day in spring 2027**.

Not everything moved the wrong way. Hanes synergies of about **US$100 million in 2026** remain on track, with a **US$250 million** annual run-rate targeted by the end of 2028. The **Hanes Australia** sale closed October 26 for about **A$700 million / US$490 million**, with proceeds used to repay debt. Net debt was about **US$4.14 billion** (leverage **2.7x**). A new **NCIB** for up to **5%** of shares (~9.26 million) starts **November 9, 2026**, and the board declared a quarterly dividend of **US$0.249** (record November 25, pay December 21).

## Business overview: Gildan vs consumer peers

Gildan manufactures everyday basic apparel — activewear, underwear, socks and intimates — sold through wholesale distributors, screenprinters and retailers, now including Hanes brand strength in Retail. On the TSX screener it sits in **Consumer non-durables**.

Liquid same-sector apparel names are thin: **Canada Goose (GOOS)** is the other apparel peer of meaningful size. We also include **Saputo (SAP)** and **Lassonde (LAS.A)** as liquid same-sector Consumer non-durables names (food/beverage — not apparel; size and valuation context). Closest-sector exception: **Aritzia (ATZ)** and **Dollarama (DOL)** from **Retail trade**, for apparel and discretionary consumer comps. Tiny same-sector names were left out.

Gildan is mid-cap versus Dollarama and Aritzia. Trailing net income is thin (~C$84 million) after Hanes accounting — which is why we lean on **forward** multiples rather than trailing P/E.

## Performance: apparel fade vs retail strength

Over twelve months Gildan (−31%) and Goose (−40%) lag badly, while Aritzia (+56%) and Dollarama (+17%) lead. Saputo and Lassonde were both up low-to-mid twenties. The one-month screen (still pre-crash) shows Gildan −4.5% versus Aritzia +24% and Dollarama +16%. The long fade looks **apparel-specific**, not a broad consumer wipeout — and the November 5–6 crash sits on top of that fade.

## Valuation: cheapest on forward earnings

Forward P/E of about **8x** is the lowest in the set (group average near **21x**). Trailing P/E is blanked as not meaningful. On sales Gildan is about **1.6x** — richer than Goose and Saputo, far cheaper than Aritzia or Dollarama. P/FCF ~15x sits mid-pack. Book value ~2.2x lines up with Goose and Saputo, nowhere near Aritzia (~11x) or Dollarama (~39x). After the guide cut, the forward multiple is the cheap side of this peer set — **if** the new guidance holds.

## Dividends: the income edge vs non-payers

Gildan’s indicated yield of about **2.2%** (≈C$1.27 annual DPS on the screen) is the income edge versus Goose and Aritzia, which pay nothing, and well above Dollarama’s token yield. Saputo (~2.0%) and Lassonde (~1.9%) are close peers on yield. The payout ratio on thin GAAP earnings looks elevated, so treat that print carefully — the cash dividend itself was just reaffirmed at **US$0.249** a quarter.

## Analyst ratings: street resetting after the print

The screener still shows a **Strong buy** rating, but named houses cut targets on **November 6**. The mean of five fresh cuts (TD, RBC, Stifel Buys; Scotia and UBS Holds) is about **C$65**. That still sits above the crash tape near C$49, but well below the older S&P consensus (~C$104 from 12 analysts) that had not fully refreshed as of November 7. In this peer set, Aritzia shows the most room to its average target; Dollarama’s average target sits near the current price.

## Why it matters

Gildan’s story used to be a clean blank-apparel compounder. Now it is a **post-Hanes integration story** with soft apparel demand, a cut guide, and **no long-term roadmap until spring 2027**. The crash asks whether the new, lower earnings power is already priced at about **8x forward earnings**.

## Bull case

1. Hanes synergies of about US$100 million this year are on track, with a US$250 million run-rate by 2028.
2. Free cash flow was still strong in the quarter; the Australia sale cuts debt; buybacks are restarting under a new NCIB.
3. After a ~19% wipe, the forward multiple is the cheapest in this peer set, and fresh targets still sit above the crash price.

## Bear case

1. Soft demand hit both Wholesale and Retail, and management expects that softness to persist into the fourth quarter.
2. Full-year free cash flow guidance was cut by roughly US$250–300 million, and the long-term roadmap was withdrawn.
3. Leverage is about 2.7x after the deal — above the company’s own 1.5–2.5 target band — until debt comes down. Apparel multiples can stay cheap if spending on basics keeps fading.

## What to watch

1. Fourth-quarter demand and whether Wholesale and Retail stabilize.
2. Delivery of the US$100 million of 2026 Hanes synergies.
3. Buyback pace under the new NCIB and the path back inside the leverage target.
4. The spring 2027 Investor Day — the next real long-term roadmap.
