## The short version: a downgrade for the cheapest name in the group

Canadian National Railway (TSX: CNR), Canada's biggest railway, was **CA$164.99** in our October 8 table and closed Monday, **October 19**, at **C$169.63**, down 1.6% on the day. Over the twelve months to October 8 the shares were up about **22%**, though they had slipped about **3%** in the month before. On October 19, **Raymond James downgraded CN to Market Perform**, arguing that its traffic is cooling just as rival CPKC speeds up.

The peer tables tell a more balanced story. At about **21x trailing earnings**, CN has the **lowest P/E in its TSX transport peer group**, where the average is near **26x** and CPKC sits near **28x**. It also books the most revenue and profit in the group. The question for a new buyer is whether a cheaper multiple is enough while growth slows. Below we go through the downgrade, the traffic numbers behind it, the second-quarter print, and how CN compares with CPKC, TFI International, Mullen Group and Cargojet on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Raymond James steps back as the Q4 handoff weakens

On **October 19, 2026**, Raymond James analyst **Steve Hansen** cut CN to **Market Perform from Outperform** and trimmed his target to **C$195 from C$200**. His case was about momentum, not quality:

- **Q4 has started slowly.** In the first two weeks of the fourth quarter, CN's **revenue ton miles fell 4.7%** from a year earlier, while **CPKC's rose 1.2%**.
- **CPKC is back in front.** Third-quarter traffic grew **3.8% at CN** but **6.1% at CPKC**, which retook the growth lead for the first time in a year.
- **Grain is fading as the engine.** CN moved a record **7.94 million tonnes** of western grain in the third quarter (its previous Q3 record was 7.48 million in 2020), but grain comparisons get much tougher from here. **Intermodal and forest products** are still weak.
- **Fuel is a near-term drag.** A sharp jump in **diesel prices in September** weighs on near-term earnings until fuel surcharges catch up.

None of this changes CN's long-term franchise, and Hansen's new target is still well above the stock price. What it changes is the **near-term story**: a railway that led on growth for most of 2026 now has to show what replaces grain. CN reports third-quarter results on **October 30, 2026**, before the market opens, with a call at 8:30 a.m. ET.

### The Q2 print behind the outlook

The last full report came on **July 24, 2026**, and it was a solid quarter:

- **Revenue** rose **11%** to **C$4.75B**.
- **Diluted EPS** was **C$2.06** (+10%) and **adjusted diluted EPS** was **C$2.08** (+11%).
- CN **raised its 2026 outlook** to **low single-digit growth in revenue ton miles** and **mid-to-high single-digit growth in adjusted EPS**, with capital spending of about **C$2.8B**.
- Free cash flow for the first half was **C$1.84B**, up 19%, and CN **bought back C$1.32B of stock** in the first half (C$454M in the second quarter).

The weak spots were costs. **Fuel expense jumped about 60%** to C$659M as the fuel price rose to C$5.67 a gallon, and the **operating ratio worsened by 80 basis points to 62.5%**. Adjusted debt was about **2.6x adjusted EBITDA**. Those numbers matter for the valuation question below: CN's earnings are growing, but less of each revenue dollar is reaching the bottom line.

## Business overview: CN vs TSX transport peers

CN runs a coast-to-coast network across Canada and down the middle of the United States to the Gulf of Mexico. It carries grain, fertilizer, coal, energy products, forest products, automobiles and containers. Some of that is steady (bulk commodities on long-term contracts), and some is cyclical (intermodal containers and forest products follow trade and housing).

The screener puts CN in the **Transportation** sector. The natural comparison is **CPKC**, the only other Class I railway on the TSX. We added the three largest freight and logistics names: **TFI International** (trucking and less-than-truckload), **Mullen Group** (trucking and logistics) and **Cargojet** (air cargo). We left out Air Canada and Chorus (passenger airlines), Exchange Income (an aviation and manufacturing conglomerate) and Westshore Terminals (a single coal terminal covered by one analyst).

On trailing earnings, CN trades at about **21x**, the **lowest in the table**, against a group average near **26x** and CPKC near **28x**. At the October 19 close of C$169.63, the trailing multiple is closer to 22x, still the lowest. On forward earnings CN is about **19x**, in line with the group, and its price to free cash flow of about **28x** is well below CPKC's **44x**. Against its only true railway peer, CN looks cheaper on almost every line.

The catch is the rest of the table. CN's **price to book is the highest in the group**, at about 4.6x, because years of buybacks have shrunk its equity. The truckers and Cargojet look much cheaper on sales and free cash flow, but they run lighter, lower-margin businesses, so a lower multiple is normal for them. If CN can show that traffic recovers after grain, the low P/E looks like a discount; if growth keeps lagging CPKC, the market may decide it is simply the right price.

## Why it matters: from steady compounder to "what replaces grain?"

For most of 2026, CN was the **steady compounder**: record grain, a raised outlook and steady buybacks, with the stock up about 22% in a year. The downgrade reframes it as a **railway that needs a new growth engine**.

If automotive and intermodal stay soft, CN's growth could lag CPKC's into 2027, and that is why a cheaper multiple may not be enough on its own. The market wants proof of the next driver, and the first chance to provide it is the third-quarter report on October 30.

## Bull case

1. **Cheapest P/E with the biggest earnings.** About 21x trailing earnings, the lowest in the group, with the most revenue (C$17.8B) and net income (C$4.8B).
2. **A raised outlook and steady buybacks.** Record grain volumes, mid-to-high single-digit adjusted EPS growth guidance for 2026 and C$1.32B of first-half buybacks support earnings per share.
3. **A long dividend record.** Nearly three decades of dividend increases, with a payout ratio under half of earnings.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Raymond James' point.** Fourth-quarter traffic is down 4.7% so far while CPKC's is up, and grain comparisons get harder from here.
2. **Costs are rising.** Fuel expense jumped about 60% in the second quarter, and the operating ratio worsened to 62.5%.
3. **Leverage and competition.** Debt is about 2.6x adjusted EBITDA, and a proposed Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern combination could reshape rail competition in the United States.

## What to watch

1. **Third-quarter results on October 30, 2026**, and whether CN reaffirms its 2026 outlook.
2. **Weekly carloads for the rest of the fourth quarter**, especially grain and intermodal.
3. **Diesel prices**, and how quickly fuel surcharges catch up.
4. **The ~C$183 average target**: whether the stock closes the gap from C$169.63, or more brokers follow Raymond James.

## Bottom line

CN has earned its place as Canada's largest and most profitable transport stock: it books the most revenue and profit in the group, has raised its dividend for nearly thirty years and is buying back stock. The issue is momentum, not quality, because CPKC has retaken the traffic growth lead and CN's fourth quarter has started slowly. At about 21x trailing earnings, the lowest P/E in the group, some of that is already in the price. The next test is October 30, when CN has to show what drives growth once the grain boom fades.
