## The short version: a profit miss, a record year, and a 5% yield

Rogers Sugar Inc. (TSX: RSI), the company behind the Lantic and Rogers sugar brands and a large maple syrup business, closed at **CA$6.90** on Thursday, November 26, 2026, up **2.8%** on the day. That is an unusual reaction to an earnings miss. Fourth-quarter adjusted earnings came in at **11 cents a share**, below the roughly 13 cents analysts expected. But the full fiscal year produced **record adjusted EBITDA of C$160.4 million**, the dividend was kept at 9 cents a quarter, and management said its big Montreal expansion, the LEAP project, is on schedule and will cost no more than C$300 million.

For income investors, the key number is the yield. At the November 20 price of C$6.64, Rogers Sugar yielded about **5.4%**, the highest of the five Canadian food makers in our peer group. Below we go through the quarter and the year, then compare Rogers Sugar with Saputo, Maple Leaf Foods, Premium Brands and Lassonde on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets, and look at whether the dividend is covered.

## What happened: fiscal 2026 results (November 26)

On **Thursday, November 26, 2026**, Rogers Sugar reported results for its fiscal year ended **October 3, 2026**. The fourth quarter had 14 weeks, against 13 a year earlier, and the year had 53 weeks instead of 52, so some growth comes from the calendar. The main numbers:

- **Q4 revenue of C$339.5 million**, up about 5% from C$322.7 million,
- **Q4 adjusted net earnings of C$14.3 million**, down from C$19.8 million, or **11 cents a share** against 16 cents a year earlier,
- **Q4 adjusted EBITDA of C$39.7 million**, about flat, as higher sugar margins were offset by higher selling, administration and distribution costs,
- **fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA of C$160.4 million**, a record and up about 7% from C$150.4 million,
- **fiscal 2026 adjusted net earnings of C$74.1 million**, up from C$72.5 million (58 cents a share, from 57 cents),
- trailing free cash flow, as the company defines it, of **C$78.5 million**, down from C$104.0 million on higher taxes and interest paid.

Reported (IFRS) net earnings were C$20.8 million in the quarter, above the adjusted figure, mainly because of non-cash mark-to-market adjustments that the company strips out of its adjusted numbers. The company also took a **C$4.8 million non-cash impairment** linked to a redesign of the Toronto part of LEAP.

### Sugar versus maple

The two segments moved in different directions in the quarter. **Sugar** adjusted EBITDA fell C$3.2 million to C$31.9 million, but for the full year it rose C$9.2 million to C$138.3 million on better margins. Sugar volume for the year was **752,800 tonnes**, down about 28,700 tonnes, mostly from lower liquid and export sales. U.S. tariffs on refined sugar have hurt exports. **Maple** had a strong quarter: adjusted EBITDA rose C$3.4 million to **C$7.8 million** on higher volume, and full-year maple volume grew 2.3 million pounds to 55.7 million.

The board declared the regular **9-cent quarterly dividend**, payable by January 20, 2027. For fiscal 2027, management expects sugar volume of **730,000 to 750,000 tonnes**, about level with this year, with fewer low-margin export sales and stable domestic demand.

Rogers Sugar yields about **5.42%** at its 9-cent quarterly rate, the highest in the group and well above the 3.41% average. It pays out about **69% of its profit**, second only to Premium Brands, which pays out about 140% of earnings while yielding about 4.5%. 

Is the dividend safe? On the company's own numbers, it was covered last year: Rogers paid **C$46.1 million** in dividends against **C$78.5 million** of free cash flow, as the company measures it. That measure excludes the LEAP project, which is funded by a separate financing plan put in place over the last three years. With about C$70 million of LEAP spending left for fiscal 2027 and the project expected to add to results from 2028, the pressure on cash flow should ease once construction ends. The dividend has been 36 cents a year for several years, so income investors should expect stability rather than growth.

## Why it matters: LEAP is nearly built

Rogers Sugar is a steady, mostly domestic business that pays out much of what it earns. The big change is the **LEAP project**, a Montreal refinery expansion that adds about **100,000 tonnes** of refining capacity, plus logistics assets to deliver more sugar into Ontario. Management now says LEAP will cost **no more than C$300 million** and be in service by the **end of June 2027**. About **C$229.6 million** had been spent by October 3, with about **C$70 million** planned for fiscal 2027. A redesign of the Toronto logistics part saved about C$30 million, which offsets extra engineering and civil costs in Montreal.

On the call, the CEO said construction will largely be done in the spring and some areas are already being commissioned. Management does not expect LEAP to add much in fiscal 2027, but expects it to start contributing in 2028. If it delivers, more capacity could lift volume and cash flow at a time when the Montreal refinery is running flat out.

## Bull case

1. **Record EBITDA**: fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA reached C$160.4 million, with both sugar and maple growing for the year.
2. **Highest yield in the group**: about 5.4%, covered by the company's free cash flow last year.
3. **Lower earnings multiple**: about 13.9x trailing and 12.2x forward earnings, below peer averages.
4. **LEAP close to done**: the budget is capped at C$300 million and most of it is already spent.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Q4 miss**: adjusted earnings fell about 28% in the quarter, and 11 cents missed the roughly 13-cent estimate.
2. **Volume pressure**: U.S. tariffs and weaker export and liquid sales cut sugar volume by about 28,700 tonnes.
3. **Trade protection review**: the Canadian International Trade Tribunal's expiry review of duties on imported refined sugar is due by May 2027.
4. **Little upside and a high payout**: analysts see about 2% to target, and a 69% payout leaves less room for error.

## What to watch

1. **New analyst targets** after the results.
2. **LEAP commissioning**, and a start by the end of June 2027.
3. **The trade tribunal decision** on sugar imports, expected by May 2027.
4. **Sugar volume** against the 730,000 to 750,000 tonne forecast for fiscal 2027.

## Bottom line

Rogers Sugar is a high-yield, low-growth stock. The fourth quarter missed, but the year was a record, the dividend is covered on the company's numbers, and the stock trades below its peers on earnings. LEAP has to deliver on time and on budget to change the growth story, and the tribunal review is the key risk to watch next spring.
