## The short version: a record quarter that analysts don't buy

Imperial Oil (TSX: IMO), the Calgary oil sands producer and refiner that is about 70% owned by ExxonMobil and sells fuel under the Esso brand, closed at **CA$188.72** on October 23, 2026, up about **51%** over twelve months. A week later it reported the most profitable quarter in its history: **net income of C$2.45 billion**, more than four times the year before, driven by a record result from its refineries. On October 30 the stock jumped **5.4%** to a new 52-week high of **CA$193.48**, on a day the TSX fell 1.7%.

The catch is the Street. Not one of the 10 analysts in the consensus rates Imperial a Buy, and their average one-year target of **C$152.83** sits about **21% below** Friday's close. Below we go through the quarter, the buybacks, and how Imperial compares with the other large Canadian oil and gas names — Suncor, Canadian Natural, Cenovus, Tourmaline and Whitecap — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 2026 results on October 30

On **October 30, 2026**, before the market open, Imperial reported its third-quarter results:

- **Net income of C$2,454M**, a quarterly record, up from C$539M a year earlier. The 2025 quarter included C$555M of one-time charges, so on a like-for-like basis profit still more than doubled, from C$1,094M.
- **Earnings of C$5.11 a share**, up from C$1.07 (C$2.17 excluding one-time items), and above the roughly **C$4.70** analysts expected, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters.
- **Revenue and other income of C$16.21B**, up from C$12.05B.
- **Cash flow from operations of C$3,228M** (C$2,957M before working-capital swings), up from C$1,798M. Capital spending was C$668M, and Imperial ended the quarter with about C$2.9B of cash.
- **Shareholder returns of C$2,506M** in the quarter: C$420M of dividends and **C$2,086M of share buybacks** under its accelerated buyback program, which it expects to finish before year end. It also declared a fourth-quarter dividend of **87 cents** a share.

### The refining windfall behind the record

The record came from the **Downstream** business — refining and fuel marketing — which earned a record **C$1,555M**, up from C$444M a year earlier and C$787M in the second quarter. Disruption to global energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on energy infrastructure have pushed diesel, gasoline and jet fuel prices far above crude, widening refining margins. The renewable diesel plant at Strathcona, near Edmonton, also had its best quarter.

Oil production went the other way. **Upstream output averaged 418,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day**, down from 462,000, and Upstream earnings fell to C$941M from C$1,299M in the second quarter on lower realized prices. At **Kearl**, Imperial's oil sands mine, production fell to 282,000 barrels a day after an **18-day shutdown** caused by a transformer failure on a third-party pipeline, and the company cut its 2026 Kearl target to **280,000 barrels a day**. Planned maintenance also lowered output at Cold Lake and Syncrude, and refinery utilization slipped to **88%** from 98% a year ago. CEO John Whelan told analysts he sees "one-offs," not systemic issues.

## Business overview: Imperial Oil vs Canadian oil & gas peers

Imperial is an integrated company: it mines and drills for heavy oil at Kearl, Cold Lake and its 25% share of Syncrude, then runs three refineries (Strathcona, Sarnia and Nanticoke) and supplies about 2,400 Esso and Mobil stations across Canada. The peers are the five largest liquid TSX names in the same screener sector (Energy minerals): **Suncor (SU)** and **Cenovus (CVE)**, the other integrated oil sands and refining companies; **Canadian Natural (CNQ)**, the largest producer; and the gas-weighted **Tourmaline (TOU)** and **Whitecap (WCP)**.

## Why it matters: from steady producer to refining windfall

The old narrative was Imperial as **a steady oil sands producer** that hands back nearly all its free cash through dividends and buybacks. The new narrative, at least for this quarter, is **a refining windfall**: fuel margins, not barrels, drove the record, while oil output actually fell.

That shift matters for anyone valuing the stock on this quarter. Refining margins are set by global fuel supply, and today's disruption could ease. Imperial's production base is still growing over time, but the next few quarters of earnings now depend more on whether those margins last than on Kearl or Cold Lake.

## Bull case

1. **Tight fuel supply.** Global fuel markets remain disrupted, and a rail logistics project at Strathcona should let Imperial run more crude through that refinery by year end.
2. **Buybacks compound.** The accelerated program is shrinking the share count quickly, so each barrel and each litre of fuel earns more per share.
3. **Policy tailwinds.** Imperial is part of the Oil Sands Alliance working on binding Pathways carbon storage agreements with Ottawa and Alberta, which are a condition for the proposed Pacific Link crude pipeline to the West Coast. The CEO said he is encouraged by the progress.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Windfalls fade.** If fuel prices cool, Downstream earnings can fall back quickly, as they did between 2022 and 2025.
2. **Valuation and sentiment.** The stock is at a 52-week high, about 21% above the average target, and no analyst in the consensus rates it a Buy.
3. **Production slippage.** Kearl's target was cut after the outage, refinery utilization fell to 88%, and the price gap between Canadian heavy oil and U.S. crude has reportedly widened sharply in recent weeks.

## What to watch

1. **November 15.** The target date for binding Pathways carbon storage agreements with Ottawa and Alberta.
2. **Refining margins.** Whether diesel and gasoline cracks hold up into the winter.
3. **Kearl's recovery.** Progress toward the new 2026 target of 280,000 barrels a day after the pipeline outage.
4. **The next buyback.** Imperial expects to finish its current program before year end, so watch for what replaces it.

## Bottom line

Imperial's third quarter was the best in its history: record profit, record refining earnings and C$2.5 billion handed back to shareholders in three months. The market rewarded it with a 52-week high. But the record came from refining margins rather than barrels, the stock already trades at about 22x trailing earnings against 13x to 15x for its big peers, and the average analyst target is about 21% below the price. The case for owning it now rests on margins staying wide and buybacks continuing at this pace. If either slows, the analysts' caution will look justified.
