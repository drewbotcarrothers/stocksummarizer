## The short version: a cheap serial buyer goes shopping again

Alimentation Couche-Tard (TSX: ATD), the Laval, Québec company behind the Couche-Tard and Circle K banners and more than **17,200** stores in 27 countries, closed at **CA$77.11** on October 8, 2026, up about **5%** over twelve months but down about **6%** over one month and about **17%** over three. Then it went shopping. On **October 9** it agreed to buy Irving Oil's retail sites in Québec and Ontario, and by **October 14** the shares had climbed to **C$80.96**. The new story is a **serial acquirer back on offence**: a home-market deal with Irving, a much bigger takeover of Poland's Żabka, and a buyback of up to 10% of its float.

The proof number is the valuation. At about **16x trailing earnings**, Couche-Tard has the lowest P/E of the large TSX retailers, against a peer average near **30x**. For a new buyer, the question is whether that discount reflects real risk in a debt-funded deal spree, or an opportunity the market has overlooked. Below we go through the Irving deal, the Żabka bet, the latest quarter, and how Couche-Tard compares with its closest TSX peers — Loblaw, Dollarama, Metro and Empire — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the Irving Oil deal in Québec and Ontario

On **October 9, 2026**, Couche-Tard signed a definitive agreement with Irving Oil to acquire certain retail assets in Québec and Ontario. The deal covers:

- **50 retail sites in Québec** that Couche-Tard already operates under its long-standing Québec Alliance with Irving,
- **2 Ontario sites** in Pembroke and Cornwall, also already run by Couche-Tard,
- **fuel supply to 71 more Couche-Tard stores** in Québec,
- **18 cardlock sites** for commercial fleets (16 in Québec, 2 in Ontario), with Irving's existing fleet customers served under a long-term agreement.

No price or closing date was disclosed, and the deal still needs customary approvals, including under the **Competition Act**. Measured against a network of more than 17,200 stores, this is a **home-market tidy-up, not a transformation**: Couche-Tard is buying the real estate and fuel economics of stores it already knows, in markets it already leads. The stock still responded well, rising **2.95%** on October 14 on a day the TSX fell **1.11%**.

### The bigger bet and the print behind it

The deal that can move the numbers is in Poland. On **July 31, 2026**, Couche-Tard launched a tender offer for **Żabka Group**, Poland's largest convenience retailer, with more than **13,000 stores** in Poland and Romania, valuing it at about **PLN 32.6B (~US$8.6B)**. Holders of about 57% of the shares have signed irrevocable agreements to tender, Romanian and EU foreign-subsidy clearances are in, and the deal still needs merger-control approval. It will be funded with cash and new and existing credit lines.

The latest quarter, the first of fiscal 2027 (12 weeks to July 19), was reported on **September 1, 2026**. Couche-Tard reports in US dollars:

- **Revenue US$21.7B, up 25.1%**, mostly from higher fuel prices.
- **Adjusted EPS of US$0.90, up 15.4%** from US$0.78; net earnings US$828.5M.
- **Adjusted EBITDA up 10.5%** to about US$1.78B.
- **U.S. fuel margin of 52.61¢ a gallon**, up 8.61¢; Canada 16.79¢ a litre, up 2.58¢.
- **Same-store merchandise sales up 1.6%** overall: U.S. +1.7%, Europe +1.2%, Canada flat.
- **Leverage of 1.77x** adjusted EBITDA, down from 1.99x in April.

The weak spots are volume and Canada. Same-store fuel volumes fell **1.6%** in the U.S. and **4.3%** in Europe as high pump prices cut demand, and Canadian merchandise sales were flat, with tobacco under pressure. Strong fuel margins did a lot of the work, and fuel margins can swing from quarter to quarter.

## Business overview: Couche-Tard vs retail peers

Couche-Tard runs convenience stores, most of them with fuel pumps, across North America, Europe and Asia. It earns money two ways: **merchandise and food** (snacks, energy drinks, tobacco and nicotine products, prepared food), which is the steadier, higher-margin part, and **road transportation fuel**, which is most of its revenue and whose margins move with supply conditions and competition. Scale lets it buy, supply and run stores more cheaply than smaller rivals, and acquisitions have been its main growth engine for decades.

On the TSX screener, Couche-Tard sits in the **Retail trade** sector. **Loblaw (L)** is the sector's largest grocer and pharmacy chain. **Dollarama (DOL)** is a high-traffic, small-basket discount retailer, like convenience. **Metro (MRU)** is the grocery and pharmacy leader in Québec and Ontario, Couche-Tard's home markets. **Empire (EMP.A)** owns Sobeys and other grocery banners. We left out George Weston (Loblaw's parent, which would double-count it), Canadian Tire (the screener carries only its thinly traded voting shares, and it is mostly general merchandise), Aritzia (apparel) and smaller names.

On trailing earnings, Couche-Tard has the **lowest P/E** on the table at about **16x**, against a group average near **30x**; Dollarama sits near **37x** and Loblaw near **26x**. Even after this week's rally, its P/E is closer to **17x**, still the lowest. The forward P/E of about **16.8x** is close to the trailing figure, so the low multiple is not flattered by a one-off gain. Its PEG of about **0.6** and price-to-cash ratio are also the lowest.

The price-to-sales ratio of about **0.6** looks very low, but that mostly reflects low-margin fuel revenue, so it says little on its own. On free cash flow Couche-Tard trades near **14x**, below the average of about **18x**. **Empire** is cheapest on sales, book value and cash flow, but its trailing P/E above 50x shows how thin its recent earnings were. If Couche-Tard can show the Żabka deal adds earnings per share without stretching the balance sheet, that P/E gap is the part most likely to change.

## Why it matters: from stalled acquirer to capital back at work

The **old narrative** was a serial acquirer **stuck in neutral** after its failed bid for the owner of 7-Eleven, with the stock drifting while grocers and Dollarama re-rated. The **new narrative** is a company **putting capital back to work**: Żabka in Poland, Irving sites at home, and a buyback of up to **10% of its public float**.

The Irving deal fits the old Couche-Tard playbook of buying assets it already operates, with low integration risk. Żabka is different: a large, franchise-based business in a new format. For investors, the open question is whether these deals add earnings faster than they add debt.

## Bull case

1. **The cheapest multiple on the table.** About 16x trailing earnings, the lowest P/E among the large TSX retailers, while adjusted EPS grew 15.4% last quarter.
2. **Strong fuel margins and a lighter balance sheet.** U.S. fuel margins rose to 52.61¢ a gallon, and leverage fell to about 1.8x adjusted EBITDA before the Żabka deal.
3. **Several ways to return cash.** An 18% payout, 20 years of dividend growth and a buyback of up to 10% of the float give it room to reward shareholders as deals close.

## Bear case and risks

1. **A big, debt-funded bet.** Żabka is about US$8.6B, funded with cash and credit, so leverage goes back up and integration risk is real.
2. **Falling fuel volumes.** Same-store fuel volumes fell 1.6% in the U.S. and 4.3% in Europe, and fuel margins can swing from quarter to quarter.
3. **Soft spots at home.** Canadian same-store merchandise sales were flat last quarter, and the stock has trailed Loblaw over the past year.

## What to watch

1. **The Żabka tender.** Acceptances close October 26, with settlement planned for early November, and the deal still needs merger-control approval.
2. **The Competition Act review** of the Irving deal and any price disclosure at closing.
3. **Second-quarter fiscal 2027 results, expected in late November**, for fuel margins, U.S. same-store sales and debt after Żabka.
4. **Analyst targets.** Whether the stock closes the gap to the C$100.92 average target, and whether targets move after the deals close.

## Bottom line

Couche-Tard has built one of the world's largest convenience networks through decades of acquisitions, and the Irving deal is a low-risk, home-market add-on. The market's issue is the bigger picture: a three-month slide, falling fuel volumes and a US$8.6B Żabka bet funded with debt. At about 16x trailing earnings, the stock is priced well below grocery and discount peers, which leaves room if the deals deliver. The Żabka closing and the late-November second-quarter report are the next real tests.
