## The short version: two catalysts, one rally, and less room to the targets

Kelt Exploration Ltd. (TSX: KEL) is a Calgary-based oil and gas producer whose growth is concentrated in the Montney, the large gas and liquids formation that straddles northeast British Columbia and northwest Alberta. In two trading sessions the stock went from **C$10.56 to C$12.72, a gain of about 20%**. The first leg came on Friday, November 13, 2026, when Kelt reported a strong third quarter and rose **8.8% to C$11.49**. The second came on Monday, November 16, when **Canadian Natural Resources agreed to buy fellow Montney producer Advantage Energy for C$13.55 a share in cash**, and Kelt rose another **10.7% to C$12.72**, a one-year high, on a day the S&P/TSX Composite fell 1.1%.

The stakes for a new buyer are simple. After the jump, Kelt trades only about **7% below the average analyst target of C$13.63**, and one broker has already moved to the sidelines. Below we go through the quarter, the Advantage deal, and how Kelt compares with Advantage, Peyto, Birchcliff, Paramount Resources and Spartan Delta on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 13, then a takeover next door

Before the open on **November 13, 2026**, Kelt reported results for the three months to September 30. All figures are in Canadian dollars, and several are the company's own non-GAAP measures:

- **Production of 47,970 barrels of oil equivalent (BOE) a day, up 27%** from 37,710 a year earlier. Oil rose 47% and natural gas liquids 43%; the mix was 41.5% oil and liquids and 58.5% gas.
- **Sales of C$193.3 million, up 75%.**
- **Adjusted funds from operations of C$106.3 million, or C$0.51 a diluted share, up 138%** from C$44.6 million. This is the cash measure most investors use to compare producers.
- **Net income of C$26.0 million (C$0.13 a share)**, compared with a C$7.4 million loss a year earlier.
- **Net debt of C$227 million** at September 30, with bank debt of C$198 million.

### The outages behind the numbers

The quarter was strong even though Kelt lost production it did not control. Three third-party gas plants were shut for maintenance and repairs: Progress for about 18 days, MacMahon for about 23 days and **Albright for 67 days**. Kelt estimates the outages cut output by **8,000 to 9,000 BOE a day** during the quarter. MacMahon came back on October 19, and the Albright operator expects to restart by **November 27, 2026**. Kelt plans an insurance claim, but no recovery has been approved.

Management **raised its 2026 adjusted funds from operations forecast to about C$427 million**, from C$410 million, mainly because it now assumes higher oil prices (WTI of US$85 a barrel, from US$79.50). It **narrowed production guidance to 50,000 to 51,000 BOE a day** (from 50,000 to 52,000) because of the downtime, lifted the capital budget by C$20 million to **C$395 million** (partly to pull six Montney wells into 2026 for production early in 2027), and expects to **exit the year at 60,000 to 62,000 BOE a day**. Net debt should fall to **about C$153 million by year-end**, roughly 0.4 times this year's cash flow. Proved reserves were up 12% from the end of 2025.

### The Advantage deal

On **Monday, November 16**, Canadian Natural Resources agreed to buy Advantage Energy for **C$13.55 a share in cash, about C$2.53 billion including debt**, a premium of about a quarter to Advantage's November 13 close. Advantage holds Montney and Charlie Lake assets near Grande Prairie, Alberta. Advantage shares jumped 23.7%, and Kelt was the third-best performer on the index that day. No direct link between the deal and Kelt was reported, but a large buyer paying up for Montney production puts a fresh price tag on assets like Kelt's.

## Business overview: Kelt vs Montney gas producer peers

Kelt explores for and produces oil, natural gas liquids and natural gas in western Canada. It is a growth company rather than an income stock: it does not pay a dividend and spends its cash flow on drilling, mostly in the Montney. Results move with production volumes, realized prices and the uptime of the gas plants that process its output.

On the TSX screener Kelt sits in the **Energy minerals** sector. The peers are liquid, gas-weighted Montney and Deep Basin producers of similar size: Advantage Energy (AAV), now under agreement to be bought; Peyto (PEY), a low-cost Deep Basin gas producer; Birchcliff (BIR), a Montney gas producer; Paramount Resources (POU), which has Montney and Duvernay assets; and Spartan Delta (SDE), a Deep Basin and Duvernay grower. Tourmaline is many times larger and ARC Resources is not in the screener merge, so they are left out.

On **trailing earnings Kelt looks expensive, at about 42 times**, versus a group average near 23. That number is high because profit over the last twelve months was small. On **forward earnings it is about 11 times**, a bit below the group average of about 12.5. Peyto is the cheapest on trailing earnings, at under 10 times, and Spartan Delta is the cheapest on forward earnings, at under 8 times. Kelt's price to sales and price to book are close to the group average, and its price to free cash flow is blank because heavy drilling has kept screener free cash flow near or below zero.

So the market is paying for **next year's growth, not last year's profit**. If Kelt reaches its 60,000 to 62,000 BOE a day exit rate and Albright stays online, the forward multiple is the one that matters. If production slips, the trailing number is a reminder of how much the price already assumes.

## Why it matters: from a quiet grower to a Montney comparable

The old narrative was **a mid-sized Montney grower held back by third-party plant downtime**. The new narrative is **a producer whose assets just got a public price tag from one of Canada's largest energy companies**. The Advantage deal shows a big buyer is willing to pay a premium for Montney production, and Kelt's two-day move suggests investors are applying that logic to its shares.

There is a catch in the product mix. In the third quarter Kelt realized about **C$113 for a barrel of oil but under C$2 (C$1.98) for a thousand cubic feet of gas**, and the company cut its gas price assumption for the year. Oil and liquids are doing most of the work while gas prices stay very low. That matters for growth investors weighing Kelt's production growth against the hope of more takeovers in the region.

## Bull case

1. **Higher output by year-end.** Kelt expects to exit 2026 at 60,000 to 62,000 BOE a day, well above the 47,970 it produced in the third quarter.
2. **Falling debt.** Net debt should fall to about C$153 million by year-end, under half a year of cash flow.
3. **A Montney price tag.** The Advantage deal shows large buyers will pay up for Montney assets.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Little room to the targets.** After a 20% jump, the stock is only about 7% below the average target, and one broker has moved to Hold.
2. **Plant risk.** Third-party outages already cut output this year, and the Albright restart is not done yet.
3. **Gas prices.** Realized gas prices are very low, so results lean heavily on oil prices.
4. **No dividend.** Returns depend on growth and the share price, not income.

## What to watch

1. **The Albright plant restart**, expected by November 27, 2026.
2. **The exit rate.** Whether Kelt reaches 60,000 to 62,000 BOE a day by year-end.
3. **More Montney deals.** Any further takeover activity after Canadian Natural's purchase of Advantage.
4. **The 2027 budget**, and how much cash goes to growth versus paying down debt. Year-end 2026 results are expected before the open on March 12, 2027.

## Bottom line

Kelt delivered a strong quarter: 27% more production, more than double the cash flow, a return to profit and a higher full-year forecast, despite losing up to 9,000 BOE a day to plant outages. Then a Montney takeover next door added a second leg to the rally. The issue now is price. At C$12.72 the stock is about 7% below the average analyst target, trades at about 42 times trailing earnings and about 11 times forward, and depends on oil while gas stays cheap. The quarter was strong, but after this jump, much of the good news may already be in the price.
