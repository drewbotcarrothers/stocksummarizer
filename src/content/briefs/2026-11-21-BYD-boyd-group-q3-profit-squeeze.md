## The short version: busier shops, almost no profit

Boyd Group Services Inc. (TSX: BYD, NYSE: BGSI) runs about **1,329 collision repair shops**, as **Gerber Collision & Glass** in the United States and **Boyd Autobody & Glass** and **Assured Automotive** in Canada. Its third quarter, reported on **Wednesday, November 11, 2026**, showed a business that is getting much bigger: sales rose **28.2% to about US$1.01 billion** and adjusted EBITDA rose **34%**.

But almost none of that growth reached the bottom line. Net earnings were just **US$81,000**, down from **US$10.8 million** a year earlier, and adjusted earnings per share of **US$0.68** missed the roughly **US$0.85** analysts expected. The shares fell more than 11% that day, and over the year to the November 13 close they were down about **52.6%**, the worst one-year result in our peer set by a wide margin.

Below we go through the quarter, why profit lagged sales, how Boyd compares with Restaurant Brands, MTY Food Group, A&W, Cineplex and AutoCanada on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets, and what to watch next.

## What happened: the Q3 report (November 11)

Boyd reports in US dollars, so the figures in this section are US$.

### Growth on the top line

- **Sales:** up 28.2% to about **US$1.013 billion**, from US$790.2 million a year earlier.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** up 34.3% to **US$132.1 million**, with the margin up 60 basis points to **13.0%**.
- **Same-store sales:** up **2.2%**, even though Boyd estimates industry repairable claims fell **1% to 3%** in the quarter. That means Boyd is still taking share in a shrinking claims pool.
- **New locations:** shops outside the comparable base added about **US$206 million** of revenue; the network ended September at **1,329 locations**, up 31% from a year earlier.

Most of that growth came from **Joe Hudson's Collision Center**, a **US$1.3 billion** acquisition that closed on January 9 and added **258 shops** across 18 U.S. states. Those shops produced about US$169.6 million of third-quarter sales.

### Why profit did not follow

The cost of that growth sits below the operating line. **Financing expenses rose to US$32.3 million from US$18.8 million** as Boyd services the debt it took on for Joe Hudson's (including US$525 million of 5.5% notes). Amortization of acquired intangibles rose to US$16.3 million from US$7.1 million, and acquisition and transformation costs were about US$7 million before tax.

Strip out those items and **adjusted net earnings rose 6.4% to about US$19 million**. But Boyd also sold about 6.36 million new shares in November 2025 to help pay for the deal, so **adjusted EPS fell to US$0.68 from US$0.83**. Margin expansion also slowed: Mizuho noted that year-over-year EBITDA margin growth slowed to 60 basis points, from 170 basis points in the first half.

### The savings program and the buyback

Boyd's cost program, **Project 360**, combined with acquisition synergies, delivered about **US$50 million of savings through September**, against a 2026 target that was raised in August to **US$65 million**. Management said it remains on track. Boyd also launched a normal course issuer bid in September and bought back about US$14 million of shares in the quarter, and a director, John Hartmann, bought 645 NYSE-listed shares at US$76.20 on November 18. On **Friday, November 20**, the TSX shares rose **4.4% to C$105.81** while the S&P/TSX Composite fell 2.5%.

## Why it matters: a roll-up that now has to prove itself

Boyd's model is simple: buy collision shops, run them better with central purchasing, scanning and calibration in-house and tight staffing outside the repair bays, and win more work from insurers who route claims to large networks. That model built one of North America's biggest collision chains. Joe Hudson's made it about 25% larger in one step.

The catch is the balance sheet. At the end of September, total debt was about **C$2.89 billion**, roughly the same as the company's **C$2.96 billion market value**, and cash was only about C$40 million. Interest is now a large claim on operating profit, while industry claim volumes are falling. The question for investors is whether Project 360 savings, synergies and same-store gains can grow profit faster than interest costs and soft claims can eat it.

## Bull case

1. **Growth is real:** sales +28% and adjusted EBITDA +34%, with the savings program on track for US$65 million in 2026.
2. **Cheap on cash flow:** about 6.5 times free cash flow and 0.56 times sales, low for a market leader.
3. **Insiders and the company are buying:** an active buyback and a director's purchase this week.
4. **Analysts still see about 69% upside**, even after the target cuts, with 13 of 15 at Buy or better.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Net profit is close to zero**, and financing costs are rising with the debt.
2. **Leverage is high:** total debt is roughly equal to the market value of the shares.
3. **Fewer insurance claims** could keep same-store growth stuck at low single digits.
4. **Margin gains are slowing** and earnings estimates are falling; the forward P/E already assumes a recovery.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter results**, and whether the US$65 million savings target is met.
2. Whether **net profit finally follows sales**, after interest and amortization.
3. **Debt levels** and how much stock Boyd buys back under its issuer bid.
4. **Same-store sales and claim trends**, and whether the 11 planned start-up locations open on time.

## Bottom line

Boyd is growing fast, but the market wants proof that growth turns into profit. At about 6.5 times free cash flow the stock already prices in a lot of disappointment; at about 20 times forward earnings it also prices in a recovery that has not shown up yet. The next two quarters of interest costs, savings and claim volumes will decide which of those numbers matters more.
