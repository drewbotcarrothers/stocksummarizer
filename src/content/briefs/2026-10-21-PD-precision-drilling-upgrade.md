## The short version: a cheap driller gets a new bull

Precision Drilling (TSX: PD), the Calgary-based land drilling contractor, was **CA$119.00** in our October 8 table and closed **Tuesday, October 20**, at **C$128.46**, up **4.7%** on the day. Over the twelve months to October 8 the shares were up about **48%**, though they had slipped about **5%** in the month before. On October 20, **TD Cowen upgraded Precision to Buy from Hold** and raised its target to **C$150 from C$116**.

The pitch is a **valuation gap**. Precision trades at about **0.96x book value** in our table and about **12x forward earnings**, while the market pays far higher multiples for premium energy-service names such as CES Energy and Enerflex. The question for a new buyer is whether the drilling cycle is strong enough, and Precision's U.S. margins recover fast enough, for that gap to narrow. Below we go through the upgrade, the second-quarter print, and how Precision compares with Ensign, Trican, Total Energy, CES Energy, Enerflex and Pason on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: TD Cowen pivots from "quality" to drillers

On **October 20, 2026**, TD Cowen analyst **Aaron MacNeil** upgraded Precision to **Buy from Hold** and lifted his target to **C$150 from C$116**, as part of a broader call on Canadian energy services:

- **The quality trade has played out.** Since the start of 2025, the average EV/EBITDA multiple for **CES Energy and Enerflex** has expanded about 85% to roughly **8.6x**, versus about 34% to roughly **4.6x** for the drilling and completions names **Ensign, Precision and Trican**.
- **The gap is historically wide.** On forward consensus EV/EBITDA, the premium names trade about **91% above** the drillers, the widest gap in more than 15 years and far above the long-run average of about **23%**.
- **Management is unusually confident.** Precision has guided to **fourth-quarter U.S. margins approaching US$10,000 a day** per rig as reactivation costs fade, with room to improve in 2027. MacNeil called guidance two quarters out "unusual."
- **Canada and the balance sheet.** Precision's Super Single rigs suit the heavy-oil Clearwater play and its Super Triples are leveraged to the Montney and Duvernay, which should benefit as export pipeline capacity grows. Debt is lower than in past cycles, and the company is buying back stock.

MacNeil values Precision at about **3.3x his 2027 EBITDA estimate**. The same day he **upgraded Ensign to Buy**, **downgraded Pason to Hold**, and named **Trican** his top pick. The backdrop helps: oil was trading above **US$100 a barrel**, and energy analysts at other firms also pointed to strong cash flow ahead of third-quarter results.

### The Q2 print behind the guidance

Precision's last report covered the second quarter of 2026, and it was mixed:

- **Revenue** rose **11%** to **C$453M**, helped by higher activity in Canada and the U.S.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** fell **10%** to **C$97M**, mainly because of **U.S. rig reactivation costs** (about US$2,387 per utilization day as seven rigs returned to work) and weaker international margins.
- The **U.S. operating margin** dropped to about **US$6,212 a day**, from about US$9,026 a year earlier.
- A previously flagged change in depreciation estimates added about **C$11M** of expense, leaving a **net loss of about C$1M**, and the shares fell after the quarter missed expectations.

The cash flow was steadier. Operations generated **C$146M**, enough to **repay C$50M of debt** and **buy back C$12M of stock**. Precision plans about **C$265M** of capital spending in 2026, aims to cut debt by about C$100M this year and allocate up to half of free cash flow to buybacks, and keeps its long-term target of **net debt below 1x adjusted EBITDA**. That is why the fourth-quarter margin guidance matters so much: it is the bridge from a weak Q2 to the earnings that the valuation below assumes.

## Business overview: Precision vs Canadian energy services peers

Precision runs one of the largest land drilling fleets in North America, with its Super Series rigs working in Canada, the United States and a few international markets, plus a smaller completion and production services business (well servicing and rentals). Day rates and utilization rise and fall with oil and gas drilling budgets, so earnings are cyclical, while long-term contracts and the Alpha automation software add some stability.

The screener puts Precision in the **Industrial services** sector, which is dominated by pipelines, waste and engineering firms. The two same-sector oilfield names are **Ensign Energy (ESI)**, another driller, and **Total Energy (TOT)**, which combines drilling with compression and rentals. We added the closest Canadian energy-services names that the screener files elsewhere and that TD Cowen compared in its report: **Trican Well (TCW)** (pressure pumping), **CES Energy (CEU)** (drilling fluids and production chemicals), **Enerflex (EFX)** (compression and processing) and **Pason Systems (PSI)** (drilling data and instrumentation).

## Why it matters: from cyclical laggard to value pivot

For almost two years, investors **paid up for steady growers** in Canadian energy services, such as CES's market-share gains and Enerflex's exposure to power demand, and **treated drillers as pure cyclicals**. The new narrative is that drilling fundamentals are improving: with oil above US$100 and Canadian export pipelines expanding, drillers' earnings are rising while their valuations stay low.

If Precision delivers on its U.S. margin guidance, the market may stop pricing it like a value trap. If it misses again, the discount will look deserved. The first test comes with third-quarter results on October 27.

## Bull case

1. **Cheap on assets and forward earnings.** About 0.96x book value and about 12x forward earnings, at the low end of its peers, with EV/EBITDA near 4.6x.
2. **A confident margin call.** Management guided to U.S. margins near US$10,000 a day in the fourth quarter, an unusual call two quarters out that signals confidence as reactivation costs fade.
3. **Debt down, buybacks up.** Debt keeps falling toward the under-1x net debt to EBITDA target, and up to half of free cash flow goes to buybacks.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Drillers are cyclical.** If a ceasefire or a demand shock knocks oil lower, rig counts and day rates can fall fast, and the forward earnings behind the 12x multiple would shrink.
2. **Execution.** The second quarter missed, with reactivation costs squeezing U.S. margins to about US$6,200 a day. Another miss would hurt management's credibility.
3. **Balance sheet and international.** Net debt is still about C$631M, and international results are weak amid geopolitical tensions.

## What to watch

1. **Third-quarter results on October 27, 2026** (after the close), with the call on October 28.
2. **U.S. margins**: whether they are on track for about US$10,000 a day in the fourth quarter.
3. **Oil prices and 2027 budgets** from Canadian producers, many of which will publish them alongside third-quarter results.
4. **Debt reduction and buybacks**, and whether the stock closes the gap to the ~C$153 average target.

## Bottom line

Precision has done the hard work of the last cycle: it has cut debt, shrunk its share count and kept generating cash even in a weak quarter. The issue is earnings, because reactivation costs and higher depreciation left it with a small loss, so the stock trades at about book value and about 12x forward earnings while premium peers trade at far higher multiples. TD Cowen's upgrade is a bet that the gap narrows as U.S. margins recover and Canadian drilling stays busy. The next test is October 27, when Precision has to show that its fourth-quarter margin guidance is on track.
