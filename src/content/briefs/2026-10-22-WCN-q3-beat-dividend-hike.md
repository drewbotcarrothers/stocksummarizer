## The short version: a steady compounder beats and raises again

Waste Connections (TSX/NYSE: WCN), the North American solid-waste collector and landfill owner, was **CA$221.28** in our October 8 table and closed **Wednesday, October 21**, at **C$236.45** in Toronto (US$168.28 in New York), just before it reported. Over the twelve months to October 8 the shares were down about **9%**, and about **3.5%** in the month before. After the close on October 21, the company **beat third-quarter estimates, raised its full-year outlook for the second time, and lifted its dividend 10%**, its sixteenth double-digit increase in a row.

The catch is the price tag. At about **39x trailing earnings** and **27x forward earnings**, Waste Connections already trades at a premium to most of its TSX industrial services peers. The question for a new buyer is whether improving volumes and rising margins can justify that premium. Below we go through the quarter, the new outlook and capital returns, and how Waste Connections compares with WSP Global, AtkinsRéalis, Stantec, Badger Infrastructure, Bird Construction and Aecon on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: a "top to bottom" beat and a second outlook raise

On **October 21, 2026**, after the market close, Waste Connections reported its third-quarter results. All figures are in U.S. dollars:

- **Revenue of US$2.638B, up 7.3%** from US$2.458B a year earlier and above the roughly US$2.61B analysts expected.
- **Adjusted EPS of US$1.58**, up from US$1.44, and about 6 cents above consensus. Reported EPS was **US$1.43**, versus US$1.11 a year ago, when write-downs weighed on the quarter.
- **Adjusted EBITDA of US$895.8M**, a **34.0% margin**. Management said underlying margins expanded by about **50 basis points**, even after a late-quarter surge in diesel prices.
- **Volumes are improving.** Solid waste unit volumes improved **70 basis points** from the second quarter, led by landfills, and E&P (oilfield) waste revenue rose to about US$213M from US$179M, roughly **19%** higher.
- **Acquisitions** closed so far this year add about **US$130M** of annualized revenue.

CEO Ronald Mittelstaedt called it "another top to bottom beat" and said the company is "firmly on track to exceed" the outlook it raised in July.

### The new outlook and capital returns

The updated 2026 outlook calls for revenue of about **US$10.09B**, net income of about **US$1.18B**, **adjusted EBITDA of about US$3.345B**, capital spending of about US$1.25B, and **adjusted free cash flow of US$1.40B to US$1.45B**, which management describes as double-digit growth. Revenue guidance moved up from about US$10.04B at the midpoint.

Capital returns stepped up too:

- **Dividend up 10%** to **US$0.385 a quarter** from US$0.35, payable **November 19** to shareholders of record on November 5. That is **US$1.54 a year**, and the sixteenth consecutive double-digit annual increase.
- **Buybacks of more than US$1B** so far in 2026, about **2.5% of the shares**, a record pace.

The weak spot is the balance sheet. Long-term debt rose to about **US$9.43B** at September 30 from US$8.81B at the end of 2025, partly to fund those buybacks, and debt to book capitalization is **54%**. The company held its conference call on the morning of **October 22**.

## Business overview: Waste Connections vs TSX industrial services peers

Waste Connections collects, transfers and disposes of non-hazardous solid waste for about nine million customers in 46 U.S. states and six Canadian provinces. It focuses on **exclusive and secondary markets**, where it often holds franchise contracts or owns the only nearby landfill, and it also runs recycling, renewable fuels and oilfield waste disposal. Collection is steady and contract-based; recycled commodities and E&P waste are the cyclical parts. Its head office is in The Woodlands, Texas, and its registered office is in Ontario.

On the TSX screener, Waste Connections sits in the **Industrial services** sector, which also holds pipelines, drillers, engineering firms and builders. The two Canadian-listed waste peers, **GFL Environmental** and **Secure Waste Infrastructure**, are not in our current export, so we compare Waste Connections with the largest non-energy names in the same sector: **WSP Global (WSP)**, **AtkinsRéalis (ATRL)** and **Stantec (STN)** (engineering and design), **Badger Infrastructure Solutions (BDGI)** (non-destructive excavation), and **Bird Construction (BDT)** and **Aecon (ARE)** (construction and infrastructure). These are not garbage haulers, so the comparison is about scale, quality and price, not identical businesses. Pipelines and oilfield service names were left out.

Waste Connections trades at about **39x trailing earnings**, right at the group average of about 38x, but that average is pulled up by Bird Construction at about 77x. ## Why it matters: from soft volumes to a volume turn

The old narrative was Waste Connections as a **classic defensive compounder whose growth had stalled**: prices kept rising, but volumes stayed soft, and over the past year the stock lost ground as its valuation came down. The new narrative is a **volume turn**: landfill volumes improving, oilfield waste recovering, and pricing plus cost control lifting margins even through a diesel spike.

That combination, rising volumes on top of steady price increases, is the setup bulls have been waiting for. The open question is how much of it the current multiple already assumes.

## Bull case

1. **Beat and raise, twice.** The full-year outlook went up for the second time, with volumes improving and acquisitions adding about US$130M of annualized revenue.
2. **Margins keep expanding.** Underlying margins rose about half a point despite a diesel spike, a sign of pricing power in mostly exclusive markets.
3. **Cash funds everything.** At least US$1.4B of adjusted free cash flow covers rising dividends, more than US$1B of buybacks and steady tuck-in acquisitions.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Valuation.** At about 27x forward earnings and about 4x sales, there is little room for a miss or a slowdown.
2. **Rising debt.** Long-term debt climbed to about US$9.4B, partly to fund buybacks, while bond yields sit above 5% and interest expense is rising.
3. **Cyclical revenue.** E&P waste and recycled commodities can reverse quickly if oil prices or industrial demand fall, and diesel costs remain a margin risk.

## What to watch

1. **The October 22 conference call**, and any early read on 2027 pricing, volumes and margins.
2. **Solid waste volumes**: whether the landfill-led improvement continues into the fourth quarter.
3. **Diesel costs and fourth-quarter margins**, after the late-quarter fuel spike.
4. **Acquisitions, buybacks and debt**: the pace of deals and repurchases, and how much more borrowing funds them.

## Bottom line

Waste Connections has earned its reputation: steady price increases, landfills that are hard to replicate, a 34% EBITDA margin and sixteen straight double-digit dividend raises. This quarter added the missing piece, improving volumes, and the company raised its outlook again. The issue is price: at about 27x forward earnings and the highest price-to-sales ratio in our peer group, much of that quality is already in the stock, and analysts see less upside here than for its peers. The next tests are whether volumes keep improving and how the company manages rising debt while it buys back stock.
