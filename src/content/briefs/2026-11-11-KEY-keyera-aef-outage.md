## The short version: the big deal works, one plant doesn't

Keyera (TSX: KEY), the Calgary-based natural gas liquids (NGL) midstream company, closed at **CA$50.69** on November 6, 2026, up about **24% over twelve months** but down about **14% over three months**. On **November 10** it reported a third quarter that showed its biggest acquisition paying off: **adjusted EBITDA jumped 64% to C$462M**. The same release said its **Alberta EnviroFuels (AEF) iso-octane plant** is in worse shape than expected and will cost about **C$85M of marketing margin in 2027**.

The stock fell as much as 5% on the morning of the release before closing slightly higher at C$51.26. At about **36x trailing earnings** Keyera looks like the most expensive name in its group, but on **expected earnings it trades near 17x**, below most peers. The question for a new buyer is whether the growing fee-based business outweighs a marketing arm that keeps tripping and a balance sheet that is still above its leverage target. Below we go through the quarter, the outage, and how Keyera compares with Pembina, Enbridge, TC Energy, South Bow, AltaGas and Gibson Energy on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 10

On **Tuesday, November 10, 2026**, Keyera reported its **third-quarter 2026** results. All figures are in Canadian dollars. It was the first full quarter including the **Canadian NGL business bought from Plains**, the largest acquisition in Keyera's history, plus the additional **50% interest in the KAPS pipeline system** that gave it full ownership.

- **Adjusted EBITDA of C$462M**, up from C$281M a year earlier (+64%), or C$466M excluding Plains transaction and integration costs,
- **Fee-based realized margin of C$398M**, up **53%**, with a **record C$272M** in Liquids Infrastructure (about C$103M of it from the acquired Plains assets) and C$125M in Gathering and Processing, where the Simonette and Wapiti plants are nearly full,
- **Distributable cash flow of C$244M, or C$0.83 a share** (C$0.79 a year earlier), covering the dividend with a **68% payout ratio**,
- A **net loss of C$68M**, against C$85M of profit a year earlier, driven mainly by **C$315M of unrealized losses on commodity hedging contracts**, an accounting mark that does not reflect cash earned in the quarter,
- **About C$115M of annual run-rate synergies** already captured or implemented, against a target of C$120M to C$140M within 12 months of the Plains closing.

### The problem: Alberta EnviroFuels

Keyera's **Marketing** segment earned C$95M of realized margin, held back by the Enbridge Line 5 disruption and lower iso-octane volumes at AEF in September. After the quarter ended, **heat exchanger performance at AEF deteriorated faster than expected**. Keyera now expects the plant to:

- run at **reduced rates for the rest of 2026** (guidance assumes about 50% utilization),
- take an **interim outage of about four weeks in early January 2027** to complete repairs,
- keep running at reduced rates until **permanent replacement equipment is installed in May 2027**, with full production expected by the end of May.

The total expected hit to **2027 Marketing realized margin is about C$85M**. Even so, Keyera **raised 2026 Marketing guidance to C$400M–C$430M** (from C$320M–C$350M), helped by about C$50M from acquired Plains inventory and hedges plus stronger iso-octane and butane margins. 2027 Marketing guidance will only come with first-quarter 2027 results.

### Leverage and spending

Net debt to adjusted EBITDA ended the quarter at **3.6x**, above Keyera's long-term target of **2.5x to 3.0x**; management plans to be back in range in **2028**. For 2027 it guided **C$630M–C$670M of growth capital** (mainly KAPS Zone 4, KFS Frac III and the ACE Rail Terminal) and **C$250M–C$280M of maintenance capital**, and said most major spending should be finished by the end of 2027. It reiterated a target of **16% to 18% compound annual growth in fee-based adjusted EBITDA per share from 2025 to 2027**, slowing to 7% to 8% from 2027 to 2029.

The market's verdict on the day: the shares opened at C$49.78, touched **C$48.31** (about 5.5% below the prior close of C$51.10), and finished at **C$51.26**, up 0.3%.

## Business overview: Keyera vs midstream peers

Keyera runs three businesses: **Gathering and Processing** (natural gas plants in western Alberta), **Liquids Infrastructure** (NGL fractionation, storage, pipelines and the condensate system around Fort Saskatchewan, near Edmonton) and **Marketing** (NGL and iso-octane sales, including AEF). The first two are mostly fee-for-service and contracted; Marketing swings with commodity prices and plant reliability.

On the TSX screener Keyera sits in the **Industrial services** sector. For peers we used the same-sector pipeline and midstream names **Pembina (PPL)**, **Enbridge (ENB)**, **TC Energy (TRP)** and **South Bow (SOBO)**. As a documented closest-sector exception, we added **AltaGas (ALA)**, which the screener lists under Utilities but which runs a large NGL export and processing business, and **Gibson Energy (GEI)**, listed under Distribution services, an Alberta liquids terminals and marketing company. The engineering, construction and waste companies in Industrial services were left out as not comparable.

## Why it matters: a bigger, more leveraged Keyera

The old narrative was a **steady fee-based pipeline and processing business** with a marketing arm on top. The new narrative is a **bigger, more leveraged Keyera**: the Plains deal and KAPS have lifted fee-based earnings sharply, but the marketing business, and AEF in particular, keeps producing surprises, and debt will stay above target until 2028. For income investors, the question is whether the growing contracted cash flow outweighs the plant problems and the higher leverage.

## Bull case

1. **Fast fee-based growth.** Management targets 16% to 18% a year of growth in fee-based adjusted EBITDA per share from 2025 to 2027, and its major projects are on time and on or below budget.
2. **Synergies arriving early.** About C$115M a year of Plains synergies are already captured or implemented, close to the C$120M–C$140M target, with more commercial opportunities identified beyond it.
3. **Cheap on forward earnings.** At about 17x expected earnings Keyera is cheaper than most peers, and it has the largest gap to the average analyst target in the group.

## Bear case and risks

1. **AEF reliability.** The iso-octane plant got worse after the quarter, and the fix depends on replacement equipment arriving and being installed by May 2027.
2. **Leverage.** Net debt is 3.6x EBITDA, above the 2.5x–3.0x target, while growth spending stays at C$630M–C$670M in 2027.
3. **Marketing volatility.** Marketing profits swing with commodity prices and plant uptime, and 2027 marketing guidance will not arrive until May.

## What to watch

1. **The January AEF outage** and whether the plant is back at full rates by the end of May 2027.
2. **Leverage** and progress back toward 3x net debt to EBITDA through 2027.
3. **Growth projects** such as KAPS Zone 4, KFS Frac III and the ACE Rail Terminal staying on time and on budget, plus the Fort Saskatchewan Condensate System debottleneck due in early 2027.
4. **First-quarter 2027 results**, when Keyera gives its 2027 marketing guidance.

## Bottom line

Keyera's core business is growing fast: the Plains acquisition lifted adjusted EBITDA 64% in its first full quarter and synergies are nearly at target. But one troubled plant is setting the tone, costing about C$85M next year, while debt sits above target and the stock has lagged its peers for three months. On expected earnings it is cheaper than most of the group and analysts see about 24% upside, so the next six months, the AEF outage, the May equipment swap and the first 2027 marketing guidance, will decide whether that gap closes.
