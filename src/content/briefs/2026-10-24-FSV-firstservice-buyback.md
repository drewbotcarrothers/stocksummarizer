## The short version: a modest beat, and a big bet on its own shares

FirstService (TSX: FSV), the Toronto company behind North America's leading condo and homeowner-association manager and brands like California Closets, Paul Davis, CertaPro Painters and Century Fire Protection, was **CA$180.95** in our October 8 table, down about **31%** over twelve months and almost **8%** over the prior month. On **Thursday, October 22**, it reported third-quarter results slightly ahead of expectations, and the TSX shares jumped **7.4%** to **C$206.06** before giving back 2.5% on Friday to C$200.92. Even after the bounce, the stock sits about **25% below** its 52-week high of C$266.83.

The quarter itself was solid rather than spectacular. The bigger story is capital allocation: since the start of April, FirstService has bought back **4.1 million shares, about 9% of the company**, and its leverage has climbed to 2.4 times EBITDA as a result. On our table it trades at about **37x trailing earnings**, the lowest P/E in its peer group, but with growth that leans on organic gains in 2027. Below we go through the print, the buyback math, and how FirstService compares with Colliers, Boyd Group and Altus Group on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: a third-quarter beat with roofing still dragging

On **October 22, 2026**, before the open, FirstService reported results for the quarter ended September 30. All company figures are in US dollars:

- **Revenue of US$1.53B, up 6%** from US$1.45B a year earlier, with **4% organic growth**, ahead of the roughly US$1.50B analysts expected.
- **Adjusted EPS of US$1.89, up 7%** from US$1.76, just ahead of the US$1.87 consensus.
- **Adjusted EBITDA of US$169.7M, up 3%**, for a consolidated margin of 11.1% versus 11.4% a year earlier.
- **GAAP diluted EPS of US$0.99**, down from US$1.24, as operating earnings fell to US$107.4M from US$115.6M.

Management reaffirmed its full-year targets: mid-single-digit growth in both revenue and EBITDA for 2026, with revenue growth at the upper end of the range it expected at mid-year, and fourth-quarter margins slightly higher than last year. The 2027 outlook comes with year-end results in February.

### The segments and the buyback behind the quarter

The two divisions told different stories:

- **FirstService Residential** revenue rose **5% to US$633.3M, all organic**, driven by new contract wins. Its EBITDA margin widened 30 basis points to 11.3%, the eighth straight quarter with some margin improvement, and management expects at least double that gain in the fourth quarter after exiting a money-losing seasonal pool business.
- **FirstService Brands** revenue rose **7% to US$898.4M** (3% organic). Restoration (Paul Davis and First Onsite) grew **10%** on strong activity in Canada and Hawaii, and Century Fire grew **more than 10%**. Roofing revenue fell **4%**, and high single digits organically, on weak demand and tough pricing in Florida and Las Vegas, so the division's EBITDA margin slipped 80 basis points to 11.3%.

The biggest move was on the balance sheet. In the quarter, FirstService repurchased **2.2 million shares for a little more than US$300M** under its normal course issuer bid, bringing the total since the start of the second quarter to **4.1 million shares, or about 9% of the shares outstanding**. It has room for another 2.6 million shares under the current bid, which runs to late August 2027. The cost shows up in the debt: net debt rose to **US$1.4B, or 2.4 times trailing EBITDA**, up from 1.8 times at the end of June. Management called mid-2s its comfort zone and said buybacks will now be "more judicious", in step with free cash flow. Operating cash flow after working capital was US$84.2M in the quarter, held back by restoration work in progress.

## Business overview: FirstService vs property-services peers

FirstService earns money in two ways. FirstService Residential manages condo towers, master-planned communities and homeowner associations across North America — a recurring, contract-based business with steady but thin margins. FirstService Brands is a group of essential property services: restoration after fires, floods and storms; Century Fire's sprinkler and fire-protection work; Roofing Corp. of America; and franchised home-services brands such as California Closets and CertaPro Painters. Residential is the steady half; restoration, roofing and home improvement are more cyclical and weather-driven.

On the screener, FirstService sits in the Finance sector, which is dominated by banks, insurers and REITs that are not business-comparable. We kept the two same-sector property-services names — **Colliers (CIGI)**, the commercial real-estate services firm that was spun out of the old FirstService in 2015, and **Altus Group (AIF)**, a real-estate advisory and analytics company — and added **Boyd Group (BYD)** as a documented closest-sector exception. Boyd sits in Consumer services, but its collision-repair roll-up is the closest TSX match for the multi-location, acquire-and-integrate model of FirstService Brands. Banks, insurers, REITs and sub-C$1B names were left out.

FirstService trades at about **37x trailing earnings**, the lowest P/E in the table; Boyd's depressed profits push its P/E to about 174 and drag the group average up to 84, so the average isn't a useful yardstick here. On sales, FirstService is about 1.1x, and on free cash flow about 17.6x, right at the group average of 18.3x. The forward view matters more than the trailing one. On the October 8 price, FirstService traded at about 20x this year's expected earnings; at Friday's C$200.92 close, that rises to roughly 22x. That is far below what investors paid for this compounder in recent years, which is why management chose to buy back stock. The catch is that the cheaper multiple reflects slower growth and a roofing business with limited visibility, not just a sell-off.

## Why it matters: from acquisition compounder to buyback story

For years, FirstService was priced as a **steady compounder that grew through small acquisitions**: buy a regional restoration, fire or roofing business, plug it into a national brand, repeat. That model earned premium multiples.

The **new narrative** is different. With good deals scarce, private-equity competition still high and the stock down sharply, management is betting on itself instead, buying back almost a tenth of the company in two quarters. That boosts earnings per share, but it also lifts leverage and makes 2027 growth depend more on organic gains in roofing, restoration and Residential. The open question is whether the roofing slump and the weak housing market fade before the buyback has to slow.

## Bull case

1. **Recurring core.** Residential is contract-based and growing 5% organically, with eight straight quarters of margin gains and a bigger lift guided for the fourth quarter.
2. **Fewer shares.** About 9% fewer shares since April means each dollar of profit goes further, helping adjusted EPS grow faster than EBITDA.
3. **Growing pieces.** Restoration grew 10% and Century Fire more than 10%, and the roofing backlog ended the quarter at its highest level in a year.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Roofing is still shrinking.** Revenue fell again, and management expects tough pricing in Florida to last, with limited visibility until a new ERP system is in place in 2027.
2. **Weak housing and consumer.** Housing activity and consumer sentiment are near historic lows, which weighs on home-services brands like California Closets and CertaPro.
3. **More debt, slower buybacks.** Leverage is up to 2.4x, so the buyback has to slow, and GAAP EPS fell in the quarter even as adjusted EPS rose.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter results** (early 2027), and whether Residential margins expand by the promised 60-plus basis points.
2. **The 2027 outlook** at year-end results in February.
3. **Roofing backlog** turning into revenue, especially outside Florida.
4. **The buyback**: how much of the remaining 2.6 million shares gets used, and where leverage settles.

## Bottom line

FirstService delivered a clean quarter in a tough market: organic growth ahead of plan, steady Residential margins, and strength in restoration and fire protection that offset a weak roofing business. Its most important decision, though, was buying back about 9% of its shares since April, which shows management thinks the stock is cheap after a 31% slide. At about 37x trailing and roughly 22x expected earnings at Friday's price, it is the lowest P/E in its peer group, but no longer a growth-premium story. The next test is whether 2027 brings organic growth strong enough to justify that bet now that debt is higher and buybacks have to slow.
