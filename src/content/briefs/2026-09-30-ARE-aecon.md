## The short version: a nuclear backlog stack after a doubled stock

Aecon Group (TSX: ARE), the Toronto-based infrastructure contractor, closed at **CA$52.22** on the September 24, 2026 screener print, up about **132%** over twelve months and about **18%** in the last month. On **September 21**, Ontario Power Generation awarded about **C$3B** of Pickering Nuclear refurbishment contracts, and Aecon's share of about **C$1.75B** goes into third-quarter Construction backlog. The new story is **a multi-year nuclear backlog**, with Darlington as the execution proof.

The catch: Aecon still shows a trailing **net loss** and trades near **28x forward earnings**. The question for a new buyer is whether a stock that more than doubled in a year has already priced the win. Below we go through the Pickering awards, the two contract scopes, and how Aecon compares with WSP, Stantec, AtkinsRéalis, Bird Construction and Badger on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the Pickering Nuclear refurbishment awards

On **September 21, 2026**, Aecon said its joint venture with **AtkinsRéalis** and its consortium with **Siemens Energy** had been awarded Pickering Nuclear Generating Station Refurbishment contracts by **Ontario Power Generation**, with an aggregate value of about **C$3B**. The details:

- Aecon's share is about **C$1.75B**, to be added to **Construction backlog in Q3 2026**.
- **Retube, Feeder and Boiler Replacement (RFBR), Unit 5:** Aecon's long-standing **50/50 joint venture with AtkinsRéalis** won a **C$1.7B** contract, the first RFBR unit, with similar work anticipated on **Units 6–8** later, pending regulatory approval.
- **Turbine Generator Replacement:** an **Aecon-majority consortium with Siemens Energy** won a **C$1.3B** contract; Aecon provides construction services and material procurement, including 14 new steam turbine rotors, generator overhauls, auxiliaries and controls.
- Prerequisite work has begun; **project execution is expected to begin in January 2027**, pending regulatory approval.

Management pointed to **lessons learned at Darlington**, OPG's other refurbishment program, where Aecon has been a key contractor. That track record is why the award matters: nuclear refurbishment is specialized, long-cycle work with few qualified bidders.

### What the award does and doesn't change

The award is a **backlog event, not an earnings event**. Execution starts in January 2027, so revenue from Pickering will build over several years, and the margin depends on how well the joint ventures manage schedule, labour and supply chains. The stock jumped on the news, with reports citing prices near **C$53.55**; by the September 29 close it was **C$52.31**, essentially in line with the screener price.

The weak spot is the earnings base underneath. Over the last twelve months Aecon reported a **net loss of about C$65M** on about **C$6B** of revenue, so its trailing P/E and payout ratio are not meaningful. **Q3 2026 results are scheduled for October 29**, the first chance to see the Pickering add in reported backlog.

## Business overview: Aecon vs construction and engineering peers

Aecon builds and maintains infrastructure across Canada and in select international markets: civil projects (transit, highways, bridges), utilities, industrial work and nuclear services, plus concessions where it co-owns finished projects. The steadier part of the business is long-term utility and nuclear work; the riskier part is large fixed-price civil projects, where cost overruns land directly on earnings.

On the TSX screener, Aecon sits in the **Industrial services** sector. The peers are the liquid construction and engineering names in that sector: **WSP Global (WSP)** and **Stantec (STN)**, the large engineering and design consultancies; **AtkinsRéalis (ATRL)**, Aecon's Pickering joint-venture partner and a major nuclear engineering firm; **Bird Construction (BDT)**, a Canadian general contractor; and **Badger (BDGI)**, a hydrovac excavation services company. Enbridge, its preferred shares and other pipeline names were left out as a different business, along with microcaps.

## Why it matters: from lumpy contractor to nuclear backlog stack

The old narrative was Aecon as **a Canadian contractor swinging between civil wins and nuclear optionality**, with thin earnings optics. The new narrative is **a multi-year Pickering backlog stack**: Unit 5 first, more units possible, on top of other recent backlog adds, with Darlington credibility as the execution proof.

Growth investors care whether C$1.75B of nuclear work converts cleanly into margin. Value investors care whether a stock that more than doubled in a year has already priced the win. The open question is whether nuclear work can lift company-wide margins enough to turn a trailing loss into steady profit.

## Bull case

1. **A step-change in nuclear visibility.** About C$1.75B of Pickering work goes into Q3 backlog, with Unit 5 first and Units 6–8 possible later.
2. **Cheap on sales and cash.** Aecon screens cheapest in the group on P/S, P/FCF and price to cash while paying the highest indicated yield.
3. **Credible execution.** Targets cluster in the mid-to-high C$50s with several Buys above C$60, and the Darlington record supports delivery.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Backlog isn't profit.** Trailing net income is still a loss of about C$65M on about C$6B of revenue.
2. **Already priced?** A 132% one-year run means a lot of good news may be in the stock before execution starts in January 2027.
3. **Execution risk.** Nuclear refurbishment carries schedule, labour, supply-chain and regulatory risk across joint ventures, and any miss hits a thin earnings base fast.

## What to watch

1. **Q3 results on October 29.** The Construction backlog print after the Pickering add, and any change in margins.
2. **Regulatory approval and the January 2027 start.** Whether execution begins on schedule.
3. **Units 6–8.** Whether OPG awards the remaining RFBR scopes to the Aecon–AtkinsRéalis joint venture.
4. **Margins.** Whether the rising nuclear mix lifts margins versus civil and industrial work, and whether targets move above the ~C$57 consensus.

## Bottom line

Aecon has earned its place among Canada's nuclear refurbishment specialists, and the Pickering awards add about C$1.75B of multi-year work on the back of its Darlington record. On sales and cash flow it is the cheapest stock in its peer group, with the highest yield. The issue is earnings: a trailing loss, a 28x forward multiple and a share price that more than doubled in a year leave the stock only about 9% below consensus. The next test is the October 29 print, and whether backlog growth starts showing up as margin.
