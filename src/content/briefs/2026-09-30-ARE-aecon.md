## What happened (news + tape)

On **September 21, 2026**, Aecon announced that its teams were awarded Pickering Nuclear Generating Station Refurbishment contracts by **Ontario Power Generation** with an aggregate value of **~C$3 billion**:

- Aecon’s share is **~C$1.75 billion**, added to **Construction backlog in Q3 2026**.
- **Retube, Feeder and Boiler Replacement (Unit 5):** Aecon’s long-standing **50/50 joint venture with AtkinsRéalis** was awarded a **C$1.7 billion** RFBR contract — the first RFBR unit, with similar work anticipated on Units 6–8 later, pending regulatory approval.
- **Turbine Generator Replacement:** An Aecon-majority **consortium with Siemens Energy** was awarded a **C$1.3 billion** contract (construction services and material procurement).
- Prerequisite work has begun; project execution is expected to begin in **January 2027**, pending regulatory approval. Management pointed to **Darlington** lessons learned.
- **Q3 2026 results** are scheduled for **October 29, 2026**.

Street notes after the award include Stifel Buy at **C$59** and RBC Hold at **C$50**. Consensus from **10 analysts** sits near **C$57** (S&P Global via stockanalysis, pulled September 30, 2026). The tape jumped on the news (reports cited ~**C$53.55**); the September 29 close was **CA$52.31**, essentially in line with the screener print of **CA$52.22**.

## Why it matters

The old story: a Canadian contractor swinging between civil wins and nuclear optionality with thin earnings optics. The new story: a multi-year Pickering backlog stack — Unit 5 first, more units possible — with Darlington credibility as the execution proof. Growth investors care whether C$1.75 billion of nuclear work converts cleanly into margin. Value investors care whether a roughly doubled one-year tape has already priced the win.

## Bull case

1. **C$1.75B Pickering into Q3 backlog** — Unit 5 first, with more RFBR units possible later.
2. Cheapest versus these peers on **P/S, P/FCF, and Price/Cash**, while still paying the **highest indicated yield** in the set.
3. Street targets still cluster in the **mid-to-high fifties**, with Darlington execution credentials supporting delivery.

## Bear case

1. Last-twelve-months net income is still a **~$65 million loss** on **~$6 billion** of revenue — backlog is not earned profit.
2. The stock already ran hard (**~+132% over 1 year**) — a lot of good news may be in the price before January 2027 execution starts.
3. Nuclear refurbishment carries **schedule, labour, supply-chain and regulatory risk** across joint ventures on a thin earnings base.

## What to watch next

1. **October 29** third-quarter results and the Construction backlog print after the Pickering add.
2. Regulatory approval path and the **January 2027** execution start.
3. Whether **Units 6–8 RFBR** scopes get awarded later.
4. Margin trajectory as nuclear mix rises versus civil and industrial work.
