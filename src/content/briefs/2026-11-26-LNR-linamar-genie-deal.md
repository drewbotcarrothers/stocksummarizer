## The short version: a big bet on lifts, at a low price

Linamar Corporation (TSX: LNR), the Guelph, Ontario manufacturer best known for auto parts, closed at **CA$90.83** on Wednesday, November 25, 2026. That is up **about 8% in two sessions**, from C$83.84 on November 23. The trigger: that Monday evening, Linamar agreed to buy **Genie**, the boom lift, scissor lift and telehandler business of Terex, for **US$1.4 billion in cash**, roughly C$2.0 billion.

This is a large deal for Linamar. It is worth about 40% of the company's market value before the announcement, and it brings in a business with about US$2.3 billion of yearly sales. Yet before the jump the stock traded at only about **7.3x trailing earnings**, the second-lowest P/E in our peer group. Below we go through the deal, the third quarter behind it, and how Linamar compares with five other TSX manufacturers — Magna, BRP, Martinrea, ATS Corporation and NFI Group — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Linamar buys Genie from Terex (November 23)

On **Monday, November 23, 2026**, after the close, Linamar signed a definitive agreement to buy the Genie access work platforms business from Terex Corporation. Genie will join Linamar's own **Skyjack** brand inside its Industrial segment, in a new unit called Linamar Access Group. The two brands will keep separate sales and service teams. The deal brings:

- **About US$2.3 billion of sales** over the last twelve months, with boom lifts just over half of that,
- **About 4,300 employees** and **six plants in five countries**: two in Washington State, plus Mexico, Italy, India and China,
- **About US$135 million a year** of targeted run-rate synergies, phased in over about 36 months, mainly from procurement, manufacturing, engineering, logistics and shared services,
- a price of **about 5.5x Genie's last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA**, counting those synergies,
- management's expectation that the deal is **immediately accretive to normalized earnings per share**.

Linamar is paying with a **new credit facility** led by National Bank of Canada, not new shares. It expects net debt of **about 1.6x EBITDA at closing**, just above its 1.5x target, and says it can get back below target within about a year. Closing is expected in the **first half of 2027**, after regulatory approvals.

The market reaction built over two days. The shares rose **2.7% to C$86.08** on Tuesday, November 24, the day of the conference call, and another **5.5% to C$90.83** on Wednesday.

### The Q3 print behind the deal

The deal follows a mixed third quarter, reported **November 4, 2026**:

- **Sales up 21% to C$3.08 billion**.
- **Normalized earnings per share up only 3.2% to C$2.59**.

Strong sales growth turning into low single-digit earnings growth is the backdrop. Genie is meant to add a higher-margin, less capital-hungry business to that mix.

## Business overview: Linamar vs vehicle and equipment makers

Linamar runs two segments. **Mobility** makes engine, transmission, driveline, structural and electrified parts for vehicle makers. **Industrial** holds Skyjack access equipment and the MacDon, Salford and Bourgault farm equipment brands. Today Industrial is about 24% of sales.

On the TSX screener, Linamar sits in the **Producer manufacturing** sector. We compare it with the largest liquid names there that make vehicles, vehicle parts or industrial equipment: **Magna (MG)**, the closest auto-parts comparable; **Martinrea (MRE)**, a smaller auto-parts maker; **BRP (DOO)**, which makes snowmobiles, side-by-sides and boats; **ATS Corporation (ATS)**, an automation systems maker; and **NFI Group (NFI)**, a bus builder. Terex and Genie are not TSX-listed.

## Why it matters: from auto supplier to half industrial

Linamar is best known as an auto parts maker, and auto suppliers often trade at low multiples because vehicle production is cyclical and customers squeeze margins. Genie changes the mix. With Genie, Industrial grows to **about 41% of sales**, from about 24%, and Linamar says it would make **about half of normalized operating earnings** (about 49%), because access equipment runs at higher margins than vehicle parts.

The end markets are different too. Rental fleets buy these lifts for **data centres, power projects, factory reshoring and infrastructure** work. Skyjack is strong in scissor lifts and Genie in booms, with little overlap, so the combined group can sell a full lineup to rental companies. Genie also adds plants in the US, Mexico, Italy, India and China, and Linamar has called this its fourth US-based acquisition in four years.

The key question for investors: if the market starts valuing Linamar more like an industrial company, the multiple could rise. TD Cowen's note says investors would need to buy into a re-rating or sum-of-the-parts valuation for that to happen, and it sees few near-term catalysts with a soft fourth quarter.

## Bull case

1. **Accretive with no new shares.** Linamar says the deal adds to normalized earnings per share right away, and it is funded with debt, not equity.
2. **A low price.** About 5.5x Genie's EBITDA after synergies — below where many industrial peers trade.
3. **A complementary fit.** Skyjack scissor lifts plus Genie boom lifts, with little product overlap and a bigger parts and service network.
4. **Balance sheet room.** Net debt only slightly above the 1.5x target at close, and a payout of only about 11%.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Cyclical demand.** Lift orders depend on rental fleet spending, which can stop quickly in a slowdown.
2. **Synergies are targets, not results.** The US$135 million takes about three years and depends on integration going well.
3. **A split Street.** Four of six analysts rate it a Hold, and some see the price as full.
4. **Autos still matter.** Mobility is still more than half of sales, so auto production volumes and tariffs keep driving results.

## What to watch

1. **Regulatory approvals** and a close in the first half of 2027.
2. **Fourth-quarter results and the 2027 outlook**, the first guidance with the deal in view.
3. **Early proof that the synergies are on track** after closing.
4. **How fast debt returns below 1.5x EBITDA.**

## Bottom line

Linamar bought a leading lift brand at a low multiple, and the stock was cheap to begin with: about 7.3x earnings and below book value before the jump. The deal could make Linamar look more like an industrial company than an auto supplier. But the lift market is cyclical, the synergies still have to be delivered, and the Street is split, so the cycle and the integration will set the pace from here.
