## The short version: the income story has been reset

TELUS Corporation (TSX: T), one of Canada's three national wireless and broadband carriers, traded near **CA$12.60** on the September 12, 2026 screener, down about **43%** over twelve months, about **24%** over three months and about **30%** year to date. On **July 31**, under new CEO **Victor Dodig**, TELUS cut its dividend by about **55%**, booked a large second-quarter loss and lowered guidance. The new story is a **balance-sheet and capital-allocation reset**, not a sleepy high-yield telecom.

The screener still showed a trailing yield of about 13%, but that number is stale: the new **C$0.75** annual rate works out to about **6%** on the current price. With the stock near **17x forward earnings**, the question for a new buyer is whether the reset marks the bottom of a long re-rating or just the start of a slower repair. Below we go through the dividend cut, the Q2 print and guidance, and how TELUS compares with its closest TSX telecom peers — BCE, Rogers and Quebecor — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst ratings.

## What happened: a 55% dividend cut and a new capital plan

On **July 31, 2026**, alongside its second-quarter results, TELUS reset its capital-return policy under Victor Dodig. The reset brings:

- a quarterly dividend of **C$0.1875**, or **C$0.75** annualized, down from about **C$1.67** — a cut of about **55%**,
- about **C$2.7B** of cumulative cash savings through 2028, earmarked for **debt reduction**,
- the end of the **DRIP discount** from **October 1, 2026**, which means less dilution from discounted share reinvestment,
- a new free-cash-flow payout band of **45–60%**, down from 60–75%.

For income investors who owned TELUS for the yield, the math changed overnight. For the company, the reset frees cash to repair a stretched balance sheet: net debt sits at **3.5x** adjusted EBITDA, and management now targets **3.0x or lower by the end of 2028** — a timeline pushed back from 2027. Asset sales in the non-core Health business and real estate are also under way. This is a **repair plan, not a growth plan**, at least for the next two years.

### The Q2 print and the guidance cut

The quarter behind the reset was weak:

- **Net loss of about C$1.8B**, or about **−C$1.17** per share, mostly from a roughly **C$2.1B non-cash impairment on TELUS Digital**.
- **Adjusted EPS of C$0.16**, still positive but down **27%** year over year.
- **Mobile network revenue up 1%** to about C$1.7B, with **ARPU of C$56.36** (down 0.4%).
- **Phone net additions of only 17,000** and **internet net additions of 20,000**, while TELUS Digital revenue declined.

Guidance came down too. For 2026, TELUS now expects service revenue **flat to down 2%**, adjusted EBITDA **down 2% to 4%**, Cap-X around **C$2.6B** and free cash flow near **C$1.8B**, down from about C$2.45B before. The lower free cash flow outlook helps explain why the dividend had to move.

## Business overview: TELUS vs Canadian telecom peers

TELUS sells wireless plans, fibre internet (its PureFibre network) and TV and home services across Canada, with a strong base in Western Canada. It also owns TELUS Health and TELUS Digital, the customer-experience and IT-services arm that drove the Q2 writedown. The steady part is the subscription base of wireless and home-internet customers; the swing factors are wireless pricing, population growth and the non-core Digital and Health businesses. Dodig's three priorities are a stronger financial foundation, operational discipline, and profitable growth in wireless, fibre and AI infrastructure.

On the TSX screener, TELUS sits in the **Communications** sector. **Bell (BCE)** is the largest Canadian carrier and went through its own dividend reset earlier. **Rogers (RCI.A)** is the biggest wireless operator and the closest competitor nationally. **Quebecor (QBR.A)**, through Videotron and Freedom Mobile, is the fourth national player and the fastest-growing of the group. Smaller and illiquid names in the sector were left out.

## Why it matters: from sleepy high-yield telecom to balance-sheet reset

The old narrative was TELUS as a **sleepy high-yield telecom**: a dependable, growing dividend backed by wireless and fibre. The new narrative is a **balance-sheet and capital-allocation reset**: cut the payout, end the DRIP discount, write down Digital, lower free cash flow guidance and push leverage repair out to 2028.

DIY investors who owned TELUS for the dividend need to redo the math at C$0.75. Growth and quality investors need to decide whether Dodig's three priorities can re-rate the stock after a multi-year drawdown. The open question is whether the cut is enough — or whether further asset sales and restructuring are needed to hit 3.0x leverage.

## Bull case

1. **Clear deleveraging plan.** About C$2.7B of cash savings through 2028 and an explicit target of 3.0x net debt to EBITDA.
2. **Core network still growing.** Mobile network revenue rose 1%, and the PureFibre build nearing completion should mean lower capital intensity later.
3. **Not a nosebleed valuation.** About 1.0x sales, 1.5x book and 17x forward earnings, with trailing free cash flow near C$2.17B and a more conservative 45–60% payout band.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Trust shock.** A dividend cut, a guidance cut and a C$2.1B impairment in one quarter may keep income holders selling.
2. **Competitive wireless.** Price competition and slower population growth are compressing ARPU (down 0.4%) and net adds (17,000 phones).
3. **Leverage still high.** At 3.5x, the path to 3.0x depends on free cash flow, lower Cap-X and asset sales actually closing.

## What to watch

1. **Stabilization.** Whether the stock finds a floor after digesting the 55% reset.
2. **The November capital-returns update** that Dodig flagged alongside Q3 results.
3. **Leverage and asset sales.** Progress toward 3.0x and any proceeds from Health or real-estate monetization.
4. **Wireless competition.** ARPU, churn and net adds versus Rogers and BCE, plus whether TELUS Digital turns around or needs more restructuring.

## Bottom line

TELUS has done the hard thing: it cut the dividend, ended the discounted DRIP and set out a clear deleveraging target under a new CEO. That makes the dividend more sustainable, but it also wrote down Digital, lowered guidance and pushed the leverage goal out to 2028. The issue is trust and execution more than price. At about 17x forward earnings and roughly 1x sales after a 43% one-year fall, the stock prices in a lot of bad news, yet the Street still rates it only Neutral. The next test is the November capital-returns update and whether free cash flow holds near the C$1.8B guide.
