## The short version: a gas compression company turns into a data center power play

Enerflex (TSX: EFX), the Calgary-based maker of natural gas compression, processing and power equipment, has spent the past year being re-rated by the market. The stock was already up about **112%** over one year and about **12%** over one month before **October 13, 2026**, when it announced an order for **more than 1 gigawatt** of gas-fired power units for a single North American data center. That session the shares jumped **14.2%** to **C$39.11**. The new story: Enerflex is no longer just an oil-and-gas equipment supplier, but a builder of on-site power for the AI build-out.

The proof point is the backlog. Together with an award of about **450 megawatts** on October 1 and other projects, Enerflex's behind-the-meter power backlog now tops **1.75 gigawatts**, with deliveries through 2028. What it has not said is what any of it is worth in dollars. For a new buyer, the question is how much of that is already in a stock that trades near **58x** trailing earnings but closer to **17x** forward earnings before the jump. Below we go through the award, the backlog, and how Enerflex compares with Hammond Power, TerraVest, ATS and Trican on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: a gigawatt-scale award for one data center

On **October 13, 2026**, before the market opened, Enerflex announced a major Engineered Systems project:

- Enerflex will **design, engineer, fabricate and assemble more than 1 gigawatt** of low-emission, **behind-the-meter** natural gas-fired power generation units
- The customer is a **large-scale North American data center development**; neither the customer nor the contract value was disclosed
- The work will run through Enerflex's **North American manufacturing operations**
- Combined with other awards, including about **450 MW** announced on **October 1, 2026**, the behind-the-meter power backlog now **exceeds 1.75 GW**, with deliveries **through 2028**
- Enerflex has authorized about **$100 million** of investment in its Engineered Systems facilities and capabilities, of which **$15 million** is in its 2026 capital spending guidance

"Behind-the-meter" means the power is generated on site and feeds the data center directly, so the developer does not have to wait for a utility grid connection. That is exactly the bottleneck AI data centers are running into, and it is why a company best known for gas compression packages is suddenly winning gigawatt-scale power orders. Management also pointed to follow-on work: commissioning, installation, aftermarket service and its ReliaCore digital monitoring platform.

### The backlog behind the rally

The October 1 award was already a sizable step: about **450 MW** for a North American data center developer, with deliveries starting in 2027 and finishing in 2028. At the time, Enerflex said its power opportunity pipeline **continued to exceed 2 GW**. The October 13 order is more than twice that size on its own.

For scale, Enerflex's whole **Engineered Systems backlog** was about **US$1.5B** at **June 30, 2026** (US$1.453B, up from US$1.227B a year earlier), after second-quarter bookings of US$488M and a **1.6x** book-to-bill. Second-quarter adjusted EBITDA was **US$128M**, slightly below the year-earlier US$130M. Most of the 1.75 GW of power work was booked after that June snapshot, so the third-quarter report on **October 29** is the first chance to see it in dollars. On **September 30**, Enerflex also closed the sale of most of its Asia Pacific operations, mainly aftermarket services, to INNIO, sharpening its focus on North America, Latin America and the Middle East.

The market did not wait for the dollar figure. On October 13 the shares rose **14.2%** to **C$39.11**, one of the biggest gains on the TSX in a session where the index rose 0.9%.

## Business overview: Enerflex vs equipment peers

Enerflex builds and services modular natural gas compression and processing equipment, power generation units and water treatment systems, with manufacturing plants in North America. It earns money three ways: one-off **Engineered Systems** projects (the cyclical part, now including data center power), **energy infrastructure** it owns and leases to customers under long contracts, and **after-market services** on installed equipment. The contracted infrastructure and service lines are the steadier half of the business.

In the TSX screener Enerflex sits in **Producer manufacturing**, so the peers come from the same sector, picked for industrial, energy and power-equipment exposure: **Hammond Power** (power transformers, another data center demand story), **TerraVest** (energy and industrial equipment fabrication), **ATS** (automation systems) and **Trican** (oilfield pressure pumping). We left out the auto-parts and vehicle makers in the sector (Magna, Linamar, BRP, NFI, Martinrea), Richelieu (a hardware distributor), and smaller loss-making names such as Ballard Power.

On trailing earnings Enerflex looks expensive at about **58x**, above the group average near **42x**, and closer to **67x** after the jump. The reason is a thin trailing profit: diluted earnings per share over the last twelve months were only about **C$0.59**, down about 59% from a year earlier. Analysts expect adjusted earnings to **nearly double** this year, which is why the **forward P/E** of about **17x** (roughly **20x** at C$39.11) sits below the group average of about 19x and far below Hammond Power and TerraVest.

On other measures Enerflex is middle of the pack: about **1.2x** sales and **2.5x** book, both below the averages, and about **12x free cash flow**, roughly in line with the group's 13x. **Trican** and **ATS** are the cheapest names on most columns, but both are shrinking or flat businesses, so cheap is not the same as better value. For Enerflex, the multiple question really turns on whether the power backlog lifts margins and cash flow in 2027 and 2028.

## Why it matters: from gas-cycle supplier to AI power builder

The **old story** was a gas compression and processing company whose orders rose and fell with oil and gas drilling budgets. The **new story** is a supplier of on-site power for **AI data centers**, with a backlog that now runs into 2028 and a pipeline that management described as above 2 GW earlier this month.

That shift matters for how investors value the business. Equipment makers tied to commodity cycles usually trade on low multiples; companies with multi-year, contracted growth tied to data centers often trade on much higher ones. The open question is whether these orders turn into real margin and cash flow, or whether the market is pricing in more than the contracts will deliver once the dollar values are disclosed.

## Bull case

1. **Gigawatt-scale wins.** A single order above 1 GW shows Enerflex can compete for the largest behind-the-meter jobs, and the pipeline was above 2 GW in early October.
2. **Modest forward multiples.** About 17–20x forward earnings and about 12x free cash flow are not demanding for a company with a multi-year data center backlog.
3. **Estimates may move up.** Many analyst forecasts and targets were set before the October 13 award, so numbers could still be revised higher after the third-quarter report.

## Bear case and risks

1. **No dollar value yet.** Investors are trading on gigawatts, not disclosed revenue or margin, and the customer is unnamed.
2. **Execution risk.** Large builds through 2028 carry delay and cost-overrun risk, while the roughly $100M plant expansion is still underway.
3. **A big run already.** The stock more than doubled in a year before the 14% jump, and trailing earnings are thin, so any disappointment could hit hard.

## What to watch

1. **Third-quarter results on October 29, 2026**, and any dollar value or margin detail for the power backlog.
2. **Margins** on the new power work versus the traditional compression business.
3. **Progress on the ~$100M facility expansion** and whether deliveries stay on the 2027–2028 schedule.
4. **More data center awards** from the opportunity pipeline, and analyst target changes after the print.

## Bottom line

Enerflex has earned its re-rating with two data center awards in two weeks and a power backlog above 1.75 GW, turning a cyclical gas equipment maker into one of the TSX's clearest AI power plays. The problem is visibility: the company has disclosed gigawatts, not dollars, and the stock has already more than doubled. On forward earnings the valuation still looks reasonable, but only if the backlog converts into higher margins and cash flow. The October 29 third-quarter report is the next real test.
