## The short version: the bidder paid first

Quebecor Inc. (TSX: QBR.B) owns **Videotron** in Quebec and **Freedom Mobile** across the rest of Canada, and since buying Freedom in 2023 it has been the country's fourth national wireless carrier and its price fighter. On **Monday, November 16, 2026**, it went public with a non-binding proposal to buy **Telus**, a company with more than three times its revenue.

The market's answer came fast. Quebecor's Class B shares fell from **C$77.06 at the November 13 close to C$54.31 by November 18**, and closed **C$58.40 on November 19**: **down about 24%** in four sessions. Because part of the offer is paid in Quebecor shares, the drop also shrank the offer, from the **C$19.45 a Telus share** Quebecor quoted to about **C$16.28** at Thursday's close. Then a report that **BCE** had held early talks with Telus put a possible rival bidder on the table.

Below we go through the proposal and the market reaction, how Quebecor compared with BCE, Rogers, TELUS and Cogeco on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets just before the bid, and what to watch next.

## What happened: the Telus proposal (November 16)

Quebecor said it first approached Telus in June with a written, non-binding proposal, and sent an enhanced version on **November 9**. On November 16 it made the key terms public.

### The offer and what it is worth now

- **Per Telus share: C$6.35 in cash plus 0.1700 of a Quebecor Class B share.**
- Quebecor valued that at about **C$19.45 a share, a 60% premium** to Telus's November 13 close of C$12.14, or about **C$31 billion** in total.
- Telus shareholders would hold about **54% of the economic interest** in the combined company, but Quebecor's controlling shareholder would keep **voting control**.
- Quebecor estimates about **C$160 million a year of operating synergies** and **C$235 million a year of capital efficiencies**, on top of Telus's own C$900 million efficiency program.
- It says it would keep its **C$1.80 annual dividend**, and would keep Telus's brands and Vancouver head office.

The stock half of the offer moves with Quebecor's share price. At the November 19 close of C$58.40, the package works out to **C$6.35 + 0.17 × C$58.40 ≈ C$16.28 a Telus share**, about 16% less than the headline value. Telus closed that day at C$15.50, a little below the offer, which suggests the market still sees a real chance that no deal happens on these terms.

### The market reaction and the Bell report

Quebecor's Class B shares fell **15.3% on November 16** (to C$65.25), **12.6% on November 17** (C$57.00) and **4.7% on November 18** (C$54.31). Telus's board, through a special committee chaired by John Manley, said the proposal undervalues the company, leaves the regulatory risk with Telus shareholders and exposes them to swings in Quebecor's share price during a long review. It is also reviewing other strategic options.

On the evening of November 18, The Globe and Mail reported, citing unnamed sources, that **Bell's parent BCE had held preliminary talks with Telus** and hired advisers. On **November 19, Telus jumped 13.7% to C$15.50** and **Quebecor bounced 7.5% to C$58.40**, even as the S&P/TSX Composite fell 1.6% to a six-month low. Quebecor responded with a statement arguing that a Bell–Telus combination would be bad for consumers, and said it meets Telus management on **November 23**.

On trailing earnings Quebecor looked ordinary: **18.4x, close to the 20.0x average** of the three companies with positive earnings. On every other measure it was the premium name: **16.5x forward earnings vs an 11.5x average**, about **3.1x sales vs 1.2x**, and the highest **EV/EBITDA at 9.7x vs a 6.7x average** (BCE 6.8x, Rogers 7.4x, TELUS 7.0x). Against the pre-bid Class A price the table shows almost no upside for Quebecor, but against Thursday's QBR.B close of C$58.40 the **C$79.50 average is about 36% higher**. That average is unlikely to have caught every cut, and the direction is down. **BMO downgraded Quebecor to Market Perform (Hold) and cut its target to C$70 from C$80**, arguing that the deal turns a simple growth and capital-return story into a multi-year integration and deleveraging program: pro forma leverage would rise to about **4.1x EBITDA** from Quebecor's **2.69x**, with buybacks paused. **Desjardins kept a Buy but cut its target to C$76 from C$82**, and assigns only a **25% probability** to a deal closing.

## Why it matters: a challenger trying to become an incumbent

Quebecor's case for years has been that it is the disruptor: Videotron in Quebec, then Freedom Mobile nationally after the Rogers–Shaw remedies, with lower prices than the Big 3. Buying Telus would **cut the national wireless market from four big players back to three**, the opposite of what Ottawa has spent years trying to build. That is why the **Competition Bureau, the CRTC and ISED** (for spectrum transfers) would all look hard at a deal, and why Telus wants protection from that risk.

The balance sheet is the other half. Quebecor's **net debt was about C$6.8B** at the latest quarter; Telus carried about **C$30.1B**, more than four times as much. Even with the stock component, the combined company would be far more levered than Quebecor on its own, and the bid also signals that Quebecor is willing to do large deals, which some investors will now price in even if this one fails.

## Bull case

- **A lot of risk may already be priced in.** The shares fell about a quarter in four sessions, erasing most of the pre-bid premium.
- **A Bell win could be a good outcome for Quebecor holders.** If BCE ends up with Telus, Quebecor keeps its cleaner balance sheet, its growth story and a fresh argument with regulators about competition.
- **If a deal happens, the savings are real money.** Quebecor targets about **C$395 million a year** (C$160M operating plus C$235M capital spending).
- **The dividend is well covered** (about 36% payout) and now yields about **3.1%**.

## Bear case and risks

- **Regulators may block a deal** that takes the national market from four carriers to three, after years spent fostering a fourth.
- **Telus's debt raises leverage** (about 4.1x in BMO's view) and pauses buybacks for years.
- **Quebecor may have to pay more**, through a higher offer, a reverse termination fee or a bidding war with Bell.
- **Even with no deal, the valuation may not fully recover**, if investors now treat Quebecor as a riskier, deal-hungry company rather than a pure growth story.

## What to watch

1. **The November 23 meeting** between Quebecor and Telus management.
2. **Whether BCE makes a formal offer** for Telus, and what remedies it proposes to Ottawa.
3. **Any signal from the Competition Bureau, the CRTC or ISED** on a four-to-three deal.
4. **Whether Quebecor improves its terms, adds deal protection, or walks away.**

## Bottom line

Quebecor made a bold bet on Telus, and its own shareholders paid for it first: about 24% of the stock's value, and about C$3 of the offer's per-share value along with it. From here the stock trades on three things the tables cannot show yet: Telus's board, a possible Bell bid and the regulators.
