## The short version: the mine behind almost half its copper is shut

Ero Copper Corp. (TSX: ERO), a Brazil-focused copper producer listed in Toronto and New York, closed at **CA$31.16** on Friday, November 27, 2026, down **2.96%** after falling as much as **6.7%** in the morning. Late on Thursday, the company said **all mining and processing at its Tucumã Operation** in Pará state is temporarily suspended, on orders from the state environment agency, after heavy rain caused two water overflows at the site. There is **no restart date** and no updated production guidance yet.

That matters because Tucumã was guided to produce about **48% of Ero's 2026 copper** at the midpoint, and it is the company's lower-cost mine. At the same time, Ero already traded at about **6x trailing earnings** on November 20, before the halt, the lowest multiple in our six-name copper peer group. Below we go through what happened, what the halt could cost, and how Ero compares with Lundin Mining, First Quantum, Hudbay Minerals, Capstone Copper and Trekor Metals on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Tucumã suspended (November 26)

On **November 26, 2026**, Ero said production at Tucumã had been stopped in compliance with a notice from Pará's environment and sustainability secretariat (SEMAS). The sequence, from the company and the regulator:

- **Rain:** about **275 mm** fell at the site in the first 20 days of November, roughly **170% above** normal rainfall for the whole month, with short bursts above 70 mm an hour, which Ero ties to a strong El Niño year.
- **Overflows:** the rain overwhelmed the pumps at a surface contact-water sump, causing **two localized overflow events**. SEMAS then found **abnormal pH readings** in local water samples near the operation.
- **Mining first, then processing:** SEMAS initially ordered mining to stop while the mill kept running; processing has since stopped too.
- **Fines:** the state fines tied to the overflows total about **US$2.9M** (BRL 16.8M). SEMAS said the penalty was raised because it was not notified within the required 48 hours; Ero says it did notify the agency.
- **A second, disputed fine:** the municipality of São Félix do Xingu, about 50 km away, issued a fine of about **US$1.9M** over two fish-death reports that came before the overflows. Ero says it has not received documentation supporting that fine and has found no link to Tucumã.

The restart path is clear in shape, if not in timing: Ero expects to file a final **remediation plan** shortly, and Tucumã can resume once SEMAS reviews and approves it.

### The market reaction

The stock fell to **C$29.97** on Friday morning, near the bottom of its one-year range of about C$29.28 to C$56.62, before recovering to close at **C$31.16**. That was a weak showing on a strong day: the S&P/TSX Composite rose 1.2% and most copper names rallied with metal prices. Ero was one of the session's worst performers on the index.

## Business overview: Ero Copper vs TSX copper miners

Ero runs two copper operations in Brazil: the **Caraíba Operations** in Bahia, its long-running underground and open-pit complex, and **Tucumã**, an open-pit mine in the Carajás mineral province that started commercial production in July 2025. Copper is the story, and Tucumã is the growth piece.

On the TSX screener, Ero sits in the **Non-energy minerals** sector. We compare it with the other copper-focused TSX producers: **Lundin Mining (LUN)** and **First Quantum (FM)**, the two large diversified copper groups; **Hudbay Minerals (HBM)**, with copper operations in Peru and Canada; **Capstone Copper (CS)**, a mid-size Americas producer; and **Trekor Metals (TKO)**, a smaller producer close to Ero in size. Teck was left out because its class B shares are not in this screener export and it is far more diversified.

## Why it matters: Ero's growth engine is on pause

Tucumã was meant to be the mine that changed Ero's profile. It was guided to produce **32,500 to 37,500 tonnes** of copper in 2026, about **48%** of the company's 67,500 to 77,500 tonne guidance at the midpoint. Through September it had already produced **26,383 tonnes**, and the fourth quarter was expected to be its strongest of the year, helped by higher mill throughput and better recoveries. New tailings filters were scheduled for commissioning before year-end to support a 4 million tonne a year processing rate in 2027.

It is also the cheaper mine to run. Tucumã's third-quarter C1 cash cost was **US$2.12 a pound**, versus **US$2.89** at Caraíba. Losing low-cost tonnes hurts margins more than the volume figure alone suggests.

Analysts have started to size the damage. **TD Securities** estimates each week offline costs about **700 tonnes** of copper and about **3%** of its fourth-quarter EBITDA forecast. **National Bank Financial** puts Tucumã at about **21%** of Ero's net asset value, with a 10-day halt trimming about 4.3% from its Q4 EBITDA estimate. **Jefferies** says a restart within two weeks is possible, but visibility is limited, and if Tucumã produces nothing more this year it would land at the low end of its 2026 range.

## Bull case

1. **A defined restart path.** The halt is tied to a remediation plan that Ero expects to file shortly, and SEMAS approval is the stated condition for restarting.
2. **The lowest multiples in the group.** About 6x trailing earnings, 4.6x forward earnings and 4.1x EV/EBITDA, all the cheapest of six TSX copper miners, at November 20 prices.
3. **Large analyst upside.** The C$54.37 average target sits far above the current price, even if some of it comes out after the halt.
4. **Growth capacity still coming.** New tailings filters were due by year-end to support higher output at Tucumã in 2027.

## Bear case and risks

1. **No restart date, no new guidance.** Until SEMAS approves the plan, investors can't size the lost tonnes.
2. **The strongest quarter is at risk.** Q4 was meant to be Tucumã's best quarter of 2026, so the timing hurts.
3. **Closer oversight.** Fines, a disputed 48-hour notification, fish-death claims and local water concerns could mean tighter rules and slower approvals in Pará.
4. **Copper price exposure.** Like its peers, Ero is tied to copper prices, which fell sharply from September's record; the group lost about 30% in a month.

## What to watch

1. **Remediation plan approval.** When Ero files the plan, and when SEMAS signs off and Tucumã restarts.
2. **2026 guidance.** Any revision to the 67,500 to 77,500 tonne consolidated range or Tucumã's 32,500 to 37,500 tonnes.
3. **Analyst targets.** Revisions to the C$54.37 average now that the halt is known.
4. **Tailings filters.** Whether commissioning stays on track for year-end and the 2027 throughput plan.

## Bottom line

Ero Copper has built a second mine that was supposed to carry almost half of this year's copper, and on November 26 a Brazilian regulator stopped it after rain-driven overflows. Before the news, the stock already traded at about **6x trailing earnings** and **4.1x EV/EBITDA**, the lowest in its TSX copper peer group, after a sector-wide fall of about a third in a month. That makes Ero look cheap on paper. Whether it is cheap or a trap now depends mostly on one thing: how quickly the cleanup plan is approved and Tucumã restarts.
