## The short version: a steady quarter, then a runoff scare

Trisura Group (TSX: TSU) is a Toronto-based specialty insurer that writes surety bonds, corporate insurance and warranty coverage, and fronts U.S. insurance programs for partners and reinsurers. On **November 6, 2026**, the shares fell **29.9% in a single session, to C$29.58 from C$42.21**, a new 52-week low. On our November 3 screener — just before the results — the stock was **up about 11% over twelve months** but **down about 1% over the prior month**, while every insurer in our peer set was rising.

The odd part is that the quarter looked steady on paper: operating earnings per share actually rose. What spooked investors was a jump in U.S. Programs claims and a disclosure that a bankrupt claims-service partner is tied to four runoff programs — with no number attached yet. After the fall, Trisura trades near **1.4x book value**, roughly level with Fairfax as the cheapest name in the group. The question for a new buyer is whether that discount already prices the unknown bill. Below we go through the print, the runoff risk, and how Trisura compares with Intact, Definity, Fairfax and iA Financial on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results and an unquantified runoff exposure

On **November 5, 2026**, Trisura reported third-quarter results (quarter ended September 30; all figures in Canadian dollars). The stock reaction came the next day, after the earnings call on November 6.

- **Operating EPS C$0.73**, up 2.8% from C$0.71 a year earlier; **reported EPS C$0.54**, down from C$0.79,
- **Operating net income C$35.0 million** (+1.7%); **net income C$26.2 million** (−32.1%),
- **Primary lines** (surety, corporate insurance, warranty) grew net insurance revenue **12.5%** and underwriting income **15.5%**; surety gross premiums rose **26%**,
- **Net investment income C$23.8 million**, up **18.5%**,
- **U.S. Programs loss ratio 80.4%** on casualty claims, including programs already in runoff — management says the core portfolio runs in the low 70s,
- **Gross premiums written C$802 million**, down **6%**, as Canadian fronting and U.S. Programs shrank.

Then came the surprise. Days before the call, a claims service provider affiliated with a reinsurer on **four runoff programs** (two in runoff, two in exited lines) went bankrupt. Trisura says it holds **cash collateral** from the reinsurer, which itself has not declared bankruptcy, and expects the impact to be smaller than similar past events — but it has **not quantified** the exposure. That is the gap the market filled with a ~30% haircut.

### The balance sheet behind the print

The capital picture did not break. **Book value per share was C$20.93**, up **10.7%** year over year (total book value slipped 1.6% from the second quarter on unrealized bond losses and buybacks). **Operating return on equity was 16.2%** (reported ROE 15.1%), and **debt-to-capital was 17.4%**, well under the company's 25% long-term ceiling. Exited lines added a net loss of about **C$3.9 million**, mostly reserve strengthening on runoff commercial auto. Management also said it has contributed more than C$200 million to its U.S. surety entity and expects the overall surety platform to grow at a **mid-to-high-teens** rate in 2027. The weak spots — U.S. Programs and the shrinking fronting book — are exactly what the valuation now has to absorb.

## Business overview: Trisura vs Canadian insurance peers

Trisura runs two kinds of business. The **primary lines** — surety, corporate insurance and warranty — are underwriting businesses where Trisura keeps the risk, and they now make up about 65% of net insurance revenue. **Fronting** (in Canada and through U.S. Programs) is a fee-style model: Trisura issues policies on behalf of program managers and passes most of the risk to reinsurers, which keeps reported margins thin and makes the quality of partners and claims handling central.

On the TSX screener Trisura sits in the **Finance** sector. For peers we used the liquid TSX property-and-casualty and specialty insurers: **Intact (IFC)**, Canada's largest P&C insurer; **Definity (DFY)**, a Canadian P&C and broker-distribution insurer; **Fairfax (FFH)**, a global P&C and reinsurance holding company; and **iA Financial (IAG)**, a life-led insurer with auto/home and dealer-services P&C arms, added as a fifth insurance comparable. Banks, life-only insurers, asset managers and REITs in the Finance sector were left out as non-comparable. No closest-sector exception was needed.

## Why it matters: from growth compounder to "show me the bill"

The **old narrative** was a fast-growing specialty insurer that had moved past its fronting problems, with surety and corporate insurance carrying the growth. The **new narrative** is that runoff programs are still producing surprises, and investors are pricing a hit that management has not sized.

Trisura has been here before: the call referenced a late-2022 reinsurance-recoverable write-down as the comparison point, which is why the market reacts fast to any sign of casualty trouble in old programs. The open question is simple: how big is the runoff bill, and does it dent the book value that the stock now trades near?

## Bull case

1. **Growth engine intact.** Primary lines are about 65% of net insurance revenue and growing by double digits, with surety guided to mid-to-high-teens growth in 2027.
2. **Collateral and capital.** Trisura holds cash collateral from the affiliated reinsurer, and debt-to-capital of 17.4% leaves room to absorb a hit without stretching the balance sheet.
3. **Price already assumes a lot.** At about 1.4x book with a mid-teens operating ROE, the shares are priced near Fairfax, the cheapest insurer in this group.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Unsized exposure.** The claims-partner bankruptcy has not been quantified, and management is just starting the Q4 reserve process — a year-end true-up is possible.
2. **Shrinking fronting.** U.S. Programs and Canadian fronting are contracting, gross premiums fell 6%, and Canadian fronting faces more competition.
3. **Trust discount.** Repeat reserve surprises can keep an insurer's multiple low for years, whatever the growth story in the primary lines.

## What to watch

1. **A number on the runoff exposure** — any update on the claims-partner bankruptcy and the collateral coverage.
2. **Q4 results (expected around February 2027)** — the year-end reserve review and the U.S. Programs loss ratio versus the low-70s core level.
3. **Surety and corporate insurance growth** heading into 2027, including the U.S. surety build-out.
4. **Buybacks near book value** — whether Trisura steps up repurchases at ~1.4x book.

## Bottom line

Trisura's primary lines keep growing, the balance sheet is conservative, and operating earnings per share still rose in the third quarter. The problem is trust: old U.S. programs produced heavier claims, and a bankrupt claims partner left an exposure the company has not sized. The ~30% drop takes the stock to about 1.4x book, roughly in line with Fairfax as the cheapest insurer here, while analyst targets still sit far above the price. The next test is the size of the runoff bill and the year-end reserve review; until that number is known, the discount may persist.
