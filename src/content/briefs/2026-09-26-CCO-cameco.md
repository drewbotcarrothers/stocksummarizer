## The short version: an IPO windfall at a triple-digit multiple

Cameco (TSX: CCO), the Saskatoon-based uranium producer and fuel-cycle company, closed at **CA$124.69** on September 24, 2026. Over twelve months the stock is up only about **2.5%**, and over the last month it fell about **13%**. That is an odd tape for a company that owns **49% of Westinghouse**, the reactor builder that is reportedly lining up a U.S. listing at a valuation **above US$50B**. The new story is no longer just the uranium price: it is a **nuclear fuel-cycle platform** with a stake that may soon get a public market price.

The catch is the valuation. Cameco trades at about **153x trailing earnings** and roughly **62x forward**, so the question for a new buyer is whether the Westinghouse windfall is already in the price. Below we go through the IPO report, the new enrichment deal, a weak second quarter, and how Cameco compares with NexGen, Denison, Teck and Agnico Eagle on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the Westinghouse IPO report and an enrichment deal

On **September 18, 2026**, Bloomberg reported that Westinghouse is targeting a valuation **above US$50B** in a U.S. initial public offering, with a public filing possible **as soon as October**. Westinghouse had already submitted a confidential draft registration statement on **July 31**. The key numbers for Cameco shareholders:

- Cameco owns **49%** of Westinghouse; Brookfield owns the other **51%**.
- At the reported value, Cameco's stake would be worth **more than US$24B** before dilution.
- Cameco and Brookfield bought Westinghouse in 2023; Cameco's share of the cost was about **US$2.1B**.
- Analysts had previously modelled Westinghouse at **US$26–30B**, so the reported target is well above earlier estimates.

Two days earlier, on **September 16**, Cameco signed a second deal. It became the **exclusive buyer and seller** of all future output from Global Laser Enrichment's planned Paducah laser enrichment plant in Kentucky. Cameco owns **49%** of that venture too, and partner Silex called the offtake a key commercial pillar for a future final investment decision.

Neither event adds a dollar to this year's earnings. The IPO would **put a public price on an asset** that today is buried in Cameco's equity accounting, and the enrichment offtake extends Cameco further along the fuel cycle. Both are **strategic and valuation stories, not earnings jolts**, and both still depend on events that haven't happened yet: an actual filing and a plant that has not reached a final investment decision.

### The Q2 print: a soft quarter under the headline

The reported numbers tell a quieter story. Cameco's second quarter, released **July 31, 2026**, showed:

- **Net earnings of C$25M**, down from **C$321M** a year earlier.
- **Adjusted net earnings of C$77M** and **adjusted EBITDA of C$391M**.
- A **C$10M net loss** from Westinghouse (Cameco's share), against **C$126M** of earnings a year earlier, when the prior-year quarter included a one-time boost of roughly **US$170M** from the Dukovany reactor project in the Czech Republic.
- 2026 uranium production guidance **held at 19.5–21.5 million pounds** (Cameco's share), despite flooding-related supply-route disruptions and interruptions at Key Lake, McArthur River and Cigar Lake.
- About **C$1.1B of cash** against about **C$1.0B of debt**.

The weak spots matter for everything below. The tough comparison against last year's Czech contribution **depresses trailing earnings**, which is part of why the trailing P/E looks so extreme. And the northern Saskatchewan disruptions are a reminder that Cameco's operating base is exposed to weather and logistics, even when guidance holds.

## Business overview: Cameco vs uranium and mining peers

Cameco mines and mills uranium in northern Saskatchewan (McArthur River/Key Lake and Cigar Lake are the flagship assets), runs conversion and fuel services, sells uranium under long-term contracts to utilities, and owns 49% of Westinghouse, whose reactor technology and fuel business serve a large share of the world's operating reactors. The steadier part is the long-term contract book and Westinghouse's service work; the swing factors are the uranium price, new reactor orders and mine performance.

On the TSX screener, Cameco sits in the **Non-energy minerals** sector. **NexGen (NXE)** and **Denison (DML)** are the closest business comparables: both are uranium developers in the Athabasca Basin, still building toward production. **Teck (TECK.B)**, the largest diversified miner in the sector, and **Agnico Eagle (AEM)**, the sector's largest company, serve as producer benchmarks for what profitable miners trade at. IsoEnergy (ISO) was skipped because it is under C$1B in market value.

This is the tension. Cameco trades at **153x trailing earnings** and about **62x forward**, against roughly **17–18x** for Agnico Eagle and Teck. Its price-to-sales ratio of about **16x** is double the group average near 8.8x, and its price-to-book of about 7.6x sits above the 5.8x average. NexGen and Denison have no earnings or meaningful sales, so only their book and cash multiples show. On almost every column, **Agnico and Teck are the cheapest names**, which is what established producers with large profits usually look like.

The other catch is **free cash flow**. At about **108x** price to free cash flow, Cameco is the most expensive name on the table, against a 53x average and about 22x for Agnico. The weak Westinghouse quarter flatters none of this. Investors are paying for **the Westinghouse stake and future contracts, not today's profits**. If a public Westinghouse price confirms the stake's value, or contract prices lift earnings, the multiples would compress; if not, the stock has little support from current earnings.

## Why it matters: from uranium miner to fuel-cycle platform

The old narrative was Cameco as **a uranium miner riding the price of U3O8**. The new narrative is **a nuclear fuel-cycle platform**: mines, conversion, enrichment offtake, and 49% of Westinghouse, whose technology runs more than half of the world's operating reactors.

A Westinghouse IPO near the reported range would give that stake a **public market price for the first time**, and that matters most to growth investors. Income investors are still waiting for the bigger payouts analysts expect as free cash flow rises. The open question is how much of a US$24B+ stake value is already reflected in a C$54.3B market value.

## Bull case

1. **A public price for Westinghouse.** A listing could re-rate Cameco's 49% stake, and any sale proceeds could fund buybacks or higher dividends.
2. **Demand locked in across the fuel cycle.** Long-term uranium contracts plus the Global Laser Enrichment offtake give Cameco a role from mine to enriched fuel.
3. **Balance-sheet room.** With about C$1.1B of cash against about C$1.0B of debt, Cameco can wait for better contract prices.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Priced for a lot of good news.** A 153x trailing P/E and about 108x price to free cash flow leave little room for disappointment.
2. **IPO risk.** Recent nuclear listings trade below their offer prices, Holtec postponed its offering, and a U.S. government participation interest in Westinghouse could dilute the owners.
3. **Operations.** Flooded supply routes and disruptions at Key Lake, McArthur River and Cigar Lake show how fragile northern Saskatchewan logistics can be.

## What to watch

1. **Westinghouse's public IPO filing, possibly in October.** The valuation, the share count and how much Cameco sells.
2. **Cameco's Q3 2026 results.** The date was not yet confirmed; watch Westinghouse's contribution and production versus the 19.5–21.5M lb guidance.
3. **A final investment decision on the Kentucky laser enrichment plant**, which would turn the offtake deal into a real revenue line.
4. **Long-term uranium contract prices** and whether analysts hold targets near C$180 after the filing.

## Bottom line

Cameco has earned its place as the TSX's main way to own the nuclear buildout: real uranium revenue, a long-term contract book, a new enrichment offtake and 49% of Westinghouse. The reported IPO target would make that stake worth more than US$24B on paper. The issue is price: at 153x trailing earnings and about 108x free cash flow, the stock already assumes a lot goes right, even after a 13% one-month drop. The next test is the Westinghouse filing itself, and whether the valuation survives contact with public investors.
