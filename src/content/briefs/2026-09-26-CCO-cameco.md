## What happened (news + tape)

On **September 18, 2026**, Bloomberg reported that **Westinghouse** is targeting a valuation **above US$50 billion** in a U.S. IPO, with a public filing possible **as soon as October**. Westinghouse confidentially submitted its draft registration statement on **July 31**.

- **Cameco owns 49%** of Westinghouse (Brookfield owns 51%). At the reported value, Cameco's stake would be worth **more than US$24 billion** before dilution — against an investment of roughly **US$2.1 billion** in 2023. Analysts had previously modelled Westinghouse at US$26–30 billion.
- On **September 16**, Cameco became the **exclusive buyer and seller** of all future output from Global Laser Enrichment's planned Paducah laser enrichment plant in Kentucky. Cameco owns 49% of GLE; partner Silex called the deal a key commercial pillar for a future final investment decision.
- On **September 24**, **TD** set a **C$190** target (Buy) and **RBC** set **C$184** (Outperform).
- Tape: CCO is **down 12.8% over one month** and 14.0% over three months, but **up 2.5% over one year** and about **954% over ten years** (TSX screener).

**Latest quarter — Q2 2026, reported July 31, 2026** (C$):

- **Net earnings C$25M** vs C$321M a year earlier; adjusted net earnings C$77M; adjusted EBITDA C$391M.
- **Westinghouse** swung to a C$10M net loss (Cameco's share) from C$126M of earnings, when the prior-year quarter included a one-time boost of roughly US$170M from the Dukovany reactor project in the Czech Republic.
- 2026 uranium production guidance **held at 19.5–21.5 million pounds** (Cameco's share), despite flooding-related supply-route disruptions and operational interruptions at Key Lake, McArthur River and Cigar Lake.
- Cash of about C$1.1B against total debt of about C$1.0B.

Cameco is the only uranium name in the group with meaningful revenue (C$3.47B over the last twelve months). It is also the most expensive stock on the table: **153x trailing earnings**, about **62x forward**, and **~108x price to free cash flow**, versus 17–18x trailing P/E for Agnico and Teck. Trailing earnings are depressed by the weaker Westinghouse quarter, but investors are clearly paying for the Westinghouse stake and future contracts rather than today's profits. Cameco pays **C$0.24 a share a year**, a yield under 0.2%; NexGen and Denison pay no dividend. MarketBeat's consensus target of **C$179.61** sits about 44% above the September 24 close.

## Why it matters

The old story: a uranium miner riding the price of U3O8. The new story: a nuclear fuel-cycle platform — mines, conversion, enrichment offtake — plus 49% of Westinghouse, whose technology runs more than half of the world's operating reactors. A Westinghouse IPO would give that stake a public market price for the first time. That matters most to growth investors; income investors are still waiting for the bigger payouts analysts expect as free cash flow rises.

## Bull case

1. **A public Westinghouse valuation** could re-rate Cameco's stake, and sale proceeds could fund buybacks or higher dividends. Scotiabank expects Cameco's free cash flow to rise sharply by 2028.
2. **Demand locked in across the fuel cycle:** long-term uranium contracts plus the Global Laser Enrichment offtake.
3. **Net cash balance sheet** gives Cameco room to wait for better contract prices.

## Bear case

1. **Priced for a lot of good news:** triple-digit trailing P/E and ~108x price to free cash flow.
2. **IPO risk:** recent nuclear listings trade below their offer prices, Holtec postponed its offering, and a U.S. government participation interest in Westinghouse could dilute the owners.
3. **Operations:** flooded supply routes and disruptions at Key Lake, McArthur River and Cigar Lake show how fragile northern Saskatchewan logistics can be.

## What to watch next

1. Westinghouse's public IPO filing (possibly October) — valuation, share count and how much Cameco sells.
2. Cameco's Q3 2026 results (date not yet confirmed).
3. A final investment decision on the Kentucky laser enrichment plant.
4. Long-term uranium contract prices.
