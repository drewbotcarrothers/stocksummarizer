## The short version: revenue up 30%, profit down, stock up 25%

5N Plus (TSX: VNP) is a Montreal-based maker of specialty semiconductors and ultra-pure performance materials, with products that end up in solar panels, satellites and medical imaging equipment. On **November 3, 2026**, the day after its third-quarter report, the shares jumped about **25%**, to roughly C$32.62, and they finished the week at **C$33.53**. On our November 9 screen the stock is up about **56% over twelve months** and about **88% year to date**.

What makes the move interesting is that the headline numbers pointed in two directions. Revenue grew **30%**, and management said it now expects the **high end** of its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance. But adjusted EBITDA grew only **7%**, gross margin fell, and **net earnings dropped 32%**. At about **38 times** trailing earnings (and roughly 29 times forward earnings), the stock now trades above the average of the growth-style peers we compare it with. The question for a new buyer is whether volumes and backlog can keep outrunning margin normalization. Below we walk through the print, the two business segments, and how 5N Plus compares on size, momentum, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 2

On **November 2, 2026**, after the close, 5N Plus reported third-quarter results for the quarter ended September 30. The company reports in U.S. dollars, so the figures below are in US$ unless noted.

- **Revenue US$136.4 million, up 30%** from US$104.9 million a year earlier, on higher volumes in both segments; the result also beat analyst forecasts,
- **Adjusted EBITDA US$31.1 million, up 7%** from US$29.1 million,
- **Adjusted gross margin US$42.8 million (+10%)**, or **31.4%** of revenue, down from 36.9% a year earlier, mainly because margins in Performance Materials were expected to normalize,
- **Net earnings US$12.3 million**, down **32%** from US$18.2 million,
- **Backlog US$447.3 million**, about **299 days** of annualized revenue, 14 days lower than at the end of June and 12 days lower than a year ago, although bookings improved in both segments.

For the first nine months, revenue is **US$376.6 million (+30%)** and adjusted EBITDA is **US$86.8 million (+17%)**. Management reaffirmed full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance of **US$100–105 million** and now expects to land at the **high end**, with full-year revenue growth of roughly 25% to 30%. It also expects second-half adjusted gross margin to stay near the second-quarter level of about **30%**, plus or minus one point, which implies a softer fourth quarter.

### Two segments, two stories

**Specialty Semiconductors** is the growth engine. Adjusted EBITDA jumped **35% to US$25.9 million**, helped by higher volumes in terrestrial renewable energy and space solar, and adjusted gross margin improved to 31.7% from 30.8%. Backlog in this segment sits at the company's **365-day maximum**, and the effective backlog is longer. Segment bookings rose to 110 days from 86 days in the second quarter.

**Performance Materials** is the drag on margins. Revenue grew **20% to US$35.7 million**, driven by bismuth-based products, but adjusted gross margin **normalized to 31.2% from 53.1%** as metal and chemical input costs rose, and adjusted EBITDA fell **31% to US$9.2 million**. Bookings recovered to 108 days from 66 days.

### Cash and the balance sheet

Cash flow was the soft spot. Operations **used US$2.3 million** in the quarter and **US$17.7 million year to date**, which management attributes to the timing and scale of working capital needed to support growth. On the screen, free cash flow over the last twelve months is negative (about −C$34 million). Net debt, by contrast, is low: **US$32.4 million** at September 30, down from US$50.3 million at year-end, or about **0.3 times** EBITDA. Management says the capacity projects at its St. George and AZUR businesses are within existing capital expenditure plans.

## Business overview: 5N Plus vs growth-style Canadian peers

5N Plus sells two things: **specialty semiconductors** (compound semiconductor wafers, substrates and related products used in space solar cells, terrestrial renewable energy and detectors for imaging) and **performance materials** (bismuth, selenium and other high-purity metals and chemicals used in pharmaceuticals, electronics and industrial applications). The first grows with long-term demand programs; the second is more sensitive to metal prices and input costs.

On the TSX screener 5N Plus sits in the broad **Non-energy minerals** sector, which is dominated by gold, silver and copper miners and royalty companies with no business overlap. That is a documented closest-sector exception: for peers we used business-comparable, liquid TSX growth names exposed to the same themes of space, power and electronics infrastructure: **Celestica (CLS)**, **MDA Space (MDA)**, **Hammond Power Solutions (HPS.A)** and **Neo Performance Materials (NEO)**. Treat the comparison as thematic rather than like-for-like.

## Why it matters: from lumpy metals refiner to growth-priced semiconductor supplier

The **old narrative** was a niche specialty-metals refiner with lumpy margins. The **new narrative** is a semiconductor materials supplier riding solar, space and medical demand, with volumes growing faster than profit. That is why a quarter with 30% revenue growth and lower net earnings could still produce a 25% one-day move: investors are looking through the margin normalization to the backlog and guidance. The flip side is that the multiple now leaves less room for a miss.

## Bull case

1. **Volumes and estimates.** Revenue is growing 30%, management expects the high end of its profit guidance, and at least one analyst's estimate sits above guidance.
2. **Low leverage.** Net debt is about 0.3 times EBITDA, which leaves room to fund new capacity without stretching the balance sheet.
3. **Bookings.** Bookings improved in both segments in the quarter, and Specialty Semiconductors backlog is at its maximum level.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Margin normalization.** Management expects second-half gross margin to stay near 30%, versus 36.9% a year ago, and Performance Materials margin has already dropped from 53.1% to 31.2%.
2. **Cash and backlog.** Operating cash flow is negative while working capital grows, and total backlog is below last year's level in days.
3. **Valuation.** At about 8.5 times book and 38 times earnings, a slowdown in growth would hit the shares hard.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter gross margin** versus the roughly 30% guide.
2. **Cash from operations** turning positive as working capital settles.
3. **Backlog days and new bookings** in both segments.
4. **Capacity projects** landing on time and within plan.

## Bottom line

5N Plus is growing fast, is more profitable than the peers we compare it with, carries little debt, and has a backlog that supports its guidance. The market has noticed: the shares are up about 88% this year and trade at the richest price-to-sales and price-to-book multiples in the group. Net earnings fell in the quarter, margins are normalizing and cash flow is negative, so the next few quarters need to show that profit and cash can catch up with the price.
