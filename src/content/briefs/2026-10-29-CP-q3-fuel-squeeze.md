## The short version: growth on the rails, fuel in the margin

Canadian Pacific Kansas City (TSX: CP), the only railway that links Canada, the United States and Mexico on one network, closed at **CA$125.52** on October 23, 2026, up about **17%** over twelve months and about **1.5%** over the past month. Its third-quarter report on October 28 showed **core adjusted earnings per share up 16%** on **15% revenue growth**, yet the U.S.-listed shares fell about **3.6% after hours**. The new story is a **fuel squeeze**: CPKC's fuel bill rose about **61%** from a year earlier, and that pushed its operating ratio the wrong way.

At about **29x trailing earnings**, the most of any company in this group, the question for a new buyer is how quickly CPKC's growth turns back into margin once diesel cools. Below we go through the quarter, the fuel hit and the buybacks, and how CPKC compares with its closest TSX freight peers — CN Rail, TFI International, Exchange Income, Mullen Group and Cargojet — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 2026 results on October 28

On **October 28, 2026**, after the market close, CPKC reported its third-quarter results:

- **Revenue of C$4.22B, up 15%** (about 13% in constant currency), on **6% more volume** measured in revenue ton-miles.
- **Core adjusted diluted EPS of C$1.28, up 16%** from C$1.10, but about **3 cents below** the roughly C$1.31 analysts expected.
- **Reported diluted EPS of C$1.04, up 3%**, held back by a **C$94M** tax charge tied to a Canada Revenue Agency reassessment of prior years linked to the Kansas City Southern deal.
- **Fuel expense of C$667M**, up from C$415M, on an average fuel price of **US$4.49 a gallon, up 53%**.
- **Core adjusted operating ratio of 61.9%**, up from 60.7%. Fuel alone was a **190 basis point** headwind.

The operating ratio is the share of revenue eaten by operating costs, so a higher number is worse. CPKC ran the network better than a year ago: train speed improved about 8% and terminal dwell about 7%, and fuel efficiency improved 3%. The miss came from the price of diesel, not from the railroad running poorly. Fuel surcharges eventually pass most of that cost to customers, but they lag the pump price, so a quarter of fast-rising diesel squeezes the margin first.

### Where the growth came from

- **Grain revenue rose 29%**, with record volumes as Canadian farmers shipped a large carry-out and exports stayed strong.
- **Intermodal revenue rose 22%**, with record domestic and international container volumes.
- **Coal volumes fell 11%** on production problems at the mines, and **automotive volumes fell 5%** on plant retooling.
- **Buybacks:** CPKC repurchased about **11.5 million shares for about C$1.49B** in the quarter. Management said more than C$4B went back to shareholders through buybacks and dividends in the first nine months.
- **New coal deal:** an expanded agreement with Elk Valley Resources moves about half of the coal that was interchanged to CN at Kamloops back onto CPKC's own route to Vancouver.

The weak spots are the operating ratio and the earnings miss, and both matter for the valuation below. A stock priced near 29x earnings needs the margin to improve, not just the volume.

## Business overview: CPKC vs Canadian transportation peers

CPKC hauls grain, potash, coal, energy and chemical products, metals, autos and containers. The 2023 combination with Kansas City Southern created a single line from Western Canada through the U.S. Midwest to Mexico and the Gulf Coast. Bulk commodities such as grain and coal swing with harvests and mine output. Intermodal, merchandise and the Canada-Mexico "land bridge", which management expects to exit 2026 above C$600M of annual revenue, are the growth parts.

On the TSX screener, CPKC sits in the **Transportation** sector. **CN Rail (CNR)** is the other Canadian Class I railway and the cleanest comparison. **TFI International (TFII)** and **Mullen Group (MTL)** are the large trucking and logistics names that compete with rail for freight. **Exchange Income (EIF)** combines regional aviation with manufacturing, and **Cargojet (CJT)** is the air-cargo carrier. Air Canada was left out as a passenger airline with no dividend, and Westshore Terminals, StorageVault and the sub-C$1B names were left out as not comparable.

CPKC trades at about **29x trailing earnings**, above the group average of **26.67** and well above CN at **22.16**, the cheapest in the group. On expected earnings the gap narrows to about **23x vs 20x**, so the market is pricing in faster profit growth at CPKC than at CN. ## Why it matters: from deal-driven growth to margin delivery

The old narrative was CPKC as **the growth railway**, built by linking three countries on one network and priced for the volume that combination would unlock. The volume is arriving: RTMs were up 6%, pricing on renewals is running about 3% to 4%, and grain and intermodal hit records.

The new narrative is **margin delivery**. The question is how much of that growth reaches profit when diesel spikes. For now fuel is taking a bigger bite of each revenue dollar, and at about 29x earnings investors are paying for growth that has to show up in the operating ratio.

## Bull case

1. **Fuel is mostly a pass-through.** Surcharges lag the pump price, so the operating ratio could recover if diesel cools, and management is targeting a fourth-quarter operating ratio below 60%.
2. **Growth is broad.** Record grain and intermodal volumes, the expanding Canada-Mexico land bridge and the new Elk Valley Resources coal deal that wins traffic back from CN add to growth that does not depend on one commodity.
3. **Spending is falling.** Capital spending is set at about **C$2.65B** for 2026, about 15% lower than last year, which frees more cash for buybacks.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Little room for misses.** At about 29x trailing earnings and 46x free cash flow, even a 3-cent miss moved the stock, and this quarter was one.
2. **Fuel and the economy are outside its control.** Coal and automotive volumes are already weak, and management said fuel surcharges are making some customers rethink how they ship.
3. **Tougher comparisons.** Management estimates the new Canadian grain crop at about 76 to 78 million tonnes, about 8% below the record, which could slow grain growth into 2027.

## What to watch

1. **The fourth-quarter operating ratio.** Management expects it below 60%. A miss there would test the valuation more than this quarter did.
2. **CN's results on October 30, 2026.** They are a direct read on Canadian rail demand and on the coal traffic shifting between the two railways.
3. **The Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern review.** CPKC wants the U.S. Surface Transportation Board to reject the proposed combination and plans its next filing on November 18.
4. **Diesel prices and fuel surcharges.** How fast surcharges catch up decides whether the fuel headwind fades in the next quarter or two.

## Bottom line

CPKC has earned its growth premium on volume: revenue up 15%, record grain and intermodal, and a network running faster than a year ago. The quarter shows the cost of that premium, though. A 61% jump in the fuel bill and a 3-cent miss were enough to push the shares down after hours, because at about **29x trailing earnings** the market wants margin, not just volume. The next test is the fourth quarter, where management has promised an operating ratio below 60%. If fuel surcharges catch up and capital spending keeps falling, the premium has support. If not, the valuation is the first thing to give.
