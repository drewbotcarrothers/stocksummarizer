## The short version: a cheaper jet maker gets its upgrade

Bombardier (TSX: BBD.B), the Montreal maker of Global and Challenger business jets, was **CA$299.37** in our October 8 table and jumped about **7%** on October 15 to roughly C$310 after National Bank upgraded the stock to Outperform. Over the past twelve months the shares are up about **50%**, but they slipped about 13% over the three months to October 8, a stretch that included President Trump's September 7 threat to Bombardier's U.S. sales. The new story is **a cash-generating jet maker that now trades at a discount to its Canadian aerospace peers**.

At about **23x trailing earnings**, Bombardier has the lowest P/E in its peer group, so the question for a new buyer is whether that discount is a trade-risk penalty that is about to fade or a fair price for a cyclical business. Below we go through the upgrade, the Wichita investment, the second-quarter print and backlog, and how Bombardier compares with Celestica, CAE, MDA Space and Magellan Aerospace on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: an October 15 upgrade, six weeks after the Trump post

On **October 15, 2026**, National Bank upgraded Bombardier to **Outperform from Sector Perform** with a **C$360** price target. The same day:

- **Scotiabank** kept its Sector Outperform rating and trimmed its target to **C$370 from C$380**,
- the shares rose about **7%** on the session, among the best performers on the TSX Composite that day,
- Bombardier announced about **$50 million** of investments at its **Wichita, Kansas** site, including a new North American Learning Center for newly hired aircraft technicians and an expanded wheel-and-tire repair shop.

The Wichita news matters because of the backdrop. On **September 7**, President Trump posted that Bombardier could not sell in the United States unless it builds there. So far, no formal ban, tariff or certification action has followed, and Bombardier's jets remain sellable in the U.S. Wichita is not a new assembly line, but it deepens a U.S. footprint that already includes service centres and parts work, and it is the kind of step that helps the "build here" argument. The upgrade suggests at least one large Canadian broker thinks the market had priced in too much trade risk.

### The Q2 print behind the upgrade

The fundamentals were already improving before the upgrade. Bombardier's second quarter, reported **July 30, 2026** (all figures US$):

- **Revenue of $2.15 billion, up 6%** year over year, with 32 aircraft delivered.
- **Record services revenue of $674 million, up 14%** — the steadier, higher-margin aftermarket business.
- **Adjusted EBITDA of $325 million, up 9%**, a 15.1% margin.
- **Free cash flow of $228 million**, a $392 million swing from cash usage a year earlier.
- **Backlog of $21.8 billion**, up $4.3 billion since December, on a 1.5x unit book-to-bill led by **Global 8000** orders.

Management says it remains on track for its raised 2026 guidance, including more than 157 deliveries, more than $10 billion of revenue and more than $1 billion of free cash flow. Debt reduction of more than $1.1 billion in the first half took adjusted net debt to adjusted EBITDA down to about **1.6x**, and the next maturity is not until November 2030. The soft spot is deliveries: 32 in the quarter versus 36 a year earlier, which puts more weight on the second half.

## Business overview: Bombardier vs Canadian aerospace and tech peers

Bombardier today is a pure business-aviation company. It designs, builds and services the Challenger and Global families, runs a fast-growing service network for a fleet of more than 5,200 aircraft, and has a smaller but expanding defence business that converts Global jets for special-mission roles. The aircraft side is cyclical and tied to the spending of the very wealthy and corporate flight departments; services and defence are steadier.

On the TSX screener, Bombardier sits in the **Electronic technology** sector. Celestica (CLS) is the sector's largest name and a useful contrast on growth and valuation. MDA Space (MDA) is the space and defence systems maker, and Magellan Aerospace (MAL) builds aerostructures and engine components. CAE (CAE), the flight-simulator and pilot-training company, sits in the screener's Commercial services sector, but it is the most business-comparable Canadian aviation name, so we include it as a documented closest-sector exception. We left out smaller and loss-making names such as Kraken Robotics.

## Why it matters: from debt-heavy turnaround to cash generator

The old narrative was Bombardier as **a debt-heavy turnaround selling jets to the ultra-rich**, priced for the risk that cash would never show up. The new narrative is **a cash-generating jet maker with a fast-growing services arm and a defence business**, with free cash flow guided above US$1 billion for 2026 and leverage near management's target.

The open question is trade. The Wichita investment deepens Bombardier's American footprint, and the National Bank upgrade suggests the market is starting to price lower risk. But until there is clarity on U.S. trade policy toward Canadian aircraft, part of the discount to peers is likely to stay.

## Bull case

1. **Cheapest in the group.** The lowest trailing P/E (~22.7), forward P/E (~21.8) and price to free cash flow (~10x) among these peers, against a ~36x average trailing multiple.
2. **Visibility.** A US$21.8 billion backlog, a 1.5x book-to-bill in Q2 and record services revenue give years of visibility, with Global 8000 demand leading.
3. **A healing balance sheet.** More than US$1.1 billion of debt repaid in the first half, net leverage about 1.6x, and no maturities until 2030.

## Bear case and risks

1. **U.S. trade risk is not gone.** A tariff, sales restriction or certification fight would hit Bombardier's biggest market, and the September 7 post shows how fast the headline risk can return.
2. **Cyclical demand.** Deliveries slipped to 32 in Q2 from 36 a year earlier, and business-jet orders can fall quickly if wealthy buyers and corporations pull back.
3. **Much of the turnaround is priced.** The stock is up about 50% in a year, and the consensus target leaves only modest upside from current levels.

## What to watch

1. **Q3 results on November 5, 2026.** Deliveries versus the 157-plus guide, free cash flow, and whether the backlog holds above US$21 billion.
2. **U.S. trade policy.** Any formal action on Canadian aircraft, or any easing of the threat.
3. **Global 8000 and defence orders.** Order momentum and new special-mission contracts.
4. **Analyst targets.** Whether the stock pushes toward the C$330.62 consensus and whether more brokers follow National Bank.

## Bottom line

Bombardier has earned its place as a cash generator: record services revenue, positive free cash flow, falling debt and a US$21.8 billion backlog. The issue has been the discount, and at about 23x trailing and 22x forward earnings it is the cheapest stock in this Canadian aerospace peer group, partly because of the cyclical end market and partly because of the U.S. trade threat. National Bank's upgrade and the Wichita investment suggest that discount may be starting to narrow. The next test is the November 5 third-quarter report, which needs to show deliveries catching up in the second half.
