## The short version: a record quarter, a quiet stock

AltaGas (TSX: ALA), the Calgary company that runs U.S. gas utilities and a Canadian export business shipping propane and butane to Asia, closed at **CA$55.34** on October 23, 2026, up about **28%** over twelve months and about **5%** over the past month. Its third-quarter report on October 29 was the best third quarter in its history: **normalized EBITDA rose 45%** to C$389 million, and the company exported a record **147,176 barrels a day** of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG). The stock closed the day flat at **CA$56.22**.

The reason is the new story. AltaGas is now mostly an **export platform with a utility underneath**, and the export side just ran into a labour dispute at the Prince Rupert port terminal that serves it. At about **28x trailing earnings** and only about 2% below the average analyst target, the question for a new buyer is how much of the export growth is already in the price. Below we go through the quarter, the strike, and how AltaGas compares with its closest TSX midstream peers — Enbridge, TC Energy, Pembina, Keyera and Gibson Energy — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 2026 results on October 29

On **October 29, 2026**, before the market open, AltaGas reported its third-quarter results:

- **Normalized EBITDA of C$389M**, up from C$268M a year earlier (**+45%**) and above the roughly C$335M analysts expected.
- **Normalized EPS of C$0.29**, up from C$0.04. Third quarters are seasonally small for a heating-gas utility, so the per-share number is low in absolute terms.
- **Reported (GAAP) loss of C$0.07 a share**, about the same as last year's C$0.08 loss. The main drag was **C$141M of unrealized losses on hedging contracts**, which are paper losses that reverse as the hedges settle.
- **Revenue of C$3.43B, up 32%**, and **normalized funds from operations of C$0.80 a share**, up from C$0.49.
- **Guidance:** management now expects 2026 results **toward the high end** of the ranges it raised in July: **C$2.0B to C$2.1B** of normalized EBITDA and **C$2.35 to C$2.60** of normalized EPS.

### Where the growth came from

- **Midstream EBITDA rose 65%** to C$337M, driven by export volumes and margins, the Pipestone II gas plant and higher fractionation and liquids-handling volumes. Gathering and processing volumes in the Montney rose 20%.
- **Record exports:** 147,176 barrels a day of LPG went to Asia on 24 very large gas carriers, **up 11%**. About 89,000 barrels a day left from the Ridley Island Propane Export Terminal near Prince Rupert and about 58,000 from Ferndale in Washington State.
- **Utilities EBITDA was roughly flat** at C$66M (from C$68M). Washington Gas won a **US$74M** rate increase in Virginia with a higher allowed return on equity (**9.80%** at the midpoint), and SEMCO won a **US$33M** increase in Michigan from January 2027.
- **Balance sheet:** adjusted net debt to normalized EBITDA fell to **4.3x**, below the company's own 4.5x–5.0x target range.

### The catch: a strike at Prince Rupert

Workers at **Trigon Pacific Terminals**, which provides rail logistics and berthing at the Ridley Island propane terminal, have been on strike since **October 2**. Until this week AltaGas said the impact was minimal. On October 27 the Canada Industrial Relations Board ordered Trigon to stop using contractors for some duties, including handling the products AltaGas exports. On the call, CEO Vern Yu said the company disagrees with the ruling and will take legal action, but will meanwhile operate under modified plans that **slow vessel loading below planned rates**. Management still expects the full year near the top of guidance, including that impact.

That is the balance the market struck on results day: a clear beat on the quarter, a less certain fourth quarter, and a stock that finished flat while Enbridge, TC Energy and Pembina each rose between about 1.5% and 2%.

## Business overview: AltaGas vs Canadian midstream peers

AltaGas has two halves. The **Utilities** business is Washington Gas (Washington D.C., Maryland and Virginia) and SEMCO (Michigan), regulated natural-gas distribution that earns most of its money in winter. The **Midstream** business gathers and processes gas in the Montney, separates natural gas liquids, and exports propane and butane to buyers across Asia from two West Coast terminals. A third Ridley Island export site, REEF, is about 90% built.

On the TSX screener, AltaGas sits in the **Utilities** sector. For this quarter we use a **documented sector exception**: 87% of Q3 normalized EBITDA (C$337M of C$389M) came from Midstream, so the peers are business-comparable TSX midstream names, which the screener files under Industrial services and Distribution services. **Enbridge (ENB)** and **TC Energy (TRP)** are the large pipeline companies, and Enbridge also owns gas utilities. **Pembina (PPL)** and **Keyera (KEY)** are the Western Canadian processing and liquids businesses closest to AltaGas's export chain. **Gibson Energy (GEI)** is the smaller terminals and marketing name. South Bow was left out because it has no Canadian-dollar target, and the regulated utilities (Fortis, Emera, Hydro One) are covered in our utilities sector note.

## Why it matters: from a utility with exports to an export platform with a utility

The old narrative was AltaGas as **a U.S. gas utility with a side business in exports**, valued for steady regulated earnings and a modest dividend. The new narrative is **an export platform** that links Western Canadian gas fields to buyers in Asia, with the utilities as a stable base underneath.

That shift brings faster growth: Midstream supplied almost nine-tenths of this quarter's EBITDA, and LNG Canada's second phase should mean more gas and more liquids to export. It also brings new risks. Port labour, shipping costs and the spread between Asian and North American propane prices now matter more to AltaGas than a rate case does. The October strike ruling is the first real test of that exposure.

## Bull case

1. **Export demand is strong.** Management says Asian buyers want reliable supply while Middle East shipments are disrupted, Western Canadian production is growing, and AltaGas says it is in talks on long-term contracts. About **91%** of its expected export volumes for the rest of 2026 are tolled or hedged.
2. **A new terminal is close.** REEF Phase I on Ridley Island is about **90%** complete and on track for the first quarter of 2027, with an optimization project adding about 30,000 barrels a day in the second half of 2027.
3. **The balance sheet and utilities are improving.** Leverage of **4.3x** is below the company's target range, and new rates in Virginia and Michigan support steady utility earnings growth.

## Bear case and risks

1. **The port strike.** If loading stays slow for weeks, fourth-quarter export volumes and margins take a hit, and Asian customers have already complained about Canadian reliability.
2. **Thin upside.** The stock is near its 52-week high of about C$57.52 and only about 2% below the average target, with one recent downgrade.
3. **Messy earnings.** Reported results swing with hedge accounting and global propane spreads. This quarter showed a GAAP loss alongside a record normalized result, and a reversal in spreads would hit the export side directly.

## What to watch

1. **The Prince Rupert strike.** Watch for a settlement or a ruling on AltaGas's legal challenge, and for any update on loading rates.
2. **REEF start-up.** Commissioning is now the focus, with first operations targeted for the first quarter of 2027.
3. **2027 guidance and the dividend.** AltaGas usually sets out the next year's guidance in December, and its payout leaves room for another increase.
4. **Long-term Asian contracts and analyst targets.** Contracts would lock in more of the export business. Targets near C$57 to C$64 will show whether the Street re-rates the stock after this quarter.

## Bottom line

AltaGas has earned its five-year run: a record quarter, normalized EBITDA up 45%, record exports and debt now below its own target. The issue is price and timing. At about **28x trailing earnings** and **22.5x** forward, in line with the midstream group, and only about 2% below the average target, the market is already paying for the export story. The next test is the fourth quarter, when the port strike shows how much it can dent volumes, followed by REEF's start-up early in 2027. If both go to plan, the targets have room to move. If not, the low yield gives less cushion than the peers' dividends do.
