## The short version: a giant deal, a cool reception

Emera (TSX: EMA), the Halifax utility that owns Nova Scotia Power and Tampa Electric, signed what the companies call **the largest merger ever between two Canadian companies** on October 6, 2026. It is combining with Canadian Utilities, the ATCO-controlled Alberta utility, to form a utility with about **C$72B of enterprise value** and six million customers. Emera's own shares did not celebrate: they fell **2.9% to C$66.31** that day, after a year in which the stock rose only about **3%** (TSX screener, October 2 close of **CA$68.42**). The new story is **scale**: two high-growth regions, Florida and Alberta, under one regulated roof.

Before the deal, Emera already screened as the **cheapest name in its peer group on trailing earnings (~21.5x) and book value**, with the **highest indicated yield, about 4.3%**. The question for a new buyer is whether the drop is a warning about dilution and approval risk, or an opening in a high-yield utility that just doubled its growth runway. Below we go through the deal terms and the ATCO spin-off, the growth and dividend math, and how Emera compares with its closest TSX peers — Fortis, Hydro One, AltaGas, Canadian Utilities and ATCO — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the October 6 Canadian Utilities merger

On **October 6, 2026**, Emera, ATCO and Canadian Utilities announced a definitive agreement for Emera to acquire all of Canadian Utilities, valued at about **C$14.3B**, in an all-share deal. The main terms:

- **Canadian Utilities Class A holders** (other than ATCO) get **0.755 of an Emera share** per share; Class B holders get **0.819**. Based on LSEG data reported by BNN Bloomberg, that valued the Class A shares at about **C$51.57**, a premium of roughly **0.7%** to the prior close.
- **ATCO**, which owns all of Canadian Utilities' voting shares and about 37% of its non-voting shares, is folded in too: ATCO holders get **0.865 of an Emera share** plus one share of **"New ATCO"**, a spin-off holding the housing, defence, ports and retail-energy businesses.
- After closing, **existing Emera holders own about 60%** of the combined company and former Canadian Utilities and ATCO holders about **40%**.
- The combined company keeps the Emera name, a public head office in Halifax, Canadian Utilities' offices in Calgary and Edmonton, and US operations in Tampa. Emera CEO Scott Balfour runs it, with a 13-member board (seven Emera, six Canadian Utilities nominees).

The market reaction split along the deal lines: Emera closed down **2.9%**, while ATCO jumped about **13%** in morning trading as its holders get both Emera shares and the spin-off. That pattern says investors saw ATCO as the clear winner and Emera as the side issuing a lot of new stock for a tiny premium.

### The growth and dividend math

Management pitched the deal as **growth, not cost cuts**. On the call, Canadian Utilities CEO Bob Myles said "this is not about synergies." The combined plan:

- **C$32B of capital spending through 2030**, supporting **7% to 8% average annual rate base growth** on a starting rate base of about **C$45B**.
- About **95% of earnings from regulated utilities**, and about **80%** from Florida and Alberta.
- **Accretive to adjusted earnings per share in the first full year after closing**, with closing targeted for the **third or fourth quarter of 2027**.
- Emera keeps its **1% to 2% annual dividend growth target** and expects its payout ratio to improve; Canadian Utilities Class A holders get about **20% more dividend income** after switching into Emera shares.

The weak spot is timing. None of the benefits arrive until late 2027, while the new shares are a known quantity today. Emera's last print was also soft: **second-quarter 2026 adjusted EPS fell to C$0.69 from C$0.79** (August 7), on higher corporate interest costs and asset sales, even though management said it remains on track for its 5% to 7% annual EPS growth target.

## Business overview: Emera vs Canadian utility peers

Emera is a regulated electric and gas utility holding company. About **70% of its earnings come from Florida**, mainly Tampa Electric and Peoples Gas, with Nova Scotia Power and other Atlantic Canada utilities making up most of the rest. Regulated rate bases grow steadily as utilities invest, but returns depend on regulators, and the heavy spending is funded with debt and new equity.

On the TSX screener, Emera sits in the **Utilities** sector. Fortis (FTS) is the largest Canadian utility holding company and the closest US-heavy comparison. Hydro One (H) is the Ontario transmission and distribution pure play. AltaGas (ALA) mixes regulated gas utilities with midstream. Canadian Utilities (CU) is the merger partner, with about 91% of its rate base in Alberta, and ATCO (ACO.X) is its parent and a deal party. Capital Power was left out because it is a power producer rather than a regulated utility, and to keep the table at six rows.

Emera is the **cheapest name on trailing P/E at 21.56**, essentially tied with Hydro One (21.57) and below the group average of about **27.8**, and it is also cheapest on **price to book at 1.62**. On forward earnings it trades near **19x**, below Fortis at about 20x and Hydro One near 21x. ATCO looks cheapest on forward P/E, cash flow and cash, but it is a holding company whose value is now tied to the deal itself.

The catch is what Emera is paying in. Canadian Utilities' trailing P/E (304) is not meaningful, but its **forward P/E is about 18.9x**, almost the same as Emera's 19.1x. So the merger is roughly multiple-neutral: Emera is not issuing cheap paper to buy an expensive asset, and it is not getting a bargain either. Emera, Fortis, Hydro One and AltaGas show no price-to-free-cash-flow figure in the screener, which is common for utilities whose capital spending runs ahead of operating cash flow.

## Why it matters: from stretched Florida utility to two-region giant

The old narrative was Emera as a **Florida growth utility with a stretched payout and steady share issuance**, priced at a discount to Fortis. The new narrative is **scale**: Florida and Alberta would supply about 80% of earnings, about 95% of earnings would be regulated, and a bigger balance sheet should make it easier to fund growth while keeping investment-grade ratings.

For income investors, the question shifts from whether Emera can keep raising its dividend to **whether a bigger Emera can fund its growth without issuing too many new shares**.

## Bull case

1. **Credit and funding.** More scale and diversification should support Emera's credit ratings, which helps funding costs for a capital-hungry utility.
2. **A second growth engine.** Alberta adds a second high-growth rate base next to Florida, so any single regulator's decision moves the needle less.
3. **Modest expectations.** Emera is the cheapest name in the group on trailing earnings (~21.5x) and book value (1.6x), with the highest yield (4.3%), so the bar for a re-rating is low.

## Bear case and risks

1. **A long approval list.** The deal needs Alberta and US regulatory approvals, an Alberta court, and several shareholder votes, with closing more than a year away.
2. **Dilution first, accretion later.** All-share deals dilute, and the earnings boost is only promised after closing in late 2027.
3. **Thin cover.** Second-quarter adjusted EPS fell to C$0.69 from C$0.79 on higher interest costs, and a ~92% payout leaves little room for error.

## What to watch

1. **Analyst target revisions.** All nine targets predate the deal; watch whether the C$76.67 consensus moves up or down.
2. **The joint circular and shareholder votes.** Meetings are expected in **early 2027**, with two-thirds approvals needed at ATCO and Canadian Utilities.
3. **Alberta Utilities Commission and US approvals.** Regulator conditions could change the deal's economics.
4. **Emera's third-quarter results.** Look for any update on how the larger capital plan gets funded and whether new equity is needed.

## Bottom line

Emera has earned a seat among Canada's largest utilities: the Canadian Utilities merger would create a roughly C$72B enterprise with two high-growth regions and about 95% regulated earnings. The market's first verdict was cool, with Emera down 2.9% while ATCO jumped, because Emera is issuing a lot of stock for a small premium and the benefits arrive only after a late-2027 close. Going in, Emera was already the cheapest name in its peer group on trailing earnings (~21.5x) and book value, with a 4.3% yield but a ~92% payout. The next tests are analyst revisions, the shareholder circular and the Alberta approvals.
