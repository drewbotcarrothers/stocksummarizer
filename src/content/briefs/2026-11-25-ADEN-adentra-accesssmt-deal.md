## The short version: a bolt-on deal at a bargain multiple

ADENTRA Inc. (TSX: ADEN), one of North America's largest distributors of architectural building products, closed at **CA$41.75** on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, up **9.6%** in a day. The trigger: on the evening of **November 23**, it agreed to buy **AccessSMT**, a Canadian distributor of commercial doors, frames, hardware and access control products, for **C$230M in cash**.

Adentra says the deal adds **10% to 15%** to its adjusted earnings per share, with no new shares issued. Even after the jump, the stock trades at only about **10x trailing earnings**, and before it, at **8.7x**, it had the lowest P/E in our peer group. Below we go through the deal, the third quarter behind it, and how Adentra compares with five other TSX distributors — Richelieu Hardware, Doman Building Materials, Taiga Building Products, Russel Metals and Wajax — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the AccessSMT deal (November 23)

On **Monday, November 23, 2026**, Adentra signed a deal to buy all of AccessSMT Holdings from Speyside CAN Holdings, a portfolio company of Speyside Equity Fund I. AccessSMT is based in Vancouver, has roots back to 1928 and runs **15 locations** across six provinces and Washington State, with about 490 employees. The deal brings:

- **About C$240M in expected 2026 sales** (about US$172M),
- a **contracted backlog larger than its last twelve months of revenue**, including major healthcare and institutional projects,
- a business where **about 70% of jobs are upgrades and retrofits** to existing buildings, not new construction,
- management's estimate of **10% to 15% accretion** to Adentra's 2026 adjusted basic EPS on a pro forma annualized basis, and higher gross and adjusted EBITDA margins.

Adentra will pay from its existing bank credit line, which had about **US$453M of undrawn capacity** at September 30, with no new financing commitment and no share offering (AccessSMT's managers will buy about C$2M of Adentra shares at closing). The cost is a heavier balance sheet: the leverage ratio rises to **about 3.0x at closing**, from **2.3x** at September 30. Closing is expected in **December 2026**, subject to **Competition Act** approval.

The market liked it. The shares rose **9.61% to C$41.75** on November 24, from C$38.09 the day before.

### The Q3 print behind the deal

The deal follows a solid third quarter, reported **November 9, 2026** (in US dollars):

- **Sales up 4.5% to US$618.7M**, helped by higher prices while demand stayed soft.
- **Adjusted EBITDA up 10.6% to US$55.2M**, with the margin up 50 basis points to 8.9%.
- **Adjusted basic EPS up 27.1% to US$0.89**; reported net income rose 56.8% to US$15.9M.
- **Leverage down to 2.3x** from 2.7x a year earlier.
- **Quarterly dividend up 6% to C$0.17**, payable January 29, 2027.

The weak spot is volume. October daily sales were only about 5% above last year, and management said demand remains soft heading into the slower fourth quarter. That is why the deal and the valuation matter more than the quarter itself.

## Business overview: Adentra vs building-products distributors

Adentra distributes architectural building products to the residential, repair-and-remodel and commercial construction markets from **82 facilities** in the United States and Canada. Most of its sales are in the US, so US housing and remodeling activity drives the top line. AccessSMT pushes it further into **commercial work** (about 13% of sales today, about 18% after the deal) and roughly **doubles its Canadian sales**, to about 14% of the total.

On the TSX screener, Adentra sits in the **Distribution services** sector. **Doman Building Materials (DBM)** and **Taiga Building Products (TBL)** are Canadian building-materials distributors. **Russel Metals (RUS)** runs metals service centres and **Wajax (WJX)** distributes industrial equipment and parts. **Richelieu Hardware (RCH)** is listed under Producer manufacturing on the screener, but it is the closest TSX business match — a distributor of specialty hardware and architectural products — so we include it as a documented closest-sector exception. The Caterpillar dealers (Toromont, Finning) and food, apparel and energy names in the same sector were left out as not comparable.

At the November 20 price of C$36.30, Adentra traded at about **8.7x trailing earnings**, the lowest in the group and well below the **13.0x average**. ## Why it matters: from a housing distributor to more commercial and more Canada

The old narrative was Adentra as a **US housing and remodeling play**, priced for a cycle that has stayed soft. With demand flat, growth has come from pricing, cost control and acquisitions — this is its eighteenth deal since 2010 and fourth large one since 2021.

The new narrative is a **more diversified distributor**: commercial work rises to about 18% of sales, and Canadian sales roughly double, with more exposure to hospitals, schools and other projects outside housing. The question is whether the extra earnings arrive fast enough to bring leverage back down before housing turns.

## Bull case

1. **Accretive without dilution.** Management expects **10–15%** accretion to adjusted EPS, funded from the credit line with no share offering.
2. **Cheapest in the group.** The lowest P/E, P/B and P/FCF of the six names, even after the jump.
3. **Visibility.** AccessSMT's contracted backlog is bigger than a full year of its sales, and most jobs are retrofits rather than new builds.
4. **Room to raise the dividend.** A payout of about 15% leaves plenty of cover after the 6% raise.

## Bear case and risks

1. **More debt.** Leverage rises to about **3.0x** from 2.3x, and the cushion stays thinner until cash flow pays it down.
2. **Soft demand.** Housing and remodeling demand is still soft, and most of Adentra's sales are tied to it.
3. **Integration and approval.** Combining two companies can bring surprises, and the deal still needs Competition Act approval.
4. **Low yield.** At about 1.9%, the yield is well below the group average.

## What to watch

1. **Competition Act approval and a December close.** The agreement can be extended by up to 60 days past December 31 if approval is still pending.
2. **The first results with AccessSMT.** Reports from 2027 onward will show whether margins and EPS move the way management expects.
3. **Leverage.** How quickly the ratio comes back down from about 3.0x toward the 2.3x of September.
4. **US housing and remodeling demand.** Daily sales trends and any comments on volumes, not just pricing.

## Bottom line

Adentra bought a profitable, backlog-heavy commercial business for C$230M and expects double-digit EPS accretion without issuing shares. The market noticed, with a 9.6% jump, but at about 10x trailing earnings the stock is still the cheapest in its peer group, and every analyst in the count rates it a Buy or Strong Buy. The price for that growth is more debt in a soft housing market. The next test is a clean December close and evidence in 2027 that the earnings arrive and leverage falls.
