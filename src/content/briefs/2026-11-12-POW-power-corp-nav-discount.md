## The short version: the assets are worth more, the discount is still there

Power Corporation of Canada (TSX: POW), the Montreal holding company behind **Great-West Lifeco** and **IGM Financial**, closed at **CA$91.36** on November 6, 2026, up about **38% over twelve months**. On **November 11** it reported third-quarter results and said its **adjusted net asset value (NAV) per share reached C$113.28**, up **32.1%** from C$85.77 at the end of 2025. That is management's own estimate of what each share is worth if you add up its stakes at market value and subtract holding-company debt and preferred shares.

The stock closed at **C$95.87** on November 11, about **15% below** that figure. Power Corp has always traded at a discount to the value of what it owns; the question now is whether buybacks, a simpler structure and a fast-growing Wealthsimple stake can keep shrinking that gap, or whether the discount is simply permanent. Below we go through the quarter, the NAV math, and how Power Corp compares with Great-West, IGM, Manulife, Sun Life, iA Financial and Onex on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 11

On **Wednesday, November 11, 2026**, Power Corporation reported results for the third quarter. All figures are in Canadian dollars.

- **Adjusted net earnings of C$964M, or C$1.53 a share**, up from C$863M, or C$1.35, a year earlier (about **+13%** per share),
- **Reported net earnings of C$1,006M, or C$1.60 a share**, up from C$703M, or C$1.10, helped by IGM's C$144M gain on taking control of Northleaf,
- **Great-West Lifeco** posted base (adjusted) earnings of C$1,323M, up 8%, and a **record 19.4% base return on equity**, its third straight quarter above its 19% target; it also closed the purchase of Milliman's retirement administration business,
- **IGM Financial** had **record adjusted earnings of C$351M**, record net inflows of C$3.4B, and assets under management and advisement of **C$381.7B**, up 26% from a year earlier,
- **Wealthsimple** reached **3.8 million clients** and C$169B of assets under administration; its October financing round values the Power group's interest at **C$5.4B**, up 13% in the quarter.

### The NAV math

Adjusted NAV is a non-IFRS measure: the fair value of the holding company's assets (listed stakes at market prices, private stakes at management's estimate) minus its net debt and preferred shares. At September 30 it was **C$70.8B**, or **C$113.28 per share**, against C$54.5B (C$85.77) at the end of 2025.

Most of that gain came from the market. Power Corp owns **68.4% of Great-West**, whose shares rose from C$67.69 to C$89.87 over the nine months, and **64.5% of IGM**, which rose from C$61.81 to C$83.20. Those two stakes alone are worth about **C$67B**, roughly 87% of the C$77.1B gross asset value; adding the 17.5% look-through stake in Belgium's GBL takes the listed holdings to about 90%. Sagard, Power Sustainable and Wealthsimple add another C$4.6B.

### Capital returns

So far in 2026, Power Corp has returned **C$2.4B** to shareholders: **C$1.2B in dividends and C$1.2B in buybacks**. Shares outstanding fell to 624.6 million from 635.7 million at the end of 2025. The board declared a quarterly dividend of **66.75 cents** a share, payable February 1, 2027.

## Business overview: Power Corp vs financial peers

Power Corp is an international holding company focused on financial services in North America, Europe and Asia. Its core holdings are insurer **Great-West Lifeco**, wealth manager **IGM Financial**, a 17.5% look-through stake in Belgium's GBL, and private platforms including Sagard, Power Sustainable and Wealthsimple. Because it controls Great-West and IGM it **consolidates their results**, so its income statement looks like a giant insurer's.

On the TSX screener it sits in the **Finance** sector. For peers we used its two listed subsidiaries, **Great-West Lifeco (GWO)** and **IGM Financial (IGM)**; the other big Canadian life insurers **Manulife (MFC)**, **Sun Life (SLF)** and **iA Financial (IAG)**; and **Onex (ONEX)**, the closest listed Canadian holding and investment company. Banks, property and casualty insurers and REITs in the Finance sector were left out as not comparable.

## Why it matters: active ownership vs a permanent discount

The old narrative was a **sleepy family conglomerate** that always traded at a discount to what it owned. The new narrative is **active ownership**: steady buybacks, selling non-core businesses such as LMPG, simplifying holdings, and growing a stake in Wealthsimple that is now valued at billions. For investors, the question is whether that discount keeps shrinking or is simply a permanent feature of owning Great-West and IGM through a holding company.

There is also a choice to make. An investor who likes Great-West or IGM can own them directly, at no discount, and collect a similar yield. Owning Power Corp instead adds the private platforms, the GBL stake and the buyback program, in exchange for holding-company costs.

## Bull case

1. **Buybacks below NAV.** Every share bought back at a discount to net asset value adds value for the remaining shareholders, and Power Corp spent C$1.2B on buybacks in nine months.
2. **Strong subsidiaries.** Great-West is hitting its return-on-equity target and IGM's assets and inflows keep growing, which supports both earnings and NAV.
3. **Wealthsimple optionality.** A fast-growing private fintech with 3.8 million clients gives Power Corp an asset most investors overlook, and any future listing would put a market price on it.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Discounts can last.** Holding-company discounts can persist for decades, so a 15% gap is not a promise of upside.
2. **Concentration.** About 90% of gross asset value sits in Great-West, IGM and GBL, so NAV falls if those shares fall; this year's NAV gain was mostly market-driven.
3. **Great-West looks fully priced.** Analysts' average target for Great-West is roughly its current price, which limits the obvious source of further NAV growth.

## What to watch

1. **Buyback pace** and whether the discount to NAV narrows from the roughly 15% gap at the November 11 close.
2. **Great-West's next results**, since its stake is the largest piece of NAV.
3. **The next Wealthsimple valuation** and any move toward a public listing, plus completion of the secondary offering expected in the fourth quarter of 2026.
4. **GBL's redeployment**, including its up to €1.4B investment in Recordati alongside CVC.

## Bottom line

Power Corp's assets are worth more than ever: adjusted NAV rose 32% this year to C$113.28 a share, adjusted earnings grew 13% and C$2.4B went back to shareholders. But the market still won't pay full price, with the stock about 15% below that value and analysts seeing only modest upside. Whether the gap closes depends mostly on Great-West's share price, the pace of buybacks and what Wealthsimple turns out to be worth.
