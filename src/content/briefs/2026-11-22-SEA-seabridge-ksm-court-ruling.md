## The short version: a court ruling puts the KSM clock back in doubt

Seabridge Gold Inc. (TSX: SEA, NYSE: SA) owns **KSM**, a gold-copper-silver project about 65 kilometres northwest of Stewart in British Columbia's Golden Triangle in northwest B.C. On **Thursday, November 19, 2026**, the shares fell **29.3% to C$25.92**, the worst move on the TSX that day, and on Friday they slipped another **4.3% to C$24.80**.

The trigger was a **B.C. Supreme Court ruling** that confirmed KSM's **"substantially started" designation has been quashed**. That designation matters because it keeps the project's environmental assessment certificate from expiring. The province now has to decide the question again, after deeper consultation with the First Nation that challenged it, and there is no set end date for that process.

Seabridge has no revenue and relies on outside money while it looks for a major mining partner, so timing is the whole story. Below we go through the ruling, what Seabridge says, how it compares with four other TSX-listed mine developers on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets, and what to watch next.

## What happened: the KSM court ruling (November 19)

### The ruling

The case goes back to **July 24, 2024**, when B.C.'s Environmental Assessment Office (EAO) decided KSM had been "substantially started". Under B.C. rules, a project that is substantially started keeps its environmental certificate in place without an expiry date. The designation was challenged by the **Tsetsaut Skii km Lax Ha Nation (TSKLH)** and by the SkeenaWild Conservation Trust.

In **June 2026**, Justice Emily Burke found that the province had not properly consulted the Nation and sent the decision back to the EAO for reconsideration, after a 90-day consultation period that ended on September 28. Both sides then went back to court asking how to read that order. The **November ruling**, released on November 18 and announced on November 19, settled it:

- the substantially started designation **was quashed**, not just sent back for review;
- the province must carry out **deep consultation** with the Nation before it decides again, and the end of the 90-day written-submission window did not end that consultation;
- the SkeenaWild petition was dismissed **without a ruling on whether the designation was reasonable**. The Nation says that question remains open.

### What Seabridge says

Seabridge says KSM's **legal status has not changed** and its environmental assessment certificate is unaffected by the ruling. If the EAO again concludes that the project was substantially started, the certificate stays in place and does not expire. CEO Rudi Fronk said the company is "very confident" KSM has more than met the requirements, and that it has the full support of the **Tahltan and Nisga'a Nations**, the principal First Nations in the area, with whom it has impact benefit agreements.

In September it gave the EAO data on work completed between July 2024 and July 2026, lifting KSM's construction investment from **C$444 million to more than C$700 million**. Seabridge is asking the EAO whether the ruling changes the timeline and is pushing for a prompt decision.

### The market reaction

The stock fell **29.3% to C$25.92** on the TSX on November 19, on heavy volume, and hit a 52-week low. On **Friday, November 20**, it fell another **4.3% to C$24.80** as gold and copper prices slid and the S&P/TSX Composite dropped about 2.5%. From the November 13 close used in our tables (C$35.55), that is a fall of about **30%**, taking market value from about **C$3.8 billion** to roughly **C$2.7 billion**.

## Why it matters: a giant asset that needs a partner and a timeline

KSM is one of the largest undeveloped gold and copper projects in the world, and Seabridge is not trying to build it alone. It is negotiating an **earn-in joint venture with a "preferred partner"**, a senior mining company, and is already planning the 2027 field program with that partner, but **no deal is signed**. A feasibility study is targeted for the **end of 2027**.

Until a partner pays in, Seabridge relies on outside money. In **July 2026** it arranged an unsecured **US$100 million loan** from a strategic investor at 7% a year, and it drew the last US$50 million on **November 2**, so the facility is now **fully drawn**. The loan can be settled in cash, shares or both. Cash was about **C$56 million at September 30**, against current liabilities of about C$67.6 million, and it spent C$102.3 million at KSM in the third quarter alone.

That is why the ruling matters more than the legal status alone suggests. A long delay on the substantially started question could make a partner more cautious and push Seabridge toward more expensive funding, possibly including new shares.

## Bull case

- **The certificate still stands.** The ruling does not cancel KSM's environmental assessment certificate, and Seabridge expects the designation to be granted again.
- **More work on the ground.** Construction investment at KSM now tops **C$700 million**, up from C$444 million when the designation was first granted, which supports its case.
- **First Nations support.** The two principal First Nations in the area, the Tahltan and Nisga'a, support the project through impact benefit agreements.
- **Cheaper than most peers.** At about **2.4 times book** after the drop, Seabridge trades well below the group average, and analysts' targets imply big upside.

## Bear case and risks

- **No timeline.** There is no set end date for the new consultation, so a new decision could take a long time.
- **Legal questions remain.** The Nation that challenged the designation says the question of whether it was reasonable is still open.
- **Funding.** No partner deal is signed, the US$100 million loan is fully drawn, and Seabridge has no revenue.
- **Metal prices.** Falling gold and copper prices are weighing on the whole developer group.

## What to watch

1. **The EAO's new decision** on whether KSM is substantially started, and any timeline the province gives.
2. **A signed joint venture** with a major mining partner.
3. **Funding for 2027 work**, and whether Seabridge issues new shares or settles the loan in stock.
4. **The KSM feasibility study**, targeted for the end of 2027.

## Bottom line

Seabridge still owns a giant gold-copper asset, and the court did not cancel its environmental certificate. But the ruling took away the certainty that the approval would not expire, with no date for a new decision, just as the company needs a partner and fresh funding. At about 2.4 times book, the market is now asking for proof of the timeline before it pays for the size of the asset.
