## The short version: a profit beat, a mine on budget, and a premium price

G Mining Ventures Corp. (TSX: GMIN) runs one producing gold mine, Tocantinzinho in Brazil's Pará state, and is building a much larger one, Oko Gold, in Guyana. After it reported third-quarter results on November 12, 2026, the stock jumped **9.9% to CA$46.21** on November 13, one of the two best moves on the S&P/TSX Composite that day, even though **revenue missed** what analysts expected. The shares are up about **52% over twelve months**, the best run in its peer group, and were the only name in that group to finish the last month in the green.

The proof that the market is already looking past the Brazil mine is the price. At about **24 times trailing earnings**, G Mining is the priciest of six TSX gold producers, against a group average near 14. The question for a new buyer is whether the market is already paying for the second mine. Below we go through the quarter, the Oko build, and how G Mining compares with Lundin Gold, Alamos Gold, IAMGOLD, Eldorado Gold and K92 Mining on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 12

After the close on **November 12, 2026**, G Mining reported results for the three months to September 30. The company reports in **US dollars**, and several figures are its own non-IFRS measures:

- **Gold output of 44,960 ounces, up 22% from the second quarter**, on record mining and milling rates and higher grades as the pit reached richer Phase 2 ore.
- **Revenue of US$172.7 million**, up from US$161.7 million a year earlier but short of the roughly US$209 million analysts expected, partly because it sold fewer ounces (43,110) than it produced.
- **Adjusted earnings of US$0.32 a share**, ahead of estimates near US$0.30; net income was US$99.5 million.
- **All-in sustaining costs of US$1,591 an ounce, down 6%** from the second quarter, with total cash costs of US$960 an ounce.
- **Free cash flow of US$80.0 million**, which the company is using to help pay for Oko.

Management expects the **fourth quarter to be the strongest of the year**, as a full quarter of higher-grade ore lifts output and lowers unit costs, and says 2026 production is tracking to the **middle of its 160,000 to 190,000 ounce range**. Cost guidance was left unchanged. For 2027 it guides to **200,000 to 235,000 ounces before any gold from Oko**, with all-in sustaining costs of US$977 to US$1,146 an ounce.

### The Oko build behind the multiple

Oko Gold is the reason the stock trades at a premium. By September 30, G Mining had spent **US$556 million, or 57%** of the roughly **US$973 million** initial budget, with about US$417 million left. The project is on budget and on schedule for **first gold in the second half of 2027**, and overall progress reached 38.4% on an earned-value basis. The July 2026 acquisition of G2 Goldfields added the neighbouring Oko-Ghanie deposits; an updated feasibility study for the larger, combined project is targeted for the second half of 2027.

The balance sheet held up through heavy spending. At quarter end G Mining had **US$137 million of cash and US$31 million of debt**, or about **US$106 million of net cash**, and it bought back 102,500 shares at an average of C$49.41 under its new buyback. The weak spots are the revenue miss, higher costs than a year ago (all-in sustaining costs were US$1,046 an ounce in Q3 2025) and a sharp drop in reported operating cash flow after working-capital outflows tied to the G2 deal.

## Business overview: G Mining vs gold producer peers

G Mining is a mine builder that became a producer. Its team built Tocantinzinho, which began commercial production in 2024, and is now repeating the job at Oko in Guyana, while studying a third project, Gurupi, back in Brazil. Today almost all of its revenue comes from one mine, so output, grade and the gold price drive results quarter to quarter.

On the TSX screener it sits in the **Non-energy minerals** sector. The peers are liquid TSX gold producers of similar size or growth profile: Lundin Gold (LUG), another single-mine producer, in Ecuador; Alamos Gold (AGI) and IAMGOLD (IMG), multi-mine mid-tiers; Eldorado Gold (ELD), a multi-mine producer with assets in Türkiye, Canada and Greece; and K92 Mining (KNT), a high-grade single-asset producer in Papua New Guinea. Agnico Eagle, Barrick and Kinross are many times larger, the royalty companies have a different business model, and the base-metal and uranium names in the same sector are not comparable, so they are left out.

## Why it matters: from one mine to a two-mine builder

The old narrative was **a single-mine producer in Brazil**. The new narrative is **a builder that can fund a much larger second mine largely from its own cash flow**. G Mining's pitch is that it builds mines on time and on budget; Tocantinzinho was the first proof, and Oko in Guyana is the bigger test.

This quarter showed the Brazil mine throwing off cash to help pay for that build while the company kept net cash. That matters for growth investors who want more gold without heavy debt. The open question is how much of Oko the 24-times multiple already assumes.

## Bull case

1. **More gold before Oko.** Management guides 2027 output to 200,000 to 235,000 ounces from Brazil alone, up from 160,000 to 190,000 in 2026.
2. **Lower costs.** 2027 all-in sustaining costs are guided below US$1,150 an ounce, well under this year's range.
3. **Balance sheet.** With about US$106 million of net cash and a buyback in place, the company can carry the rest of the Oko build.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Valuation.** At about 24 times trailing earnings and 13 times sales, much of the Oko story is already in the price.
2. **Build risk.** Oko still needs about US$417 million, and large mine builds can slip on cost or timing.
3. **Concentration.** Today almost all earnings come from one mine in Brazil.
4. **Gold price.** The stock leans on gold, which has been volatile this month.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter results, expected early in 2027.** Whether output and costs land near the middle of 2026 guidance.
2. **Oko progress.** Quarterly updates on spending, earned-value progress and any change to the US$973 million budget or the second-half 2027 first-gold date.
3. **Studies.** The Gurupi preliminary economic assessment, targeted by the end of 2026, and the updated Oko feasibility study in the second half of 2027.
4. **Cash returns.** Buybacks, and how much free cash flow the Brazil mine sends to the build.

## Bottom line

G Mining has earned some of its premium: a 22% jump in quarterly output, a profit beat, US$80 million of free cash flow and an Oko build that is on budget with net cash in the bank. The issue is price. After a 52% year, the stock trades at about 24 times trailing earnings, the highest of six TSX gold producers, while its earnings still come from one mine. The next tests are a strong fourth quarter and steady progress at Oko. This quarter backs up the build plan, but the stock already pays for a lot of future gold.
