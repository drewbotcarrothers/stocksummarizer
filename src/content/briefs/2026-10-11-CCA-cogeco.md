## The short version: a cheap telecom caught in a U.S. scare

Cogeco Communications (TSX: CCA), the Montreal-based cable and internet company behind the Cogeco and oxio brands in Canada and Breezeline in the United States, hit a **52-week low** on **October 9, 2026**. The stock fell **7.1%** to **C$48.70** as SpaceX's purchase of U.S. wireless spectrum knocked telecom stocks lower on both sides of the border. It was already down **12.5%** over the month to October 8 and **18%** over the year. The new story is that Cogeco is **a cheap, high-yield stock with a shrinking U.S. business and a lot of debt**.

On the October 8 screener data, Cogeco pays an indicated dividend yield of **about 7.5%** and trades at **about 6x free cash flow**, the lowest in its peer group. The question for a new buyer is whether that is value or a trap: can cash flow keep covering the dividend while the U.S. side shrinks? Below we go through the selloff, the target cuts, and how Cogeco compares with Bell, Rogers, TELUS and Quebecor on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the SpaceX spectrum deal and a telecom selloff

Late on **October 8, 2026**, SpaceX agreed to buy a nationwide block of U.S. low-band spectrum. The market reaction came the next day:

- SpaceX is buying Grain Management's nationwide **800 MHz** spectrum portfolio for **about US$8B**, to push its Starlink network into satellite-to-phone service. The deal still needs U.S. regulatory approval.
- On **October 9**, **Verizon fell 8.75%**, its worst day since 2002; **T-Mobile fell 13.3%** and **AT&T 9.8%**.
- The selloff spilled into Canada even though the deal doesn't touch Canadian airwaves. The **TSX communication services index fell 4.2%**; Cogeco dropped **7.1%** and Bell **5.5%**.

The deal doesn't change Cogeco's cash flow this year. What it changes is sentiment: investors are now pricing a new, well-funded competitor into every telecom stock. Cogeco is more exposed to the U.S. than most Canadian telcos, because its **Breezeline** unit serves customers in **13 states**. Two banks also cut their targets just before the drop: **Desjardins** from C$68 to **C$64** (Hold) on October 7, and **CIBC** from C$71 to **C$65** (Neutral) on October 8.

### The Q3 print behind the weakness

Cogeco's last results, released on **July 15, 2026** for the quarter ended May 31, showed the split clearly:

- **Revenue of C$696.7M, down 4.7%** (down 3.6% in constant currency),
- **Canada up 0.5%** to C$376.7M, with a third straight quarter of higher adjusted EBITDA,
- **U.S. (Breezeline) down 10.1%** to C$320.0M (down 7.8% in constant currency) on subscriber losses and price competition,
- **free cash flow of C$169M** in the quarter and **C$450M** over nine months; fiscal 2026 guidance reconfirmed.

The weak spot is the U.S. In the same quarter Cogeco recorded a **non-cash write-down of about C$1.7B after tax** (US$1.2B) on Breezeline's goodwill and intangible assets, which is why its reported results show a large loss. Management is leaning on wireless bundles and **welo**, a lower-cost digital brand now across its Ohio footprint, to steady the U.S. business.

## Business overview: Cogeco vs Canadian telecom peers

Cogeco sells internet, video, home phone and, increasingly, wireless service to about 1.6 million residential and business customers. Canada (mostly Ontario and Quebec) is the steady part; the U.S. business is the part under pressure. The company is controlled by Cogeco Inc. (TSX: CGO), which held 28.4% of the equity and about 80% of the votes as of May 31.

The TSX screener files Cogeco under **Consumer services**, next to restaurants, media and gaming names, with no cable or telecom peers. So we use a documented **closest-sector exception**: the four large, liquid names from the Communications sector. Bell (BCE), Rogers (RCI.A), TELUS (T) and Quebecor (QBR.A) all sell internet and TV in Canada, and Rogers and Quebecor compete with Cogeco directly in Ontario and Quebec. Sub-C$1B names and the parent Cogeco Inc. were left out.

## Why it matters: from steady dividend grower to cheap turnaround

The old story was **a steady cable company with a growing dividend**: Cogeco has raised its dividend for 20 straight years, by about 7% to 10% a year recently. The new story is **a cheap stock with a shrinking U.S. business and a lot of debt**, where the write-down has already admitted Breezeline is worth less than Cogeco paid.

The SpaceX deal adds a new worry on top: satellite and wireless competition for broadband customers. For income investors the yield is the attraction. The open question is whether free cash flow can keep covering it while the U.S. side shrinks.

## Bull case

1. **Priced for decline.** About 6x free cash flow leaves room for the stock to re-rate if the U.S. business simply stabilises.
2. **Canada is steady.** Canadian revenue and adjusted EBITDA are growing, and wireless is adding customers and cutting churn.
3. **A covered dividend.** Dividends use a bit under half of free cash flow, with more than half left for debt repayment.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Breezeline keeps shrinking.** U.S. revenue fell 10% last quarter, and more write-downs are possible if competition stays this intense.
2. **Debt.** Net debt of about 3.1x EBITDA limits flexibility if cash flow slips.
3. **New competition.** Satellite and fixed wireless could keep pushing broadband prices down, on both sides of the border.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 results on October 28, 2026**, after the close, with the call on October 29.
2. **Fiscal 2027 guidance**, especially for free cash flow.
3. **Breezeline subscriber trends** and the rollout of the welo digital brand in Ohio.
4. **The dividend growth policy** — any change to the pace of increases.

## Bottom line

Cogeco has a steady Canadian business and still generates real free cash flow, enough to cover a yield of about 7.5% with room left over. The issue is the U.S., where Breezeline is shrinking, and a C$1.7B write-down has already reset its value. At about 6x free cash flow the market is pricing in more decline, and the SpaceX deal added a new worry. The next test is the October 28 results and whether fiscal 2027 guidance shows free cash flow holding up.
