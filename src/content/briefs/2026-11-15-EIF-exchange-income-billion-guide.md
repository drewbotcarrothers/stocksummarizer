## The short version: a billion-dollar guide on a stock that already ran

Exchange Income Corporation (TSX: EIF), the Winnipeg company that owns northern airlines, an aircraft sales and leasing business, surveillance aircraft and a group of manufacturing businesses, reported a **record third quarter** after the close on November 12, 2026. More important, it guided to **C$1.0 to C$1.06 billion of adjusted EBITDA for 2027**, the first time its outlook has crossed the billion-dollar mark. The stock closed at **CA$127.72** on November 13, up about 2% on the day after trading as high as about C$134.

The catch is that the market got there early. The shares are up about **68% over twelve months** and sit about 7% below their 52-week high of C$136.75. At about **31 times trailing earnings**, the question for a new buyer is whether a billion-dollar year is already priced in. Below we go through the quarter, the new outlook, and how Exchange Income compares with its closest TSX transportation peers — TFI International, Air Canada, Mullen Group, Cargojet and Chorus Aviation — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 12

On **November 12, 2026**, after the market closed, Exchange Income reported results for the three months to September 30. All figures are in Canadian dollars, and several are the company's own non-IFRS measures:

- **Revenue of C$1.06 billion, up 10%** — the first quarter above C$1 billion.
- **Adjusted EBITDA of C$288 million, up 25%**, an all-time quarterly record.
- **Adjusted earnings of C$1.81 a share, up 24%**, and net earnings of C$96 million (C$1.70 a share), up 39%.
- **Free cash flow of C$219 million**, up 28% on the company's definition; free cash flow less maintenance capital spending was C$115 million, up 31%.

Both segments grew. Aerospace and aviation revenue rose 6% to C$720 million, while its adjusted EBITDA rose **20% to C$243 million**, helped by aircraft sales and leasing, higher returns on past growth spending, strong load factors and more flying on its own surveillance aircraft. Manufacturing revenue rose 21% to C$338 million and adjusted EBITDA rose **39% to C$62 million**, led by composite mats for US pipeline and power-line work. The weak spot was the multi-storey windows business, which management described as operating in very challenging market conditions.

Management stressed that the growth was **organic**: there were no material acquisitions in the year-over-year comparison.

### The guidance: a fourth raise, then a first C$1 billion

Exchange Income now expects 2026 adjusted EBITDA **at or above the top of its C$890 to C$920 million range**. That is the fourth time it has raised its 2026 outlook this year. For **2027**, it guides to **C$1.0 to C$1.06 billion**, based on current contracts and announced wins, and **excluding** future acquisitions, unannounced contracts or big new growth spending. The company says that is more than 20% above the midpoint of its original 2026 guidance.

The balance sheet improved at the same time. Leverage fell to **2.64 times**, the lowest in fifteen years, and the company has more than **C$2.2 billion of available liquidity**. After the quarter it announced two small deals: **TerraPro** (C$30 million, for its environmental access business) and **Cartel** (C$15 million, for its WesTower telecom engineering business). In the third quarter it also raised the annual dividend to C$2.88 from C$2.76.

The weak spots matter for the valuation below. The share count rose about **10%** after convertible debentures converted in 2025, which dilutes per-share growth. And on a standard basis, after all capital spending including growth capital, the screener shows free cash flow over the last twelve months as **slightly negative** (about −C$86 million), which is why the price to free cash flow cell is blank.

## Business overview: Exchange Income vs transportation peers

Exchange Income is a diversified, acquisition-oriented company with two segments. **Aerospace and aviation** includes regional and northern airlines (among them Perimeter, Calm Air, Canadian North, Keewatin Air and PAL Airlines), aircraft sales and leasing, and intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance flying. **Manufacturing** includes composite mats and bridges, precision manufacturing and engineering, and multi-storey window systems. Much of the aviation work is essential service under long contracts, which is why the company describes its earnings as resilient.

On the TSX screener, Exchange Income sits in the **Transportation** sector. TFI International (TFII) is the acquisitive trucking and logistics roll-up. Air Canada (AC) is the national airline. Mullen Group (MTL) is another acquisition-driven logistics group. Cargojet (CJT) is the air cargo carrier, and Chorus Aviation (CHR) combines regional flying with aircraft leasing. Canadian Pacific Kansas City and Canadian National are in the same sector but are railways worth more than C$100 billion each, so they are left out; Westshore Terminals, StorageVault and Algoma Central are less comparable.

## Why it matters: from deal-driven to organic growth

The old narrative was Exchange Income as **a steady dividend payer that grows by buying small companies**. The new narrative is **organic growth from planes and equipment it already owns**: management says the growth spending of past years is now paying off, and the 2027 guide is built mostly on existing businesses and announced contracts, such as the Future Air Crew Training program contract it finalized during the quarter.

That matters for income investors who also want growth. The open question is how much of it the 31-times multiple already assumes.

## Bull case

1. **A guide that excludes deals.** The 2027 outlook of C$1.0–1.06 billion is more than 20% above the midpoint of its original 2026 range and assumes no new acquisitions or unannounced contracts.
2. **Room to buy.** Leverage is at its lowest in fifteen years (2.64 times), with more than C$2.2 billion of liquidity for acquisitions and growth spending.
3. **Long-term tailwinds.** Northern development, defence surveillance and pipeline and power-line construction all support demand for its aviation services and composite mats.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Valuation.** At about 31 times trailing earnings and the highest EV/EBITDA in the group, a lot of good news is already in the price.
2. **Cash after all spending.** On a standard basis, free cash flow after all capital spending over the last twelve months was slightly negative; the business needs steady reinvestment in aircraft and equipment.
3. **Windows.** The multi-storey windows business is still in a tough market, tied to cautious developers.
4. **Dilution.** The share count rose about 10% after debentures converted, which dilutes per-share gains.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter results, expected early in 2027.** Whether 2026 adjusted EBITDA lands above C$920 million.
2. **New contract wins.** These would add to the 2027 guide, which excludes them.
3. **More acquisitions.** Integration of TerraPro and Cartel, and any larger deals using the C$2.2 billion of liquidity.
4. **The dividend.** Another raise, and whether payout ratios keep falling.

## Bottom line

Exchange Income has earned its premium: a record quarter, a fourth guidance raise for 2026, a first billion-dollar EBITDA outlook for 2027, record-low payout ratios and its lowest leverage in fifteen years. The issue is price. After a 68% year, the stock trades at about 31 times trailing earnings and near 11 times trailing EBITDA, both the top end of its transportation peer group, though its PEG of 0.56 is the cheapest. The next test is the fourth quarter and whether the 2027 guide starts to move higher with new contracts. This record quarter backs up the growth story, but the stock already prices in a lot of it.
