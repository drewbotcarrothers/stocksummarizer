## The short version: a record quarter, a big pop, and a long way back

Cargojet (TSX: CJT), Canada's overnight air cargo airline, reported **record third-quarter revenue of C$286.5M, up 30.3%**, after the close on November 11, 2026. Investors liked it: the stock jumped about **10% on November 12** to **C$108.77**, the biggest gain on the TSX Composite that day. Even before the report, the shares were up about **38% over twelve months** (C$95.12 on the screener).

But the longer picture is less flattering. Over **five years Cargojet is still down about 50%**, the weakest record in its transportation peer group, and at about **31 times trailing earnings** it already trades above the group average of about 24x. The question for a new buyer is whether the turnaround is real and durable enough to grow into that multiple. Below we go through the quarter, the UPS question, and how Cargojet compares with Air Canada, Chorus Aviation, Exchange Income, TFI International and Mullen Group on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: Q3 results on November 11

On **Wednesday, November 11, 2026**, after the market closed, Cargojet reported results for the quarter ended September 30. All figures are in Canadian dollars.

- **Revenue of C$286.5M, up 30.3%** from C$219.9M a year earlier, a third-quarter record. Higher fuel prices are passed through to customers as surcharges and booked as revenue; **excluding the fuel effect, revenue was C$265.9M, up about 20.9%**.
- **Adjusted EBITDA of C$90.2M, up 28.1%**, also a third-quarter record. The adjusted EBITDA margin was 31.5% versus 32.0%, with management estimating the fuel surcharge diluted the margin by about 190 basis points.
- **Adjusted earnings of C$1.21 a share**, up from C$0.32, and well ahead of the roughly **C$0.81** analysts expected. Net earnings rose 117% to C$19.1M (C$1.28 a diluted share).
- **Free cash flow of C$62.1M**, down from C$152.4M a year earlier, when the company had larger sale-and-leaseback proceeds.
- **Net leverage of 2.5 times**, reaching the company's long-term target range by September 30, a goal it had set for the end of 2026. Return on invested capital improved to 5.8% from 5.0% in the second quarter.

### Where the growth came from

All three businesses grew. The **domestic overnight network** brought in C$105.9M excluding fuel pass-throughs, up 6.3%, with on-time performance of 99.3%. **ACMI** flying (Cargojet supplies aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance to customers such as DHL) rose 10.8% to C$60.5M. The standout was **charter revenue of C$61.9M, up 88.1%**, helped by an added midweek flight to Liège in Belgium and its extension to Tel Aviv, flying to Central and South America, weekend charters to China, and **extra capacity for UPS** after its MD-11 disruption.

### Outlook and capital returns

Management said October domestic volumes stayed strong and it expects the **holiday peak to be broadly similar to 2025**. A new scheduled **Hamilton–Miami charter, three times a week, starts December 2**, using aircraft it already owns; the CEO said there is no new capital spending planned for it. The company bought back 76,749 shares in the quarter and has renewed its normal course issuer bid for another year. The catch: the extra UPS flying is expected to **drop significantly after the fourth quarter**, and management also warned that conditions across transportation remain challenging.

## Business overview: Cargojet vs transportation peers

Cargojet runs Canada's domestic overnight air cargo network from its Hamilton hub, flies ACMI routes for global integrators, and sells charter capacity when its Boeing 767 and 757 freighters would otherwise sit idle. Its revenue is mostly contracted, with fuel costs passed through to customers.

On the TSX screener, Cargojet sits in the **Transportation** sector. **Air Canada (AC)** is the closest airline comparison and runs its own cargo business. **Chorus Aviation (CHR)** provides regional flying and aircraft services. **Exchange Income (EIF)** owns aviation businesses, including cargo and charter airlines, alongside manufacturing. **TFI International (TFII)** and **Mullen Group (MTL)** are the large Canadian trucking and logistics carriers, the main alternatives for moving freight on the ground. The railways CN and CPKC were left out as far larger and not comparable, and StorageVault, Westshore Terminals and Algoma Central as non-freight or marine niches.

## Why it matters: from shrinking story to busier planes

For years, the Cargojet story was **slower growth, higher debt and a falling stock**. This quarter shifts it toward **better pricing, busier planes and lower debt**: net leverage is back in its target range, charter flying is filling gaps in the schedule, and management is pushing "revenue quality", in other words charging more on lanes where its capacity is scarce.

Management also says it can grow with the planes it already has, with no new aircraft planned unless a contract clears its return hurdle. That matters most for investors who want growth plus free cash flow, not just a dividend, because growth without new aircraft spending should turn more of each extra dollar of revenue into cash.

## Bull case

1. **Idle planes put to work.** Charter flights to Europe, South America and China use aircraft on weekends and during the day, when the domestic network does not need them, and the new Miami route adds connections without new capital spending.
2. **More cash for shareholders.** With net leverage back at 2.5 times, more free cash flow can go to dividend increases and buybacks under the renewed issuer bid.
3. **Room for the multiple to shrink.** At about 31x trailing earnings, continued earnings growth, as the forward P/E of about 23x implies, could bring the multiple down quickly.

## Bear case and risks

1. **The UPS work fades.** Extra flying for UPS is expected to drop sharply after the fourth quarter, and new routes need to replace it; management hopes to have that done by the first quarter of 2027.
2. **Flattered revenue and higher costs.** Fuel surcharges inflate reported revenue, and a new pilot contract effective July 1 has raised crew costs, with productivity benefits still to come.
3. **Already priced for growth.** After the 10% jump, Cargojet trades above the peer average on earnings, and management itself warned that conditions across transportation remain challenging.

## What to watch

1. **The holiday peak and fourth-quarter results**, which management expects to look broadly like 2025's peak.
2. **How fast new routes replace the UPS flying**, with management aiming for the first quarter of 2027.
3. **The Miami service launch on December 2** and the volume commitments behind it.
4. **Buybacks and the next dividend increase**, plus whether analysts lift targets toward the C$140–C$151 end of the range.

## Bottom line

Cargojet's third quarter was the cleanest in a while: record revenue and adjusted EBITDA, an earnings beat, and leverage back inside its target, all with the fleet it already has. The market rewarded it with a 10% jump. But the stock is still about half its level of five years ago and trades at roughly 31 times trailing earnings, above its transportation peers, so the price now assumes the turnaround continues. The next tests are the holiday peak and how quickly new routes replace the UPS flying.
