## The short version: a record quarter, and a stock past its targets

Mullen Group (TSX: MTL), the Okotoks, Alberta-based trucking and logistics company, was **CA$26.72** in our October 8 table, already up about **89%** over twelve months. On **Thursday, October 22**, it reported the best quarter in its history, and the shares jumped **11.5%** to close at **C$32.28**, a five-year high, on a day the S&P/TSX Composite fell almost 1%. Net income rose **55%**, revenue set a record, and margins widened in all four of its segments.

The catch is that the stock has now run past the analysts. The average one-year target is about **C$29.45** from 11 analysts, and the highest published target is C$32, so the shares sit about **9% above the average** and slightly above the top of the range. Those targets were set before this quarter, so revisions are likely. Below we go through the print, the Willson International deal, and how Mullen compares with its closest TSX transportation peers — TFI International, CN Rail, CPKC, Exchange Income and Cargojet — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the best quarter in Mullen's history

On **October 22, 2026**, before the open, Mullen Group reported its third-quarter results. All figures are in Canadian dollars:

- **Revenue of C$653.2M, up 16.3%** from C$561.8M a year earlier, and ahead of the roughly C$615M analysts expected.
- **Adjusted EPS of C$0.52**, up from C$0.38 and well ahead of the C$0.41 forecast. Diluted EPS was C$0.52 as well.
- **Net income of C$51.5M, up 55.1%**, a record for a third quarter.
- **Adjusted operating income before depreciation and amortization (OIBDA) of C$118.6M, up 23%**, with the adjusted margin widening to **18.2%** from 17.2%.

Every segment grew. Less-than-truckload revenue rose 10.4% and its OIBDA rose 20.3%, helped by denser lanes and dropping lower-margin freight. Logistics & Warehousing grew 13.5%, led by mining freight and cross-border shipments. The standout was **Specialized & Industrial Services, up 24.7%**, as pipeline hauling and stringing, water management and drilling-related work picked up, with its operating margin rising to 24.7%. The **U.S. & International logistics** unit grew **30.4%** as the U.S. freight market tightens.

Not all of the growth is organic, and it is worth being clear about where the extra C$91.4M of revenue came from: about **C$44.3M of internal growth**, **C$37.7M of higher fuel surcharges** as diesel prices rose, and **C$9.4M from acquisitions**. Fuel surcharges largely pass costs through to customers, so they lift revenue more than profit.

### The balance sheet and the Willson deal

Mullen ended September with **C$213.9M of cash** and **C$525M of undrawn bank credit**. Total net debt, as defined in its private placement notes, was 2.16 times operating cash flow (1.60 times after cash), well under the 3.5 times covenant. Its C$797.8M of private placement debt carries a fixed 6.1% rate and does not mature until 2034 and 2037.

Three days before the results, on **October 19**, Mullen agreed to buy **Willson International**, a customs brokerage and trade compliance firm founded in 1918 with more than 330 employees in Canada and the U.S. Willson is expected to add about **C$80M of annual revenue** at margins similar to the Logistics & Warehousing segment, needs little capital, and will be paid for with existing cash. Willson says the deal should take effect around **November 2**. It will become the 45th independently run business in the group and sits next to Mullen's existing customs broker, Cole International.

Chair Murray Mullen said the quarter came "when negative headlines were so prevalent" and that "it can be even better," pointing to the logistics needs of Canada's proposed nation-building projects. The weak spot was cash: **net cash from operating activities fell 23.9% to C$78.2M**, and weighted shares outstanding rose to about 96.3 million from 87.2 million a year earlier.

## Business overview: Mullen Group vs transportation peers

Mullen is a holding company for more than 40 independently run trucking and logistics businesses. It hauls less-than-truckload and truckload freight, runs warehouses and customs brokerage, and provides specialized services such as oversized hauling, pipe stringing, fluid hauling and water management for the energy, mining, forestry and construction industries in Western Canada. LTL and logistics are the steadier parts; the specialized and industrial work rises and falls with energy and project activity.

On the TSX screener, Mullen sits in the **Transportation** sector. We compare it with the largest freight-focused names in the same sector: **TFI International (TFII)**, the much larger trucking and LTL operator; **CN Rail (CNR)** and **CPKC (CP)**, the two Class I railways; **Exchange Income (EIF)**, an acquisition-driven aviation and manufacturing company; and **Cargojet (CJT)**, the air cargo carrier. Air Canada was left out as a passenger airline, and Westshore Terminals as a coal port.

## Why it matters: from sleepy yield stock to growth story

The old narrative was Mullen as a **sleepy, high-yield Canadian trucker**, tied to the domestic freight cycle and priced like one. The new narrative is **a diversified logistics group with growing U.S. and specialized businesses**: pipeline and drilling work is back, the U.S. logistics unit is growing fast, and acquisitions like Willson add steadier, less cyclical revenue.

Management also argues that Canada's large nation-building projects — pipelines, mines and infrastructure — will need a lot of trucking and specialized hauling. That is the story investors are now paying for. The open question is how much of it is already in a stock that has nearly doubled in a year.

## Bull case

1. **Operating leverage.** Net income rose 55% on revenue growth of 16%, as margins widened in every segment. If volumes hold, earnings can keep growing faster than sales.
2. **Balance sheet for more deals.** With C$214M of cash, C$525M of undrawn credit and long-dated fixed-rate debt, Mullen can keep buying businesses like Willson without new borrowing.
3. **Project tailwinds.** Major pipeline, mining and infrastructure projects could keep the Specialized & Industrial unit busy for years.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Priced past the targets.** At C$32.28, the stock is above every published target and at about 24 times expected earnings, so a lot of good news is priced in.
2. **Fuel-driven revenue.** About C$38M of the quarter's revenue growth came from fuel surcharges, which fade if diesel prices fall.
3. **Cash flow and dilution.** Operating cash flow fell 24% in the quarter, and the weighted share count is up about 10% from a year ago, so per-share growth trails headline profit growth.

## What to watch

1. **The Willson closing**, expected in early November, and any further acquisitions.
2. **Analyst targets** after the beat — whether they catch up with the price.
3. **Diesel prices** and fuel surcharge revenue in the fourth quarter.
4. **The dividend.** Whether the board raises the monthly payout after a record year.

## Bottom line

Mullen Group has earned the rally: a record quarter, wider margins in every segment, a well-funded balance sheet and a cash-paid acquisition that adds steadier revenue. The issue now is price. After an 89% gain to October 8 and an 11.5% jump on results day, the stock trades above every published analyst target, at about 24 times this year's expected earnings, with a yield that has slipped to about 2.6%. The next test is whether estimates and targets rise enough to catch up, and whether growth holds once fuel surcharges cool.
