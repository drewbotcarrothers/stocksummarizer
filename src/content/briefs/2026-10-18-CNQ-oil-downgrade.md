## The short version: a record quarter, a hot stock and a downgrade

Canadian Natural Resources (TSX: CNQ), Canada's largest oil and gas producer, was **CA$67.80** in our October 8 table and closed Friday, **October 16**, near **C$76.10**, a 52-week high, after Brent crude touched **US$110 a barrel** for the first time since May. Over the twelve months to October 8 the shares were up about **50%**. That leaves the stock roughly **10% above the C$68.75 average analyst target**, and two days before the oil spike Raymond James downgraded it to Market Perform, arguing the rally had run ahead of value.

The business itself has rarely looked better. Canadian Natural posted record production, record adjusted earnings and record adjusted funds flow in the second quarter, and it is closing in on the debt level at which it plans to hand **all** of its free cash flow back to shareholders. At about **12x trailing earnings** it is also the cheapest stock in its Canadian oil and gas peer group on that measure. The question for a new buyer is not whether Canadian Natural is a good company. It is how much of US$110 oil is already in a C$76 share price. Below we go through the downgrade, RBC's opposite call, the oil spike, the second-quarter print and how Canadian Natural compares with Suncor, Imperial Oil, Cenovus, Tourmaline and Whitecap on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: one broker steps back, another raises the bar

On **October 14, 2026**, Raymond James analyst Michael Barth cut Canadian Natural to **Market Perform from Outperform** and trimmed his price target to **C$69 from C$70**. The note did not question the quality of the assets. Instead it said there are "better ways to play that thematic in large cap," meaning Canada's improving oil policy backdrop, and it pointed to **wider discounts on Western Canadian heavy oil** as a headwind for a producer that sells a lot of heavy barrels.

A day earlier, **RBC** went the other way. On **October 13** it kept its Outperform rating and raised its target to **C$86 from C$79**, which is now near the top of the Street range.

Then the commodity moved. On **October 16**, Brent touched **US$110** a barrel for the first time since May. Canadian Natural rose about **3.2%** to **C$76.10**, a new 52-week high, and was the **biggest single contributor** to the S&P/TSX Composite's gain that day. In a single week the stock picked up a downgrade, a target raise and a new high, which is why it is today's brief.

### The Q2 print behind the rally

The rally rests on a strong second quarter, reported **August 6, 2026**:

- **Production** averaged about **1.68 million barrels of oil equivalent per day (BOE/d)**, a record and up about **18%** from a year earlier.
- **Adjusted net earnings** were a record **C$4.6 billion** (about C$2.20 a share), and **adjusted funds flow** was a record **C$6.9 billion**.
- About **C$4.0 billion** went back to stakeholders in the quarter: roughly **C$1.3 billion** in dividends, **C$1.1 billion** in buybacks and **C$1.6 billion** of net debt reduction.
- **Net debt** ended the quarter at about **C$14.5 billion**. Under the company's framework, once net debt reaches **C$13 billion**, it plans to return **100% of free cash flow** to shareholders.
- Management **raised production guidance** to **1,637,000–1,682,000 BOE/d**, helped by the roughly C$761 million Peace River acquisition.

The quarter also kept the dividend streak intact: the quarterly dividend is **C$0.625** (C$2.50 a year), and 2026 marks the **26th consecutive year** of increases. Large growth projects — including expansions at Jackfish, Pike 2, Jackpine and Horizon — remain on hold until definitive federal and Alberta agreements are signed, which the company has targeted for **November 2026**.

## Business overview: Canadian Natural vs the oil sands and Canadian producers

Canadian Natural is the largest company in this group by market value, at about **C$140 billion**, ahead of Suncor near C$114 billion. Imperial Oil and Cenovus are each worth about C$81 billion, while Tourmaline (the country's largest natural gas producer) and Whitecap (a light oil and condensate producer) sit near C$22–24 billion. Over the last twelve months Canadian Natural booked about **C$44.7 billion** of revenue and **C$11.8 billion** of net income, the most profit in the group even though Suncor, Cenovus and Imperial each report more revenue. That gap reflects Canadian Natural's model: it is mostly an upstream producer with long-life, low-decline oil sands assets, while the integrated peers carry large refining and marketing arms that add revenue at thinner margins.

## Performance: up about 50% in a year, but not the leader

Over the twelve months to October 8 Canadian Natural was up about **50%**. That is a strong year, but it trails Cenovus (about **+80%**), Suncor (about **+68%**) and Whitecap (about **+66%**). The month to October 8 was down about 2%, before the late run to C$76. Tourmaline, the gas-weighted name, is the laggard, up only about 2% over the year as gas prices lagged oil.

## Valuation: cheapest on trailing earnings

On trailing earnings, Canadian Natural is the **cheapest stock in the group**, at about **12.1x**, against a group average near **23x**. Tourmaline's ~65x multiple lifts that average a lot, so the more useful comparison is with Cenovus (~12.2x) and Suncor (~13x), which sit just above it. After the rally to about C$76, the trailing multiple is closer to **13.5x**.

On forward earnings Canadian Natural is about **11.9x**, close to the group average of about 11.3x. Cenovus is cheaper on forward earnings, price to sales and price to free cash flow, and Suncor screens cheapest on price to cash. Canadian Natural's price-to-sales (3.2x) and price-to-book (3.0x) are above average, which is the market paying for margin and asset life.

## Dividends: a 26-year streak and the second-highest yield

Canadian Natural pays **C$0.625 a quarter**, or **C$2.50 a year**, and 2026 is its **26th straight year** of dividend increases. Only Whitecap yields more in this group, at about 4%. The group average yield is about 2.9%.

## Why it matters: from oil bet to cash machine

The old story was Canadian Natural as a simple bet on oil prices. The newer story is a **low-cost, long-life cash machine**: record production, low-decline oil sands assets, a 26-year dividend streak and a plan to hand back all free cash flow once net debt reaches C$13 billion. That story has been rewarded, and the stock is at a 52-week high.

The open question is price. With oil at US$110, the market may already be paying for a lot of good news. Raymond James's downgrade is less a call that something is wrong and more a call that the easy part of the rally is done.

## Bull case

1. **Cheapest trailing P/E in the group.** About 12x trailing earnings, on record production and record adjusted earnings.
2. **Shareholder returns could step up.** Net debt is about C$1.5 billion away from the C$13 billion level at which the company plans to return 100% of free cash flow.
3. **Dividend and growth optionality.** A 26-year dividend growth streak, plus large oil sands growth projects that could restart once the federal and Alberta agreements are signed.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Above the target.** The stock is about 10% above the C$68.75 average target, and Raymond James argues the rally has run ahead of value.
2. **Oil sensitivity.** Earnings swing with oil. If Brent falls back from US$110, or the Western Canadian heavy oil discount widens, cash flow and buybacks shrink.
3. **Project timing.** New growth projects stay on hold until the government agreements are signed. A delay beyond November would push out future production growth.

## What to watch

1. **Q3 results on November 5, 2026.** Production, adjusted funds flow, buybacks and progress toward C$13 billion of net debt.
2. **The federal and Alberta definitive agreements**, targeted for November, which gate the growth projects.
3. **Oil prices and heavy oil differentials** after Brent's run to US$110.
4. **The C$68.75 average target.** Whether the stock holds above it and whether more brokers follow Raymond James or RBC.

## Bottom line

Canadian Natural has earned its premium reputation: record volumes, record cash flow, a long dividend streak and a clear path to returning all free cash flow. At about 12x trailing earnings it is still the cheapest stock in its Canadian oil and gas peer group on that measure. But after a 50% year and a fresh 52-week high at US$110 oil, the stock now sits about 10% above where the average analyst thinks it should be in a year. Our view is that the next leg depends less on the company and more on oil. The November 5 third-quarter report and the November agreement deadline are the next two tests.
