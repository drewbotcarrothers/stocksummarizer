## The short version: an income stock funding an AI build

BCE Inc. (TSX: BCE), the parent of Bell and Canada's largest telecom by revenue, traded at **CA$31.20** on the September 18, 2026 screener, down about **3%** over twelve months, **4%** over the last month and more than **51%** over five years. The new story is **AI infrastructure**: on September 14, Bell signed a Saskatchewan agreement that frames a path to **1.2 gigawatts** of AI Fabric data-centre capacity, on top of a dividend that still yields about **5.6%** after its earlier reset.

That second act costs money. Cap-X jumped about 41.5% in the second quarter and free cash flow fell 9.5%, so the question for a new buyer is whether the AI build can grow into contracted cash flow before it squeezes the dividend. Below we go through the Saskatchewan MOU, the Q2 Cap-X-versus-cash trade-off, and how BCE compares with its closest TSX telecom peers — Rogers, TELUS and Quebecor — on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: the Saskatchewan AI Fabric MOU

On **September 14, 2026**, Bell signed a **non-binding memorandum of understanding** with the Government of Saskatchewan. The agreement brings:

- up to **900 MW** of additional AI data-centre capacity in the province,
- on top of **300 MW already under contract**, with tenants including **CoreWeave** and **Cerebras**,
- a framed path to about **1.2 GW** — with public commentary describing a phased, multi-decade ecosystem investment of more than **C$50B**.

The key word is **non-binding**. The extra 900 MW still needs customers, power supply and permits, and the C$50B figure is a long-range framing rather than a Bell spending plan. What the MOU does change is the scale of the ambition: Bell is positioning AI Fabric as a second growth engine alongside its fibre and wireless networks. Analysts reacted quickly — **TD** raised its target to **C$40** and **Desjardins** to **C$43**, both with Buy ratings.

### The Q2 print: Cap-X up, free cash flow down

The MOU builds on a second quarter reported **August 6, 2026**:

- **Revenue C$6.18B, up 1.5%**, with internet revenue up about **14%** and roughly **55,000** fibre-to-the-home internet additions.
- **Adjusted EBITDA C$2.70B, up 1%**, and **adjusted EPS of C$0.65, up 3.2%**.
- **Cap-X of C$1.08B, up 41.5%** (17.5% of revenue), driven by Bell AI Fabric and U.S. fibre builds at Ziply Fiber.
- **Free cash flow of C$1.04B, down 9.5%** as spending rose.
- **Guidance reaffirmed:** revenue up 1–5%, adjusted EBITDA up 0–4%, capital intensity around **20%**, free cash flow of **C$2.1–2.3B** and an annualized dividend of **C$1.75**.

Bell expects about **C$1.3B** of 2026 Saskatchewan data-centre Cap-X, mostly in the second half, and has received its first tenant payment as part of about C$400M of set-up fees and prepayments. The weak spots are the balance sheet and cash: net debt is about **C$41.3B** against roughly C$24.2B of equity (debt to equity about 1.75), and the screener's trailing EPS of about C$6.72 is inflated by one-time items, which makes the trailing P/E of 4.6x **meaningless**. Forward earnings and free cash flow are the numbers that matter below.

## Business overview: BCE vs Canadian telecom peers

BCE sells wireless, fibre internet and TV services through Bell, runs Bell Media, and since its Ziply Fiber purchase also owns a U.S. fibre business in the Pacific Northwest. The steady part is the subscription base — wireless plans and home internet that customers pay for every month. The cyclical or uncertain parts are media advertising, price competition in wireless and, now, the large AI data-centre build.

On the TSX screener, BCE sits in the **Communications** sector. **Rogers (RCI.A)** is the closest peer in size, with wireless, cable and media assets. **TELUS (T)** is the third national carrier, now in its own dividend-reset story. **Quebecor (QBR.A)** owns Videotron and Freedom Mobile and is the fast-growing fourth player. Smaller and illiquid names in the sector were left out, and Cogeco, which the screener classes outside Communications, was not included.

If AI Fabric tenants start paying and capital intensity falls back, BCE's free cash flow should rise and that multiple should come down; if Cap-X stays near 20% of revenue, it won't.

## Why it matters: from income telecom to AI infrastructure

The old narrative was BCE as a **classic Canadian telecom income name** after a dividend reset: slow growth, heavy debt, a big yield. The new narrative asks investors to fund a **Cap-X-heavy AI Fabric build** while collecting a mid-5% yield, with named tenants on the first 300 MW and a path to 1.2 GW.

Income investors care whether free cash flow keeps covering the C$1.75 dividend through the build. Growth and AI-narrative investors care whether 1.2 GW becomes real contracted megawatts or stays mostly MOU optionality. The open question is timing: how long BCE can spend ahead of revenue before the balance sheet forces a choice.

## Bull case

1. **Named tenants and scale.** CoreWeave and Cerebras on the first 300 MW, prepayments already flowing, and a path toward 1.2 GW.
2. **Core operations still growing.** Q2 revenue, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS all rose, with internet revenue up about 14% and wireless postpaid churn near 1.02%.
3. **Clearer income math.** An indicated yield near 5.6% on a reset dividend, with analyst targets of C$40–43 implying meaningful upside if Cap-X converts.

## Bear case and risks

1. **Cap-X pressure.** Cap-X up 41.5% in Q2 and guided near 20% of revenue is already cutting free cash flow (down 9.5%).
2. **Non-binding MOU.** The extra 900 MW needs customers, power and permits, and the C$50B-plus framing is multi-decade and speculative.
3. **Weak tape, heavy leverage.** BCE is down more than half over five years, and net debt of about C$41.3B leaves little room for error.

## What to watch

1. **MOU conversion.** Whether the Saskatchewan megawatts turn into signed contracts, power agreements and permits.
2. **Second-half AI Cap-X** — about C$1.3B planned in Saskatchewan this year — versus tenant prepayments and set-up fees.
3. **Free cash flow vs guidance.** Whether 2026 lands in the C$2.1–2.3B range with capital intensity near 20%.
4. **The dividend.** Whether the C$1.75 annualized rate holds through the AI build, and whether targets near C$38 move toward the C$40–43 broker calls.

## Bottom line

BCE has a credible second act: named AI tenants, a path to 1.2 GW, a growing fibre business and core results that still edge higher. The Saskatchewan MOU makes the ambition bigger without yet adding contracted revenue. The issue is cash, not price. At about 12.5x forward earnings and a 5.6% yield, BCE looks cheap on most measures, but at about 11x free cash flow it is the priciest of its peers on the metric that pays the dividend. The next test is whether the second-half Cap-X brings matching tenant cash.
