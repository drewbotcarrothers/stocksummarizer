## The short version: the talks are real, the terms are not written yet

First Quantum Minerals Ltd. (TSX: FM) is a Toronto-listed copper miner whose biggest asset, the **Cobre Panama** mine, has been shut since November 2023, when Panama's Supreme Court ruled its operating contract unconstitutional after nationwide protests. The mine once supplied about **1.5% of the world's mined copper**. On the evening of **Monday, November 16, 2026**, Panama's president, José Raúl Mulino, announced that the government will open **formal talks with First Quantum on a new agreement** for the mine.

The stock jumped about 8% early on Tuesday, November 17, then closed **up 3.7% at C$30.90**, one of the few gainers on a day the S&P/TSX Composite fell 1.1%. Even so, it sits **about 40% below the average analyst target of C$51.95**. Below we go through the talks and Panama's conditions, the third quarter, and how First Quantum compares with Lundin Mining, Hudbay Minerals, Capstone Copper, Ivanhoe Mines and Ero Copper on size, returns, valuation and analyst targets.

## What happened: Panama opens formal talks on November 16

In a televised address on **November 16, 2026**, President Mulino said Panama will negotiate with Minera Panama, First Quantum's local subsidiary, on a possible restart of the mine. A government team led by Commerce and Industry Minister Julio Moltó will run the talks. The president set out five principles: the minerals belong to Panama, the country must get the maximum economic benefit, no company may decide on Panamanian territory, environmental standards must be the highest, and any agreement must comply with the Constitution and the law. Reported details include:

- **What gets negotiated:** jobs, economic benefits, environmental oversight and transparent records of what is mined, plus **an end to all arbitration claims** against Panama.
- **The conditions:** any deal must include a **closure plan paid for by the company, with no option to extend**, and it **will not be a mining concession**. The government will set the legal framework.
- **The process:** a **public consultation** will run alongside the talks and finish before any agreement. **No restart date** has been set.

First Quantum said on November 17 that it welcomes the process and will "engage constructively" on the basis of a government commission's report and the president's criteria. It estimates a reactivation could create **6,000 direct and 30,000 indirect jobs**. The talks follow a government-commissioned audit, completed in June, that gave the mine an overall compliance score of **88%**.

### How the stock reacted

The shares had fallen about **7.6% on Monday, November 16**, to C$29.81, on a day gold hit a one-year low and materials stocks slid. After the announcement they traded as high as **C$32.46** on Tuesday, then closed **up 3.7% at C$30.90** as the broader market sold off. The same day, one independent analyst cut the stock to Hold, arguing that a restart already looks priced in and that a firm closure timeline could cap how much copper is ever recovered.

### What Cobre Panama does today

The site is not mining. Since the second quarter of 2026 it has only been **processing ore that was already stockpiled**, and First Quantum's guidance for that program is **30,000 to 40,000 tonnes of copper in 2026**. Those stockpiles are expected to run out around mid-2027, so a new agreement is needed for anything more.

### The third quarter (October 28)

First Quantum reported its third quarter on **October 28, 2026**. All figures are in US dollars, and several are non-GAAP or supplementary measures:

- **Copper production of 108,959 tonnes, up 8% from the second quarter**, helped by the Cobre Panama processing program and higher output at Kansanshi in Zambia.
- **Revenue of US$2,086 million, up 38%** from the second quarter, on higher sales volumes and metal prices.
- **EBITDA of US$844 million** (Q2: US$400 million) and **adjusted earnings of US$0.29 a share** (Q2: a loss of US$0.13).
- **C1 copper cash cost of US$2.59 a pound**, or US$2.40 excluding Cobre Panama.
- **Net debt of US$5.31 billion**, down US$98 million in the quarter, with US$2.13 billion of liquidity.

Management **narrowed 2026 copper guidance to 405,000–435,000 tonnes** (from 405,000–475,000) because of lower grades at its Sentinel and Kansanshi mines in Zambia, kept Cobre Panama's guidance, and raised nickel guidance.

## Business overview: First Quantum vs copper miner peers

First Quantum mines copper, plus gold and nickel by-products, mostly in Zambia (Kansanshi and Sentinel), with Cobre Panama in Panama on care and maintenance and smaller operations elsewhere. It does not currently pay a dividend, so returns depend on the share price.

On the TSX screener First Quantum sits in the **Non-energy minerals** sector. The peers are liquid TSX-listed copper producers and developers: Lundin Mining (LUN), Hudbay Minerals (HBM), Capstone Copper (CS), Ivanhoe Mines (IVN) and Ero Copper (ERO). On **trailing earnings First Quantum looks very expensive, at about 110 times**, versus a group average of about 35. That is because profit over the last twelve months was small, after a weak first half that included hedging losses. On **forward earnings it is about 14 times**, a little above the group average of about 12. **Ero Copper is the cheapest on both trailing (about 8 times) and forward (about 6 times) earnings.** First Quantum trades at about **1.6 times book value**, a bit below the group average of about 1.8, and its price to free cash flow is blank because heavy spending has kept free cash flow near or below zero on the screener basis. Its PEG ratio is the lowest in the group, at 0.41, but that number leans on a large expected jump in earnings from a low base.

So the price leans on an **earnings recovery**, and Cobre Panama is the swing factor. A restart on workable terms would change the earnings base far more than any grade improvement in Zambia; tougher terms, or no deal, would leave investors relying on the Zambian mines alone.

## Why it matters: a stranded flagship gets a path back

The old narrative was **a miner with its flagship asset stranded by a court ruling and a political standoff**. The new narrative is **a miner in formal talks to restart it, on terms Panama will set**. Cobre Panama is one of the world's largest copper mines and First Quantum's biggest asset. Without a new agreement, output there ends when the stockpiles run out around mid-2027. With one, it could add hundreds of thousands of tonnes of copper a year on top of the Zambian mines; Kalkine reports the mine produced roughly 331,000 tonnes of copper in 2023, before it closed.

Timing matters too. Copper prices fell to their lowest level since July on November 17, with the London Metal Exchange three-month contract near US$13,211 a tonne, so the value of any restart depends on both the terms and the copper price when production returns.

## Bull case

1. **Talks are formal.** After years of dispute, Panama has opened negotiations, and the mine passed a government audit in June with a score of 88%.
2. **Output and cash flow.** A restart could sharply lift copper production and cash flow, which could help pay down US$5.3 billion of net debt.
3. **Priced near peers before any restart.** On forward earnings the stock trades at about 14 times, close to the group average, without a Cobre Panama restart in the base case.

## Bear case and risks

1. **No deal yet.** New terms could favour Panama, through state ownership or higher taxes and royalties, which JPMorgan calls a central issue.
2. **A fixed end date.** A closure plan with no extensions could limit how much copper is ever mined.
3. **Debt and copper.** Net debt is still about US$5.3 billion, and copper prices are falling.
4. **No dividend.** Returns depend on the share price, not income.

## What to watch

1. **The talks.** Progress and any draft terms for ownership, royalties, taxes and closure.
2. **The public consultation** in Panama, which must finish before any agreement.
3. **Arbitration.** What happens to First Quantum's arbitration claims against Panama, which the government wants resolved.
4. **Copper prices and 2027 guidance** for Sentinel and Kansanshi, where lower grades trimmed this year's outlook.

## Bottom line

Panama's decision to open formal talks is the clearest path back for Cobre Panama since the mine closed in 2023, and the stock rose on a down day. But the conditions are strict: a company-funded closure plan with no extensions, no concession, an end to arbitration and a public consultation, with no restart date. At about 14 times forward earnings and about 40% below the average target, the market is still waiting for a signed deal. The talks are a real step, but the payoff depends on terms that are not written yet.
