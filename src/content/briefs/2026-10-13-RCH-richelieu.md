## The short version: a sleepy distributor finally moves

Richelieu Hardware (TSX: RCH), the Montreal-based distributor of specialty hardware, cabinet and furniture components and renovation products, spent the last year as the laggard of its peer group. That changed on **October 7, 2026**, when the company reported third-quarter sales of **C$562.0 million**, up **12.6%** year over year, with **10%** internal growth and earnings per share of **C$0.53**, up about **23%**. The next session, **October 8**, the shares jumped **11.4%** to **C$38.63**.

Two things drove the move: first deliveries to a **major U.S. retail customer**, which pushed sales to retailers and home improvement superstores up **21%**, and the September 1 close of **Penrod**, the largest acquisition in Richelieu's history. Before the jump, the stock traded near **22x** trailing earnings with an indicated yield near **1.8%**. The average one-year analyst target is **C$39.25** — from only **two** analysts — which now sits less than a dollar above the post-earnings close.

Below we walk through the quarter, the acquisition, and how Richelieu stacks up against Toromont, Finning, Russel Metals, Wajax and Doman on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets.

## What happened: a Q3 beat with a U.S. retail kicker

On **October 7, 2026**, after the close, Richelieu reported results for the quarter ended **August 31, 2026**:

- **Sales of C$562.0 million**, up **12.6%** from C$499.2 million — **10.0%** internal growth plus **2.6%** from acquisitions (about **11.3%** in constant currency)
- **Canada** sales of C$300.2 million (**+10.2%**) and **U.S.** sales of US$186.7 million (**+12.9%** in U.S. dollars)
- **EBITDA of C$65.5 million**, up **14.8%**, for an **11.7%** margin — including about 60 basis points from a U.S. tariff refund
- **Net earnings** attributable to shareholders of **C$29.2 million**, up **22.4%**, or **C$0.53** per diluted share versus C$0.43 a year earlier
- Excluding the **C$0.04** per-share tariff refund, EPS was **C$0.49**, still up about **14%**

Consensus had been looking for roughly **C$534 million** of sales and **C$0.47** of EPS, so this was a clean beat on both lines.

### Where the growth came from

The headline driver was the **retail and home improvement superstore** channel, where sales rose about **21%**, helped by initial deliveries to a major customer in the United States. Sales to **manufacturers** — Richelieu's core kitchen-cabinet, furniture and woodworking customers — rose about **12%**. Management was candid that internal growth "primarily reflects price increases" to offset higher product and freight costs, which matters for how durable the growth rate is.

### Penrod: the biggest deal in company history

Richelieu completed two smaller acquisitions during the quarter, then closed **Penrod Hardware Division** on **September 1, 2026**. Penrod is expected to add about **US$70 million** of annual sales, making it the largest acquisition Richelieu has made. It adds U.S. scale in a market where Richelieu has been steadily building share.

### The tape reaction on October 8

The market liked it. On **October 8**, Richelieu was the top-performing stock on the TSX that session, rising **11.4%** to **C$38.63**. On **October 9**, National Bank Financial lifted its target to **C$39.50** from C$38.50 while keeping a Sector Perform rating.

## Business overview: Richelieu vs distribution peers

Richelieu imports and distributes more than 100,000 specialty products — decorative and functional hardware, panels, kitchen and closet components, flooring and finishing products — to cabinet makers, furniture manufacturers, contractors and retailers across Canada and the U.S. It is a high-SKU, relationship-driven distribution model rather than a manufacturer.

That is why we compare it with business-comparable **distribution** names rather than the manufacturers it sits beside in the data: Toromont (TIH), Finning (FTT), Russel Metals (RUS), Wajax (WJX) and Doman Building Materials (DBM). Doman, a building-products distributor, is the closest model; Toromont and Finning are larger equipment dealers.

## Why it matters

The old story was a dull, slow-growth distributor that lagged every peer. The new story is a **big U.S. retail account**, a **record-size deal** in Penrod, and growth back at **double digits**. For investors, the question is whether this quarter marks the start of a new growth phase — or one strong print that the market has already priced in, with the stock now sitting on the average target.

The mix shift is worth tracking too. The U.S. was already Richelieu's faster-growing region, and between the new retail account and Penrod, a larger share of sales will now come from south of the border and from big-box retail. That can lift growth, but it also brings more customer concentration and more exposure to U.S. housing and renovation spending than the traditional base of thousands of small Canadian cabinet and furniture shops.

## Bull case

1. The new **U.S. retail customer** is only in its first deliveries, so there is more volume still to come.
2. **Penrod** adds U.S. scale, and the U.S. remains Richelieu's biggest runway.
3. The stock still **trails every peer** over one year, so sentiment has room to improve if growth holds.

## Bear case

1. Much of the internal growth came from **price increases** to offset costs, not just more volume.
2. Big retail accounts can **squeeze margins**, and the **tariff refund** will not repeat.
3. The stock already sits **at the average target**, and coverage is thin at two analysts.

## What to watch next

1. **Fourth-quarter results** in January, and whether growth holds without the tariff refund.
2. How fast the **U.S. retail account** ramps up.
3. **Penrod integration** and its effect on margins.
4. Any **analyst target moves** after the rally.

## Bottom line

Richelieu finally gave the market a reason to re-rate it: a real sales beat, a new U.S. retail channel and its largest acquisition yet. The operating story looks stronger; the valuation case is now tighter, at about 25x earnings and right on the average target. Stack the peer tables, watch the January print for growth without the refund, and decide whether this is a breakout or a one-quarter pop — this is educational analysis, not a buy or sell call.
