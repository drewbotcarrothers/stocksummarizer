## The short version: a bigger company, a bigger dividend, a weaker stock

Tamarack Valley Energy Ltd. (TSX: TVE) is a Calgary-based oil producer focused on the **Clearwater**, a shallow heavy-oil play in northern Alberta. On **Thursday, November 19, 2026**, it closed its all-stock purchase of **Headwater Exploration**, the other big Clearwater producer. On the same day it set its **2027 budget** and raised its quarterly dividend **20% to 6 cents a share**.

The stock has not followed the good news. It closed at **C$11.65** on Friday, November 20, up 0.6% on the day, but it is down about **19.5% over the past month** as oil stocks sold off and the TSX had its worst week since March 2020. Over one year, though, it is still up about **56.6%**, the best in its peer group.

Below we go through the deal, the 2027 plan, how Tamarack compares with five other TSX oil producers on size, returns, valuation, dividends and analyst targets, and what to watch next.

## What happened: the Headwater deal closes (November 19)

### The deal

The two companies agreed to merge in **September 2026**. At closing, Headwater shareholders received **one Tamarack share for each Headwater share**, so Tamarack issued about **237.8 million new shares**. Legacy Tamarack holders own about two-thirds of the combined company and former Headwater holders about one-third.

Tamarack says the deal makes it the **only publicly traded producer focused on the Clearwater**, with more than 1,500 sections of land across the play, more than 3,000 potential drilling locations and run-rate Clearwater production of more than **80,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day** (boe/d). It now expects run-rate savings of **more than C$90 million a year**, or more than C$600 million over its development plan, from combining capital, operating, marketing and corporate costs.

### The 2027 budget

The combined 2027 capital budget is **C$600 million to C$650 million**, about **11% less** than the more than C$700 million the two companies spent in 2026. Even so, Tamarack targets **12% growth in Clearwater production** and expects to exit 2027 at more than **90,000 boe/d**, with average production of 87,000 to 91,000 boe/d. About 40% of the budget goes to waterflood projects, and sustaining capital is estimated at C$220 million to C$240 million.

### The dividend

The fourth-quarter dividend is **C$0.06 a share**, up **20%** from 5 cents in the third quarter and the second increase in 2026. The annualized rate of **24 cents** is **50% higher** than a year ago. Tamarack also says it has a **net cash** position, an undrawn bank line and strong free cash flow.

### The market reaction

The stock rose 0.6% to **C$11.65** on Friday, November 20, a day when the S&P/TSX Composite fell 2.5%. But it is still down about 19.5% over the past month, in line with most oil-weighted peers.

## Why it matters: the Clearwater and the waterflood

The Clearwater is a heavy-oil play in northern Alberta, around Marten Hills, Nipisi and Seal. Its wells are **shallow and low-cost**, and Tamarack is spending heavily on **waterflooding**: pumping water into the reservoir through injection wells to push more oil toward producing wells and slow production declines. By the end of 2027, it expects more than 60% of its Clearwater oil to be supported by waterflood, and its corporate decline rate to be below 15%.

That low reinvestment need is why Tamarack says it covers its funds-flow costs at about **US$37 a barrel** of WTI oil. It also plans to test new areas at **Seal** and **Pelican Lake**, which it says could add 10,000 to 15,000 boe/d at Seal alone if successful; that upside is not in its five-year plan.

## Bull case

- **More scale and savings.** The combined company has more land, more drilling locations and more than C$90 million a year in expected savings.
- **Low break-even, strong balance sheet.** A funds-flow break-even of about US$37 WTI and a net cash position give room for weaker oil prices.
- **A fast-growing dividend.** The annualized dividend is up 50% in a year, with a payout of only about 22% of profit.
- **Below-average multiples.** Tamarack trades below the group average on P/E and EV/EBITDA, and the average target is about 49% above the price.

## Bear case and risks

- **Oil prices.** Oil stocks just fell about 20% in a month, and Tamarack's results move with oil prices and the discount on Canadian heavy oil.
- **Integration.** Combining two companies can bring surprises, and the savings are targets, not results yet.
- **New shareholders.** Former Headwater holders now own about a third of the shares, and some may sell.
- **Low yield, high book multiple.** Its dividend yield is still the lowest of the payers here, and it trades at the highest price to book in the group.

## What to watch

1. **Fourth-quarter results**, with the first weeks of Headwater included, and the first full combined quarter in early 2027.
2. **Whether the savings show up** in operating and corporate costs.
3. **Results from Seal and Pelican Lake**, the new areas outside the five-year plan.
4. **Oil prices** and the price discount on Canadian heavy oil.

## Bottom line

Tamarack Valley is now the biggest pure Clearwater producer, with a lower 2027 budget, a growth target of 12% and a dividend that has risen 50% in a year. On earnings, it trades below most of its peers, and analysts are unanimous Buys. But the stock fell with the rest of the oil patch this month, and it carries the highest price to book in the group. Bigger and better paid, Tamarack still depends on oil prices to set the tone.
